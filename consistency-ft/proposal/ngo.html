Ngo × consistency-ft

Richard Ngo's recent agency program and our coherence-finetuning project turn out to share one spine: Garrabrant induction. His theory says what coherent beliefs are for a composite agent; our training is a way to buy them for a single model. Six connections, mapped.

The six connections

exact

RLEF is amortized logical induction

The LI criterion — no efficient bounded-risk trader makes unbounded profit — is our reward, idealized. Our LP adversary is a restricted trader; a model at LI's fixed point scores zero on the whole benchmark. Strengthening the adversary stepwise (memory, conditional strategies, cross-tuple portfolios) is the principled roadmap for new checks.

framing

Incoherence = a market with no arbitrageur

Coalitional agency says a composite agent's beliefs are market prices. gpt-oss pricing P and ¬P both at 0.10 (buy both for $0.18, collect $1) is an unarbitraged bag of heuristics. Training installs the arbitrageur — and the scale-free claim means the same objective applies to multi-agent stacks.

hazard

Self-fulfilling beliefs need quarantine

Ngo: actions and identity are beliefs that make themselves true. For propositions about the model's own outputs, coherence pressure does fixed-point selection — and stable fixed points are provably not optimal ones (performative prediction). v0 trains on world-propositions only.

framing

Our λ is his drive/anchor dial

Goals as drives (upward pressure on credences) balanced by anchors (evidence) is structurally our loss: coherence pressure + λ·anchor. Strong drive with a weak anchor distorts beliefs — that's our A₀ collapse arm, predicted by his frame. The λ sweep is a drive/anchor phase diagram.

hazard

Soft thresholds or broken self-trust

LI's self-trust theorems hold only with continuous threshold indicators; sharp conditionals fail on self-referential sentences. Reward shaping in the RL loop should avoid hard cutoffs — a free, theorem-backed design rule.

open lane

The dynamic check nobody has built

LI's martingale theorem: today's probability equals today's expectation of the future probability. Paleka's ExpEvidence check is the static version; the dynamic one — does more chain-of-thought move beliefs in expectation? — is unmeasured and untrained for LLMs. The most natural check for reasoning models, and unclaimed.
