# E1: how much of the ~1 nat is the information floor? (opus-mats, 2026-10-21)

**TL;DR.** floor_none = **0.685 nats [0.636, 0.735]**. The 3k-step bar's 1.09 at keys none splits into **0.69 floor + 0.40 model failure**. Training and width cut the model failure to **0.09-0.12** (150k steps or 256x6), with no train/dev gap, but the curve is flattening. Kelvin's question: at keys none, yes, the bar was mostly undertrained. At keys full (floor = Bayes = 0), though, the best bar is still **0.77 nats** from Bayes and gains only 0.02 from being given B_i. It does not learn the GLS inversion.

**Floor** (`scripts/e1_floor_none.py` → `results/explore/e1-floor-20261021/floor.json`, repo inconsistency-search, branch `e1floor`). Exact posterior of theta given every s_i, A_i and R_i: a Gaussian mixture over the unknown B_i, weighted by the likelihood of s (importance sampling, K=8192, the same 2000 dev episodes as every number below, regret = -log q(theta) - Bayes NLL as in report.py).
- **R_i alone does not fix sigma_i** (any sigma_i^2 < lambda_min(R_i) splits R_i), so floor_none marginalises sigma_i | R_i as well as Q_i ~ Haar O(2): **0.685 [0.636, 0.735]**.
- Rotation only (sigma_i known, the brief's definition): 0.635 [0.586, 0.681].
- Best Gaussian forecast (moment-matched mixture), the floor for a Gaussian-output model like the transformer: 0.708 (sigma unknown) and 0.679 (sigma known).
- Checks: with r=0 and with N=1 the floor equals Bayes to 1e-15. Simulation-based calibration of the sigma | R sampler passes (p=0.28). Quadrupling K moves the floor by +0.000 [-0.005, +0.006]. Median ESS is about 3000 of 8192.
- Reference: the product of local posteriors, ignoring correlations (`sum_logodds`), scores 0.827 [0.770, 0.884].
