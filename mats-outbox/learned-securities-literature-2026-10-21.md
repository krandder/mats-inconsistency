# Learned securities for non-separable information: what the literature says (opus-mats, 2026-10-21)

Kelvin's ask (10-21): traders see private evidence and send short messages (trades) to a shared market state over Y and latent variables. Under XOR/parity no single trader moves the price of Y, so the system must learn auxiliary securities. "What does the literature say about all of these ideas and problems?" ML side first, market side second. The design under test is `learned-securities-design-2026-10-21.md`.
**Not repeated here.** Message compression, the CEO problem, D-VIB, emergent communication under bandwidth limits and LLM aggregation are in `compressed-aggregation-literature-2026-10-21.md`. Parity limits of attention (Hahn), factor-graph GNNs (FGNN), NEBP, BPNN, TarMAC, Ostrovsky and the forecast-aggregation impossibility results are in `agg-literature-map-2026-10-21.md`.
**Verification.** All arXiv ids were checked on export.arxiv.org today (title, first author, year). DOIs were checked on Crossref. The three NeurIPS/JMLR papers without arXiv ids were checked on their proceedings pages via Exa. Counts in brackets are Semantic Scholar citation counts as of today, or Crossref where marked. Levels: well-cited ≥300, moderate 50–300, niche <50. Content comes from the abstracts. **(recall)** marks a claim from memory, and **(ours)** marks my own inference, not the paper's. `recall` (hub) has nothing earlier on this topic.

## 0. The condition every result below refers to (ours, elementary)
Local multiplicative trades on a latent Z reach the Bayes posterior when two things hold. (a) Each trader's evidence depends on the rest only through the latents in the clique it trades: P(X|Z) = ∏ P(X_i|Z_{c(i)}). (b) Z screens Y off from the evidence: P(Y|X) = Σ_z P(Y|z) P(z|X). Then P(z|X) ∝ P(z) ∏ factor_i, which is a product of experts, i.e. exactly an LMSR whose trades are log-potentials.
Z = X always works but is too wide. **"Learning securities" is the search for the narrowest Z, within junction-tree width w, that satisfies (a) and (b).** For k-bit noisy parity, Z = the k bits in a chain of partial parities (width 2) works. The exact Bayes answer is Gallager's check-node rule, tanh(L_Y/2) = ∏_j tanh(L_j/2), where L_j is trader j's log-likelihood ratio for its bit. So Arm C's oracle and the `bayes` bar are both closed form.

## 1. ML side
**1a. Synergy: the formal name for non-separable information**
- **Williams & Beer 2010**, arXiv:1004.2515 [802, well]. Splits I(Y; X1,X2) into redundant, unique and synergistic parts; XOR is the canonical pure-synergy case. → Gives the quantity that Arm A (a market on Y alone) cannot aggregate. Report it for each zoo environment.
- **Bertschinger et al. 2014**, arXiv:1311.2852 [372, well]. A widely used definition of unique information, and from it synergy (BROJA), computed by convex optimisation (recall). → An exact, computable synergy score for the discrete zoo.
- **Rosas et al. 2019**, O-information, arXiv:1902.11239 [267, moderate]. A signed scalar for many variables: negative values mean synergy dominates. It scales where the PID lattice explodes. → The x-axis for N > 3 traders. Predicted: Arm A's regret grows as O-information falls (ours).
- **Liang et al. 2023**, arXiv:2302.12247 (NeurIPS) [127, moderate]. PID estimators that scale to high-dimensional multimodal data. They validate them on synthetic data with known PID and use the scores to choose fusion models. → Off-the-shelf synergy estimates for continuous environments (E1–E4). **Pakman et al. 2021**, arXiv:2102.00218 [43, niche], estimates unique information for continuous variables.
- **Liang et al. 2023, FactorCL**, arXiv:2306.05268 (NeurIPS) [141, moderate]. Shows that contrastive multi-view learning assumes "multi-view redundancy" and so drops task information that lives in only one view. → Self-supervised pretraining of the trader encoders would throw away exactly the parity signal. Train them on the outcome instead.

**1b. Learning parities with gradients: what it predicts for learning which traders to combine**
- **Kearns 1998**, SQ model, doi:10.1145/293347.293351 [1058, well]; **Blum, Kalai & Wasserman 2003**, doi:10.1145/792538.792543 [Crossref 386, well]. Parities are hard in the statistical-query model, which covers gradient methods, and noisy parity (LPN) has no known polynomial algorithm. → If the k relevant traders out of N must be found from Y alone, the search costs about N^k.
- **Shalev-Shwartz, Shamir & Shammah 2017**, arXiv:1703.07950 [235, moderate]. On parity-like targets the gradient carries almost no information about the target, so training stalls. → Concretely, for the market chain (ours): ∂ log p(y) / ∂L_1 is proportional to ∏_{j≠1} tanh(L_j/2). It is near zero while the other traders are still uncertain, as they are at initialisation.
- **Barak et al. 2022**, arXiv:2207.08799 (NeurIPS) [222, moderate]. SGD does learn k-sparse parities of n bits, after about n^O(k) steps, with a long plateau and a sudden jump. During the plateau, hidden progress (a Fourier gap) accumulates. → Expect flat regret curves followed by a jump, so run long and log a progress measure. With N ≤ ~30 and k ≤ 3 this is affordable.
- **Edelman et al. 2023**, arXiv:2309.03800 [14, niche]. Width acts as parallel search ("lottery-ticket" neurons), and sparse initialisation cuts the samples needed. → Run several candidate latent links in parallel and keep the ones that fire, rather than one structure.
- **Abbe, Boix-Adserà & Misiakiewicz 2022**, merged staircase, arXiv:2202.08658 (COLT) [161, moderate]. SGD learns a sparse function in O(d) samples roughly iff it can be reached by adding one coordinate at a time to monomials already learned. → Pure parity fails this test, but biased bits or a partial parity that is already predictive pass it. A zoo knob for the bias should move a learned structure from failing to working.
- **Wies, Levine & Shashua 2023**, arXiv:2204.02892 (ICLR) [56, moderate]; **Kim & Suzuki 2025**, arXiv:2410.08633 (ICLR) [56, moderate]. Supervising the intermediate parities makes parity learnable. Kim & Suzuki: one gradient step with teacher forcing, and efficient end to end if intermediate steps are self-checked. → The simulator knows the bits b_j and the partial parities. A curriculum that supervises the latent securities, then anneals that supervision away, is the known fix. It also measures how much of the difficulty is search rather than representation.
- **Abbe, Bengio & Lotfi 2023**, degree curriculum, arXiv:2301.13105 (ICML) [83, moderate]. Networks learn low-degree interpolators, and a curriculum over degree helps. → Train k = 1, 2, 3, … in sequence and reuse the latents.

**1c. Vertical federated learning and split learning: the closest ML analogue of learned messages**
- **Gupta & Raskar 2018**, arXiv:1810.06060 [820, well]; **Vepakomma et al. 2018**, arXiv:1812.00564 [1040, well]. Split learning: each party runs the lower layers on its own data and sends the cut-layer activations; the whole network is trained end to end. → This is a one-shot learned-message aggregator with no market. It is the D-VIB baseline already ranked #2 in the compression note.
- **Liu et al. 2024**, VFL survey, arXiv:2211.12814 (TKDE) [429, well]; **FedBCD**, Liu et al., arXiv:1912.11187 [79, moderate]. In VFL, parties hold different features of the same samples, send embeddings to a server's top model, and take several local steps per communication round. → This is exactly the trader/aggregator split. The VFL literature measures communication rounds and privacy, not distance to Bayes.
- **Castiglia et al. 2023, C-VFL**, arXiv:2206.08330 (ICML) [85, moderate]. Quantising or top-k-sparsifying the embeddings proves convergence while the compression error stays bounded, and cuts communication by more than 90% without significant loss of accuracy. → b-bit embeddings are cheap on ordinary (mostly separable) data. Whether that holds under synergy is untested (§3).
- **ICAFS, Jin et al. 2025**, arXiv:2504.10851 [2, niche]. Says earlier VFL feature selection overlooked interactions across clients. → The only VFL work I found that names cross-party interactions. Nobody I found tests XOR across parties. **VFLAIR**, arXiv:2310.09827 [19, niche], is a VFL benchmark library, if a standard harness is wanted.

**1d. Learned communication**
- **Foerster et al. 2016, DIAL/RIAL**, arXiv:1605.06676 [2034, well]. Agents learn messages by backpropagating through a noisy channel during centralised training. → The LMSR update is a softmax of summed log-potentials, so it is differentiable. The whole trade sequence can be trained DIAL-style, with quantisation to b bits only at execution. Sizes and gating are in the compression note §4. I found no emergent-communication paper whose task is parity of private bits.

**1e. Product of experts, latent structure, factor graphs**
- **Hinton 2002**, PoE, doi:10.1162/089976602760128018 [5732, well]. → An LMSR with multiplicative trades on a shared latent is a PoE (§0).
- **Wu & Goodman 2018, MVAE**, arXiv:1802.05335 (NeurIPS) [535, well]. Infers q(z|x_1..x_n) ∝ p(z) ∏ q(z|x_i), with one expert per modality, and trains on random subsets of modalities. → This is Arm B with a continuous latent. The subset training is ready-made robustness to absent traders. **Shi et al. 2019, MMVAE**, arXiv:1911.03393 [391, well], argues that PoE lets overconfident experts dominate (recall). → Calibrate each trader's trade size.
- **Gallager 1962**, LDPC, doi:10.1109/TIT.1962.1057683 [Crossref 5565, well]; **Körner & Marton 1979**, doi:10.1109/TIT.1979.1056022 [379, well]. Parity checks are decoded exactly by BP; to compute the XOR of distributed sources, linear (parity) codes beat Slepian–Wolf rates. → For parity, the right messages are the parity-structured ones. Arm C is a single check node.
- **Choi, Tan, Anandkumar & Willsky 2011**, arXiv:1009.2722 (JMLR) [278, moderate]. Learns minimal latent trees from pairwise "information distances". → It cannot find parity latents (ours): pairwise statistics between the parity bits and Y are zero, so the distances carry no signal. Structure learning must use the outcome loss or higher-order statistics.
- **Elidan & Friedman 2005**, JMLR 6:81–127, jmlr.org/papers/v6/elidan05a [101, moderate]. Learns where hidden variables go, and their cardinality, in a Bayes net, using an information-bottleneck relaxation that anneals into EM. → The closest classical method to "grow a latent when trades want a scope". Use it as the non-neural structure-learning baseline.
- **Murphy et al. 2019, Janossy pooling**, arXiv:1811.01900 (ICLR) [223, moderate]. Permutation-invariant pooling restricted to k-ary interactions. → A k-ary set function is the minimum needed to see k-parity in a single pooled readout. It is the set-model counterpart of a width-k clique.
- **Yoon et al. 2018**, arXiv:1803.07710 [112, moderate]. GNN message passing trained to do inference in graphical models, and it beats loopy BP. → A learned inference engine, if the junction tree gets too wide. NEBP and BPNN are in the map.

**1f. Multi-view, co-training and information bottleneck**
- **Blum & Mitchell 1998**, co-training, doi:10.1145/279943.279962 [Crossref 3374, well]; **Federici et al. 2020**, multi-view IB, arXiv:2002.07017 [357, well]; **Tian et al. 2020**, InfoMin, arXiv:2005.10243 [1644, well]. Each assumes every view, or the information shared between views, suffices for the label. Multi-view IB explicitly discards what is not shared. → Under XOR the information shared between the views is zero, so these objectives remove the signal by design. They are the wrong prior for trader encoders, and a good negative control.

## 2. Market side
- **Ostrovsky 2012**, doi:10.3982/ECTA8479 [Crossref 91, moderate]. Separable securities aggregate in every equilibrium (see the map). → Arm A should fail and Arm B should not.
- **Chen, Ruberry & Wortman Vaughan 2012**, "Designing Informative Securities", arXiv:1210.4837 (UAI) [12, niche]. Informativeness can require securities on events the organiser does not care about, and the design depends on the traders' signal structure. → The theoretical precedent for latent securities. It designs them by hand from a known signal structure; learning them is the gap.
- **Chen et al. 2010**, "Gaming Prediction Markets", doi:10.1007/s00453-009-9323-2 (Algorithmica) [112, moderate]. Under LMSR, truthful betting is an equilibrium (a PBE, and the unique weak PBE) when signals are independent given the outcome. It is not even a weak PBE when signals are unconditionally independent, which is the XOR case. → On a market in Y alone, XOR traders have reason to bluff or wait. With a security on each trader's own bit, its signal becomes conditionally independent (ours), so incentive compatibility might be restored as well as expressiveness. Not proven.
- **Chen & Waggoner 2016**, "Informational Substitutes", arXiv:1703.08636 (FOCS) [35, niche]. Substitutes give immediate aggregation in the best equilibria; complements (XOR) give the worst, delayed aggregation. The paper also studies how to design for substitutes. → Kelvin's "trader 1 subsidises a market on trader 2's bit" is an attempt to turn complements into substitutes; start from this paper.
- **Pennock & Xia 2011**, arXiv:1202.3756 (UAI) [21, niche]; **Chen et al. 2008**, arXiv:0802.1362 [74, moderate]. Pricing a general combinatorial LMSR is #P-hard. Pennock & Xia characterise the securities that preserve a decomposable Bayes net, and show that basic securities preserve structure only on decomposable graphs. → Gives the rule for growing structure: a learned security must sit inside an existing clique, otherwise re-triangulate and pay in width.
- **Storkey 2011**, "Machine Learning Markets", arXiv:1106.4509 (AISTATS) [46, niche]; **Storkey et al. 2012**, arXiv:1206.6443 [25, niche]. Depending on the agents' utility, market equilibria implement product-of-experts or mixture-of-experts pooling, local potentials and message passing. → Kelvin's clique-trading LMSR is a known form of message passing. The traders' utility chooses the pooling family.
- **Barbu & Lay 2012**, artificial prediction markets, arXiv:1102.1465 (JMLR) [42, niche]. Classifiers trade on the outcome, budgets are updated on training data, and the result is a maximum-likelihood estimator that generalises boosting and logistic regression. → It learns how much to trust each trader, not new securities. That is the Arm A class, which cannot represent XOR (ours).
- **Hu & Storkey 2014**, arXiv:1403.0648 (ICML) [24, niche]; **Chen & Wortman Vaughan 2010**, arXiv:1003.0034 (EC) [111, moderate]. Agents optimising their own objectives drive the market toward a global objective, and cost-function markets are no-regret online learners (LMSR is FTRL with entropy). → The securities are the learner's feature space, so "learning securities" means learning the market's features (ours). The telescoping of the summed losses in the design is the special case for log loss.
- **Frongillo & Reid 2015**, NeurIPS, proceedings.neurips.cc …/66be31e4c40d676991f2405aaecc6934 [18, niche]. Sequential trading is randomised subspace descent, with convergence rates. → Each trader is one block step. With only Y securities, under XOR the block gradient at the prior is zero, so the descent stalls: Arm A in optimisation terms (ours).
- **Abernethy & Frongillo 2011**, arXiv:1111.2664 (NeurIPS) [56, moderate]. Participants bet on updates to a published hypothesis and are paid by how much each update improves the loss on a test set. → Pay for proposing a latent security by the held-out improvement in Y's log loss. This is Kelvin's "value of an aux = Y better priced given it", with the incentive analysis already done.
- **Waggoner, Frongillo & Abernethy 2015**, NeurIPS, proceedings.neurips.cc …/7af6266cc52234b5aa339b16695f7fc4 [20, niche]. A market for buying data, with a bounded budget, for Bregman losses, plus a differentially private version. → Bounds what a market maker loses by subsidising auxiliary markets.
- **Conitzer 2009**, arXiv:1205.2654 (UAI) [31, niche]. Scoring-rule mechanisms and their links to cooperative game theory. → Prior art for Kelvin's Shapley-style payout; read it before deriving one.
- **Kong & Schoenebeck 2019**, arXiv:1605.01021 [22, niche]; **Srinivasan, Karger & Chen 2023**, arXiv:2306.04305 [6, niche]. Pay for unverifiable signals by mutual information with peers, or by cross-entropy against a later, better-informed reference agent (self-resolving markets). → The latent z_j never settles. One answer to Kelvin's "?? how": settle z_j against the final market's posterior on z_j.

## 3. Summary
**Known, so reuse it.**
- (1) XOR is pure synergy, and estimators exist (BROJA, O-information, Liang et al.). Report synergy per environment.
- (2) Exact Bayes for noisy parity is the tanh check-node rule, decodable by BP on a width-2 chain (Gallager).
- (3) Local trades are exact when a latent makes the evidence conditionally independent and screens off Y (§0; PoE/MVAE; Storkey). Junction-tree LMSR is the tractable class, with trades on cliques (Pennock & Xia).
- (4) Aggregation can need securities on events nobody cares about (Chen–Ruberry–Vaughan), and separable securities aggregate (Ostrovsky).
- (5) End-to-end learned messages with compression: split learning / VFL / C-VFL / D-VIB.
- (6) Paying by held-out loss improvement (Abernethy–Frongillo; Waggoner et al.).

**Known to be hard, and why.**
- (1) Learning a parity from the outcome alone: gradients carry almost no signal (SQ, Shalev-Shwartz), the cost is about N^k with a plateau (Barak), and noisy parity is LPN. In the market chain, the gradient on one trader is a product of the others' tanh terms. Known fixes: supervise intermediate parities (Wies; Kim–Suzuki), curricula (Abbe), staircase structure, width as parallel search (Edelman).
- (2) Structure learning from pairwise statistics cannot see parity latents (Choi et al., ours), and hidden-variable learning by IB/EM is a local search (Elidan–Friedman).
- (3) With per-trader profit on Y, only the last trader in a parity chain earns anything, so purely local losses give the others no learning signal (ours). Strategically, complements delay aggregation (Chen–Waggoner) and truthful betting fails (Chen et al. 2010).
- (4) General combinatorial pricing is #P-hard, so learned structure must stay within width w.
- (5) Multi-view and co-training objectives discard synergy by design.

**Open: none found in these searches (arXiv, Semantic Scholar, Exa; 2026-10-21).**
- (1) Learning a market's securities (latents plus structure) end to end from the outcome loss, scored against exact Bayes. Earlier work designs securities by hand (Chen–Ruberry–Vaughan) or learns only trader weights or utilities (Barbu–Lay, Storkey).
- (2) Using synergy (PID or O-information) to predict how much a market on Y alone fails to aggregate. This would link Ostrovsky's separability to PID quantitatively.
- (3) VFL or split learning with XOR-type cross-party synergy under b-bit embeddings.
- (4) An incentive analysis for settling never-resolved latent securities (self-resolving or peer-prediction settlement) when the structure itself is learned.
- (5) A learned junction-tree market against the attention-based trader-transformer at equal b on parity.
