# Compressed aggregation: how others solve it, and how to measure ours (2026-10-21)

Kelvin's ask (10-21): each trader sends a message smaller than its evidence; measure how good that compression is for aggregation, and compare with state-of-the-art ways of aggregating rollouts. This builds on agg-literature-map and trader-transformer-literature (their items are not repeated).
**Verification.** Every arXiv id was checked on export.arxiv.org today (title, first author, date). Every DOI was checked on Crossref. Citation counts are Semantic Scholar counts as of today: well-cited ≥300, moderate 50–300, niche <50. Content claims come from abstracts or from paper bodies I grepped (CoA, Halawi); claims marked (recall) are from memory. `recall` (hub) has nothing earlier on this topic.
**Notation.** b = message bits per agent. w = message dimensions. The "plug" line says how the method becomes an aggbench baseline at width w, with its effort: S = under a day, M = 1–3 days, L = more than 3 days.

## 0. One identity that sets the yardstick
If the aggregator is Bayes-optimal given the messages M = (M_1..M_N), its expected log-loss regret against `bayes` is E KL(P(Y|X) ‖ P(Y|M)) = H(Y|M) − H(Y|X) = **I(Y; X | M)**. This holds because M is a function of X. At b = 0 the regret is I(Y;X). With lossless messages it is 0.
The quantity to compute is therefore min over local encoders, at b bits each, of I(Y; X | M). Information theory calls this the distributed information bottleneck / CEO problem under log loss (§3). Our harness already reports KL to `bayes`, so this needs no new metric.

## 1. What state-of-the-art LLM systems pass, and how they aggregate
- **Self-consistency / majority vote.** Wang et al., 2203.11171, 2022, well-cited [8029]. Message: the final answer only (1 bit for binary Y). Aggregation: count the votes. Failures: correlated errors make the vote converge on the shared wrong answer, and duplicate rollouts count in full. Chen et al. (2403.02419, 2024; S2 count unavailable) show the vote's accuracy can *fall* as N grows when hard and easy queries are mixed. Brown et al. (2407.21787, well-cited [1089]): coverage keeps rising with N, but voting and reward models plateau (plateau claim from recall). Plug: sign of the local log-odds (b=1), or the log-odds quantized to b bits; aggregate by vote, mean or sum. S, closed form.
- **Universal self-consistency (USC).** Chen et al., 2311.17311, 2023, moderate [226]. Message: the whole sample text. Aggregation: an LLM picks the most consistent candidate. It selects one candidate and fuses nothing. Plug: same as best-of-N below.
- **Learned aggregators.** AggLM (Zhao et al., 2509.06870, 2025, niche [49]) and SSA (Qi et al., 2506.09014, 2025, niche [34]). Message: full solutions, uncompressed, concatenated. The aggregator is RL-trained on verifiable reward. AggLM balances easy and hard examples so the aggregator learns to recover minority-but-correct answers. Generators stay frozen. Plug: this is our `full_context_transformer` (the uncompressed end, w = ∞). The "frozen local encoder + trained aggregator" variant is a useful ablation against end-to-end training. S.
- **Mixture-of-Agents.** Wang et al., 2406.04692, 2024, well-cited [595]; Self-MoA, Li et al., 2502.00674, 2025, moderate [65]. Message: full responses passed layer to layer; every agent in a layer reads all outputs of the previous layer. Failure: Self-MoA (one strong model sampled repeatedly) beats mixing different models, because aggregation is very sensitive to the average quality of its inputs. Self-MoA's sequential variant, which aggregates outputs on the fly over rounds, matches aggregating everything at once. That is a CEO-like chain. Plug: L rounds of all-to-all messages of width w (multi-round TarMAC, already in the learned ladder). S.
- **Multi-agent debate.** Du et al., 2305.14325, well-cited [2496]; Liang et al., 2305.19118, well-cited [1483]. Message: answer plus reasoning, all-to-all, over R rounds. Failures: majority voting explains most of debate's gains, and debate makes beliefs a martingale, so it does not improve expected correctness (Choi et al., 2508.17536, 2025, moderate [106]); conformity and herding (Weng et al., 2501.13381, moderate [61]). Sparse topologies match or beat all-to-all (Li et al., 2406.11776, moderate [154]). CIPHER (Pham et al., 2310.06272, moderate [56]) passes the expected output embedding instead of a sampled token, because a sampled token throws away the belief over the vocabulary. Plug: R rounds in which each agent re-encodes its own evidence plus the others' width-w messages. Report regret vs R and the herding metric (`cascade_curve`). M.
- **LLM-Blender.** Jiang et al., 2306.02561, 2023, well-cited [760]. Message: full candidates. A pairwise ranker scores the candidates, then a generative fuser merges the top K. Plug: **select-k**. A learned ranker picks k of N agents to send their full evidence, with total bits matched to N·b. M.
- **Best-of-N with verifiers.** Cobbe et al., 2110.14168 [11560]; Lightman et al., 2305.20050 [5108]; both well-cited. Message: a candidate. A verifier scores each one and the top-scored candidate wins. It discards every other source, so it is a weak floor for pooling evidence. Plug: the aggregator outputs the single local posterior that a learned scorer picks. S.
- **Map-reduce / hierarchical summarisation.** Wu et al., 2109.10862, 2021, well-cited [388]. BooookScore (Chang et al., 2310.00785, 2023, moderate [198]) compares hierarchically merging chunk summaries with incrementally updating one running summary. LLM×MapReduce (Zhou et al., 2410.09342, 2024, niche [20]) names the failures as dependencies between chunks (our XOR) and conflicts between chunks; its fixes are a structured message format and a confidence field. Message: a bounded-length summary, which is exactly a compressed message. Plug: **tree aggregator**. A shared learned merge net maps two width-w messages to one width-w message, over log2 N levels. S–M.
- **Chain-of-Agents (CoA).** Zhang et al., 2406.02818, NeurIPS 2024, well-cited [316]. This is the literature twin of Kelvin's sequential CEO. Each worker computes CU_i = LLM(I_W, CU_{i−1}, chunk_i, query), and a manager reads the last CU. The CU is the only state passed along, so its size is the bottleneck. CoA beat its own Hierarchical (tree) and Merge (vote) baselines on all 8 datasets, and the authors attribute this to parallel workers being unable to communicate. Order effects are untested. Plug: `sequential.py` broadcast mode with a fixed-width state (consensus = CU, w_view = w_msg = w) and a manager readout. S. Difference from Kelvin's queried mode: CoA's state stays at width w, while Kelvin's KV memory grows to N·w. Measure both (§3, Hellman–Cover).
- **Forecasting systems.** Halawi et al. (2402.18563, 2024, moderate [149]): an LM first summarises the retrieved articles (a RECOMP-like compression), then the system takes the trimmed mean of 6 scalar forecasts. Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd (Schoenegger et al., 2402.19379, moderate [119]): 12 LLMs, aggregated by median, are not statistically different from 925 humans, and show an acquiescence bias (mean forecasts above 50%). AIA Forecaster (2511.07678, 2025, niche [7]): a supervisor agent reconciles disparate forecasts, then statistical calibration. Plug: the scalar-report ladder (median, trimmed mean, extremized mean). It exists already. Add a "supervisor" variant: scalar plus a width-w rationale vector. S.
- **Latent communication between LLMs.** This is the prior art for Kelvin's keys and values as messages; it positions his design rather than serving as a baseline. Ramesh & Li (2501.14082, niche [18]) pass activations between models; Cache-to-Cache (2510.03215, moderate [73]) fuses KV caches; KVComm (2510.03346, niche [21]) matches the merged-input upper bound while sharing the KV pairs of only 30% of layers, the only size sweep found; LatentMAS (2511.20639, moderate [67]). Hogwild! (2504.06261) and Group Think (2505.11107), both niche: concurrent workers attend to each other's KV cache, which is the trader-transformer at inference time. None of these measures the information content of the messages or compares against a Bayes ceiling.

## 2. Learned compression of evidence for a downstream reader (candidates for the message)
- **Gist tokens.** Mu et al., 2304.08467, 2023, well-cited [511]. A modified attention mask forces the prompt into k gist tokens. Plug: per-agent transformer over its own pieces plus k learned slots; message = slot outputs (w = k·d). S.
- **AutoCompressors.** Chevalier et al., 2305.14788, moderate [446]. Summary vectors are carried from segment to segment: a CoA chain in vector space. Plug: CoA chain with a vector state (above).
- **ICAE.** Ge et al., 2307.06945, moderate [232]. Memory slots pretrained by autoencoding plus LM loss; about 4× compression. Plug: **task-agnostic messages**. Train encoders to reconstruct their own evidence, freeze them, then train the aggregator. Compared with task-trained messages, this measures how much of the efficiency comes from aggregation-aware compression. S.
- **xRAG.** Cheng et al., 2405.13792, moderate [168]. A frozen retriever embedding becomes one token through a trained bridge. Plug: fixed PCA or random projection of the evidence to w dims, plus a learned aggregator. S.
- **RECOMP.** Xu et al., 2310.04408, well-cited [336]. Extractive or abstractive compressors trained for the downstream LM; they may output nothing (selective augmentation). **LLMLingua** (2310.05736, well-cited [399]) drops tokens instead. Plug: **extractive top-w**. The agent sends its w most informative pieces as (index, value) pairs, ranked by |LLR| or by a learned score, or stays silent. S, and a strong, interpretable baseline.
- **Recurrent Memory Transformer.** 2207.06881, moderate [264]. A recurrent memory; same family as CoA.
- **Perceiver / Perceiver IO.** 2103.03206 [1718] and 2107.14795 [952], both well-cited. Compression on the reader side: cross-attention into m latents. This is the ISAB experiment already planned. Keep it apart from agent-side compression and give it its own axis.
- **Cramming.** Kuratov et al., 2502.13063, 2025, niche [31]. Per-sample optimisation fits 1568 tokens into one vector, while learned encoders reach at most about 10×. **Consequence for us:** with infinite precision, w real dimensions are not a bit budget. A width-w float message must be quantized, sent through channel noise, or rate-penalised before "w" means anything.
- **Deep VIB.** Alemi et al., 1612.00410, well-cited [2395]. Adds a β·KL rate penalty, which gives a measured rate in nats. Plug: wrap any encoder above in VIB and sweep β. S.

## 3. Theory: bounds and exact one-shot baselines
- **Decentralized detection** (Crossref counts). Tenney & Sandell 1981, doi:10.1109/TAES.1981.309178 [640]. Tsitsiklis 1988, doi:10.1007/bf02551407 [289]: with many conditionally independent sensors, likelihood-ratio quantizers are optimal, and identical ones are asymptotically optimal (recall). Tsitsiklis & Athans 1985, doi:10.1109/tac.1985.1103988 [309]: optimal design is NP-hard in general. Chamberland & Veeravalli 2003, doi:10.1109/TSP.2002.806982 [404]: under a total-rate budget, many 1-bit sensors beat fewer multi-bit ones in Gaussian cases (recall).
  Plug: **optimal one-shot quantizer**. Each agent quantizes its sufficient statistic (local log-odds, or its likelihood vector over the world states w in the zoo) to 2^b cells. Thresholds are tuned by person-by-person coordinate descent, and the decoder is exact Bayes over the cells. S–M. It is the strongest exact baseline at b bits, and it gives an achievable curve.
- **Learned quantizers.** Nguyen, Wainwright & Jordan, math/0510521 (Ann. Stat. 2009), moderate [70]. Trains the quantizer and the classifier jointly via surrogate losses, which correspond to f-divergences. This justifies training b-bit messages with log loss through straight-through or Gumbel estimators.
- **Distributed information bottleneck.** Aguerri & Zaidi, 1709.09082 [58] and 1807.04193 [94], moderate. Gives the optimal complexity–relevance region when X_i are conditionally independent given Y, a Blahut–Arimoto algorithm to compute it, and **D-VIB** (neural per-agent encoders, one decoder). Courtade & Weissman (1110.3069, moderate [185]) solve the m-encoder CEO problem under log loss. Plug: (a) Blahut–Arimoto gives a regret lower bound vs sum-rate for E2-like environments (M); (b) D-VIB is the textbook learned baseline (S).
  Caveat: these bounds are asymptotic (block coding over many episodes). They are lower bounds, not one-shot achievable. Zoo structures that are independent only given the world state w (XOR, common cause) violate the theorem's assumption, so use the brute-force one-shot curve there.
- **CEO problem: bound only** (Kelvin: "what we should avoid"). Berger, Zhang & Viswanathan 1996, doi:10.1109/18.490552 [446]. Quadratic Gaussian CEO: Viswanathan & Berger 1997, doi:10.1109/18.623151 [204]; Oohama 1998, doi:10.1109/18.669162 [262]. These give the closed-form MSE-vs-sum-rate floor for E1.
- **Single-agent compression for a target.** Information bottleneck: Tishby et al., physics/0004057, well-cited [4717]. Indirect rate-distortion: Witsenhausen 1980, doi:10.1109/TIT.1980.1056251 [145].
- **Correlated or duplicated sources.** Slepian & Wolf 1973, doi:10.1109/TIT.1973.1055037 [3047]. Separate encoders can jointly reach a total rate of H(X_1..X_N), so in principle duplicates cost no extra bits.
- **Minimax rates with b bits per machine.** Duchi et al., 1405.0782 [67], covers one-shot and interactive (broadcast) protocols; Braverman et al., 1506.07216 [199]; Acharya, Canonne & Tyagi, 1812.11476 [127]; Barnes, Han & Özgür, 1902.02890 [18]; for hypothesis testing, Ahlswede & Csiszár 1986, doi:10.1109/TIT.1986.1057194 [207]. For a d-dim Gaussian mean, b < d bits per agent cuts the effective sample size by about b/d (recall). Plug: a scaling check in E1. Regret vs (N, b) should follow this law.
- **Sequential chains with a bounded state.** This theory covers Kelvin's CEO with a fixed-width consensus. Hellman & Cover 1970, doi:10.1214/aoms/1177696958 [134]: an m-state memory has an error floor set by m and the maximum likelihood ratio (recall). Drakopoulos, Ozdaglar & Tsitsiklis, 1209.1122 [100]: when agents see the last K decisions, learning fails almost surely for every K and fails in probability for K = 1; it succeeds in probability for K ≥ 2 with a designed rule.
  Predicts: a fixed-width consensus has a regret floor as N grows, and a growing KV memory avoids it.

**"Best achievable regret at width b" is a sandwich.** Lower bound: the DIB / CEO-log-loss region, computed by Blahut–Arimoto, or the Gaussian closed form for E1. Upper bound: the optimal one-shot quantizer with an exact Bayes decoder. Where the two meet, we have the curve.

## 4. Emergent and learned communication under bandwidth limits
- **Architectures.** CommNet (1605.07736, well-cited [1450]): continuous messages, mean-pooled. DIAL/RIAL (1605.06676, well-cited [2034]): backpropagates through a noisy channel, which pushes messages toward discrete values (recall). IC3Net (1812.09755, well-cited [347]): a learned gate decides when to speak. TarMAC (1810.11187, [597]): signature and value messages.
- **Bandwidth limits.** SchedNet (1902.01554, [277]): a shared medium lets only k agents broadcast, and a learned scheduler decides which. VBC (1909.02682, [140]): 2–10× less communication on StarCraft. IMAC (1911.06992, [156]): proves that limited bandwidth requires low-entropy messages, and uses an IB penalty plus a scheduler.
- Referential games: Lazaridou et al. 1612.07182 [519]; Havrylov & Titov 1705.11192 [339]; Mordatch & Abbeel 1703.04908 [844]; survey 2006.02419 [294].
- **Message size and form.** Kottur et al. (1706.08502, [253]): without restricting the vocabulary, the language does not become compositional. Resnick et al. (1910.11424, [64]): capacity and bandwidth jointly decide compositionality, and the upper limit is unresolved. Chaabouni et al. (1905.12561, [123]): agents drift to the maximum message length ("anti-efficient") unless length is penalised. VQ-VIB (Tucker et al., 2108.01828, niche [34]): discrete tokens drawn from a continuous space.
- **What they found about size.** Without a rate penalty, agents use all the bandwidth they are given. Under tight budgets, choosing *who* speaks (gating, scheduling) recovers most of the performance (recall). IB penalties give sparse messages that are still sufficient. None of them sweeps width against a Bayes ceiling; they measure task reward only.
Plug: IC3Net/SchedNet gating is the select-k baseline in §1. VQ-VIB is the discrete-message variant of D-VIB.

## 5. Ranked shortlist: baselines to run against Kelvin's design at width b
1. **Optimal one-shot quantizer + exact Bayes decoder** (Tsitsiklis line). The achievable reference, built on exact likelihoods.
2. **D-VIB** (Aguerri & Zaidi): per-agent VIB encoder, joint decoder, sweep β. The standard learned baseline.
3. **Extractive top-w** (RECOMP / LLMLingua analogue), with silence allowed.
4. **Chain-of-Agents chain**: fixed-width state plus manager. The literature twin of the CEO; tests the fixed-width vs growing-memory question.
5. **Tree map-reduce** with a learned width-w merge (BooookScore, LLM×MapReduce).
6. **Select-k full evidence** (LLM-Blender ranker / SchedNet), at matched total bits.
7. **Practitioner floor**: vote, median or trimmed mean of b-bit local log-odds (self-consistency, Halawi, silicon crowd).
8. **Task-agnostic autoencoder messages** (ICAE / xRAG-style, frozen). Measures what aggregation-aware compression buys.
Anchors: b = 0 (prior, regret I(Y;X)) and b = ∞ (`full_context_transformer` and exact Bayes).
Kelvin's arms: trader-transformer with KV width w; `sequential.py` queried vs broadcast mode.

## 6. Proposed measurement
- **Unit.** Bits per agent. For float messages, either quantize to q bits per dimension (b = w·q), or report the measured rate (the VIB KL bound, or the plug-in entropy of quantized messages on test episodes). Report sum-rate N·b alongside, for select-k and tree schemes.
- **Curve per environment.** Regret R(b), the mean KL to `bayes` in nats, at b ∈ {0,1,2,4,8,16,32,64,∞}, with seeds and paired CIs, and N ∈ {8, 32, 128}.
- **Environments.** Use only those where an agent's evidence exceeds the message: E1, E2/E3 (2–6 continuous pieces), E4, Z8. Zoo Z1–Z9 give each agent one symbol, so the evidence is at most log2|alphabet| bits and compression is nearly trivial there. Add a `pieces_per_agent` knob (p symbols per agent) before running the zoo. For XOR with p bits, one bit (the noisy parity of the agent's own readings) may be nearly sufficient: a sharp test.
- **Fraction of the full-evidence benefit:** ρ(b) = [R(0) − R(b)] / [R(0) − R(∞)].
- **Fraction of the Bayes gap closed:** G(b) = 1 − R(b)/R(0), which equals I(Y;M)/I(Y;X) with a Bayes decoder.
- **Compression efficiency:** η(b) = [R(0) − R(b)] / [R(0) − R*(b)], where R*(b) is the best known at b: the one-shot optimum where computable, else the lower envelope of all baselines.
- **Headline scalar:** b₉₀ = the smallest b with ρ ≥ 0.9, also reported as b₉₀ divided by the agent's evidence entropy (the compression ratio at 90%). Raw "gap per bit" G(b)/b is misleading at large b, so report the curve.
- **Two stress axes.** Duplication or overlap: do the bits per agent needed fall as copies rise (the Slepian–Wolf saving)? N, at a fixed width of the consensus state: Hellman–Cover predicts a floor for the fixed-state chain.

## 7. Open, as far as these searches found
- No work plots regret against an exact Bayes posterior as a function of message size for a learned many-agent aggregator. LLM aggregation papers never vary message size (KVComm's layer fraction is the exception), MARL papers score task reward, and D-VIB was tested on multi-view datasets with no Bayes ceiling (recall).
- No controlled comparison of a fixed-width chain (CoA, AutoCompressors), a growing KV memory (Kelvin) and a tree at matched bits. CoA compared chain vs tree only in LLM text, without controlling message size.
- No measurement of whether learned one-shot encoders exploit redundancy among agents (Slepian–Wolf savings) or carry XOR-type information through a narrow channel.
- No test of whether queried (interactive) messages beat one-shot messages at equal rate. Duchi et al. bound interactive protocols, but nobody has measured learned ones.
- No sandwich (Blahut–Arimoto lower bound vs optimal one-shot quantizer) has been computed for the overlap, duplicate and XOR structures. With aggbench's exact likelihoods it is cheap.
