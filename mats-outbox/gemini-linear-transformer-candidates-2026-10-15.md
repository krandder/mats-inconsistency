### 1. What `arXiv:2402.14180` Actually Does (and Why It Works with a Tiny Network)

In `2402.14180`, the model is given $n = 20$ in-context examples $e_i = (x_i, y_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{d+1}$ ($d = 10$) generated by an unknown linear law $y_i = \langle w_\tau, x_i \rangle + \xi_i$ with noise $\xi_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_\tau^2)$, plus a query token $e_{n+1} = (x_t, 0)$. Critically, **the noise level $\sigma_\tau$ varies wildly from episode to episode** ($\sigma_\tau \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0, 7)$ or categorical $\{1, 3, 5\}$) and is never told to the model.

They use an **attention-only Linear Transformer** (no softmax, no MLP, no LayerNorm) of depth $L = 1\dots 7$. At each layer $l$, attention just multiplies by the empirical second-moment matrix of the tokens:
$$\sum_{j=1}^n e_j^l (e_j^l)^\top = \begin{pmatrix} \Sigma^l & \alpha^l \\ (\alpha^l)^\top & \lambda^l \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{where } \Sigma^l = \sum_{j=1}^n x_j^l (x_j^l)^\top, \;\; \alpha^l = \sum_{j=1}^n y_j^l x_j^l, \;\; \lambda^l = \sum_{j=1}^n (y_j^l)^2.$$

Even when they restrict the weight matrices $P^l, Q^l$ to block-diagonal scalars (**`Diag`**, which has **only 4 scalar parameters per layer**: $w_{xx}^l, w_{xy}^l, w_{yx}^l, w_{yy}^l$, so **$28$ parameters total** at $L=7$), two remarkable things happen:

1. **Every layer maintains an exact implicit belief vector $w^l$, residual $y_i^l$, and momentum buffer $u^l$ (Theorem 4.1 & Lemma 4.3):**
   - The $y$-coordinate of each context token automatically tracks the **prediction residual** $y_i^l = a^l y_i - \langle w^l, x_i \rangle$ (how much token $i$ disagrees with the current global consensus $w^l$).
   - The $x$-coordinate $x_i^l = M^l x_i + y_i u^l$ does two things simultaneously: $M^l$ **whitens/preconditions the feature covariance $\Sigma$**, while $u^l \in \mathbb{R}^d$ acts as a **Heavy-Ball momentum buffer** across layers.
2. **Each of the 4 scalars per layer performs a distinct algorithmic operation:**
   - **$w_{yx}^l$ (Gradient step):** Updates the consensus $w^l$ using the correlation $\alpha^l = \sum_j y_j^l x_j^l$ between current residuals and keys.
   - **$w_{xx}^l$ (Quadratic Newton-Schulz preconditioning, Theorem 5.1):** Updates keys via $x_i^{l+1} = (I + w_{xx}^l \Sigma^l) x_i^l$. Instead of needing $O(\kappa)$ gradient steps on correlated features, each layer squares the eigenvalue convergence ($\kappa_{l+1} - 1 \approx (\kappa_l - 1)^2$), inverting an ill-conditioned Gram matrix in $O(\log \kappa + \log\log(1/\epsilon))$ layers.
   - **$w_{yy}^l < 0$ (In-context noise shrinkage, §5.2):** Because $\lambda^l = \sum_{j=1}^n (y_j^l)^2$ is the **sum of squared residuals** after $l$ steps of fitting, $\lambda^l$ measures how noisy/inconsistent the context is. A negative $w_{yy}^l$ scales down the prediction by $(1 + w_{yy}^l \lambda^l) \approx \frac{n}{n + \sigma_\tau^2}$—implementing Bayes-optimal ridge shrinkage without ever being told $\sigma_\tau$.
   - **$w_{xy}^l < 0$ (Two-step lookahead step-size damping, §5.3):** Injects the residuals into the keys $x_i^{l+1}$ so that in the *next* layer $(l+1)$, the gradient step size is automatically damped by $(1 + w_{xy}^l \sum_j r_j^2)$ when the context has high residual error.

Because `AdaRR` (the standard textbook baseline that estimates $\hat{\sigma}^2$ from OLS residuals and inverts $(\Sigma + \hat{\sigma}^2 I)^{-1}$) suffers from high variance when $n=20, d=10$, the 7-layer Linear Transformer (`Full` = 1,694 params, `Diag` = 28 params) **beats both `AdaRR` and constant-tuned ridge regression (`TunedRR`)** across every noise regime (`0.057` vs `0.092` MSE at $\sigma_{\max}=7$).

### 2. The Exact Bridge to Your Rollout Search & LIR Architecture

If we replace `2402.14180`'s data tokens $(x_i, y_i)$ with **$K$ search rollouts** $z_r = (k_r, v_r, c_r)$—where $k_r \in \mathbb{R}^d$ is the fingerprint of which latent variables/clauses rollout $r$ used, $v_r$ is the rollout's claim (log-odds shift), and $c_r$ is its local reliability/conflict metadata—the `2402.14180` equations map **1-to-1** onto Richardson's LIR ([STUDY-LIR.md](file:///home/agent/workspaces/eurisko-study/li-net/STUDY-LIR.md)) and your attention-based deduplication:

| `2402.14180` Mechanism | Rollout Aggregation / LIR Meaning | What `E-CA` / `E-SUD` Did Instead |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Token residual** $v_r^l = a^l v_r - \langle w^l, k_r \rangle$ | **LIR Negative-Attention Market Copy:** Subtracts the current global belief $\langle w^l, k_r \rangle$ from rollout $r$'s claim $v_r$ so only *unexplained* evidence remains. | Subtracted only the static 1-pass prior $P_0(Y)$ once (`sw_mix`), never updating what later rollouts already explained. |
| **Multi-layer $w_{xx}^l$ preconditioning** | **Iterative Gram Inversion $(K^\top B K + \lambda I)^{-1}$:** Decouples overlapping/bridge rollouts and assigns clique weight proportional to precision $b_r$. | **`dd` 1-step row-sum** $c_r = \sum_b \max(0, \langle k_r, k_b \rangle)$: if 1 clean rollout and 1 noisy rollout share a key, `dd` cuts the clean rollout's weight in half; if a rollout bridges two cliques, `dd` penalizes it most! |
| **$w_{yy}^l < 0$ and $w_{xy}^l < 0$ on $\lambda^l = \sum_r (v_r^l)^2$** | **Cross-Rollout Contradiction & Noise Damping:** Detects when rollouts mutually clash ($\lambda^l$ large) and shrinks step sizes / downweights dead-end branches. | Used only single-chain internal confidence (`sw_mix`), which is blind to whether $K$ chains agree or contradict each other. |
| **Preconditioned query key $k_q^L = M^L k_q$** | **1-Pass Active VoI Routing:** Since $M^L \approx (\Sigma + \lambda I)^{-1/2}$, the inner product $\|\langle k_q^L, k_z^L \rangle\|^2$ gives the exact expected belief change from probing $z$ next in **1 forward pass**. | `VoI` in [RESULTS-R3.md](file:///home/agent/workspaces/eurisko-study/li-net/eca/RESULTS-R3.md) had to run **12 separate forward passes per step** ($3{,}344$ passes/query) to measure lookahead belief change. |

**Why `E-CA` and $6\times 6$ Sudoku didn't need this:**
1. **1D Scalar Target Bottleneck ($d=1$):** Every rollout was collapsed to a single scalar probability $q_r(Y) \in (0,1)$ about **one cell $Y$** *before* the aggregator ever saw it. When $d=1$, $\Sigma$ is a $1\times 1$ scalar, so matrix preconditioning collapses to scalar division.
2. **No Cross-Rollout Synthesis:** In `E-CA`, if $Y = z_1 \oplus z_2$ and rollout 1 finds $z_1$ while rollout 2 finds $z_2$, each rollout alone outputs $q_r(Y) = 0.5$. Pooling scalar outputs $(0.5, 0.5)$ still gives $0.5$! To combine partial deductions across chains (`AND`-composition), each rollout must output a **vector** $(k_r, v_r)$ describing the intermediate subspace it resolved.

### 3. Two Minimal, Cheap-to-Train Experiments ($<\$0.20$, $<10\text{ min}$)

We can test this at tiny scale in two ways—first in a clean synthetic environment with an exact closed-form oracle (like `2402.14180`), and second directly on the **already-saved `E-CA` Round-3 checkpoints** (`B1`/`B2` bridges):

#### Experiment A (Pure `2402.14180` + Search Bridge: Correlated Subspace Rollouts + Active VoI)
- **Setup:** A hidden state $w_\tau \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ ($12$ core variables + $4$ high-variance "noisy TV" distractor variables). We observe $K = 20$ partial rollouts $z_r = (k_r, v_r, c_r)$:
  - *Correlated coverage ($\kappa \approx 50$):* 12 rollouts cluster redundantly around 3 "hubs", 6 are sparse 2-variable parity/bridge links $(e_i - e_j)/\sqrt{2}$, and 2 hit the noisy-TV distractors.
  - *Mixed + corrupted rollout noise:* Each rollout has a known chain depth $m_r \in \{1\dots 6\}$, an unknown episode noise level $\sigma_\tau \sim U(0.1, 3.0)$, and $20\%$ of rollouts are "bad branches" with $9\times$ higher noise variance (detectable only because their values clash with overlapping rollouts).
  - *Query & Active Search:* Query $k_q = (e_1 + e_{12})/\sqrt{2}$ asks for the long-range bridge across the chain, and the model also gets to **actively pick 2 additional probes** from 8 candidates (2 true missing bridge links, 3 redundant hubs, 3 noisy-TV distractors) using its layer-$L$ attention state.
- **Model & Cost:** $L \in \{1, 2, 4, 6\}$ Linear Transformer (`GD++` 32 params, `Diag` 120 params, `Full` 17k params). Trains in **~3 minutes on 1 L4 GPU ($<\$0.05$)**.
- **What we check:**
  1. Does $L=4\text{--}6$ `Diag`/`Full` beat `mix`, `sw_mix`, 1-step `dd`, and `GD++`, and match/beat closed-form `AdaRR` across noise levels (reproducing `2402.14180` Figs. 3–4)?
  2. Do the learned weights show $w_{xx}^l$ compressing condition number $\kappa(\Sigma^l) \to 1$ and $w_{yy}^l, w_{xy}^l < 0$ automatically downweighting corrupted rollouts?
  3. Does the 1-pass attention inner product $\langle k_q^L, k_z^L \rangle$ select the exact missing bridge probes while avoiding the noisy-TV distractors?

#### Experiment B (Applied Bridge on Saved `E-CA-R3` Checkpoints: Solving the `B1`/`B2` Bridge Gap)
- **Setup:** Reuse the frozen 4-layer Rule-110 checkpoints from [RESULTS-R3.md](file:///home/agent/workspaces/eurisko-study/li-net/eca/RESULTS-R3.md) (`$0` backbone training). On `B1`/`B2` bridge queries (where top row 0 and bottom row 9 are clamped, and 1-pass `P0` has a large `0.301` nat KL gap), run $K=16$ short rollouts at mixed temperatures $\tau_r \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0\}$.
- **Vector Rollout Output:** Instead of collapsing each rollout to 1 scalar $q_r(Y)$, each rollout emits a 20D token $z_r = (k_r, v_r, c_r)$:
  - $k_r \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$: the rollout's attention-source fingerprint over the interior cells (`ecanet.py`),
  - $v_r \in \mathbb{R}^4$: log-odds shifts at $Y$ *plus* the top-3 highest-attention intermediate bridge cells,
  - $c_r \in \mathbb{R}^4$: target stiffness $\beta_{\text{int}}$, **boundary conflict score** (mean log-loss of the clamped bottom row 9 under chain $r$'s materialized interior cells—a direct CDCL conflict signal!), trajectory log-probability, and temperature $\tau_r$.
- **Model & Cost:** A **12.8k-parameter, 4-layer Linear Transformer** over the 16 rollout tokens. Trains in **~8 minutes on 1 L4 GPU ($\sim\$0.12$)**.
- **What we check:** Does multi-layer linear attention over vector rollout tokens beat every 1-step scalar pool (`mix` `0.253`, `sw_mix` `0.228`, 1-step `dd` `0.277`) on `B1`/`B2` by (a) rejecting rollouts whose interior cells clash with the bottom boundary and (b) stitching top-down and bottom-up partial chains across layers?
