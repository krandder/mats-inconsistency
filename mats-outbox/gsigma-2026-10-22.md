# G(Σ) and the log-likelihood span, measured exactly (2026-10-22)

**TL;DR.** One-shot G(Σ) is close to vacuous: a single generic security (majority, AND) gives G = 0 on every parity cell, and G({Y}) = 0 on AND, COPY, Z5 and E3. The quantity that matters is R, the regret of summing trades (additive pooling). R = G + a pooling loss, R = 0 whenever every agent's log-likelihood lies in span(Σ), and near 0 it grows as residual². The smallest Σ with R = 0 is the set of variables the agents read (the k bits on Z9). Two requested test claims are false as stated (see the last section). No decision needed.

**How to read this.** Units are bits. Results are exact enumeration unless marked MC; ± is a 95% CI over random structures. **G** = I(Y; X | each agent's own forecast on each security in Σ), for a decoder that knows the whole joint. **seq** = the same after one pass in which each agent also sees the prices already posted (readers go in bit order). A **trade** moves the market from the prior to the agent's own forecasts on Σ (LMSR, i.e. moment matching). The **residual** is the KL from the agent's posterior over world states to the market after its trade; it is 0 iff log p(x_i|ω) ∈ span(1, Σ). R was checked against brute force. **d_ω** = rank of the row-centred log-likelihood matrix (its truncated SVD gives the best L2 span). **Bits (a)** = the entropy of one trade when the decoder knows which securities the trade touches and the agent's accuracy. **Bits (b)** = the cost when the decoder does not know this, so the agent also sends the support pattern (in brackets) and trade values at 0.05 nats. Each table entry is G / R.

**Findings.**
- **Span implies exact.** Over 2,008 (cell, Σ, structure) evaluations, a zero residual always gave R ≤ 3e-12 bits. The converse fails in 261 cases (residual > 0, R = 0), where the residual sits in directions that do not touch Y: distractor readers, AND with {Y,b1}, E3 pieces that only one agent reads. In the mixing probes, R ∝ residual^2.00 near 0. R ≤ residual held in every evaluation (observed, not proven). KL ≤ osc(r)²/8 held for every observation tuple (worst ratio 0.99997, 0 violations). On average it is loose: 111 nats against 0.46 nats on Z9 k=4 with {Y}.
- **Principal angles.** On Z9, span(bits + the distractor bits that are read) equals the SVD span (all angles < 1e-5°). Bits alone leave about 15% of the log-likelihood energy outside the span (the distractor readers), yet R = 0. {Y} and {maj} leave 80-100% outside.
- **The partial-parity chain does nothing one-shot.** A b2 reader's forecast on b1⊕b2 is 1/2. With b1 listed and readers posting in bit order, one pass closes the gap. This is the reason to list it: it cuts the market's tree width from k to 2.
- **Bits per agent.** Under (a), a minimal-Σ trade costs at most 1 bit per reading. On Z9-p2 that is 1.67 mean and 2 max, the same 2 bits/agent as compression.py's closed-form raw code, which is lossless at b = 2. compression.py's closed forms assume (a). Under (b), add the support pattern (3.5 bits on Z9-p2, 4.2 if distractors are listed) and about 6.5 bits per coordinate: 16.4 bits/agent. Coarsening values to 0.5 nats costs at most 0.005 bits of R.

**Two requested test claims are false as stated (tests encode the true versions).** "XOR: listing b1 drops G to 0" holds only sequentially (b1 reader first); one-shot, G({Y,b1}) = 1 bit. "AND has G > 0 for {Y}" is backwards: G({Y}) = 0, while R = 0.10 bits and the residual is 1.38 bits.

**Limits.** One-shot plus one sequential pass. Every G and R assumes a decoder that knows the joint. E3 is discretised.
