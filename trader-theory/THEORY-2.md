## 0. Hindsight-posterior settlement (Kelvin's idea, mail #2554)

**What it is.** A trader emits p(z | x) over a discrete latent; a downstream conditional market holds P(y | z). When y resolves, the regulator forms q(z | x, y) ∝ p(z | x) P(y | z) and pays the trader the log score of p against the soft outcome q; the chain z1 → z2 → y is settled from the top down.

**Is it principled? Yes, and it is known.** Scoring p(z | x) against q with the log score is proper for the conditional mean, so the trader's best report is the posterior. Forming q is the E-step of EM (Dempster, Laird and Rubin 1977) and training each local model toward it is the M-step; Neal and Hinton (1998) show this coordinate ascent on a free energy never decreases the marginal log-likelihood of y given x, including incremental and per-example variants. Along a chain the exact posterior is forward-backward, which is EM for input-output HMMs (Bengio and Frasconi 1996), calibrated-ai's arm Bx. The sampled top-down form (arm B) is the wake phase of wake-sleep (Hinton, Dayan, Frey and Neal 1995) and target propagation with posterior targets (Bengio 2014; Lee et al. 2015). So the market frame adds a ledger and a budget to generalised EM; the learning rule itself is 1977.

**What it buys that "approximates backprop" does not (K1).** EM gives a *monotone global guarantee with only local updates*: no gradient crosses a trader, memory is O(depth × K) messages instead of O(depth × width) activations, and the guarantee holds at any depth as long as the posterior is exact (Bx) rather than sampled (B). That is a stronger basis than predictive coding's approximation argument, and it is the best candidate we have for the stream-3 mechanism at arbitrary depth: a discrete scratch settled by forward-backward is an HMM over depth with the output labels as emissions.

**Where it approximates and where it breaks.** (i) Sampled settlement (B) is stochastic EM and can drift; at extrapolated depth the messages compound, which EM theory does not cover. (ii) Collapse: with K ≥ |Y| the cheapest solution copies y; the known VAE posterior-collapse fix is a rate cost, which is arm D's KL to the batch marginal, i.e. an information bottleneck (K4); calibrated-ai's "different views" is the co-training version. (iii) KL(q || p) averaged over y is I(Z; Y | X) under the model: it is a *learning-progress* signal (how much hindsight still moves the latent), not value of computation; VOC needs the counterfactual of running versus not running (K3). (iv) The posterior uses the downstream markets, which are themselves learning; EM tolerates that (generalised EM), but the settlement is then influenced by the traders being settled, which section 3 flags.

**Falsifiers (P7).** B or Bx fails to recover the mod-3 state at chain depth 4 while arm A (straight-through backprop) does; or B matches A at depth 4 but not at length 256 while Bx does (then it is the sampling, not the idea); or D is needed to stop collapse and then loses the latent (bottleneck too tight). The result that would make it the stream-3 mechanism: Bx ≥ A at depth 4, length 256, with the latent recovered by probes, and peak memory flat in depth.

## 1. Cruxes

A "real new class of learning algorithm" means, concretely: a rule that is local in depth, has a global guarantee, and does something gradient descent on a fixed architecture cannot (open-ended representation, budgeted trust, or selection with regret bounds). Each crux below names the cheapest decisive experiment and what kills it.

1. **Does any economic rule beat backprop anywhere at equal compute?** Not yet (E-MLP-1, E0-2, MATS block-local market). The only place it can is where backprop through depth is unavailable: depth extrapolation. Decisive: P6 R1 (chunked carry plus per-chunk LMSR pay) and F (auction economy) against R0 (DT-Recall, full BPTT) on P2 mazes 9 → 59 and prefix parity, depth ratio 10 to 1000, 5 seeds. Killed if R0 is at least as good at every ratio, or if Bansal's progressive loss alone matches the economy. Cheapest and most decisive of the list.
2. **Does exact hindsight settlement (Bx) reach BPTT accuracy through a chain of depth 4 and extrapolate?** (Section 0.) Killed as above. If it passes, crux 1 gets a second contender that needs no chunked gradient at all.
3. **Do priced events find the sufficient statistic, or just a correlate?** P4 plants gates, copies, epiphenomenal and inert nodes in Boolean circuits and has exact coalition tables; the test is whether Aumann–Shapley and Shapley pay rank gates above copies and epiphenomena at every level. Killed if pay ranks an epiphenomenal node as high as the gate it copies, or if MI-1BIT (a plain information probe) ranks as well as the market pay, since then pricing adds nothing over probing.
4. **Is bounded trust (the wealth cap) a learning advantage or only robustness?** P11: cap on, cap off, gradient clipping on belief moves, at depth ratio 100 to 10000. Killed if cap off is as stable, or if clipping the belief move by a fixed constant does the same, which would make the ledger decorative.
5. **Does entry and exit by wealth beat population-based training with the same schedule?** P6: EC (bankruptcy, clone of a wealth-sampled parent) against PBT-uniform and EMA-clone, which replay EC's exit times with a different victim rule. Killed if PBT-uniform matches EC: then the economy is PBT with a different selection statistic, which is known.
6. **Does a priced value of computation choose depth better than a gradient-trained halting head?** Arm C with epsilon-continuation against PonderNet (arm D) on the accuracy-per-compute frontier, steps against maze path length. Killed if PonderNet dominates the frontier, or if steps do not grow with path length.
7. **Are priced events the same events across seeds?** The interpretability claim (section 2) needs listed predicates to be reproducible as partitions. Measure adjusted mutual information between the event partitions of two seeds on held-out examples. Killed if agreement is at chance, which means the "auditable internal variables" are seed noise.
8. **Does an installed arbitrageur do reasoning work at depth (P12)?** Flow conservation on maze edge securities, against chained TD on the same securities. Killed if TD alone equals it; then coherence enforcement is not the primitive we hoped.

Cruxes 1 and 2 decide whether this is a learning algorithm at all; 3 and 7 decide whether the representation claim is real; 4, 5, 6, 8 decide which of the mechanisms is more than a known method renamed.

## 2. Where it could sit on a frontier without being the most efficient

- **Small samples.** Honestly weak. HRM and TRM learn Sudoku and mazes from 1000 examples with deep supervision and augmentation, not markets (K8). The one economic lever is capacity control by rent: an active variable pays per step and dies if it does not earn, which is MDL model selection at the variable level (Hinton and van Camp 1993; Rissanen 1978; P8). Test: Sudoku with 1000 examples, rent swept, against weight decay and dropout at matched parameter counts. Success is better held-out log loss at n ≤ 1000; killed if matched dropout equals it (E-MLP-1 says Shapley training *is* dropout).
- **Shifts and continual learning.** The strongest niche in theory. Fixed-share redistribution has a regret bound for tracking the best expert under changes (Herbster and Warmuth 1998), and the MATS run found 0.1% fixed share beats Kelly after a shift in all seeds. Gradient methods protect weights instead (EWC, Kirkpatrick et al. 2017) and have no such bound. Test: prefix parity or mazes with a rule change mid-stream; economy with fixed share against EWC and plain SGD with restarts, recovery time and final loss. Killed if SGD with restarts recovers as fast.
- **Interpretability by construction.** Priced events are a concept bottleneck the model chose itself (Koh et al. 2020), each with a price history and a settlement record. Post-hoc features from sparse autoencoders (Cunningham et al. 2023; Bricken et al. 2023) have no price and no settlement trail. The offer is: "the network used E to decide" is a ledger query, and "how much E mattered" is its Aumann–Shapley pay. The limits are K5 (events are identifiable only as partitions) and crux 7 (reproducibility across seeds).
- **Compute-adaptive depth.** ACT and PonderNet learn halting by gradient with a prior on steps (Graves 2016; Banino et al. 2021). A value-of-computation market makes the exchange rate between nats and compute explicit and uses the same price for halting, routing and entry. This is a design-clarity niche, not an efficiency one, unless crux 6 passes.

## 3. Alignment

Kelvin's premise, that a scheme with a large tax still beats the status quo, is fair: E-MLP-1 measures the tax (0.609 against 0.435 nats) and it is paid in accuracy, not in safety.

**What explicit budgets, settlement and priced events offer.**
- *Auditability.* Every internal variable that matters has a listing, a price history and a settlement record; the ledger is a complete account of who paid whom for which claim. Eliciting latent knowledge (Christiano, Cotra and Xu 2021) asks how to get a model to report what it knows; a priced event is knowledge that is reported by construction, because the trader is paid for the report. The hard part of ELK survives unchanged: if settlement is by human labels, traders learn to predict the labeller, not the world. Markets make the settlement source the objective, visibly, which is an improvement in transparency and not a solution.
- *Incentive design.* Proper scoring makes truthful reporting each trader's best myopic play; bounded loss per trade bounds any one trader's influence on the belief; bankruptcy is a kill switch that needs no retraining. This is debate with money (Irving, Christiano and Amodei 2018): many parties, a judge that is a scoring rule, and the same requirement that the final claim be verifiable.
- *Corrigibility.* A trader holds no utility over the world, only wealth in the regulator's currency, so switching it off costs it nothing it can act on; the off-switch problem (Hadfield-Menell et al. 2017; Soares et al. 2015) moves from the trader to the regulator, which is code we write. Indifference payments (Armstrong and O'Rourke 2017), paying a trader as if the market had continued, are implementable as a settlement rule.

**What goes wrong.**
- *Collusion and bluffing.* With a market maker, truthful play is an equilibrium only when signals are conditionally independent; otherwise traders bluff (Chen et al. 2010). Traders that share parameters or read each other's prices are not independent. Manipulators can still improve accuracy when informed traders profit from them (Hanson and Oprea 2009), and strategic traders do aggregate for separable securities (Ostrovsky 2012), so security design decides the outcome.
- *Manipulation of settlement.* Any trader that can influence its own settlement variable has an instrumental control incentive (Everitt et al. 2021). Chained settlement (type 2), decision settlement (type 4) and the hindsight posterior (section 0) all let traders touch their settlement. This is performative prediction (Perdomo et al. 2020) and auto-induced distributional shift (Krueger et al. 2020): a trader that steers routing or halting is paid to make the world predictable. Design rule: no trader may influence the examples, labels or downstream markets it is settled on; settle listings on held-out examples, settle chains only against markets the trader does not trade in, keep terminal outcomes as the anchor.
- *Self-fulfilling prices.* Decision markets are only incentive-compatible with full-support decision rules (Othman and Sandholm 2010; Chen et al. 2014); arm C's deterministic halt was this bug.
- *Goodhart.* Regressional Goodhart on noisy information estimates is met by held-out settlement; adversarial Goodhart, a trader modelling the regulator, is not (Manheim and Garrabrant 2018).
- *Mesa-optimisers.* A trader with an internal multi-layer module is a learned optimiser only if it searches; the economy does not stop that, it makes the trader's outer objective explicit (wealth under a stated settlement), which turns the inner-alignment question into "does it maximise wealth or a proxy" (Hubinger et al. 2019). Bounded budgets cap what deception earns per episode, not what it accumulates.

## 4. Theory links, and exactly where we approximate

- **MDL and prequential coding.** The market maker's total subsidy over a stream is the log-loss improvement, which is the reduction in prequential code length (Dawid 1984; Rissanen 1978). For Kelly bettors, log wealth equals minus the prequential code length of the trader's predictions plus a constant (Cover 1991; Blume and Easley 2006), so wealth *is* a compression score. Our regulated rules split that code-length saving among parts by Aumann–Shapley; P8's rent is a model-cost term. Where we differ from MDL: the model cost is paid in compute per step, not in bits of parameters (Hinton and van Camp 1993; Blier and Ollivier 2018 measure the latter), so we do not select shortest models.
- **Speed prior and Levin complexity.** Kt(x) = min over programs of length plus log runtime (Levin 1973; Li and Vitányi 2008); the speed prior weights programs by 2^(−length)/runtime and gives near-optimal predictions for resource-bounded sources (Schmidhuber 2002; Hutter 2002 for the search side). A trader's log-wealth growth is its code-length saving minus compute cost times runtime, so survival selects for short-and-fast, which is a Kt-style criterion. Where we differ: selection is by local mutation and respawn among a few traders, not a universal search, so no optimality bound transfers.
- **Bounded rationality (Ortega and Braun).** Their decision-maker maximises expected utility minus (1/β) KL from a prior, and the solution is a Gibbs distribution with free energy as the certainty equivalent (Ortega and Braun 2013; Ortega et al. 2015). The LMSR cost function C(q) = b log Σ exp(q_i / b) is that free energy with b = 1/β, and the price is that Gibbs distribution. A trader's expected pay for moving the price from p to p' is KL(p' || p) in nats: exactly their information cost. The sequential LMSR along depth is therefore a chain of bounded-rational updates, each paying the KL from the previous price. This link is exact, not approximate; what we lack is their hierarchy of resource parameters (one β per level), which would be one liquidity per depth band.
- **Rational metareasoning.** The gain head predicts the next step's payment: myopic value of computation in nats, and "stop when it is below cost" is the Russell and Wefald (1991) rule. Known failure: myopic VOC undervalues sequences where no single step changes the decision but several do (Hay et al. 2012 treat it as a bandit over computations; Griffiths, Lieder and Goodman 2015 and Lieder and Griffiths 2020 for the resource-rational programme). We approximate with one-step lookahead and epsilon exploration.
- **Logical induction.** The criterion is inexploitability by polynomial-time traders over sentences settled by a deductive process (Garrabrant et al. 2016). Our internal markets are a finite version whose deductive process is the network's own later computation anchored on labels. What transfers: the market maker's bounded loss (b ln n) is the only exploitability bound we have. What does not: the consistency guarantees, which in logical induction come from the trader class being rich enough to include every arbitrageur; we get them only for constraints we install (P12), which is the MATS soup's lesson.

## 5. Honest odds

Chance that this programme yields a real theoretical advance, meaning a depth-local learning rule with a global guarantee or an open-ended representation result that a careful referee would call new: **about 10 to 15%**. Chance of a useful engineering result (valuation, pruning, routing, auditable events, an adaptive-depth controller): about 50%. Chance that hindsight settlement or the chunked economy beats DT-Recall at 100× depth on mazes: about 25%.

The base rates behind this: three fleet rounds and the MATS track found that counterfactual pay values parts well and never trains better (E-MLP-1, E0-2, block-local market); the equivalences in K1, K2, K9 and section 0 show most of the mechanisms are 1977 to 2017 methods with a ledger. The novelty that is not yet ruled out is the combination: open question listing, bounded trust and selection with regret bounds, at depths where backprop cannot run.

What would move the odds most in the next two weeks:
- **Up, strongly:** P6 R1 or F beating R0 (DT-Recall) on P2 mazes 9 → 59 at depth ratio ≥ 100 over 5 seeds (to about 35%); P7 Bx ≥ A at chain depth 4 and length 256 with the mod-3 latent recovered by probes and flat memory in depth (to about 30%).
- **Up, modestly:** P4 pay ranking gates above copies and epiphenomena at every level while MI-1BIT does not.
- **Down, strongly:** R0 dominating at every depth ratio and P7 B or Bx collapsing or failing at depth 4 (to about 5%); then the honest product is a valuation and control layer on top of a gradient-trained belief stream, which is still worth shipping.
- **Down, modestly:** P11 cap-off as stable as cap-on, and PBT-uniform matching EC in P6.
