## 1. What the negatives rule out, and what survives

**Ruled out, with the evidence and the theory that predicted it.**

1. *Pay rules as training signals.* E-MLP-1, E0-2, P3, P4, P8, P9, P10 and the MATS block-local market: no pay rule ever beat backprop, a cheap selector or a free heuristic. Wolpert and Tumer (2001) predicted this: the payoff that is both aligned with the global objective and learnable is the difference utility, which is leave-one-out, whose gradient is the global gradient (K1, E0-1). Every other split trades alignment for fairness. Close this line; the catalogue's "stop testing pay rules as training signals" is right.
2. *A market over depth.* The audit shows the trader payoff's gradient equals detached per-step log loss plus the covariance loss (cosine 1.000000), the cap never binds (final wealth about 2,900 from 20; nocap 0.92), and the auction adds nothing. The 0.93 against 0.12 at 100× is real, but it is truncated backprop with k = 1, deep supervision and random module order: the one-step gradient of HRM and Bansal's progressive loss (K8). Early phase 3 says order alone moves the number from 0.325 to 0.80 to 0.93. This is a finding about detachment and order, not about money.
3. *Value of computation as a source of gain.* P0 measured 1 to 5% halting headroom; an entropy threshold matches VOC within 0.02 (audit); KL(q ‖ p) loses to predictive entropy as a predictor of where depth helps (P7, Spearman 0.64 against 0.90). Callaway et al. (2018) and Hansen and Zilberstein (2001) are the known ways to learn or plan a stopping rule; nothing here improves on them.
4. *The wealth cap as a trust region.* P11: a hard stop that 98 to 99% of on-path pixels exhaust; ties the uncapped model; progressive loss beats it by 58 points. There was no overthinking to fix. This kills THEORY.md moonshot 2.
5. *Pricing as a way to find or choose latents.* P3 (pay rules find factors 3/20, same as additive pay; Locatello et al. predicted no neutral mechanism finds factors), P4 (Aumann–Shapley ranks worse than sampled Shapley at equal cost, 0.924 against 0.981), P8 (the MDL price ranks worse than importance alone, tau 0.33 against 0.70, because the learnability charge is tens of thousands of bits), P9 (clamping is teacher forcing on the network's own latents plus importance-weighting noise), P10 (every learned event collapsed to a constant; possibly an initialisation bug). This kills THEORY.md moonshot 1 as implemented. The binding problem is proposing candidates, not pricing them: P8's non-oracle pool listed nothing.
6. *Joint markets and installed arbitrageurs.* P2 failed its own gate and then lost to hard-EM labels by 18 points; XOR-type structure is invisible to prices (Feigenbaum, Fortnow, Pennock and Sami 2005). P12's flow-conservation trader helps in distribution but post-hoc decoding of plain DT does more (73% against 66%), and it is null on wide mazes. This kills THEORY.md moonshots 4 and 6 as stated.
7. *Hindsight settlement as a new learner.* Only the exact inference forms work: Bl (likelihood messages) trains depth-4 maze chains locally with aligned latents (nmi 0.66 to 0.80), and Bx (forward-backward) is the only arm that learns the mod-3 counter. The literal q-form collapses at depth 4; the rate cost collapses to the marginal. So local settlement is exact inference in a small discrete model, input-output HMM EM (Bengio and Frasconi 1996), and the market frame adds nothing to it (THEORY-2 §0 said this before the data).

**Survives.**

- *Prices as diagnostics.* Shapley-type pay ranks units at 0.97 (E-MLP-3); arbitrage prices detect wrong mazes at AUROC about 0.86 within a size (P12); KL(q ‖ p) flags mis-encoded intermediates at 0.86 to 0.90 (P7). Cheap, useful, not new (Neuron Shapley; semantic-loss checks).
- *Detached per-step training in random order extrapolates.* If the queued tb1_rand arm matches the economy, this is a clean result about local deep supervision and order randomisation on prefix parity, worth a short paper on its own terms and worth testing on mazes at width 128, where DT-Recall is already at 1.0 (P0 M-ref).
- *Learned local costs cleared by an exact solver* (CostNet+BB): 27% exact on wide mazes against 3% for DT, one seed, width 32. Known as blackbox-solver differentiation (Vlastelica et al. 2020). The lesson that matters is the constraint on the signal, local and size-invariant, not the solver.
- *Exact local inference for discrete latent chains* (Bl, Bx): a depth-free trainer in the narrow case of small discrete latents, with no backprop across sites.

## 2. Is "checkable local signals cleared by exact rules" the right reframing?

**As engineering guidance, yes. As a claim of novelty, no.** The reframing says: traders sell local, size-invariant, checkable signals (edge costs, validity, residuals); an exact rule (shortest path, a constraint checker, flow propagation, a greedy knapsack by value per cost) clears them into a global decision; settlement is checkable at run time and needs no labels. Each piece is known:

- Learned local costs plus an exact solver is structured prediction with exact decoding, and its modern form is Vlastelica et al. (2020); exact inference inside a network is Kim et al. (2017).
- A proposer whose claims a cheap verifier checks is a prover-verifier game (Anil et al. 2021; Kirchner et al. 2024, where legibility comes from a verifier weaker than the prover).
- P17's clearing rule, bids by probability of a valid grid per unit cost with a pooled budget, is monitoring an anytime algorithm (Hansen and Zilberstein 2001) and learning to select computations (Callaway et al. 2018). Its "market" is a parimutuel pool of logistic traders on signal subsets, whose price is the wealth-weighted mean (Beygelzimer, Langford and Pennock 2012): an online Bayesian model average of small classifiers, that is, a stacked ensemble.

**The one piece of economic content that is not just a rename:** when settlement is a checker, the Kelly reweighting of traders runs online at test time with no labels, and it carries a regret bound (Cover 1991; Blume and Easley 2006). Label-free online model averaging with a checkable settlement is a real property. It is small, and P17's information-only ablations (one trader, random prices, and I would add frozen wealth) are exactly the test of whether it does anything. Prior on P17 passing both its kill rule and its secondary test: about 10%. EqR's smallest-residual and majority-vote rules already capture most of the 93.0 to 99.8 headroom, and restart-until-valid captures the checker's share.

**What would make the reframing genuinely new.** A market for *which checks to run* when verification itself costs compute (a verification budget), or a clearing rule that is learned yet provably exact. Neither exists in the programme today. Without one, drop the word "market" from this line and call it what it is: verifier-settled structured prediction. Keep the discipline it imposes (every signal checkable, local, size-invariant), because that discipline produced the only extrapolation result we have.

## 3. The find-bit/use-bit chicken-and-egg (Kelvin, mail #2582)

**The problem, precisely.** The value of a candidate bit z is the prequential gain of the best reader that uses it, minus the cost of learning to use it. Measuring it needs a reader that already uses z; training that reader needs to know z is worth it.

**What is known.** This is the oldest problem in constructive learning and has three standard answers, which map onto Kelvin's three options.

1. *Alternate with a first-order price (Kelvin's option 1).* Fix the reader; score every candidate by the correlation between z and the functional gradient of the whole-output log-likelihood with respect to a new additive contribution; add the best; retrain the reader; repeat. This is cascade-correlation (Fahlman and Lebiere 1990), gradient boosting (Friedman 2001) and, in market form, constraint generation, where the security with the largest price violation is listed next (Dudík, Lahaie and Pennock 2012). The first-order price is exact for a linear reader and a lower bound otherwise. Kelvin's worry about global effects ("the output should be a poem") does not bite here: the functional gradient of the *whole-output* likelihood with respect to the bit's contribution is one scalar per example, so per-output counting is never needed. What does bite is zero first-order value: XOR-type bits that only matter jointly with another bit, the same obstruction as Feigenbaum et al.'s XOR result for prices. Small simultaneous updates are generalised EM (Neal and Hinton 1998) and are what backprop into a new unit does; P7 shows the local version survives only in exact-inference form.
2. *Adversarial proposer and user (option 2).* Proposer and user want the same thing, so the game is cooperative, not a GAN (Goodfellow et al. 2014). The adversarial ingredient is needed only against Goodhart, a proposer that games the user, which is the "sneaky prover" of Kirchner et al. (2024). Not principled as a value measurement; principled as a robustness device once a value measurement exists.
3. *A fast universal reader (option 3).* Define the value of z as the prequential code-length reduction when a fixed-capacity fast reader is given z as an extra input: prequential MDL with fast learners (Bornschein et al. 2022; Blier and Ollivier 2018), where the fast reader is a meta-learned or in-context learner (Finn et al. 2017; Xie et al. 2021; von Oswald et al. 2023). This is the most principled option: it is P8's "bits saved minus bits to learn" with the right learner. P8's price failed because its learning charge was computed for a slow learner and reached tens of thousands of bits, so the price ranked by learnability. A few-shot reader makes the charge small and puts the price back near importance for the bits that a reader can actually pick up. The "copy of Y" objection dissolves when candidate bits must be computed forward from x and the state (self-certifying events, THEORY.md §2): a copy of y is not computable before y unless the network already knows y, and then it deserves the pay. P8's ruler rewarding a noisy copy of y was a ruler bug.

**What remains open and is the real bottleneck: proposing.** Cascade-correlation's proposer trains candidates to maximise the first-order score, which is backprop into a new unit, and P8's gradient-trained pool listed nothing worth pricing, while brute force recovered the parity tree 3/3. The alternative proposer is enumeration over a small discrete program space, priced by fast-reader prequential gain per unit compute: Levin search with the speed prior (THEORY-2 §4). That is the only place left where "selection by wealth per compute" is not a rename of gradient descent.

**Cheapest decisive experiment (CPU, about $5).** Candidates: the P8 oracle ladder g_k on 9×9 mazes plus distractors (a 5% noisy copy of y computed only in hindsight, permuted versions of g_k, unrelated events, constants). Three pricers: (i) first-order boosting score against the current reader; (ii) prequential gain of a fast reader (a small reader adapted for at most 100 steps from a meta-initialised state, or an in-context linear probe); (iii) the full wrapper (P8's R7b, tau 0.83, the ceiling). Ruler: pre-registered gain over base at N = 4096, as in the queued P8 follow-up. Kill rules: if (ii) does not reach tau ≥ 0.70 at ≤ 5% of the wrapper's compute, the chicken-and-egg has no cheap answer and proposing must be done by search; if (i) matches (ii), the fast reader is unnecessary and boosting's price suffices. Either outcome closes the question.

## 4. Alignment and auditability, given the negatives

The nulls remove the mechanism the alignment story rested on. THEORY-2 §3 offered auditability of priced events, bounded influence via caps, bankruptcy as a kill switch and corrigibility moved to the regulator. After this round: priced events collapse when trained (P10) and are not the network's actual variables when computed post hoc; caps never bind, and when forced to bind they are a hard stop that hurts (P11); no economic trader had a function to switch off. An alignment tax is worth paying only when the mechanism does something, and no market mechanism did.

What survives is exactly the reframing in section 2: *verifier-settled local signals with exact clearing* gives outputs that are checkable by construction, and the training signal is label-free at run time. That is the prover-verifier literature's offer (legibility, checkability), plus the diagnostic prices (unit Shapley, arbitrage price as an error detector, hindsight KL as a mis-encoding flag), which are interpretability tools that work on networks trained by anything. The risks in THEORY-2 §3 (settlement manipulation, self-fulfilling decision rules, bluffing, adversarial Goodhart) stay as stated for any design that keeps chained or decision settlement. Honest summary: the salvageable alignment content is "make every internal claim settle against a checker", which is a design principle, not a market.

## 5. The two or three experiments most likely to change our minds

1. **Close the market-ResNet attribution, then test the survivor on mazes.** The queued tb1_rand arm against the economy at L3200 on the fresh 200-string set, plus bptt_cov_dscov_rand (full BPTT, random order). Kill rule, as the audit's: if tb1_rand reaches the economy's accuracy, the economics attribution is retired for good. Then the positive test: detached per-step deep supervision in random module order on P2 mazes 9 → 59 at width 128, 5 seeds, against DT-Recall with progressive loss (alpha swept over 0, 0.5, 1). Kill: no gain over progressive loss at 59 → the order and detachment lever is prefix-parity only. Pass: a paper about training order, not about markets. This is the cheapest experiment with a real chance of a positive.
2. **The find-bit pricing test of section 3** (CPU, about $5), with its two kill rules. It decides whether any pricing of candidate variables can be cheap, which every remaining representation idea depends on.
3. **P17 as pre-registered, with one added ablation:** M-chk with wealth frozen after val, against online Kelly updating on test. Kill rules as pre-registered (paired bootstrap lower bound ≤ 0 against SR or MV → No gain; against SEQ-chk or SR+chk → checker gain, not market gain). Added reading: if online updating does not beat frozen wealth, the last economic ingredient does nothing, and the line closes with the word "market" retired.

**Odds, revised from THEORY-2 §5.** A real theoretical advance from the economy framing: about 5% (was 10 to 15%). A useful engineering result from what survived, none of it economic (order-randomised detached deep supervision; local costs plus exact solvers; verifier-settled selection): about 40%. The economy framing itself producing anything not already named in the literature: under 5%. Recommendation: keep prices as diagnostics and checkable settlement as a design rule; stop calling detached deep supervision a market; spend the next dollars on experiments 1 and 2.
