- **Stop rule: all four pool arms fail** (learnable BCE ≥ (a)+1% in 3/3 seeds; `verdict.json`
  rel_vs_a: cg +35/+40/+44%, cq +36/+41/+43%, d0 +10/+33/+46%, dfs +38/+24/+6%). All beat the market
  l0 alone in 3/3 (−21% to −45%). base_arms_survive (w2 part) = false.
- **The failure is one family.** Learned-k pools never learn lfsr16 (0.69 in 6/6 runs, ≈ ln 2) while
  (a) learns it 3/3; wealth pools learn it in 1/3 (d0) and 2/3 (dfs). Without lfsr16
  (`sensitivity_no_lfsr.json`, not preregistered) the pools tie or beat (a): cg −3.7/−4.0/−1.0%,
  cq −2.7/−3.5/−0.9%, d0 −0.2/−2.3/+1.0%, dfs −0.7/0.0/+0.4%.
- **Humility is real but small in absolute terms:** noise excess on ca30/prng falls from 0.0118
  (a) to 0.0022 (cg), a 5x cut, because the pool anchors at l0 and averages overconfidence away.
- **Wealth lock-in:** pure Bayes wealth (d0) concentrates on one trader per question (top share
  0.98 ± 0.02, eff. traders 1.08), i.e. the pool degenerates to a single head; fixed share keeps
  ~14 effective traders. Neither fixes lfsr16 reliably.
- Mechanism (hypothesis, not tested): a convex mixture of sigmoid beliefs can be no more confident than
  its most confident trader and splits gradient by k (≈1/16 each), so a family that needs the trunk to
  build a sharp feature from scratch (LFSR XOR structure) gets ~16x less readout gain than a logit sum.
  Consistent with the settled premise that a convex pool cannot add independent evidence.
  A gain-matched pool (heads scaled ×16 or trained on their own loss) is the obvious next arm.
