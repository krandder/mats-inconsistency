## What commitment does to wealth

1. **Block-local commitment makes clones.** Heads trained on the same target from the same
   residual converge to the same prediction: mean pairwise correlation of head corrections
   0.9934 ± 0.0015 (Cm, Cw) vs 0.8115 ± 0.0053 (U) and 0.5551 ± 0.0388 (Cs). Each clone's
   leave-one-out value is ~0 (|Delta| < 1e-6 for most traders; `results/byhead.csv`), while
   each earns the full stand-alone settlement K ~ 655 nats (`byhead.py` output, Cm head 0 vs 1-15
   equal). Committed settlement pays every clone for the same information: it measures
   stand-alone skill (pooled Spearman K vs stand-alone oracle 0.9142 ± 0.0238), not marginal
   value (0.0 within noise: Spearman K vs Delta -0.0016 ± 0.0221 Cm, -0.0296 ± 0.0547 Cw).
   The sum of committed wealth is 16x the market's gain (book_sumK 754388 vs book_market_gain
   47229, Cm): not conserved.
2. **Wealth stakes concentrate with no skill difference.** Cw's Bayesian stakes end with a mean
   max stake of 0.3355 ± 0.0210 per question among clones, and change nothing (Cw BCE = Cm BCE
   to 4 digits).
3. **Sequential commitment conserves and gets the sign right, but costs accuracy.** In Cs the
   committed settlements sum exactly to the market's log-score gain (book_sumK 25754.70 ±
   4496.79 = book_market_gain 25754.70 ± 4496.77). Head 0 takes the signal (K +579, Delta +20.5e-3,
   stand-alone +47.5e-3) and heads 1-15 have negative settlement and are harmful to the market
   (K -14.8, Delta -0.71e-3) (`results/byhead.csv`, means over seeds). Among traders that are
   useful (Delta > 0), committed settlement is positive for 0.7387 ± 0.0591 of them and gradient
   credit G for only 0.2803 ± 0.0296; the most important head has negative gradient credit
   (G -19.2) and the largest settlement. Within a question, K ranks the later heads against
   Delta (within-question Spearman -0.2736 ± 0.0508): an order effect among harmful heads.
4. **The sign problem at the output layer (U).** Among useful traders, gradient credit is positive
   for 0.6441 ± 0.0847, the counterfactual committed settlement for 0.9867 ± 0.0057. Pooled over
   questions G tracks Delta (Spearman 0.8689 ± 0.0109) because questions differ in how much the
   heads matter; within a question the ranks are weak for every measure (G 0.2046 ± 0.0415,
   S 0.2372 ± 0.0274, K 0.2662 ± 0.0032). Mean Delta is negative (U heads overshoot the market;
   frac useful 0.3330 ± 0.0612).
5. Gini of K vs the shuffled-label null is far above null in every arm (z 29-81) because every
   head has real skill over l0; it does not separate the arms here.

## Hypotheses (as preregistered)

- H1 (U: Spearman(G, Delta) < 0.3 and sign agreement < 0.7): pooled Spearman 0.87 fails; within-question
  0.20 and useful-sign agreement 0.64 pass. Partly supported.
- H2 (committed K beats U's G by >= 0.2 within-question Spearman in 3/3 seeds): **fails** for Cs, Cm, Cw.
- H3 (Cw within 1% of U): holds (Cw is better).

## Answer to the RETARGET item

Making output heads commit changes what wealth means, but not into importance: with parallel
commitment wealth becomes stand-alone skill (clones all rich, none needed, not conserved); with
sequential commitment at the trade price wealth is conserved and has the right sign for the one
head that matters (where gradient credit has the wrong sign), but the market is 1.3-2.0% worse
and within-question ranks are not better than gradient credit. Units that commit in parallel to
the same question need something that forces them apart (disjoint inputs, niches, or trading
against each other's price) before wealth can mean marginal value.

Not done here: stress tests, heads inside the trunk or MoE experts as sub-market traders, the
block-local architecture test from the 01:00Z addendum.
