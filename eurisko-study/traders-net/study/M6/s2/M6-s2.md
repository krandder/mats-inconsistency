**Setup.** w2's T=60k harness (16 trader heads per question, market l0 = per-question logistic). Heads 8-15 are
replaced by untrainable noise traders at sigma = 1 logit: `iid` (fresh N(0,1) per sample) or `fixed` (a frozen
random linear readout of the question's window: consistent, confident, wrong). Arms: a, b, cg, cq, fb01, fb05,
kill controls wd and boost (ν = 0.1). The noise heads enter where a trainable head would. 72 runs on G1 VMs.

## TL;DR
- **iid noise traders: the learned-k convex pool is the one stress win.** cg's learnable BCE moves by
  +0.0011 ± 0.0012 (0.2342 ± 0.0191 noisy vs 0.2330 ± 0.0189 clean), and it puts k_noise = 5e-5 on the eight
  noise heads. It beats the best kill control in 3/3 seeds, with and without LFSR16 (H1, H2, H3 pass). cq also
  passes H1 and H2 (k_noise 0.050 ± 0.005, Δ +0.0026 ± 0.0005) but not H3 (+0.8 to +1.4%). `verdict.json` `iid`.
- **Sums cannot fire a trader.** a, b and wd lose 0.25-0.27 nats of learnable BCE (+118% to +178%) and end worse
  than the market alone. Their noise-family excess is 0.65 nats. Boost pays 0.1 × the noise: +0.025 ± 0.034 (+0.006
  ± 0.003 without LFSR16), and its noise-family excess goes from 0.0036 to 0.0142. Bounded bets cap each noise
  trader, but they do not silence it: fb01 +0.019 ± 0.002, fb05 +0.060 ± 0.027 (noise excess 0.150). `summary.csv`.
- **Fixed (consistent) noise traders: no stress win.** Backprop learns to cancel a fixed linear readout. a's
  change is −0.003 ± 0.006 (+0.007 ± 0.002 without LFSR16). cg defunds the noise heads again (k_noise 4e-5,
  Δ +0.0008 ± 0.0008), but it loses on learnable BCE to the kill controls in 3/3 seeds because it never learns
  LFSR16. Without LFSR16 it beats them in only 2/3 seeds (0.1794/0.1352/0.1560 vs min(kc) 0.1824/0.1355/0.1541).

## Reading
1. **The convex pool's real asset is the exit.** A convex pool can set a trader's weight to zero, and learned k
   does so within the run. A logit sum cannot remove a head it does not control, and shrinkage only scales it.
   This is the "fire the noise trader" property, and the base pass could not show it, because there every head
   was trainable. It is expected: under BCE, an iid noise head has a pure-loss gradient on its k (known behaviour
   of mixture weights; no new theory claimed).
2. **Humility against noise *traders* is not humility against noise *questions*.** Clean cg already had the lowest
   noise-family excess (0.0022). Under iid noise traders it stays there, while boost rises about 4x and fb05 20x.
3. **Consistent wrong traders are a different stress.** Backprop cancels them inside a sum, at a cost of 0.006-0.009
   nats without LFSR16. For boost the cost is 0.001: its trainable heads carry the cancelling readout at 10x.
4. **Confound: frozen random readouts make LFSR16 learnable.** Under `fixed` noise, LFSR16 BCE falls to 0.13-0.33
   in 3/3 seeds for a, b, wd, boost and fb05. In clean runs it was not learnt in 2/3 seeds for boost, 2/3 for fb05
   and 1/3 for wd (`per_seed.csv`, column `lfsr16`). The pools stay at 0.695 in 9/9 runs. So the learnable-BCE
   gains of those arms under `fixed` noise (e.g. boost −0.042 ± 0.027) are LFSR16, not robustness. A plausible
   mechanism (unverified) is that cancelling a random linear readout of the window forces the residual to carry
   the window linearly, which helps the XOR-linear LFSR. This matches the base pass's finding that LFSR16 luck
   decides the BCE clause.

## Hypotheses (PREREG) → outcome
- H1 (pool defunds noise traders, k_noise < 0.1 in 3/3): **pass** for cg and cq, under both kinds of noise.
- H2 (noisy learnable BCE < min(wd, boost) in 3/3, with and without LFSR16): **pass for cg and cq under iid;
  fail for every arm under fixed noise** (cg fails on LFSR16; without it, 2/3).
- H3 (harm ≤ 1% of clean in 3/3): iid: cg only. Fixed: a, cg, fb05 and boost pass. For fb05 and boost this is via
  LFSR16, see Reading 4.
