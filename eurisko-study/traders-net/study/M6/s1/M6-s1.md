- **No arm beats the kill controls in either stress regime** (`verdict-s1.json` `any_beats_kill_controls_3of3: []`).
  Survivors under the plan's stop rule: B, CQ, FB05. Dropped: CG and FB01 (IE regime only).
- **MS: humility layers limit overfitting, but early stopping wins.** With 2400 train positions and 100 epochs, the plain
  sum A memorises the noise questions: 2.61 ± 0.16 nats above ln 2 on the test set (train −0.68). At the final epoch, bounded bets
  FB01 are the least damaged arm (humility 0.224 ± 0.013, learnable 0.362 ± 0.068), ahead of CG/CQ (0.49/0.51), BOOST (0.77) and
  WD (2.34). Early stopping on A reaches 0.297 ± 0.021 learnable and 0.011 ± 0.003 humility, better than every arm at its final epoch.
  Once each arm is early-stopped itself, all arms tie (learnable 0.296-0.302) (`SECONDARY-s1.json` `own_es_*`).
- **IE: a convex pool is not blocked from adding independent evidence, because each head reads the whole residual.**
  Regret over Bayes on the `indep` family: CQ 0.028 ± 0.006 (the lowest), ES 0.031 ± 0.005, WD 0.032 ± 0.002, BOOST 0.034 ± 0.006,
  FB05 0.040, B 0.042, A 0.043 ± 0.005, FB01 0.052 ± 0.008, CG 0.066 ± 0.054 (one bad seed, 0.129). The market alone: 0.195.
  My prediction that FB01's 1.6-nat cap would double A's regret was wrong: it costs 1.2x.
- **LFSR16 again decides the IE BCE clause.** CG, CQ and BOOST learn LFSR16 in 1/3 seeds (A in 3/3). Without it, their learnable BCE
  ties A: 0.185/0.183/0.185 vs 0.185 (`SECONDARY-s1.json` `learn_exlfsr_ms`).

Arms: A Σd; B l0+Σd; CG/CQ convex pool logit(Σ k_i σ(l0+d_i)) with k = softmax(θ) global / per question; FB01/FB05
l0 + Σ b·tanh(d_i/b); kill controls WD (AdamW 0.1), BOOST (l0+0.1Σd), ES (A at the val-chosen eval).
- **MS**: S0 stream Q=64, T=24032; train [0,2400), val [16800,19200), test [19200,24000); 100 epochs, eval every 5.
- **IE**: w1's ME split (train 16800 positions, 20 epochs) plus 8 `indep` questions: y_t ~ Bern(σ((2y_{t−1}−1) + Σ_{j≤3}(2x_{a_j,t−1}−1))),
  a_j = three PRNG questions. The market sees only its own lag; the three cross-question terms are visible only through the trunk.
  Bayes BCE is computed from the true p_t (`job/run_s1.py` `make_stream`).

## Readings
1. **Under heavy overfitting, the humility mechanisms are partial regularisers.** Bounding bets caps how far the net can move
   away from the market on memorised noise, but 16 × 0.1 nats is still 1.6 nats of freedom: FB01 ends 0.22 nats above ln 2. The
   convex pools do this by averaging (final k_eff 1.1-3.1 heads). Neither replaces a validation set. Once each arm is early-stopped,
   the differences vanish (own-ES learnable 0.296-0.302; FB01 own-ES humility 0.046 ± 0.061 because seed 3 picked epoch 55).
2. **"A convex pool cannot add independent evidence" is true of traders with separate information, not of this layer.** Every head
   reads the full residual, so one head can compute the summed evidence, and the per-question pool CQ then has the lowest
   regret of any arm (k_eff 5.4-7.1 of 16). The premise binds only if traders are given disjoint views. That arm is not run here.
3. **Bounded bets cost on learnable families, not only on IE.** FB01's learnable BCE is +27 to +42% vs A with LFSR16 learnt in 3/3
   seeds (ex-LFSR 0.203 vs 0.185), in line with w3's base pass (+7-8% vs B). Its IE regret is only 1.2x A's: the cap mostly limits
   the sharp deterministic families, not a ±3-nat evidence sum at these frequencies.
4. **The market-inside arms (CG, CQ, BOOST) miss LFSR16 in 2/3 seeds here as in the base pass.** Ex-LFSR16 they tie A.
   Any M6 verdict that turns on learnable BCE is decided by this one family.
