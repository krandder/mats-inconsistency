**TL;DR.** In a Kelly economy with skill differences, no rebalancing fund beat buy-and-hold. Across 3 rules x 2
topologies x 2 scales, and in 3/3 seeds each, the rebalancing funds lost wealth share to buy-and-hold groups trading
in the same market. Buy-and-hold works as Bayes/market selection; rebalancing keeps putting money back into the
losers. Under shuffled beliefs (no skill), the same leaf-level rebalancing gains share (volatility pumping). Funds
barely move the market price (Brier +0.0035 at most).

## Setup
- The economy is Q=64, B=16 questions per step, T=10000, c=1e-4, subsidy 0.01, split at 4, die at 0.01 (worker1's
  defaults). There are 3 seeds; the stream seed equals the economy seed.
- The leaves are grouped at t=0 into groups of 8. Half of the groups are active funds and half are inert
  buy-and-hold groups, so both kinds sit in the same market.
- Rules:
  - **bh:** no transfers. In this engine it equals the flat economy by construction (T1 lemma 6.1).
  - **crp:** each step, rebalance to equal fractions over the live members.
  - **eg:** exponentiated-gradient fractions, eta 0.05.
  - **fof:** a two-level fund. Its subfunds are within-niche groups of 8, held buy-and-hold. The top fund holds 4
    random subfunds and rebalances across them each step.
- Topologies: **within** (all members trade the same question) and **cross** (members are random).
- Split children stay in their parent's fund; dead members leave it.
- The shuffled-beliefs null runs in lockstep with the same fund structure.
- Transfers are zero-sum. The ledger residual is at most 4.1e-13 (`cons_resid_max` column).

## Preregistered checks
- **P1: bh = flat. Holds** (identical Brier and wealth path), but by construction in this engine. The non-trivial
  version is T1 C6c (`study/T1/THEORY-part3.md`).
- **P2: crp-within moves the market.** The preregistered test compares the paired ΔBrier with 2 sd of the flat arm
  across seeds (0.019).
  - At 1k, ΔBrier is +0.0035 ± 0.0007. It is positive in 3/3 seeds (worse), but below 2 sd of the flat arm across
    seeds, so P2 **fails as written**.
  - At 10k, ΔBrier is 0. The funds die early and their slots are refilled by the offspring of buy-and-hold groups.
  - Effective species per question rose, as predicted.
- **P3: rebalancing adds power (premium > 0 by 2 sd in 3/3 seeds). Refuted for every rule and topology at both
  scales.** Every rebalancing arm's premium is negative in 3/3 seeds.
- **Amendment 2 prediction: fof beats crp-cross and crp-within. Holds** at both scales (-0.68 vs -1.34 and -5.92 at
  1k; -0.89 vs -6.18 and -21.2 at 10k). fof is still negative in 3/3 seeds.

## Reading (for K1.md)
- T1 lemma 6 says a fixed-fraction hierarchy is a flat mixture and only rebalancing changes growth. In this
  economy, rebalancing changes growth for the worse.
  - Buy-and-hold funds are Bayes mixtures, so their wealth concentrates on the best member.
  - Constant or EG rebalancing is anti-selection: every step it tops up the members the market is taking money from.
  - The null shows the other side. Without skill, rebalancing earns the volatility-pumping gain (null premia
    +0.3 to +1.9 at 1k). The real sign is the skill-selection loss outweighing that gain.
  - This is Blume-Easley market selection seen from the fund side (Blume & Easley 2006,
    https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00680.x; Kelly = Bayes, Beygelzimer, Langford, Pennock 2012,
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.6655).
- The loss is front-loaded. Most of the share goes in the first ~500 steps (`out/fund_share_N1000.png`), while the
  members are still learning. In the second half, fof and the 1k within-niche arms are within about 1 sd of 0.
  At 10k, leaf-level rebalancing keeps losing.
- The least-bad structure keeps selection inside the niche (buy-and-hold subfunds) and rebalances only across
  niches (fof).
- Kelvin's "big trader = sum of small traders" (hierarchical ownership) is neutral as accounting (bh). It becomes
  harmful when a fund actively redistributes on a fixed rule.
- Any power from hierarchy would need an inner allocation that is a nonlinear, skill-seeking function of history.
  That is lemma 6.3's condition, and EG with eta 0.05 is not strong enough here.
