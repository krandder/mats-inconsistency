## Stop rule: not triggered
Clade monoculture (top clade >= 0.9 at T) occurs only in 1k Q4096-off (2/3 seeds). Every other cell has 0/3. So the plan
continues; no Blume-Easley stop.
But at the species level (type x memory k), niches-off is a monoculture in every cell and every N: the top species holds
>= 0.997 of wealth (pat4 in 12/12 multi-question runs). Q1 is 0.999 (log4/pat1/pat0 by seed, whichever fits that seed's
stream). This is Blume-Easley market selection acting on the belief rule, not on the lineage. Many clades carry the same
winning rule.

## Findings
1. **Niches decide survival.** Niches-on keeps every slot alive and reaches the oracle Brier at Q64 (0.119 vs oracle 0.119, all N).
   Niches-off loses 88-90% of the population, and its Brier sticks at 0.187-0.191. That run pays 16 questions x c per step, and its
   cumulative compute sink exceeds the inflow: 100k Q64 off sink 237k vs inflow 161k; Q64 on sink 25k vs inflow 388k
   (`cells.csv` sink_compute / R_total).
2. **Skill lowers inequality.** Gini is below the shuffled null in 14/15 cells (1k Q4096-on ties at 0.55 vs 0.55, where most
   questions have no trader). The null's wealth dispersion is luck compounded under Kelly. Trained traders converge in
   belief, so their bets and returns correlate. This refutes the prereg prediction "niches-on Gini above the null".
3. **Scale dilutes lineage concentration but not rule concentration.** Top clade share falls with N in every niches-off arm
   (Q64 off: 0.84 -> 0.51 -> 0.10), while the top species stays >= 0.997.
4. **Q4096 niches-on is coverage-limited.** Its Brier improves with N (0.234 -> 0.176 -> 0.147, oracle 0.123) as more questions
   get traders. At 1k it barely beats the null (0.234 vs 0.235), as the prereg predicted.
5. **Beliefs carry the signal.** The trained market beats its null's Brier in every cell except 1k Q4096-on (where they tie).

Prior art: Kelly bettors = Bayes model averaging, Beygelzimer, Langford and Pennock, https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.6655 (read).
Market selection: Blume and Easley 2006, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00680.x (the DOI resolves; the title "If you're
so smart, why aren't you rich?" is from memory, unverified).

Caveats: 4 runs (100k q64-off s1-3, q4096-off s1) used the econ.py before the 03:1xZ speed edit (no econ_sha in cfg). That edit
changes only the computation order (tests 4/4, same statistics, not bit-identical). There is one stream family mix and one c (1e-4);
the c sweep and the shift are w2's work, and funds of funds are w3's.
