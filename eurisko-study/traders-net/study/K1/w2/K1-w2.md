# K1 worker 2: mid-run shift on/off and compute-price c sweep

2026-09-28, worker 2 of 3 (split in `K1/STATUS.md`: w1 engine + niches + scale, w2 shift + c sweep, w3 funds of funds).
Engine `K1/econ.py` (worker1; read-only). Driver `K1/w2/w2run.py`, aggregation `K1/w2/analyze.py`. Prereg
`K1/w2/PREREG.md` (two amendments, both written before the runs they cover). 54 runs, all rc = 0, CPU only, seeds 1-3,
values are mean ± sd over the 3 seeds. Per-run rows: `K1/w2/results/shift_runs.csv`, `K1/w2/results/csweep_runs.csv`;
aggregates: `K1/w2/results/summary.json`; per-run live CSVs for V1: `K1/runs/w2-<arm>-s<seed>/metrics.csv`.
Ledger conservation holds in every run: `cons_resid_ledger` ≤ 5e-13 and `cons_resid_settle` ≤ 6e-13 (summary.json).

Setup: 64 binary questions (6 families), 16 resolve per step, pari-mutuel Kelly pool with a maker staking 1% of
initial wealth per step at 0.5, split at w ≥ 4, death at w < 0.01, lockstep shuffled-beliefs null.
Two populations: **N = 10k with niches on** (each trader trades one question) and **N = 1k with niches off**
(each trader trades all 16 questions with one pooled model). c is given as c/c*, where c* is the stake-based
break-even (0.04 with niches on, 6.25e-4 with niches off).

- With niches on, the shift is absorbed: shifted questions spike to 0.13 excess regret and fall to 10% of the peak
  in about 830 steps; afterwards they are priced as well as the rest (0.0003 excess, last 2k steps). Recovery is by

  (regret 0.066, 150x the niche market), so a shift makes no measurable difference and nothing locks in or out

- The compute price only destroys. Survivors fall monotonically in c and market Brier never improves: the best
  regret is at c = 0 (within sd up to c/c* = 0.03). Removing "noise traders" does not help because in a Kelly pool
  they already carry almost no wealth.
- Niches on is robust up to c/c* = 0.03 (all 10k slots filled, no monoculture); the collapse is between 0.3 and 3.
  Niches off reaches monoculture (top clade ≥ 0.9) at c/c* ≥ 0.1 in 2/3 seeds and at c/c* = 1 in 3/3.

## Preregistered predictions

- P1 coexistence: **half holds.** Niches on: no monoculture at any c/c* ≤ 1 (top clade ≤ 0.16) and 10 species
  coexist. Niches off: near-monoculture as predicted. But "Gini above the shuffled null" **fails in every arm**: the
  null is more unequal (0.73-0.75 vs 0.38-0.49).
- P2 shift recovery ≤ 3000 steps and newborns > 50%: **holds** (833 ± 58 steps, 0.98).
- P3 c sweep: monotone survivors **holds**; the Brier optimum at an intermediate c **fails** (c = 0 is best); the
  Gini-gap part is ambiguous as worded. The collapse point (amendment 2 predicted c/c* ≈ 0.03) **fails**: the collapse is at 0.3-3.
- K1 stop rule (monoculture in every arm): **not triggered** (`stop_rule_monoculture_every_arm = false` in summary.json).

## Findings for the orchestrator (and caveats)

1. **The shuffled-beliefs Gini null has the wrong sign for this question.** In a Kelly pool, a trader at an accurate
   price earns little relative to the price, so a skilled market's wealth stays flat (Gini ~0.44). Random beliefs
   compound luck and turn over 7x more (213k vs 30k births, `births_null` in `K1/runs/w2-S-on10k-s1/metrics.csv`),
   so the null Gini is ~0.75. The plan's "exciting" outcome ("Gini near 0.8 that beats the null") is therefore the
   signature of a *worse* market here. Suggested replacement test (not run): the correlation between wealth and
   held-out skill (log-score gain over the price) against the null.
2. **The maker's "subsidy" pays for overconfidence (engine semantics, flagged to worker1 in STATUS.md).** The
   pari-mutuel maker's expected P&L is sub·(1 − ½(p_true/p + (1−p_true)/(1−p))): zero at p = p_true, positive for
   p between ½ and p_true, negative only when the price overshoots. Realised inflow is ~3% of the maker's stake
   (66k of 2M in w2-S-on10k-s1), and the null with worse prices receives 2.7x the skilled market's inflow
   (W_traders_null 190k vs 71k at t = 20000, same file). That is why the stake-based c* overstates break-even,
   and why wealth totals should not be compared across main and null.
3. **Niches do the work.** The same engine without niches is a one-species economy (pattern-4 generalists) whose
   price is 150x worse in regret and immune to shifts and prices alike. Coexistence here comes from question-level
   niches (resource partitioning), in line with market-selection results where a single Kelly market selects the
   most accurate trader (Blume & Easley 2006, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00680.x, citation from
   econ.py, not re-read here; Beygelzimer, Langford, Pennock 2012, https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.6655).
4. **Adaptation is by birth, not by learning.** After a shift, the recovered wealth in shifted niches is 98%
   newborns; incumbents' clade ranks elsewhere persist (Spearman 0.87). The recovery rate is set by the split/death
   thresholds (not swept here).

Caveats: the c-sweep baseline (c/c* = 0.0025) comes from the first 10000 steps of the shift-off runs (amendment 1,
identical dynamics to t = 10000). Recovery steps for niches off are noise (peak ≈ placebo). The shift metrics use a
price recomputed by the driver before settlement; its Brier matches the engine's `brier_all` to 1e-15
(`brier_check_sum` column). Box load was 50-90 on 16 cores during the runs (wall times in metrics.csv `wall_s`).
