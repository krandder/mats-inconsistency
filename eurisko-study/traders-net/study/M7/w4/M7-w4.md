# M7 worker 4: global wealth prior on the router (stage 1, "expected leak")

Written 2026-09-28 ~08:55Z. PREREG: `w4/PREREG.md` (06:45Z, before any main run). All paths below are
relative to `study/M7/`.

## Verdict

- **The global wealth prior does not beat DeepSeek and is stopped** under the pre-registered rule: at
  both E=8 and E=64 it collapses routing (max load > 0.5 in 3/3 seeds; beta=1000 sends 100% of tokens
  to one expert and kills 75% (E=8) / 97% (E=64) of experts). **wealth_beats_deepseek = false.**
- The per-family prior (comparator) also fails the rule: collapsed (max load > 0.5) in 3/3 seeds at both E.
- BCE is almost blind to all of this at this scale: every arm, including no balancing at all, sits
  within 0.001-0.005 nats of every other, and the between-seed sd (0.014-0.020, driven by the stream
  seed) is 3-10x larger than any arm gap. Paired-by-seed, every arm, including `none`, is 0.2-0.7%
  *lower* than DeepSeek (u=1e-3) at E=64, 3/3 seeds. So the wealth prior's small paired edge over
  DeepSeek is the same as the no-balancing edge: it comes from not forcing balance, not from wealth.
- The "leak" predicted by the item shows up as **collapse and loss of specialisation**, not as money
  lost: leak_nats is ~0 for every arm, because leave-one-out settlement of a routed expert is almost
  always positive. The global prior destroys expert x family specialisation (MI 0.06 bits at beta=100,
  0 at beta=1000), while the per-family prior raises it (0.9 bits E=8, 1.4 bits E=64).

## Setup (as pre-registered)

Harness `w4/job/moe_w4.py` (S0 substrate copied into `w4/job/substrate/`): a 2-block transformer,
d=32, H=4; block 0 dense MLP; block 1 MoE with E in {8, 64} experts, hidden 32, top-2. Gates are a
softmax over the two selected logits; any bias affects selection only. Stream:
`streams_ext.make_streams(Q=64, T=40032, seed, regime_every=10000, regime_frac=0.25)`. Prequential
single pass: B=8, 5000 steps, Adam 1e-3. Settlement follows CURRENCY.lock: exact leave-one-out log
score at the output, with the router paid nothing. The global prior is
`b_e = beta*(R_e - mean R)`, where R is the EMA of settlement per live token (half-life 1000 steps).
The per-family prior uses R_{e,f}. DeepSeek is the loss-free bias with u=1e-3
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15664). 6 arms x 2 E x 3 seeds = 36 runs, all completed (rc=0).

## Pre-registered rules applied

- Beats DeepSeek (mean lower by > larger seed sd at that E, not worse at the other E): **no arm passes.**
  The largest gap, perfam b100 at E=64 (-0.0054), is below the sd (0.0199). Even on the stricter paired
  view, it is -1.6% with paired sd 0.0045 and one seed carrying it (seed 1 -0.0079 vs `none`; seeds
  2 and 3 +0.0002 and -0.0012).
- Stop rule (max load > 50% in 2/3 seeds): **global b100, global b1000, perfam b100 and perfam b1000 are
  stopped at both E** (3/3 seeds each, `n_collapsed` in `w4/paired.csv`). Honest caveat: `none` also
  collapses 3/3, and DeepSeek at E=64 exceeds 0.5 in 2/3 seeds (0.56, 0.69; `w4/runs.csv`). At u=1e-3
  and 5000 steps, DeepSeek does not balance 64 experts either, and 17-31% of its experts are dead.
- Predictions: "global leaks more than perfam" is partly supported. leak_frac is higher for global
  in 3 of 4 (E, beta) cells. global b1000 shows 0 leak only because a single expert serves every
  family, which is the degenerate end of the leak. "global does not prevent collapse (max load >= none)"
  is supported at both E. "perfam ties or loses to DeepSeek" is supported: a tie within the sd.
- Paired against `none` (same seed, `w4/runs.csv`), the global prior changes bce_last by -0.0004 to
  +0.0010: it neither helps nor hurts loss, and it only removes specialisation.

## Interpretation

A wealth prior that pools earnings across families is a rich-get-richer feedback. The expert with
the most total settlement gets selected more, trains more, earns more, and within ~1000 steps it
serves everything (the E=8 b1000 runs reach max_load 1.0 in all 3 seeds). That is the "leak" in
this substrate: experts do not keep losing money in foreign families, because leave-one-out
settlement for a routed expert is almost never negative, so it cannot flag them. Instead the market
stops routing by family at all. Making the prior per-family removes the pooling and raises
specialisation over DeepSeek (MI 5x at E=8, 7x at E=64 for beta=1000; 1.4-2.4x at beta=100). Collapse remains, though, because a per-family winner still
takes the family. Neither version gives a loss or load-balance reason to replace DeepSeek. At
this model size, balance itself does not buy loss (`none` ties everything). So this test cannot show
a balancing benefit, and a larger-scale or capacity-constrained test is needed before re-asking the question.

## Caveats and unverified

- The settlement signal is exact LOO at the output, which is nearly always >= 0. A negative-revenue leak
  metric is therefore weak here. A priced/collateralised variant (settle against a stake) could leak
  differently; that is not tested (unverified).
- 3 seeds, 5000 steps, d=32. The between-seed sd is dominated by the stream seed. The paired analysis is
  the only view with power, and it is post hoc relative to the prereg's unpaired rule.
- Different harness from w1/w2/w3/w5 (d=32, own copy). The DeepSeek baseline is re-run here and is not
  shared, so cross-worker BCE numbers are not comparable in absolute terms.
- GPU route failed. G1 job `m7-w4-globalprior` ran on spot `tn-gpu-m7-w4-globalprior-09280720-10852`,
  was preempted at 07:37Z, then hit an L4 stockout on requeue, and I withdrew it. The G1 COST-AUDIT
  ceiling is $0.164, at a rate marked UNVERIFIED-ceiling (`G1/GPU-LOG.md`, `G1/infra/state/audit.log`).
  The 8 GPU-synced runs in `w4/gpu/results` carry no raw.npz (sync copies csv/json/log only), so no
