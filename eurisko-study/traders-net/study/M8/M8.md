**Cross-part consistency.** The two parts' ungated 2x256 baselines were coded independently (w1 `none`,
w2 `dense256`) and agree: whole run 0.3554 vs 0.3546, post-shift switched 0.4533 vs 0.4533 (seed-level
differences ≤ 0.0015 nats; `check.py`). w1 holds out the last 512 positions for the census, which explains
the small whole-run gap.

**Reading.** The sign census leans the right way (important neurons are less often negative than the base
rate) but a third of the neurons that matter are still paid negatively, consistent with M1's sign result
on wealth-net (`../M1/CURRENCY.lock` (b)) and M2's tracr ground truth (`../M2/BAKEOFF.md`). The damage at
η = 1 comes from gate concentration, not neuron identity (shuf1 ≈ pos1). Q6 answer for generic neurons: no
income currency survives a regime shift better than random reinit; lock-in for committed traders is in
`../M3/CURRENCY.md` part B.

**LFSR16 caveat.** LFSR16 is bimodal under prequential training (`../S0/SUBSTRATE.md` section 2); both
parts read their rules with lfsr16 targets removed (w1 prereg amendment 2, made before results were read).

**Prior art.** Continual backprop https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13812; Taylor pruning
https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.06440, https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.10771; movement pruning
https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07683 (soft movement pruning ≈ exp(η·income) gating, `../L1/NOVELTY-FLAGS.md` F18);
RigL https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11134 (not run).

**Not done.** One gate family and half-life per part, one rebirth rate and 50% budget, T = 40k, one model
size; the w1 gate is detached (weights can compensate); single-neuron census on 128 positions × 72
questions is noisy per neuron. w2's bulky logits sit in `/tmp/traders-net/M8w2/` (259 MB, not synced;
needed only by `w2/family.py`).
