## TL;DR

1. **The K/Q law is real in the soup and absent in the net.** In the soup, Brier = 0.25 − Δ(1 − (1 − π)^{K/Q})
   fits the priced families with R² 0.94/0.92/0.94/0.79 (periodic/prime/AND/OR), against a shuffled-table
   null with 95th percentile 0.16-0.17 (p = 0.001) (`w1/lottery_fit.csv`). In M6's trader layer, K/Q ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64}
   shows no trend in any of 4 series (`w3/results/VERDICT.json`: `trend=false` ×4, `law_present_B_FT=false`,
   `law_present_A_FT=false`). **The law is about search by independent programs, not about markets.**
2. **What shared computation buys at equal FLOPs:** with a shared trunk, K = 1 already prices what K = 64 prices
   (excl. LFSR16, B-FT paired K64 − K1: +0.0007/+0.0015/−0.0006 nats, `w3/results/EXLFSR.json`). Extra traders per
   question cost: noise excess max(BCE − ln 2) on prng/ca30 rises from ~0.002 (K ≤ 16) to 0.0068-0.0072 at K = 64
   in all 4 series, 3/3 paired seeds (`w3/results/SUMMARY.csv`, metric `humility`).
3. **The net prices what the soup never did, at more compute.** The net prices CA110 and LFSR16 (wealth-net held-out
   0.0046 ± 0.0019 and 0.0004 ± 0.0002, 3/3; `b/final_brier_summary.csv`), which no soup run did (0/21). It needs
   10^9.0 FLOPs/q (CA110) and 10^10.0-10.5 (LFSR) against the soup's maximum budget of 10^7.54 VI/q
   (`b/compute_to_brier_summary.csv`, 1 VI = 1 op assumed). On easy families the soup is cheaper per op
   (prime 10^6.17 VI/q vs 10^7.70 FLOPs/q). The net is data-efficient and compute-hungry; the soup is the reverse.

**Stop rule (plan §3: "two sweep points with no trend: report the law as absent"): triggered. The law is reported
absent in the net.** Expected outcome in plan §4 ("The law is absent in gradient markets"), not the exciting one.

## (a) Soup: lottery fit and why LFSR/CA are unpriced — `M9-a.md`

| family | π (pooled) | log10 π per seed | R² | shuffled p | K/Q for half gain | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| periodic | 0.066 | −1.18 ± 0.04 | 0.94 | 0.001 | 10.1 | `w1/lottery_fit.csv` |
| prime | 0.107 | −0.96 ± 0.09 | 0.92 | 0.001 | 6.1 | same |
| AND | 0.117 | −0.92 ± 0.07 | 0.94 | 0.001 | 5.6 | same |
| OR | 0.042 | −1.39 ± 0.19 | 0.79 | 0.001 | 16.2 | same |
| CA30 / CA110 / pi | n/a | n/a | 0.10 / 0.04 / 0.02 | 0.09 / 0.27 / 0.41 | none | same |

- Lottery beats log-linear by ΔAIC 8.9/11.9/14.0 on periodic/prime/AND (2.8 on OR) and fits far better than K alone
  (R² 0.28-0.44) or Q alone (0.14-0.25), `LK_r2`/`LQ_r2`. Out-of-sample on the 9 K/Q=4 ablation runs: MAE 0.005-0.010 (`variants_mae`).
- Residual K effect at fixed K/Q: c·ln K with c = −0.007 to −0.009 (priced families except OR, `L2K_clogK`).
- Stream simulation (`w1/streams_check.csv`): CA30 needs age 32, outside the 32-bit history (4 reads: 0.2497,
  unlearnable); LFSR16 is a 4-sparse parity (0.25 for k ≤ 3 reads, 0.0 at k = 4); CA110 needs 2 reads (0.093),
  1 read gives only 0.239. CA110 is the one real search failure.
- Prediction (`w1/predictions.csv`): CA110 needs K/Q ≥ 1358 for half its achievable gain (π ≤ 5.1e-4). At
  K/Q = 1024 its Brier should be 0.185 (r = 2^-7) to 0.240 (r = 2^-10); LFSR stays ≥ 0.25. The read-ladder factor
  r is an assumption.
- Lottery-ticket link: soup traders are monolithic tickets, so the density needed grows like 1/π, exponential in
  program length. Nets assemble tickets from shared parts (Ramanujan https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.13299, Malach
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.00585, Pensia https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.07990: log extra width; Frankle & Carbin
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03635), and SGD makes hidden progress on sparse parities (Barak et al.
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08799). This predicted (c)'s null before (c) ran.
- Side finding for the soup report: soup prime 0.091 at K/Q=64 equals the base-rate Brier p(1−p) = 0.0899, not
  "below" it; a 4-read table reaches 0.076 (`w1/streams_check.csv`).

## Answer to Q7

The soup's K/Q law is a law of search among independent, monolithic programs: each extra trader per question is
another lottery ticket, and π falls geometrically with the number of reads that earn no partial credit. A gradient
net finds a pattern once, in the shared trunk, and extra heads are extra readouts of the same features, so K/Q
adds nothing to pricing and, at K = 64, adds noise memorisation (a humility loss, M6's shrinkage problem). At equal
FLOPs the soup is cheaper on easy families; the net alone prices the recurrences, at 1.5-3 decades more compute than
any soup run spent.
