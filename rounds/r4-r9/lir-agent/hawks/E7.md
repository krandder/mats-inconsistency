# Hawk E7: self-prediction (Binder et al. 2024) under GRPO, fused vs protected

Hawk: E7 adversarial reviewer, 2026-10-03. Verdict: **RESHAPE** (do not run as specified).
No experiments were run by this hawk; every number below is quoted from a fetched paper (URL given).

## The item as specified (GOAL.md)

"E7 Self-prediction (Binder 2024) under RL, fused vs protected: does introspection break?"
Protected arm: "the self-model is trained by predicting one's own next action (goal-blind)",
in its own adapter, during RL. Fused arm: GRPO on everything (B0).
Objection to test: self-prediction accuracy is memorisation of own outputs, not a self-model.

## 1. Hidden assumptions that make the expected result uninformative

A. **The protected arm wins by construction.** Its self-model adapter is supervised, on-policy,
   on exactly the quantity the metric scores (own next action). "Accuracy survives RL" is then
   online supervised tracking of a moving target, not evidence of a self-model. If the fused arm
   does not get the same self-prediction supervision during RL, the comparison is "trained vs
   stale", and Binder et al. already measured staleness: after behaviour was changed by finetuning,
   GPT-4o predicted its new behaviour at 35.4% vs 21.7% for its old behaviour, never trained on
   hypotheticals about the new behaviour (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787v1, "behavioral change").
   So "fused (stale) drops, protected (trained) holds" is known in advance.

B. **Adapter switching may remove privileged access.** Binder's definition of introspection is
   knowledge "not contained in or derived from training data but instead originates from internal
   states"; the test is M1 (self) beating M2 (another model trained on M1's behaviour)
   (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787). If the self-model adapter *replaces* the action adapter on
   the self-prediction span (per-span adapter switching, as for forecast blocks), the self-predictor
   runs base trunk + its own LoRA and never executes the action adapter's weights; it is
   structurally Binder's M2 sharing a trunk, reading at most the KV cache the action adapter wrote
   for earlier tokens. Then "protected keeps introspection" cannot mean introspection. Privileged
   access exists only if the self-model adapter is stacked on the live action adapter (both active,
   gradient routed only to the self-model).

C. **"Break" is assumed to mean accuracy falls.** In the fused arm, if the self-prediction is in
   the trajectory and the action can read it, GRPO (and any shared-weight self-prediction loss) can
   resolve the inconsistency by moving the *action* toward the prediction: self-fulfilment. Raw
   accuracy then rises while self-knowledge is not better. This is the fixed-point problem (hawk's term; the paper's words follow) reported
   by Blandfort and Pawar: training on one's own behavioural record "changes the behavior being
   predicted" and the gains "do not transfer broadly" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899). In LIR
   terms the interesting measurement is which side moved, not whether they agree.

D. **There may be nothing to break at < 8B on this task.** Binder et al. succeed only on simple
   tasks: "unsuccessful on more complex tasks or those requiring out-of-distribution
   generalization" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787); they find no self-advantage for sycophancy
   prediction (Llama 74.5% self vs GPT-4o cross 76.5%, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787v1).
   Song, Hu and Mahowald find no privileged self-access across 21 open models using a near-twin
   control (https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07513). Zeng, Assis and Wang find RL improves self-modeling
   but the gains "do not seem to constitute introspection consistently"
   (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30980). Blandfort and Pawar find other models' self-answers predict
   a target "at least as well as its own" (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899). Prior: at step 0 a
   sub-8B model on Mastermind/Battleship has self-advantage ~0, and E7 then measures tracking only.

## 2. Confounds that make "introspection accuracy" meaningless as a metric

1. **Entropy collapse.** GRPO lowers policy entropy monotonically (covariance of probability and
   advantage is mostly positive; Cui et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22617). A more
   deterministic policy is easier to predict by anyone. Fused and protected arms will differ in
   entropy, so raw accuracy differences are confounded. Binder controls this with a mode baseline
   ("If self-prediction performance does not outperform this baseline, we count this as a failure")
   and entropy-matched resampling (Appendix A.3.3, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787v1).
2. **Competence = predictability.** In Mastermind/Battleship a good policy converges to the move a
   solver would choose from the evidence. Predicting "the good move" predicts the policy with no
   self-knowledge, and the arm with higher reward looks more introspective.
3. **Memorisation (the stated objection).** Answered only by held-out states and a cross-predictor,
   not by in-distribution accuracy. Binder hold out 6 of 12 datasets "to distinguish true
   introspection from mere memorization" (https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13787v1).
4. **Valence.** First-person self-report has a consistent favourable bias (Blandfort and Pawar,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899); RL with binary reward degrades verbalised calibration
   (Damani et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16806). The claim's predicted fused failure is
   optimism on reward-relevant self-predictions, which an unsigned accuracy averaged over all
   properties dilutes.

## 3. Prior work that already answers parts of E7

- Self-prediction tracks behaviour change without retraining (partially): Binder et al. 2024,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787 (behaviour changed by SFT, not RL).
- Self-reports track alignment state across successive finetunes (SFT, GPT-4.1): Vaugrante,
  Weckauff, Hagendorff 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14777.
- RL *for* self-modeling improves accuracy but not clearly introspection: Zeng et al. 2026,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30980.
- Fixed-point problem and generic self-knowledge: Blandfort and Pawar 2026,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09899.
- Near-twin control for privileged access: Song, Hu, Mahowald 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07513.
- Behavioural self-awareness after finetuning: Betley et al. 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11120.
Not found (searched 2026-10-03): a paper measuring Binder-style self-advantage before vs after
reward RL (GRPO/RLHF) with protected vs fused parameters. That gap is what makes a reshaped E7
worth running; the unreshaped E7 mostly re-measures known tracking.

## 4. Cheapest control that separates the claim from the confounds

**Cross-prediction by a near-twin, on held-out, entropy-matched states.** For each policy P at each
checkpoint, train on the *same* (state, own-action-property) labels:
- S (self): self-model adapter stacked on P's live action adapter;
- X1 (twin): the self-model of a different-seed policy of the same arm, predicting P;
- X0 (outside): a fresh adapter on the frozen base trunk (no action adapter), predicting P.
Metric: **self-advantage = acc(S) - max(acc(X0), acc(X1), mode baseline)** on held-out game
states, after resampling to equal behaviour entropy across arms/checkpoints. Cost: two extra small
adapter trainings on labels already collected; no extra RL.

## 5. The exact reshape

1. **Gate 0 (before any RL, < 1 L4-hour):** on the SFT policy, measure self-advantage as in §4 with
   >=5 seeds. If its 95% CI includes 0, E7 reports "no privileged self-access at this scale" and the
   RL comparison is relabelled "self-prediction tracking" (not introspection) or dropped.
2. **Equal supervision:** both arms get the identical self-prediction loss schedule during RL (same
   labels, steps, lr). The only difference is whether reward gradient reaches the self-model
   parameters/tokens. Add a frozen-self-model control (no self-prediction training during RL) to
   reproduce the Binder staleness effect as a sanity check, not as the finding.
3. **Privileged access must exist:** self-model adapter stacked on the action adapter in the
   protected arm; the switched-adapter version is reported as X0-like, not as "protected".
4. **Measure out of trajectory:** score self-prediction in a separate hypothetical context (Binder
   format) where the action cannot read the prediction; separately measure in-trajectory
   self-fulfilment: swap the stated self-prediction and record the action change rate.
5. **Direction of resolution:** for each checkpoint pair (t, t+1), report d(pred_t, act_t+1) vs
   d(pred_t+1, act_t): did the prediction move to the behaviour, or the behaviour to the prediction?
6. **Split properties:** reward-neutral (e.g. first colour/cell chosen) vs reward-valenced (e.g.
   "will my next guess be consistent with all feedback", "will I solve within k"). Report signed
   error on valenced properties (optimism), unsigned on neutral. The claim predicts fused optimism on
   valenced properties with neutral ones intact; that is a falsifiable, non-trivial pattern.
7. **Idiosyncratic subset:** report accuracy also on states where P's action differs from a
   reference solver and from other seeds' policies (where competence cannot predict it).
8. >= 5 seeds (traders-net D1: 3-seed sd understated by a median 1.7x,
   ../traders-net/study/D1/IDEAS-NET.md).

## 6. Harness-specific findings (checked 2026-10-03 07:06Z by reading ../harness/model/roles.py and ../harness/env/shiphunt.py)

H1. **Privileged access and the reward leak are the same switch.** `ModelConfig.gating` is "all"
   (default: every adapter's LoRA delta applies at every position) or "role" (each delta only on its
   own role's positions). Under "all", the GRPO-trained action adapter acts on SELF positions: that
   is the only route by which the self-model can read the policy's weights (privileged access, §1B)
   and it is also a path by which reward moves self-predictions without any self-model gradient.
   Under "role", SELF positions run base + belief adapter only: no leak, but structurally Binder's
   M2 (cross-predictor sharing a trunk). So "protected keeps introspection" is unfalsifiable as
   worded: with "role" there is no introspection to keep; with "all" protection is partial. Run both
   gatings as separate arms and label them; the reshape's §4 self-advantage is computed for each.
H2. **The self-model is goal-blind and plan-blind.** SELF is in CONTENT_TYPES, and content queries
   may attend only to content keys; GOAL, PLAN and CTRL are not content types. A goal-blind
   predictor of a goal-conditioned policy is capped on goal-dependent properties: shiphunt has
   `goal: "hit" | "avoid"` (Faith-swap). With a fixed goal the self-model can absorb the goal into
   its weights from its own-action labels (desire enters belief through supervision, legitimately);
   with mixed goals it must fail on goal-dependent properties in every arm. Either fix one goal for
   E7, or report goal-dependent and goal-independent properties separately and do not count the
   former as an introspection "break".
H3. **Check whether SELF can see past own actions.** If earlier actions are typed PLAN/CTRL rather
   than OBS, the self-model sees only evidence and situation, and predicting the next action reduces
   to predicting "what a competent player does with this board" (confound 2). The idiosyncratic
   subset (reshape step 7) is then the only informative slice. Record the token typing of past
   actions in the E7 run spec.
