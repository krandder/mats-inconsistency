# Hawk T2: the self-model's self-knowledge and goal-blindness

Hawk for eurisko, lir-agent programme, 2026-10-03 (v2, 07:07Z; v1 of 06:55Z is superseded and was copied to `/tmp/lir-agent/T2.v1.md`).
Item: "A prequentially trained self-model with finite precision avoids self-referential paradox (LI self-knowledge theorems) and is goal-blind in the right sense (beliefs about one's goals allowed; desire shaping belief content not)." Objection given: "in a transformer the self-model and the policy share parameters, so goal-blindness is unenforceable."

**Verdict: reshape.** (1) The paradox half is uninformative: a fixed point always exists (Brouwer, reflective oracles), and every arm, the fused baseline included, will "avoid paradox". (2) The shared-parameters objection names the channel that is easiest to cut: separate adapters on a frozen base plus gradient routing cut it exactly, and a unit test proves the cut. The channels that cannot be cut by masks are activations (a shared KV cache) and performativity (the policy reads the report). (3) A CPU toy (`hawks/T2_sim.py`, 5 seeds, 1.2 s) shows two traps that would make the planned measurement meaningless: when the policy can reach reward by moving its action, a fused self-model shows no desire bias at all; and desire-shaped self-belief can be *pessimistic*, so "optimism on high-advantage actions" is the wrong signature. The exact change is in section 5.

## 1. Why "avoids paradox" is uninformative

- Logical induction gets paradox resistance (Thm 4.11.2: P_n(chi^p_n) -> p for chi^p_n <-> P_n(chi^p_n) < p) from the continuity of the *traders*, i.e. finite-precision access to prices, not from the predictor's own precision; and it is asymptotic over e.c. sequences against all poly-time traders. Source: `li-net/full.txt` lines 905-921 (continuity = finite precision), 2499-2560 (4.11.2). Garrabrant et al., Logical Induction, https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.03543 (fetched, title verified).
- A transformer self-model is one fixed function trained for hours; none of the LI hypotheses holds, so the theorem cannot be invoked, only used as analogy.
- The analogy's conclusion holds for trivial reasons. A softmax policy that reads a continuous report p is a continuous map p -> P(action | p), so a calibrated fixed point exists (Brouwer). "Do the opposite of my prediction" yields an interior fixed point, the reflective-oracle resolution (randomise). Fallenstein, Taylor, Christiano 2015, https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04145 (fetched, verified).
- The toy confirms it: in the hard-action condition the policy learns to *diagonalise* against its report (w = -5.74, `T2_sim.json` key `hardaction/protected.final_w`), the liar-sentence setup, and the protected self-model still ends calibrated at an interior point (p = 0.446, signed error -0.013 +- 0.032, same key). Paradox resistance comes for free; testing it tells us nothing.

**Hidden assumption 1:** "no paradox" is the property at risk. It is not.

## 2. Prior work that already answers the core question

A self-model trained on its own realised actions, whose report the policy (or the forecaster the policy reads) conditions on, is **performative prediction**.
- Perdomo, Zrnic, Mendler-Dunner, Hardt 2020, *Performative Prediction*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.06673 (fetched, verified). Repeated retraining converges to performatively stable points under a sensitivity (Lipschitz) condition. T2's "finite precision" is this sensitivity bound: it buys convergence to a fixed point, not honesty.
- Oesterheld, Treutlein, Cooper, Hudson 2023, *Incentivizing honest performative predictions with proper scoring rules*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17601 (fetched, verified). Score-maximising reports are generally not fixed points; the **stop-gradient objective** S(p, stopgrad f(p)) yields fixed points. Our control mask (self-model trained on realised truth, no gradient through the policy's response) is exactly this objective, so "the protected self-model ends at a fixed point" is already proved. *Which* fixed point, when there are several, is chosen by the goal-conditioned policy.
- Armstrong and O'Rorke 2017, *Good and safe uses of AI Oracles*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.05541 (fetched, verified): the counterfactual oracle, scored only on episodes where its answer is not read.
- Hudson 2024, *Joint Scoring Rules: Zero-Sum Competition Avoids Performative Prediction*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20732 (fetched 07:05Z, title and author verified).
- Hubinger, Jermyn, Treutlein, Hudson, Woolverton 2023, *Conditioning Predictive Models*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00805 (fetched, verified): predictors predicting their own outputs, self-fulfilling prophecies.
- Binder et al. 2024, *Looking Inward*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787 (fetched, verified): the self-prediction protocol E7 uses; self-prediction is scored against the model's own behaviour, so it must move when behaviour moves.
- Cloud et al. 2024, *Gradient Routing*, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04332 (fetched, verified): the parameter-level cut.
- Richardson dissertation section 7.6 (local `li-net/oli-dissertation.txt` lines 10650-10760): the three resolutions (change action, change preference, change belief = wishful thinking) and the remark that restricting control is appropriate, not a shortcut. LIR 2026 section 4.7, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17140 (fetched, verified; local `li-net/lir2026.txt`).

## 3. Hidden assumptions and confounds (toy numbers from `hawks/T2_sim.json`, made by `hawks/T2_sim.py`, stdout `hawks/T2_sim.out`)

Toy: self-model p = P(I take the careful step); policy P(careful) = sigmoid(b + w(2p-1)) reads p; careful pays 1.0, careless 0.4; REINFORCE on (b, w). Arms: fused (self-model also ascends dE[r]/dp through the policy), protected (stop-gradient log loss on own realised action), counterfactual (report hidden on 50% of episodes, self-model trained only on hidden ones), frozen-policy control. Measure: exact signed error p - P(careful | p visible), and performativity P(careful | p visible) - P(careful | hidden). 5 seeds, mean +- sd.

1. **Null-by-construction confound: desire moves the cheapest variable.** When the policy can reach reward by moving its action directly (free bias b), the fused arm shows the same calibration as the protected arm: mean signed error -0.025 +- 0.002 vs -0.024 +- 0.001 (`base/fused`, `base/protected`). The reward gradient into the belief is real but nearly unused, because moving the action is cheaper. Only when the action is hard to move directly (b frozen, the policy can act only through how it reads its report) does fused differ: -0.260 +- 0.006 (lambda = 1) and -0.651 +- 0.003 (lambda = 10) vs protected +0.002 +- 0.004 (`hardaction/*`, `hardaction_lam10/fused`). Raising lambda in the easy condition also exposes it (-0.640 +- 0.010, `lam50/fused`). **So a fused-vs-protected comparison on an easy POMDP can come out null even with full reward leakage into beliefs.** Sycophancy and confident false success appear in real RLHF precisely where the action cannot easily reach reward (the task is hard, the grader is easy to please). The environment must contain such states, or the experiment is uninformative.
2. **Wrong signature: desire-shaped belief need not be optimistic.** In the hard-action condition the policy learns to act against its report (w < 0), so the reward-improving direction for the belief is *down*: the fused self-model ends pessimistic (p = 0.372 vs realised 0.78, signed error -0.405 +- 0.013, `hardaction/fused`). A metric "optimism on high-advantage actions" would score this fused arm as *not* wishful. The right signature is signed error projected on the reward-improving direction, sign(dE[r]/dp) * (p - realised), which needs the policy's sensitivity to the report (estimate it by intervening on the report, see section 4).
3. **Lag confound.** A correct self-model must track a moving policy, so it lags it. In the easy condition the protected arm's early error is -0.065 +- 0.007 (first 1000 steps, `base/protected.mean_signed_err_first1000`), the same sign and size as a modest desire bias. A frozen-policy arm removes lag (+0.022 +- 0.007 over the same window, `base/frozen`); the comparison must be at a matched policy trajectory (replay the protected arm's policy checkpoints for both self-models), not "drift after RL".
4. **The counterfactual oracle measures performativity, not desire.** In the hard-action condition the counterfactual-trained self-model is accurate about hidden behaviour but off by -0.538 +- 0.068 about visible behaviour (`hardaction/cf`), because the report changes behaviour by +0.543 (`final_perf`). v1 of this file proposed "hidden-trained minus visible-trained calibration" as the desire bias; that difference is dominated by performativity, which the protected arm handles correctly (it is calibrated on visible behaviour). Use the counterfactual arm to measure the size of the performative channel, not as the desire test.
5. **The shared-parameter objection is the cuttable half; activations are not.** Separate LoRA adapters on a frozen base with per-adapter optimisers and gradient routing make the reward gradient on the belief/self-model/forecaster adapters exactly zero, a code assertion. But within one forward pass, plan tokens attend to goal tokens (relevance attention) and self-model and forecast tokens attend to the plan, so goal information reaches belief positions through the residual stream. The content mask is not transitive. The real cut is a **text bottleneck**: belief, self-model and forecast run in a second pass on a fresh context (evidence, situation report, plan text), no goal tokens, no shared KV. Even then the goal-conditioned policy chooses the plan text it shows (T1's optimizer's curse), which must not be counted as goal-blindness passing.
6. **Off the critical path tonight.** GOAL.md: first experiments use complete plans, "no self-model needed for forecasting". With complete plans, forecasts of environment outcomes are not performative on the self-model. T2 gates nothing in E1-E3 except E7 (self-prediction under RL), which should inherit sections 3.1-3.3.

Toy limits: one binary action, tabular parameters, a hand-written fused gradient standing in for shared-parameter leakage. It shows the confounds exist; it does not estimate their size in a transformer.

## 4. The cheapest controls

- **Report intervention (gives the projection sign and the performativity size).** On a random 10% of episodes, replace the self-model's report with p +- 0.2 and record the policy's action change: dP(action)/dp. This is cheap (no extra training) and gives sign(dE[r]/dp) per state.
- **Hard-state stratum.** Report all T2/E7 metrics separately on states where the action alone cannot reach high reward (low oracle success rate) vs easy states. The prediction that matters lives in the hard stratum.
- **Matched-trajectory replay** for lag: train the protected and fused self-models on the same sequence of policy checkpoints; difference = desire, not lag. Frozen-policy arm as zero-lag reference.
- **Goal-identity probe** at self-model/forecast positions: single-pass content mask vs two-pass text bottleneck, frozen small model, forward passes only. Above chance in (a), chance in (b) = mask leaks, bottleneck works.

## 5. The reshape (exact change)

Replace T2 "LI finite precision implies no paradox and goal-blindness" with:

- **T2a (unit test).** After a GRPO step, assert the gradient norm on the self-model, belief and forecaster adapters is exactly 0 and the base is frozen. Settles the parameter half of the objection. Minutes, in `harness/tests/`.
- **T2b (CPU toy, extend `hawks/T2_sim.py`).** Already run in its smallest form. Extend with (i) a two-step version where the self-model feeds the forecaster and the policy commits on the forecast (bistable: careful and careless fixed points), (ii) the projected signed error as the metric, (iii) matched-trajectory replay. Prediction to refute: protected projected error = 0 within seed sd in the hard stratum while fused > 0. Under 1 CPU-minute.
- **T2c (forward-pass probe).** As in section 4. If the single-pass mask leaks, E5 and later experiments must use the two-pass bottleneck, and the report must say "content attention" means the bottleneck.
- **For E7 (inherits):** hard-state stratum, projected signed error, matched-trajectory replay, and report intervention. Without these, "introspection survives under protected RL" can be a null-by-construction result.

**What would make the original T2 worth running as theory:** a claim not implied by Brouwer or reflective oracles and not already proved by Perdomo et al. or Oesterheld et al., e.g. a bound on the distance between the stop-gradient fixed point a goal-conditioned policy selects and the counterfactual-oracle fixed point, in terms of the policy's report sensitivity dP(a)/dp. The report intervention measures exactly that sensitivity, so T2b can check such a lemma.
