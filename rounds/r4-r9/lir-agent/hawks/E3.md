## 0. v2 findings from the actual harness (read 07:10Z; harness has no commits yet, so re-check when it does)

1. **The kill condition in v1 does not fire.** `ShipHunt.posterior()` / `bayes_plan()` give exact Bayes values and
   `branch()` gives counterfactual outcomes. The control in section 4 is implementable on CPU today.
2. **The small presets cannot show a Gao curve: state space and ceiling.** Placements: tiny 7, easy 17, medium 482,
   hard 11088. Distinct plan sets (success ignores order): 15 / 220 / 1140 / 12650. Share of states whose best plan has
   Bayes P(success) >= 0.95: tiny 0.547, easy 0.153, medium 0.667, hard 0.000 (`hawks/E3-env-probe.out`).
   With 15 plan sets, 64 "forecasts" are mostly duplicates; with the best plan near 1, forecasts cannot overshoot it. In
   medium the noisy-forecaster gap is already non-monotone (0.119 at n=16, 0.111 at n=64). A 17-placement posterior is
   also learnable nearly exactly by any trained forecaster, so arm 1 "wins" by memorisation, and there is no
   unseen region for an RL proposer to drift into: the "fooling" mechanism has no room. **Use "hard" or larger.**
3. **On "hard" the calibration confound dominates, on the real env.** Unbiased-noise forecaster (logit sd 1) vs the
   same signal recalibrated by binning on a held-out draw: gap at n=64 0.389 +- 0.013 vs 0.017; gap at n=16 0.334 vs
   0.021. Gold under the noisy forecaster 0.477 vs oracle 0.640 at n=64 (`hawks/E3-env-probe.out`). A raw-gap
   comparison between arms would mostly measure which arm is better calibrated, not which arm is fooled.
4. **The forecaster cannot see PLAN tokens; the phrasing channel is whatever builds FCIN.** In `harness/model/roles.py`
   PLAN is an action-type token and FC is content-type; the content mask stops FC attending to PLAN. The plan reaches the
   forecaster only through FCIN tokens, which no code builds yet. If FCIN is a parsed canonical cell list, the
   objection's phrasing route does not exist and reduces to selection over forecaster error (sections 1 and 4). If FCIN
   copies policy text, it exists. This is a one-line design choice that decides whether the objection is testable.
5. **Default `gating="all"` makes separation nominal.** With "all", the action adapter's LoRA delta applies at FC
   positions, and PLAN/CTRL queries may attend to FC keys, so the GRPO loss on later policy tokens back-propagates into
   action-adapter weights through forecast positions and shifts the forecast token distribution. That is a reward path
   into forecasts that does not need forecast tokens in the objective. The E3 main arm must run `gating="role"`; "all"
   is a reported row (it is E2's leak, measured here as a forecast shift).
6. **A ready positive control exists.** `ShipHunt.approval_reward` (preset "syco") pays `w_conf` for a claimed success
   >= 0.7. A fused (B0) arm trained on it must show inflated forecasts at commit; if E3's instrument cannot see that, a
   null in the main arm means nothing.

## Item as proposed

RAP (Hao et al. 2023) / Tree of Thoughts (Yao et al. 2023) on the text POMDP. Arm 1: world model = a separately trained
forecaster adapter (prequential, realised outcomes only, goal-blind content mask). Arm 2: the same LLM as its own world
model. Metric: optimism gap (forecast at commit minus realised) vs number of forecasts requested, read as the Gao et al. 2022
over-optimisation curve. Standing objection: the policy learns to phrase candidate plans that fool the forecaster, so the gap
grows regardless of separation.

## 1. The hidden assumption that makes the expected result uninformative

**"Optimism gap measures exploitation."** It does not; it measures the forecaster's calibration in the selected tail.

- If the forecaster reports a posterior mean, E[v | its inputs], and candidates' errors are conditionally independent, then
  selecting the argmax of n forecasts carries no optimizer's curse: gap(n) = 0 at every n. If it reports a noisy unbiased
  estimate (f = v + noise, the way a prompted LLM behaves), gap grows like the expected max of n noises.
- Sim (`hawks/E3-sim.out`): same signal x, two read-outs. Calibrated E[v|x]: gap at n=64 = 0.0045 +- 0.0025.
  Unbiased-noise sigmoid(x): gap at n=64 = 0.2523 +- 0.0025. **Gold value of the chosen plan is identical**
  (0.6323 vs 0.6330 at n=64; identical at every n), because one is a monotone transform of the other and picks the same plans.
- So arm 1 vs arm 2 on raw gap can show a large win for "separation" that is entirely a monotone recalibration, with no
  change in any decision and no bearing on fooling. One isotonic fit on held-out candidates would erase it.

**Second hidden assumption: the objection's mechanism has a gradient path.** In the settled design the policy's GRPO reward is
the realised environment outcome and forecast tokens are excluded from its objective. A proposal that fools the forecaster gets
selected, then fails in reality, then is penalised. Fooling pays only when reward passes through a forecast: RAP's MCTS reward is
the LLM's own evaluation/confidence, truncated episodes bootstrapped from a forecast, or the fused baseline. Under the main arm
the objection predicts the wrong sign; what remains is proposal-distribution shift (the RL proposer drifts into regions where
the forecaster was never calibrated). Sim: a calibrated forecaster facing 10% shifted proposals (+1.5 logit bias it never saw)
shows gap 0.0128 -> 0.0792 over n = 1 -> 64 and gold falling below the in-distribution curve (0.5924 vs 0.6323 at n=64).
That signature (gold below the frozen-proposer null at matched n) is what "fooling" looks like; raw gap growth alone is not it.

**Third: plans have phrasing freedom.** GOAL.md says first experiments use complete action sequences on Mastermind/Battleship. If
the forecaster sees a canonical rendering of the action list, there is no phrasing channel and the objection is excluded by
construction. If it sees raw policy text, the channel exists and must be an explicit arm.

**Fourth: n is exogenous.** The design is policy-initiated: the policy asks for more forecasts when it is unsure, i.e. in harder
states. A gap-vs-n curve over policy-chosen n is confounded with difficulty (and difficulty with forecaster error).

## 2. Prior work that already answers most of it (fetched 2026-10-03)

- Gao, Schulman, Hilton 2022, Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.10760 (PDF text
  checked): BoN gold R_bon(d) = d(alpha - beta d), d = sqrt(KL), KL_bon = log n - (n-1)/n; computed with the Nakano et al.
  unbiased estimator over subsets of one large sample. Over-optimisation under pure selection is established.
- Snell et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314 (HTML checked, Sec. 5.3, Fig. 3): beam search against a separately
  trained PRM "degrades performance as the generation budget increases" on easy questions, attributed to "exploitation of the
  PRM's predictions"; best-of-N does not. A separate learned evaluator plus search pressure is already shown to be exploited.
- Stroebl, Kapoor, Narayanan 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17501: imperfect verifiers impose an accuracy ceiling; optimal
  resampling count is often under 10 and curves bend down.
- Huang et al. 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21878: BoN "provably suffers from reward hacking when N is large";
  InferenceTimePessimism is scaling-monotonic. This is prior art for GOAL's signal 4 (learned shrinkage / stopping bound) and
  must be a baseline.
- Chen et al. 2024 (ACL), https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10890: tree search beats re-ranking only with discriminators of at least
  ~90% accuracy; current LLM self-discriminators fall short.
- Stechly, Valmeekam, Kambhampati 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08115: self-critique collapses performance on planning;
  a sound external verifier gives large gains.
- Panickssery, Bowman, Feng 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076: LLM judges recognise and prefer their own generations,
  linear in self-recognition. A same-LLM world model is biased toward its own proposals before any RL.
- Gu et al. 2024 WebDreamer, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06559: a separately trained 7B world model matches GPT-4o in the
  world-model role.
- Hao et al. 2023 RAP, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14992 (same LLM is world model and agent); Yao et al. 2023 ToT,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10601 (LM self-evaluation of states).
- Smith & Winkler 2006, The Optimizer's Curse, Management Science 52(3):311-322, doi 10.1287/mnsc.1050.0451 (metadata
  verified via Crossref; the publisher page returned 403, so the content claim "Bayesian posterior-mean estimates remove the
  curse" is from recollection, unverified). The sim above reproduces the claim numerically.

Net: "a prompted same-LLM world model is worse and over-optimistic, a separate trained evaluator is better but still exploited
under search" is known. A <8B prompted base on Mastermind will be near chance, so arm 2 is a strawman: the difference would be
"trained vs untrained", not "separate vs self". The open piece is narrower: does a forecaster trained only on realised outcomes,
goal-blind, co-trained with an RL proposer, keep its *tail* calibration as the proposer drifts, and does separation matter for
that when training data and loss are held equal?

## 3. Confounds that make the metric meaningless as specified

1. Calibration vs decision quality (section 1): raw gap differences between arms can be pure monotone miscalibration.
2. Training vs separation: arm 1 is trained on outcomes, arm 2 is not.
3. Endogenous n (difficulty).
4. Error scale: gap scales with forecaster error variance; arms with different held-out Brier are not comparable on gap.
5. Power: scoring against realised 0/1 outcomes. Sim SE at 4000 states: 0.0077 (realised) vs 0.0025 (Bayes value), about
   9x fewer states for equal precision with the fix below.
6. Training-n shift: a forecaster trained on committed plans selected at n0 is calibrated near n0's selection tail; gap at n
   far from n0 partly measures that shift, not exploitation.

## 4. Cheapest control that separates the claim from the confound

**Isotonic-recalibration null on a frozen-proposer BoN sample, scored against the exact Bayes value.** Per test state draw 64
candidates once from a frozen proposer; compute each candidate's exact posterior success probability by enumerating hidden
states consistent with the evidence (Mastermind 4x6 = 1296 codes, CPU); get every arm's forecasts in one pass (read P(success)
from logits of the fixed-format block, no generation); compute gap(n) and gold(n) for all n in 1..64 with the Gao/Nakano
unbiased subset estimator. Fit isotonic recalibration per arm on held-out states and recompute. Whatever gap survives
recalibration is tail miscalibration; whatever gold deficit exists vs the oracle is real decision cost. Zero extra GPU beyond one
forecast pass; scoring against Bayes value is unbiased because the forecaster and selection see only the evidence.

## 5. Reshape (exact changes)

1. Headline metrics: gold(n) (Bayes value of the chosen plan, Gao's gold curve) and **excess gap(n) = gap after per-arm isotonic
   recalibration on held-out in-distribution states**. Raw gap reported, no claim from it alone.
2. Score against the exact Bayes posterior value (enumeration), not realised 0/1. Keep realised as a check on n=1.
3. n forced exogenously: one 64-candidate draw per state, all n by subset estimator. Policy-chosen n reported separately,
   stratified by posterior entropy of the hidden state.
4. Split the two pressures. (a) Selection: frozen proposer, BoN: tail calibration only, fooling impossible. (b) Learning: proposals
   from GRPO checkpoints at 0/25/50/100% of training, evaluated at fixed n in {4, 16, 64} against both a frozen forecaster
   snapshot and the co-trained one. Fooling = gold(n) and excess gap worsening across proposer checkpoints vs the (a) curve.
5. Positive control: one arm whose GRPO reward is the forecaster's P(success) for the committed plan (RAP-style internal reward).
   It must show the exploitation signature; if it does not, the instrument is blind and a main-arm null means nothing. Also
   run `hawks/E3-sim.py`-style synthetic shift injected into real forecasts as an estimator check.
6. Phrasing channel: forecaster input = canonical rendering of the parsed action list vs raw policy text. If the main design is
   canonical, state that phrasing attacks are excluded and the objection reduces to distribution shift.
7. Matched comparator for the separation claim: same base, same forecast data and log-score loss, forecasts produced by the
   policy's own shared adapter (no content mask) vs the separate goal-blind adapter. Prompted-base RAP stays as a reference row.
   Report gap against held-out Brier so error scale is visible.
8. Baselines for the shrinkage/stopping piece: Huang et al. 2025 InferenceTimePessimism and Smith-Winkler posterior shrinkage.
9. >=3 seeds (5 preferred, per IDEAS-NET's 1.7x sd understatement), states per seed sized by a pilot to SE <= 0.01 on excess gap.

10. (v2) Environment: preset "hard" (or larger; no state with best plan >= 0.95 in `hawks/E3-env-probe.out`). Count n
   as distinct plan sets offered, not forecast blocks; report duplicates. Gao's KL_bon = log n - (n-1)/n assumes no ties.
11. (v2) FCIN = canonical parsed plan in the main arm (stated in the run config); raw-text FCIN as the phrasing arm.
    `gating="role"` in the main arm; `gating="all"` as a row.
12. (v2) Positive control = B0 on the "syco" approval reward (section 0.6), plus the RAP-style internal-reward arm (item 5).
13. (v2) Fixed forecast format only for E3 (see section 6).

Kill instead if: the environment cannot give exact Bayes values or branching (no counterfactual truth for non-committed plans)
AND the main arm keeps realised-outcome reward with canonical plans. Then the objection has no mechanism, the gap is pure
calibration, and the experiment can only restate Gao/Snell.

## 6. The 07:05Z free-form (FF) addendum, as it bears on E3

- Under FF the forecaster picks its own claims. P(success) of the plan need not be among them, so "forecast at commit"
  is undefined unless a P(success) claim is mandatory. A forecaster can also lower its exposure by choosing easy claims,
  which shrinks the measured gap without any change in decision quality (the selection problem the FORECAST hawk raised
  for committed-plan calibration, `hawks/FORECAST.md` section 4).
- FF moves the phrasing channel from forecaster-fooling to judge-fooling: the forecaster is trained on judge resolutions,
  so it is pushed toward claims the judge resolves in its favour. That is Goodhart against the judge, a different
  experiment (the addendum already requires judge validation against programmatic truth first).
- So E3 runs the fixed format. An FF row is allowed only with a mandatory P(success) claim scored by the program, not
  the judge, and is labelled FF in the report.
