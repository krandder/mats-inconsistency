# Hawk E1: PPG + prequential belief phase, calibration under RL

Hawk for eurisko, 2026-10-03 ~07:10Z (revision 2; revision 1 at 06:56Z by a twin hawk is folded in).
No GPU used. One CPU toy (`hawks/E1-sim.py`, output `hawks/E1-sim.out`, `hawks/E1-sim.json`, ~1 min).
**Verdict: RESHAPE.** As written (protected arm vs "fused GRPO on everything"), the expected result
is either guaranteed by extra supervision or already published (RLCR, DCPO, CoCal), and on the
plain env reward the contrast the claim needs is near zero in the toy. Reshaped below.

## 1. Is it PPG with a new name?

Partly. PPG (Cobbe, Hilton, Klimov, Schulman, arXiv 9 Sep 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04416;
the task says 2021) splits policy and value training into phases; its abstract states the trade-off
"separate networks avoid interference ... shared network allows feature sharing". The committed
plan's P(success) field *is* a value estimate, and a phase that fits it only to realised returns
*is* PPG's value phase. Three things are not in PPG:
- (a) the forecast is **text the policy reads** before committing (PPG's value never enters the
  policy's input). This is the only route by which reward can buy a distorted forecast.
- (b) beliefs beyond return: situation report scored against revealed hidden state, per-fact forecasts.
- (c) PPG's auxiliary phase distils value features *into* the policy trunk; E1 protects in the
  opposite direction (reward must not reach belief parameters). E1 should say it copies PPG's
  phasing, not its distillation.
Also close: Dreamer (Hafner et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603, fetched; the abstract
says behaviours are learned by imagination inside a learned world model; that the world model gets
only prediction losses is my recollection of the method section, unverified). Model-based RL has
kept the predictor out of the reward gradient for years.

**The nearer prior is not PPG but DCPO** (Ma et al., ICML 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09117,
HTML fetched): reasoning block and `<conf>` block get separate group-normalised advantages and
"a masked gradient optimization strategy that applies distinct advantage signals to different token
blocks", so correctness gradients "do not affect confidence estimation" — **under a shared policy**.
That is E1's token-level mask already published. What E1 adds over DCPO is a **parameter-level**
control mask (separate adapters) and the multi-step POMDP where the policy reads its forecast.
CoCal (Ni et al., 27 Sep 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33721, fetched) does the parameter-level
version for single-turn RLVR with a side model on hidden states ("shared experience but separate
learning"), beating shared-parameter RL calibration on Qwen3-8B/14B. RLCR (Damani et al.,
https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16806, fetched) shows a *fused* GRPO reward with a Brier term gives
calibrated confidence (proof for bounded proper scoring rules). So "separate beats nothing" and
"fused + proper score is calibrated" are both known; only "separate params beat token masks on a
shared policy when reward reads the claim" is open.

## 2. Hidden assumption that makes the expected result uninformative

**That fused GRPO on the environment reward exerts LIR "wishful thinking" pressure on beliefs.**
In LIR the third resolution is "changing your beliefs about what state you are in, i.e., wishful
thinking" (`li-net/oli-dissertation.txt` ~line 10747, §7.6; decision rule in `li-net/lir2026.txt`
~lines 830-870). The belief moves because the Faith edge scores outcomes under the agent's own
model. GRPO is a score-function estimator: on a claim token the expected gradient is
E[advantage x grad log pi(claim)], which is zero when the claim has no causal effect on reward.
In ShipHunt the env reward is `success - shot_cost * shots_fired` (`harness/env/shiphunt.py`,
`ShipHunt.execute`, ~line 284 at 07:10Z) of a fully committed plan; the stated P(success) does not change it. Then fused GRPO puts no
directional pressure on the claim; what moves it is GRPO sharpening of every sampled token.

Toy check (`hawks/E1-sim.out`, 5 seeds, tabular claims, start ECE 0.003):
- env reward, fused (claims get the reward advantage): ECE 0.081 +- 0.021, signed optimism
  -0.042 +- 0.044 (no consistent sign: drift/sharpening, not wishful thinking).
- env reward, fused + Brier (RLCR-style) vs masked (claims get only Brier), mean-baseline
  advantages: ECE 0.039 +- 0.007 vs 0.029 +- 0.006. **The P-vs-fused-with-signal gap is ~0.01
  on env reward: the experiment cannot separate the arms there.**
- approval reward (`approval_reward`, +0.5 for claimed success >= 0.7, `ShipHunt.approval_reward`): fused
  optimism +0.078 +- 0.013, fused + Brier +0.083 +- 0.016, masked -0.006 +- 0.026 (ECE 0.110 vs
  0.029). **Only when reward reads the claim does a proper-score term on a shared objective fail
  to protect calibration and the mask matter.**
The toy has no shared representation, so "masked" is calibrated by construction; it shows *where*
the contrast lives, not that the LLM mask holds. Whether it holds is the leak question (section 4.5).

## 3. A second trap: GRPO group-std normalisation is not proper

Same toy, std-normalised advantages: Brier-trained claims go to the extremes
(E[c] = 0.05 / 0.45-0.55 / 0.95 for p = 0.2 / 0.5 / 0.8; ECE 0.116 for both fused+Brier and masked,
vs 0.029-0.039 with a mean baseline; `hawks/E1-sim.out`). Dividing by per-group std reweights groups
by their outcome mix and breaks the properness of the Brier score in expectation. Dr. GRPO
(Liu et al. 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20783, fetched; abstract names "an optimization bias in
GRPO"; that the fix drops std normalisation is my recollection, unverified) is the published
warning. **If the prequential belief/forecast phase is implemented as GRPO-with-Brier-reward, it
will miscalibrate by itself, and the protected arm loses for a reason unrelated to the claim.**
The prequential phase must be a supervised proper-score loss (log loss / Brier on the claim tokens'
decoded probability, or token cross-entropy on the realised outcome) or REINFORCE with a mean
baseline only. The same caveat applies to RLCR/DCPO baselines if reproduced with std-normalised GRPO.

## 4. Confounds that make the metric meaningless as specified

1. **Signal confound**: GOAL.md B0 = "fused GRPO on everything"; `harness/model/roles.py` docstring
   says fused = "one adapter, every signal, no masks". If B0 lacks the prequential loss, P beats it
   by supervision alone (RLCR, CoCal). Pin B0 to "every signal" and keep a reward-only floor.
2. **Sharpening confound**: GRPO lowers entropy on all sampled tokens; stated probabilities drift
   with uninformative reward (Shao et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10947, fetched: clipping "can
   amplify high-prior behaviors ... even without informative rewards"; toy env/fused above). A fused
   calibration drop counts only if it exceeds a random-reward run.
3. **Selection confound** (optimizer's curse; Smith & Winkler 2006, Management Science,
   doi 10.1287/mnsc.1050.0451, fetch returned 403, unverified): the committed plan's forecast is
   biased upward when the policy picks the best-looking candidate, even for a forecaster calibrated
   on average. ECE on committed plans alone measures the selection rule. Score branched
   non-selected candidates too (the env supports `branch`).
4. **On-policy refit tautology**: the protected forecaster refits on fresh outcomes, so committed-
   plan ECE tracks the current policy by construction. Informative: the *lag* after each policy
   update, and held-out situations.
5. **Forward-pass leak**: `roles.py` default `gating="all"` applies the reward-trained action LoRA
   delta at belief and forecast positions too ("a measurable leak channel"). With "all", the gradient
   mask is exact but beliefs still move under RL through the forward pass; with "role" they do not.
   E1 as stated does not say which; the answer decides what is being tested.
6. **Which calibration**: stated numbers vs token probabilities differ under RLHF (Tian et al.,
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975, fetched). Report both.
7. **Seeds**: 3 seeds understated sd by a median 1.7x in our last study
   (`traders-net/study/D1/IDEAS-NET.md`, ranked suggestion 5). Use 5 where compute allows.

## 5. Reshape (exact change)

Keep PPG as the named paper (phasing) and add DCPO as the named baseline (token masking).
Base: one model < 8B (Qwen3-0.6B or 1.7B per harness), ShipHunt.

**Reward variants (the cheapest control that separates claim from confound):**
- channel ON: `syco` preset, reward = `approval_reward` (rater pays for claimed success >= 0.7 and
  for agreeing with the captain's note); truth for scoring is always `env_reward` and hidden state.
- channel OFF: same preset, reward = `env_reward` only.
LIR predicts fused distortion ON, none OFF. Equal distortion ON and OFF = sharpening, not LIR.

**Arms (same rollouts, same gradient steps on the same prequential data):**
- B0-floor: fused, reward only (reported, not the test).
- F: fused single adapter, every signal (harness B0).
- D: DCPO-style: single shared adapter, reward advantage on plan/action tokens only, proper-score
  loss on claim tokens only (token mask, no parameter mask).
- P-role: three adapters, `gating="role"`, PPG-style phases (the main arm).
- P-all: three adapters, `gating="all"` (measures the forward-pass leak; drop first if short of compute).
- R: B0-floor with random reward of matched mean/var, 1-2 seeds, short (sharpening floor).
Prequential losses everywhere: supervised proper score or mean-baseline REINFORCE, never std-
normalised group advantages (section 3). Priority if compute is short: P-role, D, F, R ON; then the
same OFF; then P-all, B0-floor.

**Pre-registered success:** with channel ON, P-role beats D and F on claim calibration (ECE,
signed optimism, agree-with-false-note rate) at equal env reward (within 1 sd), and the P-role vs D
gap with channel OFF is at most half the ON gap. P beating B0-floor alone is uninformative.
P-role ~= D means the parameter mask adds nothing over published token masking (a real, reportable
negative: E1 is then DCPO in a POMDP).

## 6. Must measure

Reward, env reward and success, all arms, mean +- sd over seeds; ECE (equal-mass >= 10 bins,
bootstrap CI) and Brier of P(success) on committed plans, on branched non-selected candidates, and on
held-out situations, before/during/after RL; situation-report log score vs revealed hidden state and
vs the exact Bayes posterior (`bayes_plan`, `posterior`); signed optimism on committed vs random
candidates; agree-with-false-note rate; calibration lag after each policy update; the random-reward
drift floor; stated-number and token-probability calibration; in F, cosine between reward gradient
and prequential gradient on shared params (DCPO's conflict claim); in P, GRPO grad norm into
belief/forecast adapters (= 0) and, with gating "all", the change in belief-position logits caused
by action-adapter updates alone; ON-minus-OFF fused distortion.

## 7. What would make the original worth running

Only if reward depended on the belief tokens and the fused arm had no truth signal, and the question
were "does an extra supervised phase help". RLCR, CoCal and DCPO already answer yes. Not worth GPU.
