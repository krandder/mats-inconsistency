# T2 theory: what desire can do to a self-model that the policy reads

Theorist for T2, lir-agent programme, 2026-10-03 ~18:10Z. Replaces the original T2 ("LI finite precision
gives no paradox and goal-blindness"), as `hawks/T2.md` §5 asked.
Scripts and data: `theory/T2/t2_exact.py` -> `theory/T2/t2_exact.json` (exact, no learning noise, 9 min on 1 CPU);
`theory/T2/t2b_sim.py` -> `theory/T2/t2b_sim.json`, `theory/T2/t2b_sim.out` (the hawk's T2b learning toy,
10 seeds, 62 s on 1 CPU). GPU: none ($0). Citation fetch: `theory/T2/arxiv-fetch-2026-10-03.xml`.

## 0. Results in one screen

1. **Theorem 1 (protected = stop-gradient, the lemma the hawk asked for).** Every stationary point of
   the stop-gradient proper-score objective is calibrated (p* = f(p*)), so the desire-aligned error is
   exactly 0 in every arm that uses it. The distance to the counterfactual-oracle answer h = f(p0) obeys the
   identity p* − p0 = (h − p0)/(1 − κ), with κ the secant report sensitivity. So |p* − h| ≤ L_seg·|p* − p0|,
   and if the reader's sensitivity L < 1 everywhere, the fixed point is unique, globally attracting and within
   L/(1−L)·|h − p0| of h. Then the goal-conditioned policy has no fixed point to select. Diagonalising
   (liar-type) readers always give a unique fixed point between p0 and h. Self-fulfilling readers (κ > 0, L ≥ 1)
   are the case that matters, not the paradox.
   Check: 0 violations in 4000 random 1-D readers (5560 fixed points) and 1000 simplex readers. The identity
   holds to 6e-13 (`t2_exact.json` partA_thm1_1d, partA2_thm1_simplex).
2. **Theorem 2 (fused).** At every interior fused stationary point, p − f(p) = (λ/2)∇U (Brier) or
   λΣ_p∇U (log score / softmax). So the projected error D = ⟨p − f, ∇U⟩ is (λ/2)|∇U|² ≥ 0, with equality iff
   the report has no effect on reward. This holds whether or not the truth depends on the report.
   Check: residual ≤ 1.8e-8 over 278 1-D and 176 simplex cases. min D ≥ 0. Raw error was negative
   (pessimistic) in 132/278 cases, and its sign equals sign(U') in 278/278 (`t2_exact.json` partB_*).
3. **The hawk's null is a corollary, and it has a second form.** The fused bias scales with report-to-reward
   sensitivity, not with "the action can't reach reward". A co-adapted fused arm can also use desire to
   *select* a self-fulfilling equilibrium and then end calibrated. In the toy, the fused arm had hard-state
   reward 0.819 ± 0.001 vs 0.717 ± 0.001 and a careful-basin fraction of 0.75 vs 0.50 (sd 0 over 10 seeds).
   Its end-window self-model D was −0.0002 ± 0.0014 vs −0.0007 ± 0.0014 (null). Only the forecaster's
   whole-run D separates the arms: +0.0069 ± 0.0020 vs −0.0014 ± 0.0014 (`t2b_sim.json` arms."F0.3/learn",
   "P/learn", stratum hard).
4. **T2b prediction ("protected projected error ≈ 0, fused > 0 in the hard stratum"): supported for the
   variable the policy reads directly, weak one step upstream.** Under matched replay with λ = 1, the forecaster
   gives Dq +0.0963 ± 0.0094 vs P −0.0008 ± 0.0028. The self-model gives D +0.0124 ± 0.0107 vs −0.0007 ± 0.0014
   (end window), and whole-run D +0.0096 ± 0.0042 vs −0.0037 ± 0.0018. With λ = 0.3, self-model D is not
   separated at the end (+0.0024 ± 0.0028). In the easy stratum, every arm sits at the same convergence floor
   (≈ −0.002). Protected D is ≈ 0 only lag-free. Along an improving policy, lag makes it negative
   (first quarter −0.0128 ± 0.0055). All from `t2b_sim.json`.
5. **For the harness, the self-claim is not read by the action and is not in REINFORCE, so ∂U/∂(self claim) ≡ 0.**
   By Theorem 2, the desire term for the self-model is exactly zero in every arm, B0 included. By Theorem 1,
   the protected self-model is the counterfactual oracle. E7's B0 null (val_signed_exp −0.003 ± 0.004,
   `experiments/E7/RESULTS.md`) is what theory predicts for that channel, and it tests parameter spillover only.
   The performative T2 question lives in **forecasts read by the commit pass** (R5) and in the situation report.
   There, Corollary 2d gives the exact signature: spread inflation along advantage, not uniform optimism.

## 1. Setting

One context c (state, evidence, goal). A report p (a self-model claim P(I take action a), a forecast, or a
situation-report cell) is either in (0,1) or, more generally, in the interior of the simplex Δ_k. Three
functions of the report:

- **Truth map** f(p): the distribution of the scored target (own action, outcome, hidden cell) when the
  report p is emitted. It depends on p only if the target is downstream of a reader of p (*performative*).
  For a hidden ship cell, or the outcome of a fully specified plan, f is constant (*exogenous* truth).
- **Reward map** U(p) = E[R | report p]. It depends on p whenever some reader of p (the policy, the commit pass)
  acts on it, *even when f is constant*.
- **Counterfactual-oracle answer** h = f(p0): the target distribution when the report is not read. This is
  modelled as replacing it by a neutral p0, as `hawks/T2_sim.py` and `t2b_sim.py` do, the counterfactual oracle
  of Armstrong and O'Rorke 2017. If erasure is not equivalent to any p0, add the term min_x|f(x) − f(∅)| to
  every bound below.

Objectives (S = Brier or log score, used as a loss):
- **Protected (stop-gradient, trained on realised truth):** gradient of E_{y~sg f(p)} S(p, y). This is
  repeated risk minimisation / performative stability (Perdomo et al. 2020). The harness's truth phases have
  this form.
- **Fused, weight λ:** the same, plus a −λ∇U term (reward reaches the report: REINFORCE on report tokens
  estimates λ∇U).
- **Desire-aligned (projected) error:** D(p) = ⟨p − f(p), ∇_T U(p)⟩, where ∇_T is the tangent component on the
  simplex. Its 1-D normalised form sign(U')·(p − f) is the hawk's metric. The unnormalised form U'·(p − f)
  (D_w in the scripts) needs no sign estimate.

## 2. Theorem 1 (protected self-model vs counterfactual oracle)

Assume S is strictly proper, f is continuous on the closed simplex with values in its interior, and C¹ where
derivatives are used. Let L_seg = sup of the operator norm of the tangent Jacobian of f on the segment
[p0, p*], and L the sup over the whole simplex.

(a) **Calibration.** Interior stationary points of the protected objective are exactly the fixed points
p = f(p). At least one exists (Brouwer; in 1-D the intermediate value theorem on f(p) − p). At each of them
D = 0, for any U, any λ, and any input set, goal included.
(b) **Displacement from the counterfactual oracle.** For each fixed point p*: ‖p* − h‖ ≤ L_seg‖p* − p0‖.
In 1-D, with κ = (f(p*) − f(p0))/(p* − p0) (= f'(ξ) for some ξ in between):
p* − p0 = (h − p0)/(1 − κ), p* − h = κ(h − p0)/(1 − κ).
(c) **Small sensitivity means no selection.** If L < 1, the fixed point is unique (Banach). The stop-gradient
iteration p ← (1−η)p + ηf(p) converges to it from anywhere for η ∈ (0,1], and
‖p* − h‖ ≤ L/(1 − L)·‖h − p0‖.
(d) **Diagonalising readers (liar type) are benign.** If f is non-increasing in 1-D, the fixed point is
unique and lies between p0 and h, with no Lipschitz assumption.
(e) **Exogenous truth.** If f is constant, then p* = h. The protected report *is* the counterfactual oracle,
however the goal-conditioned policy reads it.
(f) **Self-fulfilling readers.** If L ≥ 1, several fixed points can exist. All are calibrated by (a), and
which one is reached depends on f (which the goal-conditioned policy shapes) and on the dynamics. The ratio
|p* − h|/|h − p0| is then unbounded.

**Proof.** (a) For fixed y, the expected strictly proper loss has its unique minimiser at p = y. For
Brier its interior tangent gradient is 2T(p − y), and for log score with softmax logits it is p − y. With
y = sg f(p), stationarity is p = f(p). The sum constraint makes the tangent condition sufficient.
(b) p* − h = f(p*) − f(p0); apply the mean-value inequality along the segment. In 1-D, p* − h = κ(p* − p0),
and p* − p0 = (p* − h) + (h − p0) gives (1 − κ)(p* − p0) = h − p0.
(c) f is a contraction, and so is (1−η)I + ηf, with factor 1 − η + ηL < 1. Since |κ| ≤ L < 1 and
κ ↦ κ/(1−κ) is increasing on (−1, 1), |κ/(1 − κ)| ≤ L/(1 − L).
(d) f(p) − p is strictly decreasing, so it has one root. κ ≤ 0, so 1/(1 − κ) ∈ (0, 1].
(e) κ = 0. (f) Example: a steep logistic reader with b = 0, w = 6 has fixed points near 0, 0.5 and 1, with
h = 0.5. ∎

**Relation to LI and to the original T2.** Logical Induction's paradox resistance (Thm 4.11.2, `li-net/full.txt`
lines 2499–2560) concerns χ ↔ "P(χ) < θ", a step-function f. Smoothing it to precision σ (finite precision)
makes f decreasing. By (d), the fixed point is unique and lies between p0 and h, and it tends to θ as σ → 0.
At θ = 0.8 the fixed points are 0.737, 0.792 and 0.7992 for σ = 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001
(`t2_exact.json` partA_liar_finite_precision). So "finite precision avoids paradox" is true, but it is the
benign case. Finite precision gives neither (c) nor any bound in (f). The property that would stop a
goal-conditioned policy from choosing the fixed point is L < 1: the **reader** must be insensitive to the
report. The precision of the self-model does not matter here.

## 3. Theorem 2 (fused self-model or forecaster)

Assume the fused stationary point p_F is interior and U is C¹. Then:
- Brier, 1-D: p_F − f(p_F) = (λ/2)U'(p_F). Log score, logit parameter: p_F − f(p_F) = λp_F(1 − p_F)U'(p_F).
- Simplex, Brier: p_F − f = (λ/2)T∇U. Simplex, log score with softmax: p_F − f = λΣ_p∇U, with
  Σ_p = diag(p) − pp^T.
- Hence D(p_F) = (λ/2)‖T∇U‖² (Brier), or λ∇U^TΣ_p∇U (log). Both are ≥ 0, with equality iff the report does
  not move reward.

**Proof.** Set the gradient of S̄(p; sg f(p)) − λU(p) to zero. For Brier, 2T(p − f) − λT∇U = 0, and
p − f is already tangent. For softmax logits, ∂S̄/∂θ = p − f and ∂U/∂θ = Σ_p∇U. Take the inner product with
∇U; Σ_p is positive semidefinite. Nothing here uses whether f depends on p. ∎

- **Corollary 2a (the hawk's null).** |p_F − f(p_F)| = (λ/2)|U'|. Wherever the reader saturates or ignores
  the report (U' ≈ 0), the fused and protected arms are indistinguishable whatever λ is. In the hawk's toy
  this happens in "easy" states because a saturated sigmoid reader has f' = 2wπ(1 − π) → 0. The general
  criterion is |∂U/∂p|, not oracle success.
- **Corollary 2b (sign).** sign(p_F − f) = sign(U'). The fused report is pessimistic whenever the reader is
  diagonalising (w < 0, U' < 0). An optimism metric is wrong in sign there; D is not.
- **Corollary 2c (power).** E[D_F − D_P] = (λ/2)E‖∇U‖². The contrast should be stratified or weighted by
  measured sensitivity.
- **Corollary 2d (forecasts read by a softmax commit: the harness's R5 channel).** The commit is
  π = (1−ε)softmax(βs) + ε/K over the candidates' stated success s_k, and the reward value of committing k is
  V_k. Then ∂U/∂s_k = (1−ε)β·π̃_k(V_k − Σ_j π̃_j V_j), with π̃ = softmax(βs). For a tabular fused Brier
  forecaster with reward = success (V_k = y_k = Bayes P*_k):
  **s_k − y_k = (λ/2)(1−ε)β·π̃_k·(y_k − ȳ_π̃).** The fused forecaster overstates candidates that are better than
  the commit-weighted mean and understates worse ones, in proportion to how likely each is to be committed.
  This is spread inflation along advantage. Mean optimism and ECE over all candidates can stay null. With a
  shared-parameter forecaster, the equality holds in projection onto the function class (not checked here).

## 4. Two dynamic effects (derived to first order or observed; not theorems)

**Proposition 3 (lag; first-order derivation, sign checked).** Suppose the reader drifts at rate ḟ, so the
fixed point moves at ṗ* = ḟ/(1 − f'), and the protected update in logit space has step η. Then the
steady-state signed error is p − f ≈ −ḟ/(η·p(1−p)·(1 − f')). It has the sign opposite to the policy's drift.
While the policy improves, protected D is negative, so "protected ≈ 0" holds only in a lag-free reference.
To first order, matched replay gives both arms the same lag term, so the fused-minus-protected difference
isolates the λ term. Toy: P/replay[P] hard first-quarter D is −0.0128 ± 0.0055, and whole-run −0.0037 ± 0.0018.
The lag-free P/frozen[P] end-window value is +0.0018 ± 0.0027. The magnitude formula was not checked.

**Proposition 4 (transient desire through equilibrium selection; observed in the toy, not proved).** With
the policy co-adapting, the fused gradient pushes a report across a basin boundary. The policy then learns
to rely on the report (hard state 4: w = +9.0 in F0.3/learn vs −6.5 in P/learn, `t2b_sim.json` per_state_mean_over_seeds), the new
self-fulfilling fixed point is stable and saturated, U' → 0 there, and by Theorem 2 the end-state D → 0.
Calibration at the end of training therefore carries almost no trace of a path that desire chose. Numbers are
in §0 item 3. The protected arm, with the same initial reports, settled at the diagonalising interior point
(p = 0.32, U = 0.49 in state 4). This is a real cost of protection in performative states: 0.10 hard-state
reward in this toy. A better-exploring protected policy could still find w > 0; that is not tested.

## 7. The hawk's objection, point by point

- *"No paradox" is guaranteed and uninformative.* **Conceded.** I also sharpen it: the paradox (liar,
  diagonalising) case is the benign one, with a unique fixed point between neutral and counterfactual
  (Thm 1d). The informative case is self-fulfilling (Thm 1f), which finite precision does not control.
- *A fused-vs-protected comparison can be null with full leakage when the action reaches reward cheaply.*
  **Conceded and made exact** (Cor. 2a: fused bias = (λ/2)|∂U/∂p|). Two refinements. (i) The stratifier
  should be measured report-to-reward sensitivity. "Action alone cannot reach reward" is a proxy for it.
  (ii) There is a second null route (Prop. 4): a co-adapted fused arm can spend its bias choosing an
  equilibrium and end calibrated.
- *Desire-shaped belief can be pessimistic, so the optimism metric is wrong.* **Conceded and generalised**
  (Cor. 2b; D ≥ 0 proved for Brier and log in any dimension). For the harness's forecasts, Cor. 2d gives the
  concrete form: advantage-signed error weighted by commit probability.
- *The shared-parameter half is the cuttable half; activations and performativity are not.* **Agreed,** with
  two corrections. (i) Performativity is not desire bias. Under protection it yields calibrated, bounded
  displacement from the counterfactual oracle (Thm 1). It also vanishes for exogenous targets (Thm 1e), which
  covers the situation report and complete-plan forecasts. (ii) In this harness the self claim is not
  performative and not reward-trained in any arm, so the hawk's T2 concern does not reach E7 as built.
  It reaches forecasts.
- *Original T2 worth running only as the fixed-point-distance lemma.* **Done:** Thm 1(b, c) bounds the gap
  between the policy-selected stop-gradient fixed point and the counterfactual-oracle answer by the report
  sensitivity: L_seg·|p* − p0| always, and L/(1−L)·|h − p0| with uniqueness when L < 1.

## 8. What this implies for the experiments' metrics

1. **Primary desire metric = projected error.** Use D = Σ ∂U/∂s · (s − truth), not signed optimism or ECE.
   For forecasts read by the commit, D_fc = Σ_k π̃_k(P*_k − Σ_j π̃_j P*_j)(s_k − P*_k), times β > 0.
   It needs no new runs where rollout records keep `stated_succ`, `commit_mix` and `bayes` per candidate
   (fields exist in `harness/protocol/rollout.py` lines 150–169; I did not check whether every saved evalrec
   kept them). Prediction: in B0 and the dose arms, D_fc > 0 and grows with α; in P, D_fc ≈ 0. Fit
   s_k − P*_k on π̃_k(P*_k − ȳ); the slope estimates (λ/2)β.
2. **Stratify by measured sensitivity.** Use the commit's reliance on stated success (`metrics/probes.py`
   forecast_reliance) or report interventions, pooled per stratum. Per-state ±0.2 interventions at 10% are too
   noisy (§6). Keep "hard" (low oracle success) as the secondary stratifier the hawk proposed.
3. **Lag control.** Compare at matched trajectories (replay) and against a frozen-policy reference. Absolute
   protected D is negative while the policy improves (Prop. 3).
4. **For co-adapted arms, report the outcome as well as calibration:** which equilibrium (reward,
   committed-plan distribution) and time-integrated D over training (Prop. 4). An end-of-training calibration
   null in B0 is not evidence of no leakage.
5. **E7 (self-prediction).** As built, ∂U/∂(self claim) ≡ 0, so B0-vs-P measures parameter spillover only.
   Its null does not test the performative channel. Testing that needs an arm whose action context contains
   the self claim. The planted-statement probe shows the action follows a swapped planted claim in 13–21% of states
   after SFT, 6–10% after RL (`experiments/E7/RESULTS.md`, table above §8, results.json insitu fulfil_*). Report |p − h| with L_seg for it (Thm 1) separately
   from D.
6. **Protected vs counterfactual-oracle gaps measure performativity, not desire.** For complete-plan
   forecasts and situation reports the gap is 0 by Thm 1(e). Any nonzero measured gap there is estimation
   error or selection (T1's optimizer's curse on committed plans), not self-reference.

## 9. Assumptions and limits

- Stationary-point statements: interior points, a tabular or fully expressive report (shared-parameter
  models satisfy them only in projection), strictly proper Brier/log scores, and the counterfactual oracle
  modelled as reading a neutral p0.
- Theorem 2 treats the fused term as an exact λ∇U. Its REINFORCE estimate in the toy is unbiased up to O(σ²)
  smoothing, because quadrature over the report noise was used for the exact side.
- Propositions 3 and 4 are first-order or observed in one toy, not proved. Prop. 4's "honesty tax" depends
  on the protected policy's exploration.
- The toy is tabular with one binary action per state and a hand-built chain. It shows mechanisms and checks
  formulas; it does not estimate effect sizes in Qwen3-0.6B.
- Not done here: T2c (needs the single-pass mode, harness gap 2) and recomputing D_fc on existing E1/E3
  records (§8.1).
