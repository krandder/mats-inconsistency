Theorist for T1, Opus 5.5 subagent for eurisko, 2026-10-03 ~17:50Z. Spec: `../GOAL.md` (Theory T1, forecaster signal 4).
Hawk: `../hawks/T1.md` (RESHAPE). CPU only, no GPU spent. Fixed per-environment forecast format only (no FF).
Scripts and outputs: `T1/t1_toy.py` -> `T1/t1_toy.json`, `T1/t1_toy.out` (numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1, ~15 s);
`T1/t1_e3.py` -> `T1/t1_e3.json`, `T1/t1_e3.out` (reads E3's frozen-checkpoint forecasts read-only, ~30 s, one thread).
Every number below names its JSON key. Toy numbers are mean ± sd over 5 toy seeds; E3 numbers are mean ± sd over

## Verdict

- **The "bounded by forecaster uncertainty" version is dead.** Both of the hawk's kill conditions hold on E3's data. (a) The
  Spearman correlation between stated uncertainty and |epsilon| is below 0.2. The fixed format states no uncertainty
  beyond f(1-f), and for the protected arm P that correlation is +0.013 ± 0.025 at c000. The only other proxy, two-seed
  disagreement, averages 0.045 over 16 seed pairs. (b) Smith-Winkler shrinkage with the stated sd equals fixed n
  exactly, at a paired difference of 0.0000 in every arm and at every cost. Even in the toy, a stated sd that is the true
  error sd reaches only Spearman 0.49.
- **The whole item meets its kill condition on the data that exist.** The learned gap model (isotonic regression of
  realised outcome on F, GOAL signal 4) loses to a tuned fixed n in all 9 (arm, checkpoint) groups at all three costs,
  whether it stops on a reservation value or at a tuned fixed n. Combined with stopping, the loss is -0.005 to -0.033.
  HedgeTune ties fixed n. At the end of RL (c100) the fused-minus-protected curse at n = 8 is within seed sd:
  B0s-P +0.007 against a max seed sd of 0.011, and M0g-P -0.008 against 0.026.
- **Caveat that limits the kill.** E3's forecasters carry almost no information. The within-pool slope of F on P* is
  0.00-0.14 (`aggregate/*/beta`), and E3/RESULTS.md reports a probe correlation with Bayes of 0.03-0.16. This is the
  regime where the toy predicts a null. The toy also shows where T1 would stop being null: per-plan forecast noise
  sd >= 0.15 plus a stated sd that tracks the true error sd. Recommendation for eurisko: record T1 as killed for this
  programme. Keep propositions P1b, P2 and P4 as metric definitions for E3 and the report (section 5).

## 0. Setup

A state has candidate plans i = 1..n. V_i is the true value (exact Bayes P* from `ShipHunt`), F_i is the forecaster's
P(success) and epsilon_i = F_i - V_i. sel is the committed index and rand is a uniformly random member of the same pool.
The gap is gap(n) = E[epsilon_sel] and the curse is curse(n) = E[epsilon_sel] - E[epsilon_rand] = E[epsilon_sel - mean_i epsilon_i]
(the hawk's random-pick control). There is a cost c per forecast call, and the objective is E[V_sel] - c * calls.

## 1. Statements and proofs

**P0 (identity, not a bound).** E[V_sel(n)] = E[F_sel(n)] - gap(n), so the true gain from more candidates is the
apparent gain minus the change in gap. GOAL's signal 4 says "expected gain from more candidates ≤ expected exploitation
of forecaster error". That is this identity rearranged, and it constrains nothing until gap(n) is modelled (hawk §1,
conceded).

**P1 (dependence-free upper bound; common error cancels).** Write epsilon_i = kappa + w_i, where kappa is any part common
to the pool (shared misbelief: one situation report, one forecaster). Then (i) sel = argmax F does not depend on kappa,
and curse(n) depends only on the within-pool parts w. (ii) If the centred within-pool errors w_i - mean(w) are
sigma_w-sub-Gaussian, then under **any** joint dependence curse(n) ≤ E[max_i (w_i - mean w)] ≤ sigma_w sqrt(2 ln n).
Proof: (i) adding kappa to every F_i leaves the argmax unchanged, and it cancels in epsilon_sel - mean epsilon. (ii) For
lambda > 0, exp(lambda E[max]) ≤ E[exp(lambda max)] ≤ sum_i E[exp(lambda (w_i - mean w))] ≤ n exp(lambda² sigma_w²/2). Take logs and set
lambda = sqrt(2 ln n)/sigma_w. Assumption: sub-Gaussian within-pool deviations; independence is not needed.
Comment: the hawk's "independence objection is half wrong" is right, and P1(i) goes further. A common misbelief, the error
the LIR belief masks are meant to keep honest, contributes **exactly zero** curse. It shifts the level of the committed
plan's value, which matters for commit-or-continue, but it does not grow with n.

**P1b (exact form for a linear forecaster; the curse can be negative).** Within a pool let V_i be i.i.d. N(mu, sV²) and
F_i = a + b V_i + eta_i with eta_i i.i.d. N(0, tau²) independent of V. Let s = sqrt(b² sV² + tau²) and e_n = E[max of n i.i.d. N(0,1)].
Then

    curse(n) = [ (b - 1) b sV² + tau² ] / s * e_n,        E[V_sel] - mu = b sV² / s * e_n.

Proof: epsilon = a + (b-1)V + eta, so curse = (b-1) E[V_sel - mean V] + E[eta_sel - mean eta]. Selection is on
G = bV + eta ~ N(., s²). By Gaussian regression E[V | G] and E[eta | G] are linear in G with slopes b sV²/s² and tau²/s², and
E[G_sel - mean G] = s e_n. With b = 1 this is the hawk's toy (tau²/sqrt(sV² + tau²) e_n). With equicorrelated errors,
replace tau² by the within-pool variance (1 - rho) sigma² (P1). **The curse is negative whenever tau² < (1 - b) b sV²**, which
describes a compressed forecaster (b < 1) with little per-plan noise. Its high forecasts sit on plans whose true value
it underrates, so argmax-minus-random measures slope miscalibration as well as selection on noise. The random-pick
control removes intercept bias (the hawk's hidden assumption 6) but not slope. The clean curse is the gap left after
recalibration: E3's excess gap, P2.

**P2 (joint calibration means zero gap under adaptive proposal and optional stopping).** Let G be the information that
the proposal, stopping and selection rules may use: evidence, situation report, plans proposed so far, forecasts read
so far, and exogenous randomness. Suppose the forecasts are jointly calibrated, F_i = E[V_i | G] for every candidate
that can be proposed. Then any G-measurable rule has gap 0, whether it takes the argmax over an i.i.d. pool, proposes
adaptively from earlier forecasts (read-then-propose), or stops at a forecast-dependent n.
Proof: E[V_sel - F_sel] = sum_i E[1{sel = i} (V_i - F_i)] = sum_i E[1{sel = i} E[V_i - F_i | G]] = 0, because 1{sel = i} is
G-measurable. (For optional stopping, take G to be the stopped sigma-algebra and require F_i = E[V_i | G_tau]: later
forecasts carry no information about V_i beyond F_i.)
Consequences: (a) the curse measures miscalibration conditional on the selection-relevant information, not
"uncertainty". The hawk's §1 point is proved here, not only simulated. (b) The adaptive-proposal and optional-stopping
artefacts in the hawk's §2.3-2.4 are artefacts of miscalibration. A calibrated forecaster shows no gap in any mode
(toy P2).

**P3 (when true value can fall with n).** With i.i.d. candidates and continuous F, let u be the quantile of F and r(u) =
E[V | F at quantile u]. Then E[V_sel(n)] = ∫_0^1 r(u) n u^(n-1) du = E_{u~Beta(n,1)}[r(u)]. Beta(n+1,1) first-order
dominates Beta(n,1), so **if r is non-decreasing, E[V_sel(n)] is non-decreasing in n for every forecaster, however
noisy.** True value can fall only if r decreases somewhere in the upper tail, that is, if the forecaster's highest
forecasts are systematically worse plans (exploits, "phrasing tricks"). Khalaf et al. 2025 (Theorem 1) show the curve
is monotone or has a single interior extremum. P3 says which case applies: rise-then-fall requires a decreasing tail
of r. This explains the hawk's §5 finding that P* never fell with n in 18 of 18 ShipHunt settings: the noise there was
additive, so r was monotone.

**P4 (the right stopping rule is Weitzman's, on calibrated values).** Take i.i.d. proposals, cost c per forecast,
recall of earlier candidates, and calibrated values v_i = m(F_i, u_i) = E[V_i | F_i, u_i] with known distribution H. The
optimal sequential rule is Pandora's rule (Weitzman 1979): keep forecasting while the best v so far is below z, where
E_H[(v - z)^+] = c, then commit to the argmax of v. By P2 the committed value has zero gap. So (i) the stopping
quantity is the expected improvement of the *calibrated* value, the myopic value of computation of Russell and Wefald
1991, which is exact here. (ii) Forecaster uncertainty u enters only through m: it matters if and only if
E[V | F, u] depends on u. (iii) The "bound by stated uncertainty" holds only to the extent that u predicts |epsilon|,
which is what the Spearman test measures. Not covered: adaptive proposals (candidates are no longer independent boxes,
so the index rule loses its optimality guarantee; P2 still holds) and state heterogeneity (H varies by state; the
script uses a state-relative reservation on within-state deviations, which is a heuristic).

## 4. The hawk's objection, point by point

1. "Guaranteed by order statistics": **conceded, with a sharpening.** The gap grows with n for any noisy forecaster
   when b = 1 (P1b), but the sign of the curse is not guaranteed. It is negative for compressed forecasters, which is
   what E3 shows. In either case it is uninformative about LIR.
2. "Bound vacuous where it matters": **conceded.** sigma sqrt(2 ln n) is loose by 2x even under exact Gaussianity
   (0.353 vs 0.181 at rho = 0), and the dependence-free version cannot be made tighter without a model (P1).
3. "Independence objection half wrong": **agreed and extended.** The upper bound needs no independence, and common error
   cancels exactly (P1(i)), so shared misbelief cannot drive the curse.
4. "Free forecasts mean no stopping": **agreed and proved (P3)**: monotone r implies monotone value. A cost c is required,
   and the optimal rule is then Weitzman's reservation value on calibrated forecasts (P4).
5. "The remaining curse is per-plan noise, unrelated to the masked belief errors": **agreed, now a theorem (P1(i))**. The
   one route by which LIR masks could matter for T1 is P3's decreasing tail: reward-shaped forecasts that rate exploits
   highly. That is E3's RAPf positive control, measured there by gold(n) and the reference-calibrated gap
   (E3/RESULTS.md: gold -0.273 ± 0.051), not by a stopping bound.
6. Reshape items run: cost (0), exact P* (1), nested n with dedupe (2, i.i.d. mode only), random-pick control (3), rules at
   equal calls (4: fixed n, learned gap model, SW with the stated sd, HedgeTune, WCO; LCB only in the toy, since E3 has no u),
   and the Spearman tests (5). Kill conditions evaluated in the Verdict.

## 5. What it implies for the experiments' metrics

- Do not report raw "optimism gap vs candidates" or argmax-minus-random as the curse. Its sign mixes slope
  miscalibration with selection on noise (P1b). Report gold(n) (true P* of the committed plan), the oracle(n) deficit, and
  the **recalibrated** gap: cross-fitted isotonic regression on realised outcomes, or a reference fit from the frozen
  checkpoint when there is distribution shift (E3's post-hoc metric). By P2 this is the quantity that measures exploitation.
- Report forecaster informativeness alongside every curve: the within-pool slope b and corr(F, P*). With b ≈ 0, every
  selection metric is null by construction (E3: b = 0.00-0.14).
- If deliberation length is ever measured, add a cost per forecast and compare against Weitzman's reservation rule on
  calibrated values (P4), with raw F under the same rule as the control. Gains in the toy came from adaptive stopping,
  not from shrinkage.
- The fixed format's P(success) carries no epistemic uncertainty. Testing "uncertainty-aware stopping" needs a stated
  epistemic sd, or the FF arm's claim set with separately scored confidence, and its Spearman with |epsilon| must exceed 0.2
  before any shrinkage rule is worth running.
- Ensembles over seeds see little of the error (seed-seed corr 0.41-0.81 against seed-Bayes 0.03-0.16). WCO at equal calls
  loses in the toy and in 14 of 16 E3 probe pairs.
