## Bottom line

- **The headline reproduces exactly.** The TEST table, the paired wins over k0 and cell-by-cell at every board count, and the 0.28 crossing (CI 0.20-0.37) all recompute from the 81 raw final-run files. No loop run ever evaluated TEST. The cached TEST boards and their exact posteriors match a fresh regeneration from the harness bit for bit.
- **rbtd8's training regime is real-world-valid under Kelvin's rule.** Its target uses only the realised board and the network's own outputs. No posterior, no noise model, no extra or resampled probes. No true cell is an input when the reported forecasts are made.
- **The research process broke the rule's letter once.** The champion, and the per-N learning rate and EMA, were chosen by validation KL to the exact posterior, which counts as model selection. This changes nothing here: an outcome-only selection (no posterior) picks the same champion, and on TEST the win also holds on outcome log-loss at every N and every seed.
- **Overstated:** the compute price (44x per board at 100 boards, not 14-22x), "with 10k boards it beats k0 trained on 30k" (the paired interval includes 0), and "0.158 is an upper bound" (it is not a bound).
- **CPU rerun on fresh boards:** On fresh training boards (seed 5, N=300, CPU, the repo's own scripts), rbtd8 reached **0.51x k0's TEST KL** (3.29 vs 6.42 x1e-3). That was with its training cut at 460 steps, where the reported runs ran 1220-2060. So the direction and the rough size of the gap reproduce on boards no part of the experiment used. k0 did worse on these boards than on any reported seed. A CPU control on the reported seed-3 boards gave 3.33 against the reported GPU run's 3.19, so CPU matches GPU and this board sample is simply harder.

## What rbtd8 actually trains on

Configuration: `smallnet.py --arm rbtd --set rbtd=8` (lines 334-366): `k0w=0` (no plain 0/1 loss), `rbtd_lam=0`, one chain, no EMA teacher, `stages=even`.

Each step draws 64 training boards with replacement. Each board brings the evidence after 0, 2, 4 and 6 probes of its one stored trajectory, in the observed order, under a random board symmetry. Up to here every arm is the same. Then, for each of the 256 board-stages:

1. Draw a random order of the 25 cells and 7 random cut points 1 <= c1 < ... < c7 <= 24 (c1 averages 3.1).
2. Run the same network 8 times. Level 0 sees only the evidence. Level i (1-7) sees the evidence plus the TRUE contents (ship or water) of the first c_i cells in the random order, through the "reveal" input channel.
3. At every level, each cell still hidden at that level gets a cross-entropy loss toward one of three targets:
   - its forecast at the next level, gradient stopped, if it is still hidden there;
   - its true 0/1 value, if the next level reveals it;
   - its true 0/1 value, at the top level (7).

The reported forecast is level 0: evidence only, nothing revealed, read from the same head. So the reported forecast is trained toward **what the same network predicts for that cell once about 3 random other cells of the same board are shown to it**. When the cell is itself one of the shown cells (about 1 target in 8), the target is the cell's true value. The chain of predictions ends at the true board. Early stopping uses the outcome log-loss of the level-0 forecast against the true cells of the 3000 validation boards. At test time the only input is the probes.

For comparison, the two plain arms. **k0** (`--arm k0`) takes the evidence only (each cell: no probe / reported miss / reported hit) and outputs 25 logits in one pass; its target is the true 0/1 board, with per-cell cross-entropy. **Cell by cell** (`--arm k0cell`, `cell_q=5`) takes the evidence plus a one-hot query flag on one cell and outputs that cell's logit; its target is the cell's true 0/1 value. It asks 5 random cells per board-stage per step (weighted x25/5); evaluation asks all 25. Neither ever sees a true cell as input.

## Real-world validity under Kelvin's rule

| rule | rbtd8 | where |
|---|---|---|
| one observed evidence trajectory per board | yes. One stored (cell, report) list per board; training uses its 0/2/4/6-probe prefixes in the order observed | `data.py` 45-57; `smallnet.py` 112-131 |
| no resampled probes | yes. The reveal chain resamples which TRUE cells of the realised board are shown, never the evidence | `smallnet.py` 338-348 |
| no simulator queries beyond the episode | yes. The environment is called once per board to create the episode (`data.episodes`); training never calls it | `smallnet.py` (no environment import) |
| exact posterior and noise model only in evaluation | training: yes. Early stopping: yes (outcome log-loss, line 620). Model selection: **no**, the champion and the learning rate / EMA were chosen on validation KL, but the outcome-only version selects the same (row 9) | `smallnet.py` 100-108, 302-303, 611-628 |
| only probes as inputs at evaluation | yes, for every reported forecast and KL. The secondary "joint LL (AR)" metric feeds true cells by design (chain rule); no forecast claim rests on it | `smallnet.py` 439, 473-497 |

The rule does not cover what decides whether rbtd8 would work on real questions. rbtd needs many outcomes that resolve together for one evidence trajectory (25 per board here), plus a model that accepts resolved outcomes as inputs. A real question with a single outcome leaves nothing to reveal. The gain comes from strong dependence between outcomes (ships cover neighbouring cells), and real question sets have much less of it. Every arm also uses ShipHunt-specific priors (symmetry augmentation, 2-D attention bias) and early-stops on 3000 validation boards with known outcomes, 30x the 100-board training set. These are equal across arms, so the comparison is fair, but they do not carry over to real questions.

## What the report overstates or leaves out

1. **Validity of the process.** RESULTS section 8 says "The posterior is used only to score". It also chose the champion and the per-N learning rate and EMA, and it motivated the final early-stopping change. Section 2 discloses the selection by KL, but the TL;DR and section 8 read as if the posterior only scored. Here this has no effect (row 9).
2. **Compute price.** "14-22x" is the 1k and 10k figure. At 100 boards the ratio is 44x, at 300 boards 23x (to the best checkpoint).
3. **"With 10k boards it beats k0 trained on 30k".** The paired interval includes 0 (3 seeds).
4. **"0.158 is an upper bound".** It is a likely overestimate, not a bound. The 30k comparison also gave rbtd8 a 30000-step cap against k0's 40000.
5. **"Target noise is the bottleneck".** This is an interpretation: refpost differs from the arms in information as well as noise, and no ablation tests it (row 16).
6. **"k0 does not get there with more passes".** The cited example is a smoke run at another setting. The final runs do support the claim (row 13).
7. **Board counts.** The data-efficiency numbers count training boards only, while every arm also early-stops on 3000 validation boards with outcomes. SPEC-REVIEW A.7 says so; the TL;DR's "about 2.8k boards" does not.
8. **Scope.** One synthetic task (5x5, 7 ship cells, flip 0.2) and one network. "Real-world-valid" means rule-compliant, not shown on real questions.

## CPU rerun (N = 300, fresh boards, 1 seed)

Setup: the repo's `smallnet.py`, unchanged, with the final protocol (`es_boards=3000`, patience 25, `--test`), on CPU (torch 2.14.1+cpu). The fresh training boards are train seed 5 (env seeds 1,500,000 onward, first 300 used), which no loop or final run touched. The validation and TEST caches are the ones verified in row 5. The shared 16-core box went from load 13 to 64 and back to about 20 during the reruns (other users' builds). Two earlier rbtd8 attempts were stopped: one after 60 steps, and one that had not reached step 20 after 11 minutes at load 60. The kept rbtd8 run was capped at 25 minutes of training with the repo's `--max-minutes`.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k0 | fresh, seed 5 | 820 (320) | patience | **6.42** | 4.22 +- 0.75 (seeds 3.19-4.67) |

| k0, CPU control | reported seed 3 | 920 (420) | patience | **3.33** | 3.19 (GPU run on the same boards; best step 460) |

- **Paired on the fresh boards,** rbtd8 / k0 = 0.51 (reported at N=300: 0.44, 95% interval 0.37-0.52). Outcome log-loss is lower by 3.3e-3 nats per cell, and rbtd8 is better on 99.7% of the 5000 TEST boards. That last spread reflects TEST-board noise only, not seed noise.
- **The truncated run is on the reported trajectory.** At its best step (420), its validation KL is 3.28, against 3.21-3.68 for the five reported runs at the same step. Its final number is above the reported final numbers because training stopped early; at the same step it sits inside the reported range.
- **What this shows and what it does not.** With one seed, the direction and the rough size of the gap reproduce on boards no part of the experiment used, with the repo's code, on another device. It does not reproduce rbtd8's final N=300 number (training was cut). k0 did worse on these boards than on all five reported seeds, so board-sample variance at N=300 is wider than five seeds show. Because the comparison is paired, that does not favour rbtd8.
