## A. Rules (L0, outcomes only)

1. **[changes design] The world model must learn where probes land, so it can also learn a wrong dependence.** In T1 probe cells are independent of the board. A world model (WM) whose location head sees the board can pick up a spurious board-to-location link from N episodes, and dreams then teach the forecaster to treat locations as evidence. In T2 that link is the point. Fix: the location head sees the board in every eligible WM (generic, nothing hard-coded). Every WM reports a location-information diagnostic: mean log P(next cell | true board) - log P(next cell | another episode's board) on validation episodes. Truth: 0 in T1, positive in T2 (the exact value is computed for reference, evaluation-only).
2. **[changes design] A board-blind location head hand-codes "locations carry no information".** It is not L0 in T1 (it assumes the probe process). It is used only as the T2 ablation ("a world model that cannot learn the selection effect") and tagged L0: no.
3. **No repeated probes is a fact of the data format here.** The forecaster's input holds one reading per cell (round-1 format), and every real episode has 6 distinct cells. The dream sampler therefore draws the next cell among unprobed cells. The WM's own probability mass on already-probed cells is logged (it should learn about 0).
4. **The 8 symmetries and the 2-D attention bias are task knowledge.** The spec asks for the 8 symmetries; round 1's audit noted they do not carry over to real questions. Kept, equal across arms, flagged in RESULTS.
5. **Arm 4 (WM posterior as target) follows the letter of the rule** (the model's own outputs) but Kelvin has not ruled. Fix: run it, report it in its own section, tag it "flagged", never eligible as champion. Its estimator is self-normalised importance sampling over a pool of the WM's own prior samples, weighted by the WM's own evidence likelihood (locations and readings).
6. **The exact posterior is for scoring only.** Nothing in training, early stopping, hyperparameter choice or champion selection reads it. The WM's implied posterior KL (a diagnostic of how good the WM is) is computed after each run and is evaluation-only.

## B. Measurement and selection (round-1 audit lessons, now binding)

7. **[changes design] Selection on validation outcomes only.** Round 1 selected by validation KL (audit row 9). New score, pre-registered in PREREG.md: per N, the seed-paired mean difference of validation outcome log-loss (per cell, against the true cells of the 3000 validation boards), arm minus rbtd8, divided by rbtd8's own gain over k0 at that N (also outcome-based, floored at 5e-5 nats). S_out = mean over the 5 N. 0 = rbtd8, -1 = as far beyond rbtd8 as rbtd8 is beyond k0. KL scores are reported, never used.
8. **[changes design] Identical step caps and stopping protocol at every N.** Every phase of every arm (baselines, WM training, forecaster training) uses round 1's per-N learning rate, weight EMA and evaluation cadence (calibrated on k0 in round 1; the audit found outcome log-loss picks the same cells), patience 25 evaluations at N <= 1000 and 40 at N >= 3000, early stopping on all 3000 validation boards, and the same cap of 40000 steps at every N. The baselines stop by patience long before that cap (round 1: k0 <= 33k, rbtd8 <= 33k steps at 10k), so the common cap only removes cap limits from new arms. The round-1 per-N cadence favours k0 (it was tuned for k0's early overfitting); that is conservative for the dream arms.
9. **Compute is reported per board at every N, all phases counted:** WM training, dream generation, importance sampling (arm 4) and forecaster training, both to the best checkpoint and in total (round-1 audit row 10).
10. **Decision rule made exact** (PREREG.md): the champion beats rbtd8 if, on validation outcomes, its seed-paired log-loss difference is below 0 at every N; confirmed if, on TEST (5 seeds, scored once), the paired KL difference to rbtd8 has a 95% t-interval below 0 at every N. Otherwise the report names the N where it fails.
11. **Validation boards are data too** (round-1 SPEC-REVIEW A.7): both the WM and the forecaster early-stop on the 3000 validation episodes' outcomes. Same for every arm; stated with the results.
12. **Hypotheses before runs:** each wave's hypotheses go into LOOP.md before launch, with UTC times, and a sha256 of LOOP.md at each launch goes into the launch log (round-1 audit row 15 could not verify order).

## C. T2: board-dependent probe selection (pre-registered here)

13. **Rule (fixed 2026-10-11 12:58Z, before any model ran on T2):** for each probe t = 1..6, let U be the unprobed cells and A the cells of U that are occupied or edge-adjacent to an occupied cell. With probability q = 0.7 the probe cell is uniform on A (if A is empty, uniform on U); otherwise it is uniform on U. Readings flip with probability 0.2 as in T1. Boards are T1's boards (same harness seeds); probes come from a separate stream `default_rng([seed, 20261011])`. The harness env is not edited; `wm2/t2data.py` generates T2 episodes with the harness's board and placements.
14. **Why q = 0.7 and 4-neighbours** (evaluation-side calculation on 300 validation boards, `/tmp/wm2/scratch/t2_rules.py`): ignoring the selection costs 9.2 / 20.3 / 31.7 x 1e-3 nats per cell at 2 / 4 / 6 probes (mean over the 4 stages 15.3e-3), two to three times the best round-1 model's KL at N = 100, so the effect is measurable at every N. Locations then add about two thirds as much information as readings at 6 probes. The weaker setting (q = 0.5) costs 7.2e-3, too close to model error at N = 100.
15. **T2 exact posterior includes the selection term** P(cell_t | board, earlier cells) for each of the 11088 placements; the selection-unaware posterior (T1 formula on T2 data) is stored as a reference: "a Bayesian that ignores where probes land".
16. **T2 question, made testable:** the WM learned the selection effect if (a) its location head carries information about the board (diagnostic 1 > 0, with seeds), and (b) the dream-trained forecaster's KL to the selection-aware posterior is below both the selection-unaware exact posterior and the same pipeline with a board-blind location head (paired, 95% CIs on TEST).

## D. Arms and model

17. **Generic joint world model, concretely:** the round-1 cell-token transformer (same size: d 128, 4 layers, 2-D relative bias), plus a probe-order embedding and two per-cell heads (next-probe location logit, reading logit). One network gives the joint probability of a whole episode: board cells in a random order (any-order autoregressive, the 8 symmetries as augmentation), then the 6 probes in their observed order, each (cell, reading) conditioned on the full board and the earlier probes. Trained by maximum likelihood on the N real episodes only.
18. **Dreams:** sample a board cell by cell in a random order (25 passes), then 6 probes (6 passes), from the early-stopped WM. The forecaster trains on dreamed evidence after 0, 2, 4, 6 probes with the dreamed board's 0/1 cells as target (arm 3). With unlimited dreams its fixed point is the WM's own posterior, so the WM's quality sets the floor; the WM-implied posterior KL is reported for every WM.
19. **Arm 4 cost** (importance sampling): K prior samples x 6 passes per dreamed evidence, so it uses a fixed pool of dreams, priced in the smoke run.
20. **Shared vs separate:** separate = a fresh forecaster network trained on dreams from a frozen WM. Shared = the WM network itself continues training: forecaster loss on dreams (from its frozen phase-1 copy) plus its own WM loss on the real episodes; the forecast is its single-cell head with evidence only. Same dreams, same budget.
21. **Baselines rerun:** k0 and rbtd8 run through round 1's `smallnet.py`, unchanged, on the same boards, with the protocol of point 8.
