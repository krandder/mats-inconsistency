# Reactions to Scientist AI / LawZero from Yudkowsky, MIRI, MIRI-adjacent researchers, LessWrong and Zvi

eurisko-study, 2026-10-03. Kelvin's question: "Has Eliezer Yudkowsky or the MIRI-adjacent people, or the LessWrong
community, reacted to the Bengio/LawZero approaches?" Window: 2024 to 2026-10-03.

Method: LessWrong/AF GraphQL and search APIs (all posts and comments since 2024-01-01 by about 60 named accounts, grepped
for Bengio | LawZero | Scientist AI | Bayesian oracle | disinterested | non-agentic; all 626 Zvi posts since 2024 scanned
by paragraph; every dedicated thread read), web search, WebFetch, ifanyonebuildsit.com via web.archive.org (live site:
HTTP 429), MIRI PDFs, EA Forum GraphQL. All fetched 2026-10-03; karma (k) and comments (c) as of then; quotes verbatim.
"Unverified" = seen only in a search snippet. LW links are lesswrong.com/posts/<id>[?commentId=<id>].

**TL;DR.** Yudkowsky and MIRI: no public reaction to Scientist AI, the Bayesian-oracle paper or LawZero found; their book
site argues against "pure predictors", "just tools" and narrow-AI plans in general, without naming Bengio. Agent-foundations:
Demski mildly positive, Byrnes one 2024 comment, Wentworth/Kosoy/Garrabrant silent. LessWrong: modest engagement (top
dedicated post k=76, 2024); critiques: tool-to-agent, ELK, Bayesian intractability, paralysis, usefulness. Zvi: "a highly
worthy effort" (2024); "super skeptical" but "a sane civilization would give this a shot" (2026).

## What the recurring objections are

1. **A tool or oracle does not stay a tool; it will be scaffolded or used to build agents.** Khoriaty, Lorxus, Raymond
   Douglas, jbash, mishka, Michael Dickens (EA Forum); in general form, without naming Bengio, the IABIED resources.
2. **Prediction good enough to matter needs steering or action; passivity costs usefulness and competitiveness.** IABIED
   "Impure Predictors" and "Passivity is in tension with usefulness", MIRI's "The Problem" (all general); Khoriaty
   (causal models need action; "always be behind"), Cole Wyeth ("much less useful"), Lorxus (accuracy, arms race), Linch.
3. **The hard part is ELK: knowing whether the model's hypotheses imply harm; contextualization needs "magic OOD
   generalization".** Greenblatt, Fabien Roger, LawrenceC (2024), leogao (2026); acknowledged in advance by MacDermott.
4. **The Bayesian posterior is intractable or adds little over ensembling.** Greenblatt, LawrenceC, leogao ("bayesian stuff
   is hard"), Gleave (via Satron), habryka ("confused (and skeptical)").
5. **Conservative rejection of possibly harmful actions paralyses the AI, or will be relaxed.** Kokotajlo, Joseph Miller,
   Greenblatt, Byrnes (programmers will "dial the "conservatism" knob down").
6. **Self-reference and effects of its own outputs break "disinterest" / consequence invariance.** Lorxus, Raymond
   Douglas, leogao (label-swap test).
7. **The paper's premise about RL is wrong.** TurnTrout (two posts); a co-author says the authors disagree among themselves.
8. **An old idea / partial solution.** LawrenceC 2024 ("not a new approach"), Lorxus ("long line of "oracle AI" ideas"),
   Wei Dai ("over-optimistically proposing ... partial solutions"), Khoriaty (citing Yudkowsky's tool-AI posts).
9. **No visible progress.** Sodium, CamilleBerger ("no prototype"), LawrenceC 2026 ("none ... has really paid off").
- **Endorsements:** Demski ("excited about"), Zvi ("highly worthy effort"; "a sane civilization would give this a shot"),
  leogao ("worth pursuing"), Raymond Douglas ("excited by the direction"), Hoogland, the UK AISI agenda, Greenblatt (the
  interpretable-hypothesis part only), Dickens ("a better starting point", conditional on a ban).
