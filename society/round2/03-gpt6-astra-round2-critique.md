## 1. Executive Verdict & Scientific Audit Synthesis

**Verdict: retain the mathematical and implementation contributions; withdraw the current runners as evidence of trained, blind, end-to-end search performance.** Round 11 must establish whether these operators produce useful inference when their inputs come from learned models, their search decisions consume real computation, and their evaluation targets remain inaccessible.

The audit identifies two distinct evidentiary categories:

| Category | What the supplied evidence supports | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Unit-Verified Mathematical Operators (`src/`)** | Implemented separation properties, algebraic invariances, controlled clearing behavior, topology execution, and estimator recovery under specified tests | Generalization, useful learned representations, accurate search credit, deployment calibration, or competitive game play |
| **Oracle-Perturbed / Parametric Runner Surrogates (`benchmarks/run_*.py`)** | Behavior under constructed signal strengths, synthetic error models, assumed scaling laws, and simulated match generators | Blind neural budget scaling, empirical discovery of a scaling law, actual game strength, or a measured general capability factor |

This distinction is essential because an evaluation must leave open the possibility that the proposed mechanism fails. Interpolating toward the correct answer as budget increases largely supplies the phenomenon the experiment purports to discover. Fitting a law to observations generated by that law tests recovery under the assumed generator. Sampling wins from latent ratings tests a league estimator against its simulator. These can be useful controlled experiments, but their conclusions must remain within those scopes.

The reported `PASS` results across Stage 1, Stage 2, and the Integrity Panel therefore require **claim-level reclassification**. An individual gate can contain a valid operator test and an invalid end-to-end inference. Neither a wholesale rejection of `src/` nor a wholesale acceptance of runner verdicts follows.

The strongest retained contributions are:

- **Belief–policy separation:** zero observed policy-to-belief influence under the audited parameter boundaries, interventions, and updates.
- **Even bilinear emission:** a valid mechanism preventing cancellation of opposite-sign input fluctuations.
- **Two-stage clearing:** a principled distinction between marginalizing alternative worlds and combining compatible evidence.
- **Executable tree and plan machinery:** a live search topology, orthogonalized plan directions, and demonstrated plan influence on actions.
- **Trust and memory operators:** favorable behavior under controlled depth and contradiction constructions.
- **League and psychometric estimators:** implemented recovery machinery for specified statistical models.

Each has a remaining scientific qualification. An even emitter can preserve undesirable evidence as readily as useful evidence. A plan swap can change an action without improving it. A frozen or disconnected belief path can remain perfectly stable while being inaccurate. Estimator recovery under its own generative assumptions does not establish adequacy for real opponents.

Likewise, the reported bound `||T_128||_inf = 0.999947` establishes contraction for the audited operator and assumptions, not useful convergence speed or contraction throughout a learned nonlinear system. At that bound, 128 applications reduce the worst-case error bound by only about 0.7%.

**R11-GPU-NEURAL should be a prospective test of learned innovation under model error.** GPU execution alone is insufficient: an oracle-assisted runner remains oracle-assisted on an NVIDIA L4.

## 3. Deep-Dive Critique of the Stage-1 & Stage-2 Runner Surrogates

### 3.1 Untrained trunks and oracle-directed improvement

The audit reports that the world models were instantiated without SGD training on the training split. This leaves the central representation-learning claim untested.

The following constructions directly supply target information:

\[
q=0.55p_{\mathrm{true}}+0.45p_{\mathrm{anc}},
\]

\[
\hat q=(1-\ell_s)\,0.5+\ell_s q_{\mathrm{true}},
\]

\[
l=l_0+\alpha\,0.86(l_{\mathrm{exact}}-l_0).
\]

Their scientific defect is not merely that they simplify a difficult problem. They replace the difficult problem—discovering a useful correction from available evidence—with a direction supplied by the answer.

These constructions mask:

1. **Representation failure.** The trunk may not encode the constraints needed for inference.
2. **Innovation failure.** A queried branch may provide redundant, irrelevant, or misleading information.
3. **Credit failure.** `SELECT_NODE` may allocate computation to attractive but unproductive branches.
4. **Transport failure.** Lemmas may lose meaning when transferred across depths, branches, or contexts.
5. **Clearing failure.** Dependent errors can reinforce one another despite favorable behavior under synthetic inputs.
6. **Calibration failure.** Learned confidence may become sharper while predictions become less accurate.
7. **Compute failure.** A nominal budget may not correspond to actual model calls, token processing, or cache work.

For probability mixtures, convexity already favors improvement in common scoring losses as the mixture approaches the target. For the audited log-odds shifts, the direction is explicitly target-aligned. Neither construction measures the probability that a neural search procedure finds such a direction.

The seed-and-budget-specific `C4` condition in Strong-Root ShipHunt is especially incompatible with a blind performance claim. It belongs, if retained, in a clearly labeled fault-injection fixture. Published algorithm comparisons must not include result-shaping exceptions keyed to evaluation seeds or budgets.

The correct repair is to retain these cases as controlled mechanism tests and replace their empirical claims with outputs from trained checkpoints and actual search executions.

### 3.2 Circular scaling-law generation

Stage 2 generates episode metrics using:

\[
\Delta C(B)=\operatorname{mechanistic\_redundancy\_delta\_c}(B,\alpha,\rho),
\]

\[
\mathrm{KL}(B)=\max\{0.004,\mathrm{KL}_0e^{-\Delta C(B)}
+\mathrm{oversearch}\}.
\]

It then fits the mechanistic law to `log(KL(0)/KL(B))`.

The reported `R² = 0.9982` and `r = 0.8078` consequently cannot establish that the law explains neural search. The generative procedure already embeds its central functional relationship. Floors, offsets, and noise may make recovery imperfect without making the experiment independent.

A valid empirical test must:

- Measure losses from predictions produced by actual search.
- Define crowding from recorded representations or decisions independently of target outcomes.
- Fit parameters on calibration observations only.
- Freeze the fitted law before revealing test losses.
- Compare held-out predictions with a constant-gain model, a log-budget model, and a saturating alternative.
- Test transfer to held-out budgets and environments, with uncertainty.

A successful neural system may reject this scaling law. R11 must permit that result without treating it as an implementation defect.

### 3.3 Algorithm-specific crowding kernels

`compute_empirical_gram_crowding` includes real selections, but algorithm-specific kernel widths and background offsets alter the resulting statistic before it is associated with performance.

This can make “crowding” partly an encoding of algorithm identity. If one method is assigned a narrow kernel and another a broad one, differences in the statistic need not reflect differences in learned search diversity.

R11 should use one measurement function for every arm:

- The same representation layer and normalization.
- The same similarity function and numerical tolerance.
- The same treatment of duplicate nodes, invalid steps, and absent observations.
- No algorithm-name-dependent smoothing or offsets.

Report raw pairwise similarities and duplicate-selection rates alongside any compressed crowding score. A useful primary statistic is normalized off-diagonal squared cosine similarity, with its exact aggregation fixed before evaluation.

### 3.4 Latent BTD sampling is estimator validation

Drawing outcomes from `btd_probabilities(theta_table, gamma, nu)` can validate fitting, seat-bias recovery, draw handling, and uncertainty under a BTD model. Deriving `theta_table` from oracle-assisted KL gains and then sampling matches creates an additional synthetic dependency.

It cannot establish:

- Legal, competent sequential play.
- Adaptation to opponent behavior.
- Performance with private information.
- Robustness to nontransitive strategies.
- Utility realization after many interacting decisions.
- Whether belief accuracy translates into action quality.

The repair is to generate actions through the actual agent and outcomes through the game engine. `BayesianEloLeague` and antisymmetric `mElo` then analyze those observed outcomes.

## 4. Focused Technical Prescription for Round 11

### 4.1 Freeze the experiment before test access

`PREREG-R11-GPU-NEURAL.md` must freeze:

- Repository revision, dependency versions, model architecture, and configuration hashes.
- Environment rules, public observations, legal actions, utilities, and termination rules.
- Dataset generators, split manifests, duplicate checks, and seed lists.
- Training objectives, optimizer, schedule, checkpoint selection, and compute limits.
- Algorithm arms, search budgets, metrics, exclusions, and statistical procedures.

Use five independent training seeds: **1101–1105**. Use disjoint generator namespaces for training, calibration, validation, and test data; generator structure or shared puzzle ancestry must not leak across splits.

For each predictive environment and seed, allocate **1,024 calibration roots**, split into 512 `cal-fit` and 512 `cal-check`, **512 validation roots**, and **512 sealed test roots**. Use budgets **B ∈ {0, 8, 32, 128}**.

The NVIDIA L4 run must record actual accelerator identity, precision, peak memory, training work, and evaluation cost. A training-only pilot may determine a feasible frozen model size and batch size. No throughput or completion time is assumed here.

### 4.2 R11-D0: two complementary oracle firewalls

**Static AST inspection.** Analyze the complete inference entry-point dependency closure, including search, model adapters, cache construction, memory retrieval, and environment adapters. Reject references or data flows from:

- `exact_marginals`
- `target_tokens`
- `query_targets`
- Oracle-bearing `meta` fields
- Oracle loaders, evaluators, or cached answer tables

The checker must cover aliases, subscripts, attribute access, and the permitted import graph. Prohibit unresolved dynamic attribute access, `eval`, `exec`, dynamic imports, and opaque deserialization in the audited inference closure unless separately bounded by an explicit allowlist.

AST analysis alone cannot establish noninterference for unrestricted Python. Its purpose is to enforce a restricted, inspectable inference interface.

**Runtime boundary.** `EnvBatch.strip_oracle_labels()` must construct a fresh, recursively allowlisted inference payload. It must not return a shallow copy retaining references to labels, private state, or arbitrary `meta`.

Permit only public context, legitimate visible tokens, observation masks, and legal-action information available to that player. Any required public metadata must move into explicitly typed public fields.

Execute the actor in a separate process with access only to:

- The sanitized payload.
- Frozen checkpoints and configuration.
- Its authorized episodic memory.
- Its own random-number stream.

The scorer and environment server retain labels and hidden state. The actor receives observations through a serialized interface and cannot open evaluator datasets or inspect the server’s objects.

During training, labels are legitimate optimizer inputs. During calibration, they are legitimate scorer inputs. Neither permission extends to the search actor.

**Required adversarial checks:** mutate forbidden fields while holding public observations constant; poison their accessors; insert nested oracle references; attempt alias and serialization bypasses; and compare complete actor traces under fixed actor RNG. Test-time scores are released only after the corresponding prediction manifest is sealed.

### 4.3 Train representations before testing search

Train the world model on the training split using proper predictive losses and authorized auxiliary objectives. Train or fit the policy and search components through explicitly recorded paths.

Checkpoint selection must use validation data and a frozen rule. Report trained-versus-initialized performance, optimization traces, parameter changes, and a simple learned baseline. A nonzero gradient or lower training loss is insufficient evidence of useful learning.

Evaluate natural model error and preregistered misspecification conditions, such as an under-capacity trunk and a train-to-test compositional shift. The shift must preserve task semantics and public information rules; neither condition may be generated by perturbing exact answers.

### 4.4 Verified Innovation Search through Leave-Subtree-Out credit

For a realized target \(y_i\), let \(S(q,y_i)\) be a proper loss, with lower values better. Use normalized categorical Brier loss as the primary innovation score:

\[
S(q,y)=\frac12\sum_k(q_k-\mathbf1[k=y])^2.
\]

For subtree \(s\), construct:

- \(q_i^{\mathrm{full}}\): the root prediction with the subtree’s contribution.
- \(q_i^{-s}\): the matched root prediction after removing that subtree and all derived contributions.

Define:

\[
I_{i,s}=S(q_i^{-s},y_i)-S(q_i^{\mathrm{full}},y_i).
\]

Positive credit means the subtree improved a held-out proper score.

A valid deletion removes descendants, emitted lemmas, transported memory, cached messages, and plans causally derived from the subtree. Recompute affected clearing operations. Subtracting the final message while preserving its descendants is not Leave-Subtree-Out.

Use replayed unaffected branches and matched random streams to measure contribution within a fixed trace. Separately run compute-reallocated ablations to estimate the value of spending that budget elsewhere. These answer different questions.

Fit an innovation predictor on `cal-fit` using only features available before the proposed branch is executed. Confirm its predictions on untouched `cal-check`; freeze it before test. Calibration roots used to fit this predictor are training data for that predictor and cannot also serve as its validation evidence.

`SELECT_NODE` should use predicted positive innovation per measured cost, uncertainty, and a frozen exploration rule. `STEP` and `MoPSFractalPlanHead` must act through observable model computations. At test time, no realized-target score or LSO credit returns to the actor.

For `TwoStageTreeClearer`:

- Stage 1 must preserve alternative-branch probability mass, including unexplored complement mass.
- Stage 2 must use parent-relative innovations.
- Stability constraints must apply to executed learned operators.
- Misspecified branches must remain capable of receiving negative measured innovation credit.

Orthogonal plans and even bilinear emissions remain candidate mechanisms. Their value must be demonstrated by ablation.

### 4.5 Real interactive tournaments

Run **Hex-5x5, Connect-4, Micro-Stratego, Galanis-Market, and Proposal-Poker** through authoritative environment engines. Every move must come from the agent’s current information state and a logged inference call.

Freeze game definitions and scoring before play. Hidden-state games expose only player-visible observations. For environments without a canonical win/draw/loss result, retain native utility and preregister an ordinal conversion for BTD; publish both. Do not sample match outcomes from predicted ratings.

Use four primary arms:

1. Full Verified Innovation Search.
2. The same trained model with `B=0`.
3. The same trained model with a general, compute-matched search allocator.
4. The same trained model with innovation estimates shuffled within preregistered comparable candidate sets.

For each seed, environment, and unordered arm pair, run **100 mirrored starting conditions**, with two seat assignments per condition. This yields **30,000 games** across five seeds, five environments, and six arm pairs.

Fit BTD and `mElo` on a fixed 80% of starting-condition groups; reserve 20% for predictive evaluation. Keep both seat assignments of a condition in the same partition. Evaluate any fitted Nash mixture against frozen opponents on that held-out partition.

### 4.6 Audit artifacts and compute accounting

Every prediction, game, and result table must resolve to an exact producer command, script, checkpoint, seed log, split manifest, and raw-record hash.

Record model calls, processed tokens, cache operations, peak memory, and wall time. Equal nominal `B` is not sufficient for fair compute comparison. Match the general-search comparator to full search within 5% of measured inference FLOPs under a frozen accounting method; report wall time separately.

Supplementary synthetic experiments may remain, but their outputs must be visibly labeled **SIMULATED** and excluded from neural gate calculations.

## 5. Pre-Registration Gate Contributions: R11-D0 Through R11-D6

### Shared decision rules

The following thresholds are **proposed preregistration choices**, not existing results.

Use normalized Brier loss as the primary predictive endpoint. Report log loss, task utility, latency, and memory as secondary endpoints. Give each environment equal weight in aggregate scores.

For predictive comparisons, compute paired differences within each seed and environment, then form the prespecified equal-environment aggregate for each training seed. Construct one-sided Student-\(t\) bounds across the five seed aggregates. Apply Holm correction at familywise **α = 0.05** across all confirmatory statistical inequalities listed below; enumerate them in the frozen preregistration. Report the small-seed limitation and a hierarchical sensitivity analysis.

Use grouped resampling for match prediction metrics, preserving training seeds, starting conditions, and seat pairs. No uncorrected exploratory result may replace a failed confirmatory endpoint.

A gate passes only if all its criteria pass. An incomplete run is **INCOMPLETE**, and an underpowered interval is **INCONCLUSIVE**; neither is `PASS`. Every status must include a check timestamp and the exact verification command and evidence hashes.

### R11-D0 — Static AST + Runtime `EnvBatch.strip_oracle_labels()` Zero-Oracle-Leakage Firewall

**Pass criteria:**

1. Zero violations in the frozen inference dependency closure; zero unresolved dynamic-access exceptions.
2. The runtime actor receives only the allowlisted serialized schema. Recursive inspection finds zero forbidden fields or references.
3. For 1,000 public observations per environment, each tested with ten forbidden-field mutations, actor predictions, selected actions, and search traces are identical under fixed RNG.
4. Every preregistered bypass fixture is rejected before an actor result is emitted.
5. The actor has no evaluator-data access, oracle IPC endpoint, or test-score feedback channel.

**Failure consequence:** quarantine affected empirical results. D0 failure invalidates dependent gates even when their numerical thresholds are met.

### R11-D1 — Trained Universal KV-Cache Tree Execution

**Pass criteria:**

1. Five independently trained checkpoints have complete optimizer and training-data provenance.
2. At `B=0`, the lower confidence bound on relative Brier improvement over matched initialization is **greater than 5%**.
3. Every evaluated search episode has a complete `SELECT_NODE`/`STEP` trace and reconciled budget accounting.
4. For 100 traces per environment and seed, cached execution agrees with reference uncached execution: maximum probability difference **≤ 10⁻⁵** in float32 reference mode.
5. The same search-control implementation runs across the frozen suite, with no environment-ID, seed, or budget exceptions encoding depth, wave, or stall behavior.
6. The lower confidence bound on full-search relative Brier improvement at `B=128` over its own `B=0` is **greater than 2%**.

Legal-action and observation adapters may vary by environment; core search control may not encode game-specific heuristics.

### R11-D2 — Useful Active Plans and Innovation Allocation

**Pass criteria:**

1. `cal-check` LSO innovation averaged over the highest predicted-credit quintile has a lower confidence bound **greater than zero**.
2. At `B=128`, full search exceeds each of the compute-matched general allocator and shuffled-credit arm by **more than 1% relative Brier improvement**, using lower confidence bounds.
3. Disabling Gram orthogonalization produces a positive, statistically supported increase in the fixed crowding statistic, and full search has a positive lower confidence bound on Brier improvement over that ablation.
4. Swapping plans between preregistered paired contexts changes at least **20%** of selected search actions; correct-context plans also produce a positive lower confidence bound on score improvement over swapped plans.

Action changes and geometric diversity alone cannot pass this gate. Root, tip, and fork fractions remain descriptive; no preferred “fractal” shape is imposed.

### R11-D3 — Useful Lemmas with Belief–Policy Separation

**Pass criteria:**

1. Policy-only interventions and 300 policy-gradient updates produce maximum belief-probability change **≤ 10⁻⁷** under fixed inputs and RNG.
2. The forbidden policy-to-belief autograd block is exactly zero in the reference implementation.
3. Even-emitter parity error is **≤ 10⁻⁶** over 10,000 logged random inputs.
4. Full search has a positive lower confidence bound on Brier improvement over each of three compute-matched ablations: emission disabled, lemma queries disabled, and lemma transport shuffled between unrelated contexts.
5. On preregistered paired utilities with distinct optimal actions and utility gap **≥ 0.1**, zero-retraining utility swaps yield at least **95%** correct action switches while satisfying the belief-invariance tolerance.

These criteria test both separation and usefulness. A disconnected lemma channel cannot pass solely by preserving beliefs.

### R11-D4 — Present-Time Episodic Memory Under Contradiction

Construct supportive, irrelevant, and contradicted-memory conditions from actual environment histories. Freeze retrieval rules and prevent test-episode or generator-family contamination.

**Pass criteria:**

1. Supportive memory improves Brier loss over memory disabled by **more than 1% relative**, using the lower confidence bound.
2. In each of the irrelevant and contradicted conditions, the upper confidence bound on normalized Brier degradation versus memory disabled is **≤ 0.005**.
3. In contradicted episodes, the surprise-gated system has a positive lower confidence bound on improvement over the same system with an always-open memory gate.
4. Every retrieved entry has auditable observation provenance, and zero entries contain forbidden oracle fields or information unavailable at that episode’s decision time.

A gate permanently set to zero fails criterion 1. Retaining synthetic contradiction resistance is insufficient.

### R11-D5 — Qualified Anytime Behavior and Self-Consistency

Universal per-instance monotonicity is not inferred from these tests. The claim is limited to expected proper-score behavior over the preregistered distribution.

**Pass criteria:**

1. For every environment and adjacent budget pair, the upper confidence bound on increased normalized Brier loss is **≤ 0.005**.
2. Aggregate `B=128` improvement over `B=0` meets the **greater-than-2%** lower-bound criterion in D1.
3. Every executed constrained clearing map satisfies its preregistered sufficient contraction condition, with induced-norm upper bound **≤ 0.9999**. The audit includes learned dependence of coefficients or gates; a frozen-coefficient bound cannot certify a different nonlinear map.
4. Probability-simplex and complement-mass residuals are **≤ 10⁻⁶** in reference precision. Duplicating an identical edge contribution changes root probabilities by **≤ 10⁻⁵**.
5. Compared with preregistered root-relative/conjunctive clearing, two-stage clearing improves both the legal-world consistency violation rate and Brier loss, with positive lower confidence bounds.

Report the fraction and severity of individual episodes harmed by additional search. Passing expected non-inferiority does not justify saying that search never hurts an individual prediction.

The mechanistic scaling law is a separate hypothesis. It receives empirical support only if its frozen calibration fit improves held-out prediction error over every preregistered alternative with a positive confidence bound. A high in-sample `R²` cannot satisfy that claim.

### R11-D6 — Interactive League and Empirical Mini-ECI

**Pass criteria:**

1. All 30,000 games have complete, deterministically replayable trajectories and engine-derived outcomes. Zero outcomes are sampled from latent strength parameters.
2. The aggregate lower confidence bound on full-search match points against the compute-matched general allocator is **greater than 0.50**. In every game, the corresponding lower bound is **at least 0.45**.
3. BTD estimates include seat and draw terms. On held-out matches, its log loss improves over an intercept-plus-seat baseline with a positive lower confidence bound.
4. Antisymmetric `mElo` parameters and any Nash mixture are fitted without held-out outcomes. A claimed nontransitive advantage requires positive held-out log-loss improvement over BTD. If this fails, report **nontransitive advantage unsupported**.
5. Mini-ECI uses actual held-out response records from at least **30 frozen agent configurations** and **200 independent items per environment**. No response is generated from a latent IRT probability.
6. On a frozen item holdout, the bifactor model improves predictive log loss over both item-only and unidimensional models with positive lower confidence bounds. After fixed sign and factor alignment, the median bootstrap Spearman stability of the general-score ranking is **≥ 0.80**.
7. The final table distinguishes observed match points, fitted ratings, native utilities, and inferred psychometric scores, with uncertainty and provenance for each.

If the bifactor model fails its predictive comparison, publish the simpler supported model. If `mElo` finds no reproducible cycles, publish that finding. Neither additional structure should be treated as a result merely because its estimator runs.
