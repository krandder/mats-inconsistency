## 1. What `arXiv:2610.18649v1` (*Tiny Recursive Message Passing*, `TRMP`) Actually Does

Salvatori et al. (2026) combine **typed recurrent message-passing graph neural networks (MPNNs / factor graphs)** with the **training and test-time inference machinery of modern Tiny Recursive Models (`TRM` / `HRM` / `LDT`)**:

1. **Two Graph Wirings (Peer Graph vs. Bipartite Factor Graph):**
   - **Peer Graph (`PG`):** Only variables (e.g., the 81 Sudoku cells) are vertices, with cliques connecting variables that share a constraint (20 neighbors per Sudoku cell, 810 undirected edges; cell-to-cell diameter = `2` rounds).Variables run a permutation-invariant **Pooling Module** ($\text{MLP} \to \text{mean/sum} \to \text{GRU}([\bar{e}_v; c_v; a_v], h_v)$).
   - **Factor Graph (`FG`):** Variables (81 cells, or $N$ SAT variables, or $N^2$ N-Queens cells) connect to constraint/unit vertices (27 Sudoku units: 9 rows, 9 cols, 9 boxes; or $M$ SAT clauses; or $4$ line types in N-Queens). Constraint units run a memoryless **Deep Sets Module** ($m_{c \to w} = \text{MLP}([W_{\text{emb}} m_{w \to c}; \sum_{u \in N(c)} W_{\text{emb}} m_{u \to c}])$), which is equivariant to member order and replies individually to each member. Crucially, the Factor Graph has **size-invariant node degree** (e.g., 3 ports per cell in Sudoku, 4 ports per cell in N-Queens, 2 polarity ports in SAT), enabling **zero-shot transfer across board/formula sizes ($N=10 \to 16, 20$ in N-Queens; $N \in [40,200] \to [200,400]$ in SAT)**, at the cost of doubling the variable-to-variable graph diameter (`4` rounds vs. `2`).
2. **Two-Timescale Nested Recurrence ($C \times R$ per Deep-Supervision Step):**
   - **Fast Variable (`Memory` $h_v^{(t)} \in \mathbb{R}^m$):** Updated at every inner-loop message-passing round $r \in \{1..R\}$ while holding the clue $c_v = E x_v$ and the current answer state $a_v$ fixed.
   - **Slow Variable (`Answer State` $a_v^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^{m_a}$):** Updated once per outer cycle $k \in \{1..C\}$ via a dedicated lightweight GRU cell $a_v^{(k)} = \text{GRU}_a(h_v^{(kR)}, a_v^{(k-1)})$ (`< 10%` of total parameters).
3. **Truncated Gradients (`Credit Horizon` $h = T \cdot R$ where $T = C - D$):**
   - Within each deep-supervision step of $C$ cycles, the first $D$ cycles execute inside `torch.no_grad()` (`stop-gradient` $\text{sg}(s_D)$), and backpropagation only flows through the last $T = C - D$ cycles ($h = (C - D)R$ message-passing rounds), plus detached persistent state carry across up to $n_{\text{sup}} = 32$ staggered batch slots.
   - **Central Theoretical/Empirical Finding on Truncation vs. Graph Diameter:** Truncating gradients discards gradients traveling along graph edges beyond $h$ hops. Consequently, **bipartite Factor Graphs (diameter 4) degrade severely under short credit horizons ($h=5$)** (`82.9%` vs. `98.5%` at 12k params on Sudoku-Extreme) and require doubling $h$ (`h=10+`), whereas **dense Peer Graphs (diameter 2)** survive `h=5` out of `60` rounds (`99.1%` at 24k params).
4. **Two Test-Time Inference & Readout Modes:**
   - **Mode A — `Discrete Deduction Lattice` ($\Pi$, adapted from Davis et al. 2026 `LDT`):** Maintains an external boolean candidate mask $x_d \in \{0,1\}^{|V| \times |\Sigma|}$ across deep-supervision steps. Three linear heads predict candidate keep probability $p^{\text{keep}}_{v,j}$, symbol distribution $p^{\text{sym}}_v$, and conflict probability $p^{\text{conf}}$. After each step, non-differentiable projection $\Pi$ prunes candidates with $p^{\text{keep}}_{v,j} < 0.1$, checks for conflict (empty domain or $p^{\text{conf}} > \varpi_{\text{cls}}$, triggering a **full restart from the root puzzle** across $k=64$ parallel chains), or commits one random undecided variable at temperature $1.5$.
   - **Mode B — `Adaptive Computation Time (ACT) Smooth-Minimum Halting + Best-of-k Noisy Rollouts` (adapted from Sghaier et al. 2026):** Each vertex emits a halting/confidence logit $q_v = w_q \text{sg}(a_v)$ (with stop-gradient so halting does not corrupt $a_v$!), pooled into a global board confidence via the **smooth minimum** $q = -\log \sum_{v \in V} \exp(-q_v)$ (so a single unconvinced vertex prevents halting). At test time, $k=64$ rollouts inject Gaussian noise ($\sigma \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0\}$) into $a_v$ after each step, and the rollout with highest $q$ is selected (**`99.9%` on `Sudoku-Extreme` with `32k` params; `99.1%` with `16k–24k` lattice model; `100%` on `Maze-unique` `30x30`**).

## 2. Exact Mathematical & Architectural Comparison: `TRMP (arXiv:2610.18649v1)` vs. Our Program (`GLC-AOM`, `GLCSearch-v2`, `LIR`)

There are **five striking structural convergences** where Salvatori et al. (2026) independently validate core pillars of our architecture — and **four sharp limitations in `TRMP`** where our framework goes fundamentally further.

### A. Where `TRMP` Independently Validates Our Core Architectural Decisions

| **1. ### B. Critical Differences: Where `TRMP` Falls Short and Where Our Research Goes Further

Following Kelvin's mandate to be rigorously critical rather than merely praising analogies:

1. **`TRMP`'s Lattice Search Is Memoryless Restart (`DPLL without CDCL`), Whereas `GLCSearch-v2` Uses `TreePagedKVBank` + `CDCL` Non-Chronological Backjumping + `Eureka` Persistent Lemmas:**
   - Look closely at `TRMP` `§B.2.1` (`Inference by chain search`):
     > *"At test time each puzzle is attacked by $k$ independent stochastic chains run in parallel... a chain that reaches a conflict **restarts from the original puzzle**."*
   - And look at what happens on **Hard SAT (`G4SATBench` hard `n=200..400`)** in `§5.3 Results`:
     > *"On the hard splits, however, [the lattice model] falls behind the no-lattice model (`8.0%` on hard SR, `17.7%` on hard 3-SAT)."*
   - **Why `TRMP`'s lattice fails on Hard SAT:** When a chain commits a variable at step 1, 2, 3 and hits a contradiction at step 8, `TRMP` **throws away the entire trajectory and restarts from scratch with zero memory of why the branch failed!** It has no shared search tree (`TreePagedKVBank`), no conflict clause learning (`CDCL` nogood mask $\mathcal{M}_{\text{nogood}}$), and no cross-chain Gram deduplication ($K_{\text{done}}$ repulsive prospectus plans). Running $k=64$ independent i.i.d. chains with blind restarts suffers from severe duplicate-branch collisions and exponential restart waste on hard phase-transition SAT!
   - By contrast, `GLCSearch-v2` stores all explored branches in `TreePagedKVBank`, turns every contradiction into a persistent nogood lemma (`R15-C3 Eureka`), and uses `K_done` Gram orthogonalization so rollout $r+1$ cannot re-enter a refuted subspace.

2. **`TRMP` Only Solves Static, Perfect-Information, Single-Agent CSPs — It Cannot Handle Imperfect-Information Adversarial Games (`Stratego`, `Poker`, `Phantom TTT`, `Dark Hex`) or Probabilistic Belief Aggregation (`PDGs`):**
   - `TRMP`'s smooth-minimum halting head ($q = -\log \sum_v \exp(-q_v)$) and monotone candidate elimination ($p^{\text{keep}}_{v,j} < 0.1$) assume a **deterministic, fully observable constraint graph where every constraint must be satisfied with probability 1**.
   - In **imperfect-information games (`Pillar 1`: `Ataraxos Stratego`, `OpenSpiel` `Leduc Poker`, `Liar's Dice`, `Dark Hex`)** or **noisy information markets (`Pillar 3`: `Galanis` / `Kyle-LMSR`)**:
     - You cannot "eliminate" an opponent's hidden piece or card just because its probability is `< 0.10` (doing so makes your policy infinitely exploitable by bluffs / rare setups!).
     - You cannot select actions by taking `argmax` of a single deterministic trajectory (`Nash` equilibria in imperfect-information games are strictly mixed strategies Requiring **Counterfactual Regret Matching** over $\mathcal{M}_{\text{infoset}}$!).
     - When multiple noisy rollouts return correlated partial evidence, `TRMP`'s `Best-of-k` (`argmax_r q^{(r)}`) simply picks ONE winning rollout and discards the other $k-1$ rollouts, whereas our **`TwoStageTreeClearer` / `Kyle-LMSR` AMM** aggregates all $k$ rollouts into a calibrated posterior distribution whileinverting their correlation matrix $(K^\top B K + \lambda I)^{-1}$.

3. **`TRMP` Uses Uniform Synchronous Message Passing (No Market Clearing / No Precision Weighting / No Anti-Wishful-Thinking Firewall):**
   - In `TRMP`'s Pooling Module (`Eq. 6`), every incoming neighbor message is averaged with **equal uniform weight $\frac{1}{|N(v)|}$**, and in the Factor Graph Deep Sets module (`Eq. 9`), member messages are summed with **unweighted $\sum_{w \in N(c)}$**.
   - As our `Round 8–17` experiments proved, uniform unweighted message pooling is vulnerable to **correlated-neighbor double counting** (on loopy graphs like 3-SAT and Peer-Graph Sudoku where triangles/4-cycles abound!). Replacing uniform sum/mean pooling with **Precision-Weighted Preconditioned Attention / Market Clearing ($\beta_{\text{trust}} \cdot m_{\text{ql}}$ deflated by Gershgorin row-crowding $D_{\text{crowd}}$)** directly dampens loopy belief propagation echoes.

4. **How We Can Immediately Steal `TRMP`'s Best Engineering Ingredients for Our Pillar 2 (`R19`/`R22B` Reasoning) & Pillar 4 (`R21` CLRS OOD):**
   - `TRMP` gives us three concrete, high-leverage techniques we can incorporate directly into `GLC-AOM`:
     1. **Two-Timescale Fast-Memory ($h_v$, $R$ rounds) + Slow-Answer ($a_v$, $C$ cycles) Split with Truncated Gradient Horizon ($h = (C-D)R$) & Staggered Persistent Batch Slots ($n_{\text{sup}}=32$):** Let `GLC-AOM` run $D$ detached message-passing cycles before the $T$ differentiated LIR clearing cycles, cutting GPU memory by `6x–12x` and allowing `60–192` message-passing rounds per step!
     2. **Bipartite Factor-Graph Port-Numbered Deep Sets + Our `Tseitin2HopQueryCoupler`:** Using `TRMP`'s exact port-numbered bipartite factor graph gives exact geometric equivariance (`0` positional parameters, zero-shot size transfer $N=10 \to 20$), while adding our `Tseitin2HopQueryCoupler` (2-hop cell-to-cell shortcuts) eliminates `TRMP`'s factor-graph diameter-4 truncation penalty!
     3. **Replacing `TRMP`'s Memoryless Chain Restarts with `TreePagedKVBank` + `CDCL` Nogood Backjumping on the Discrete Lattice $x_d \in \{0,1\}^{|V| \times |\Sigma|}$:** Instead of resetting $x_d$ to the blank puzzle when a conflict occurs, record the conflict clause in $\mathcal{M}_{\text{nogood}}$ and backjump to the decision level that caused the domain wipeout!
