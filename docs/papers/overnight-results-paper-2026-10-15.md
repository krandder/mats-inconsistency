## Abstract

We test whether a single any-order transformer trained only on realized outcomes over homogeneous variables with no domain topology or causal direction can (1) identify which of its own $N 2^{N-1}$ conditionals are stiff one-step laws versus soft multi-step marginals, (2) improve long-range beliefs by sequentially materializing intermediate variables along stiff or high-Value-of-Information (VoI) arcs, (3) deduplicate redundant conditioning views via learned key-value fingerprints, and (4) use failed search ("searched $M$ branches and found nothing") as calibrated negative evidence. Across three experimental rounds in two domains—permuted 1D cellular automaton spacetime grids (`E-CA`, Rules 110 and 90, $\varepsilon = 0.015$, tested at $W=8, T=6$ in Rounds 1–2 and on a zero-overlap disjoint-row-0 $W=12, T=10$ grid in Round 3) and 36-cell $6\times 6$ Sudoku with ambiguous and unsolvable boards (`E-SUD`, tested on standard corruptions in Rounds 1–2 and on deep $\ge 3$-step contradictions in Round 3)—learned stiffness tracks exact conditional information gain with Spearman $0.923\text{--}0.948$ (`0.925` on zero-overlap $12\times 10$ Rule 110) and classifies true local Markov blankets at $0.845\text{--}0.871$ AUC ($0.8165$ on descriptive Round-3 Rule 90; `D1` passes 3 of 4 cells), though the network's own predictive entropy and 1-step self-consistency rank pairs and epistemic errors equally well (`R3-D3` Spearman $0.700$ vs. $0.697$ vs. $0.697$). Sequential search cuts posterior KL only when two conditions hold simultaneously: the network has accurately learned the one-step local law (`S1` KL $\le 0.008$) and the query exceeds single-pass capacity (`E-SUD` at 150k steps, where confidence-first search cuts KL by $46.3\%$; `E-CA` $8\times 6$ Rule-110 bridges, where random-order chains cut KL by $27\text{--}51\%$ on `T1 B1`, $25\text{--}43\%$ on `T1 B2`, $42\text{--}44\%$ on `T2 B1`, and $28\text{--}32\%$ on `T2 B2`; and zero-overlap $12\times 10$ `E-CA` in Round 3, where removing row-0 attractor memorization opens a real forward gap [`F` KL `0.128`] that stiff-gated `VoI` cuts by $31.0\%$ and stiff/confidence-first search cuts by $13.0\text{--}14.6\%$, beating random order by $15.3\text{--}16.8\%$). When the network is under-trained (`E-SUD` at 30k steps, `E-CA` 1-layer net) or when 4 layers already solve multi-step propagation via attractor memorization (`E-CA` $8\times 6$ Rule 90, KL $0.006\text{--}0.010$), search fails or degrades beliefs (`D0`, `D2`, `R2-D2`, and `R3-D2` fail their pre-registered bars). Key-value attention fingerprints reduce log-odds drift across 5 redundant views by $95\text{--}98\%$ (`D3a` ratio $0.017\text{--}0.057$) and, once target hidden-state variance dominance ($20{,}000:1$) is removed in Rounds 2–3, separate forward from backward chains ($\cos(k_{\text{fwd}}, k_{\text{bwd}}) = -0.096$ and $+0.066$ at $T=6$, `R2-D3a` pass; $0.25$ and $0.21$ on Rule 110 and $-0.40$ and $-0.30$ on Rule 90 at $T=10$). Finally, on $6\times 6$ Sudoku at 150k steps, recall-floored null search cuts `Solvable` log-loss by $93.6\text{--}99.99\%$ on standard boards (`R2-D1a` pass, 9/9) and—once Round 3 filters out 1- and 2-step contradictions so one-pass `P0` fails on unsolvable boards (`1.84` nats log-loss, `R3-D1a` pass)—cuts deep-contradiction unsolvable log-loss by $99.38\text{--}99.64\%$ across all selectors (`R3-D1b` pass). Untempered believed mass $C_M^{\text{bel}}$ strongly ranks unresolved boards (`AUC 0.87–0.93` on `P3`, `0.71–0.85` on `P1` vs. `0.50` by construction for chain count), but the $(1 - C_M)^\theta$ parameterization pins $\theta$ at the grid ceiling and does not beat the chain-count control in log-loss (`R2-D1b` and `R3-D1c` fail).

## 1. Setup

### 1.1 Domains and Data

**Permuted 1D Cellular Automaton Spacetime Grid (`E-CA`).**
- **Rounds 1 and 2 ($W = 8, T = 6$, $N = 48$ cells; plus a Round-1 hedge at $W = 8, T = 8$, $N = 64$):** Row 0 is drawn i.i.d. $\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0.5)$; each subsequent cell evolves under periodic boundary conditions via $x_{t+1, c} = \mathrm{rule}(x_{t, c-1}, x_{t, c}, x_{t, c+1}) \oplus \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)$ with noise $\varepsilon = 0.015$ for Wolfram Rule 110 (glider-supporting) and Rule 90 (linear XOR lightcones). A single fixed random permutation of the cell indices is applied before the model ever sees the data; the model receives only permuted token IDs with learned absolute position embeddings and no spatial or temporal coordinates. Exact ground-truth posteriors $P^*(X_i = 1 \mid X_S = x_S)$ for any evidence mask $S$ are computed by exact forward-backward message passing over the $2^W = 256$ row states (verified against brute-force enumeration over all $2^{16}$ grids at $W = T = 4$, max absolute difference $5.3 \times 10^{-14}$). Splits per rule: $50{,}000$ training, $5{,}000$ calibration, $3{,}000$ validation, and $3{,}000$ test grids. Five evaluation query families are defined:
  - **`F` (Forward):** Row 0 revealed (8 cells); query in rows 3–5.
  - **`B1` (Two-Row Bridge):** Row 0 and row $T-1$ revealed (16 cells); query in middle rows 2–3.
  - **`B2` (Boundary-Anchor Bridge):** Half of row 0 (cols 0–3), row $T-1$, and column 0–1 anchors revealed; query in the unrevealed interior.
  - **`R` (Random):** Reveal count $k \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\{1, \dots, 47\}$.
  - **`S1` (One-Step Check):** Target cell's 3 true past parents revealed.
- **Round 3 Zero-Overlap Grid ($W = 12, T = 10$, $N = 120$ cells):** After diagnosing that $W = 8$ has only $2^8 = 256$ row-0 states so $81.5\text{--}82.1\%$ of test grids occurred verbatim in the $50{,}000$ training grids, Round 3 widened the grid to $W = 12, T = 10$ ($120$ permuted cells) and partitioned the $2^{12} = 4{,}096$ row-0 states into **strictly disjoint sets** of `3,072 / 384 / 320 / 320` for `train / cal / val / test` ($50{,}000 / 4{,}000 / 2{,}000 / 2{,}000$ grids; measured row-0 and whole-grid train/test overlap: **`0.000`**). Exact posteriors at $W = 12$ use bit-factored $O(W 2^W)$ forward-backward propagation (deterministic row scatter/gather followed by $W$ per-bit flip-channel updates; verified against dense forward-backward and brute force to $9.1 \times 10^{-14}$, running in $20\text{ ms/grid}$ on 1 CPU core). Query families at $T = 10, W = 12$: `F` (row 0 revealed, query in rows 6–9), `B1` (rows 0 and 9 revealed, query in rows 4–5), `B2` (row 0 cols 0–5, row 9, and cols 0–1 revealed; query in rows 4–5, cols 3–10), and `S1` (3 true parents revealed).

**Any-Order $6\times 6$ Sudoku with Ambiguous and Unsolvable Boards (`E-SUD`).**
- **Rounds 1, 1b, and 2:** Boards are $6\times 6$ grids with $2\times 3$ boxes ($C = 36$ cells) with 10–20 givens (test mean $14.3$ overall: $13.57$ on solvable boards, $14.83$ on unsolvable boards). Solvable boards are restricted to have between 2 and 400 valid completions (test mean $12.11$, range 2–348) so cell marginals are non-degenerate probabilities; exact cell marginals are computed by bitmask DFS over all completions (cross-checked against an independent row-permutation enumerator on 300 test boards, max difference $2.9 \times 10^{-8}$). Unsolvable boards are generated by altering 1–2 givens on a solvable board so that zero valid completions exist without creating any direct row, column, or box peer clash (`peer_conflict`) among the givens. Splits: $200{,}000$ training, $2{,}000$ validation, and $2{,}000$ test boards ($857 / 2000 = 42.85\%$ solvable).
- **Round 3 Hard Split (`s6_hard_val.npz`, `s6_hard_test.npz`, $1{,}000$ boards each, $50\%$ solvable / $50\%$ unsolvable, 10–15 givens [mean $11.7$ solvable vs. $12.2$ unsolvable]):** Pre-round diagnostic inspection of the Round-1/2 test set (reported in the Round-3 spec, `overnight-followups-round-3.md`, rather than in `RESULTS.md` / `RESULTS-R3.md`) found that $93.7\%$ (`1,071 / 1,143`) of unsolvable boards had a **Depth-1 contradiction** (an empty cell with 0 legal digits or a unit with 0 places for a digit directly from the givens, where one-pass `P0` predicted `mean P0 = 0.0004`), and $77.9\%$ of solvable boards had zero dead ends under greedy MRV. Round 3 filtered corruptions (~$220\text{k}$ candidates per split) to require **`prop_depth >= 3`** (no contradiction in 1 or 2 rounds of naked + hidden singles; test depths: $392$ at depth 3, $69$ at depth 4, $8$ at depth 5, and $31$ at depth 999 requiring branching) and filtered solvable boards to require a random-MRV dead-end rate $\ge 0.25$ (test mean $0.39$).

### 1.2 Models, Training Arms, and Heads

Both experiments use pre-LayerNorm Transformer encoders ($d = 128$, 4 attention heads, feed-forward dimension $256$ in `E-CA` and $512$ in `E-SUD`, $\sim 0.8\text{M}$ parameters at $L = 4$) with learned token and position embeddings and zero domain geometry. Training uses outcomes only (exact posteriors and rule checkers are never seen during training or hyperparameter fitting):
- In `E-CA`, reveal count is drawn $k \sim \mathrm{Uniform}\{1, \dots, N-1\}$. Two training arms (3 seeds each) ran in Round 1 (`L = 4`): **`T1`** (standard any-order masked binary cross-entropy) and **`T2`** (`rbtd8`, 8-level nested reveal-chain temporal-difference bootstrapping). Round 2 (`R2a`) tested $L = 1$, and Round 3 tested $L \in \{2, 3, 4\}$ (`T1`, 3 seeds each, 18 runs total) gated by `R3-D0`. After backbone early stopping on validation outcome log-loss, the backbone is frozen and two heads are trained on the calibration split:
  1. **Stiffness head (`Mechanism A`):** A 3-layer MLP $\hat{s}(i \mid S)$ predicting realized held-out surprise $-\log q_\theta(x_i \mid x_S)$, mapped to baseline-subtracted precision $\beta_{\text{int}}(i \mid S) = \max(0, (\hat{s}(i \mid S) + 0.01)^{-1} - (\ln 2 + 0.01)^{-1})$.
  2. **Key head (`P5`):** A unit-norm vector $k(i \mid S) \in \mathbb{S}^{15}$. In Round 1, $k(i \mid S) = \mathrm{normalize}(W_K [\bar{e}(i \mid S) ; h_i])$ where $\bar{e}(i \mid S) = \sum_{j \in S} \alpha_{i \to j} \mathrm{pos}(j)$ is token $i$'s last-layer attention-weighted source position summary. In Rounds 2 and 3, after diagnosing that LayerNormed $h_i$ had $20{,}000\times$ the coordinate variance of $\bar{e}(i \mid S)$, $h_i$ is removed and $k_{\text{v2}}(i \mid S) = \mathrm{normalize}(W_2 \, \mathrm{LayerNorm}(\bar{e}_{S \setminus E_0}(i \mid S)))$.
- In `E-SUD`, the 4-layer model takes 37 homogeneous tokens: 36 cell tokens (MASK or digit $1\dots 6$) plus 1 binary `Solvable` token (MASK, $0$, or $1$). Round 1 trained for $30{,}000$ steps; Round 1b (and all Round-2 and Round-3 evaluations, with zero retraining) used $150{,}000$-step checkpoints.

### 1.3 Inference Search Chains and Aggregators

At test time, given initial evidence $E$, each selector runs $K = 16$ sequential materialization chains. At step $t = 1 \dots M$ ($M \in \{0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$ in `E-CA`; full board completion of $M \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16\}$ chains in `E-SUD`), the chain chooses an unrevealed cell $z_t$, samples $z_t \sim q_\theta(z_t \mid S_{t-1})$, and updates $S_t = S_{t-1} \cup \{z_t\}$:
- **`P0` (One-Pass Direct):** $q_\theta(Y \mid E)$, 1 forward pass.
- **`P1` / `P4-Rand` (Random Order):** Uniform random unrevealed cell $z_t$.
- **`P2-Stiff` (Stiffness-First, `E-CA`):** Sample $z_t \propto \exp(\tau \beta_{\text{int}}(z \mid S_{t-1}))$ ($\tau = 1$ in Round 1 and `R2b`; $\tau = 5$ in `R2a` and Round 3).
- **`P3-Conf` (Confidence-First):** Argmax of $|q_\theta(z \mid S_{t-1}) - 0.5|$ in `E-CA`, or lowest predictive entropy $H(q_\theta(z \mid S_{t-1}))$ in `E-SUD`.
- **`P2-VoI` (Stiff-Gated VoI):** Among the 6 unrevealed cells with highest $\beta_{\text{int}}$ (or lowest entropy in `E-SUD`), pick $z_t$ maximizing expected squared belief change $\mathbb{E}_{z_t}[\|\mu(Y \mid S_{t-1}, z_t) - \mu(Y \mid S_{t-1})\|^2]$ ($1 + 12 = 13$ passes/step in `E-CA`; $1 + 6 \times 6 = 37$ passes/step in `E-SUD`).
- **`P2-VoI-Unc` (`voiu`, Rounds 2–3 `E-CA`):** Same expected squared change objective, but gated to the 6 unrevealed cells with highest predictive variance $q(1 - q)$.
- **`P6-Hybrid` (`hyb`, Round 3 `E-CA`):** Materialize the lowest-entropy unrevealed cell while any unrevealed cell has $H(q_\theta(z \mid S_{t-1})) < 0.20$ nats ($p_{\max} > 0.95$), else branch with `voiu`.

In `E-CA`, six pooling rules over the $K = 16$ chain claims $q_r = q_\theta(Y \mid S_M^{(r)})$ are compared:
1. **`mix` (Plain Mixture):** $\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=1}^K q_r$.
2. **`g0` (Plain $\gamma = 0$ Log-Opinion Pool):** $\sigma(\frac{1}{K}\sum_{r=1}^K \mathrm{logit}(q_r))$.
3. **`g1` ($\gamma = 1$ Unnormalized Product):** $\sigma(\mathrm{logit}(p_0) + \sum_{r=1}^K (\mathrm{logit}(q_r) - \mathrm{logit}(p_0)))$.
4. **`lw` (Round-1 Prior-Corrected Stiffness Pool):** $\sigma(\mathrm{logit}(p_0) + u \sum_{r=1}^K \beta_{\text{int}, r}(\mathrm{logit}(q_r) - \mathrm{logit}(p_0)))$, where $p_0 = q_\theta(Y \mid \varnothing)$ and $u \ge 0$ is fitted on validation outcomes.
5. **`dd` (`P5` Gram-Deduplicated Pool):** $\sigma(\mathrm{logit}(p_0) + u \sum_{r=1}^K \frac{\beta_{\text{int}, r}}{c_r}(\mathrm{logit}(q_r) - \mathrm{logit}(p_0)))$ with crowding count $c_r = \sum_{b=1}^K \max(0, \langle k_r, k_b \rangle)$.
6. **`sw_mix` (Rounds 2–3 One-Pass-Anchored Stiffness Pool):** $\frac{\lambda_0 P_0(Y) + \sum_{r=1}^K w_r q_r}{\lambda_0 + \sum_{r=1}^K w_r}$ with $w_r = \beta_{\text{int}}(Y \mid S_M^{(r)})^\eta$ and $(\lambda_0, \eta)$ fitted on validation outcomes (matching `E-SUD`'s cell estimator where $w_r = q_\theta(\text{Solvable}=1 \mid \text{completed board})$).

In `E-SUD`, a completed chain $r$ is accepted as a witness if the model's own masked `Solvable` check satisfies $q_\theta(\text{Solvable}=1 \mid Z^{(r)}) \ge 0.5$. If any of the first $M$ chains finds a witness, $\hat{P}(\text{Solvable}=1) = \max(p_0, \max_{r \le M} \text{check}_r)$. If all $M$ chains are refuted, three null-search updates are compared using the cumulative mass of distinct refuted completions—either proposal mass $C_M^{\text{prop}} = \min(1 - 10^{-9}, \sum_{z \in \text{refuted}} q_\sigma(z))$ (Rounds 1–2) or untempered believed mass $C_M^{\text{bel}} = \min(1 - 10^{-9}, \sum_{z \in \text{refuted}} P_{\theta, \tau=1}(z \mid \text{board}, H_1))$ (Round 3):
- **`witness-only`:** Ignores null search and returns $p_0$.
- **`null` (Round 1 Unfloored / Rounds 2–3 Recall-Floored):** $\sigma(\mathrm{logit}(p_0) + \log[(1 - \eta_{\max}) + \eta_{\max}(1 - C_M)^\theta])$, with $\eta_{\max} = 1$ in Round 1 and $(\theta, \eta_{\max})$ fitted on validation outcomes in Rounds 2–3.
- **`count` (Control):** Replaces $(1 - C_M)^\theta$ with $\exp(-\kappa M)$.

### 2.2 Stiffness Discovery (`D1`) and Epistemic vs. Aleatoric Stiffness (`R3-D3`)

Across all 4-layer `E-CA` runs, the learned stiffness head $\beta_{\text{int}}(i \mid S)$ strongly ranks 2,000 held-out $(i, S)$ pairs by their exact conditional information gain $\ln 2 - H^*(X_i \mid X_S = x_S)$: Spearman $\rho = 0.9484 \, [0.9413, 0.9556]$ (`r110-T1`), $0.9454 \, [0.9316, 0.9593]$ (`r110-T2`), $0.9232 \, [0.8954, 0.9511]$ (`r90-T1`), and $0.9249 \, [0.9021, 0.9477]$ (`r90-T2`) on $8\times 6$ in Round 1, and $\rho = 0.9254 \, [0.8885, 0.9622]$ (`r110`) and $0.8897 \, [0.8740, 0.9053]$ (`r90`) on the zero-overlap $12\times 10$ grid in Round 3. On the expected-entropy version (500 pairs $\times$ 8 evidence draws), Spearman $\rho$ is $0.952$ (`r110-T1`) and $0.936$ (`r90-T1`). For classifying whether $S$ contains a valid local Markov blanket of cell $i$ (its 3 past parents or a child plus its other 2 parents), $\beta_{\text{int}}$ achieves AUC $0.8708 \, [0.8663, 0.8752]$ (`r110-T1`), $0.8692$ (`r110-T2`), $0.8476$ (`r90-T1`), and $0.8567$ (`r90-T2`) at $8\times 6$ (and $0.8455$ on `r110`, $0.8165$ on `r90` at $12\times 10$). The Round-1 audit (`AUDIT.md` Finding M4) noted that $6.3\%$ of Rule-90 and $11.5\%$ of Rule-110 positive labels in the pre-registered blanket definition came from child triples whose rule output does not depend on $i$; under a rule-aware label, `r90-T1` AUC rises from $0.8476$ to $0.8502$.

However, **neither Mechanism A nor 1-step self-consistency extracts a stiffness signal beyond the network's own calibrated one-pass predictive entropy**:
- In Round 1 ($8\times 6$), ranking the same 2,000 pairs directly by the network's own claimed information gain $\ln 2 - H(q_\theta(X_i \mid x_S))$ yields identical Spearman correlations with $\ln 2 - H^*$ ($0.9487$ on `r110-T1`, $0.9459$ on `r110-T2`, $0.9338$ on `r90-T1`, and $0.9321$ on `r90-T2`). Moreover, because $42.8\text{--}48.7\%$ of non-blanket pairs on the 48-cell grid are also nearly determined ($H^* < 0.1$ nats) through multi-step lightcones, even the exact information gain $\ln 2 - H^*$ achieves only $0.8703$ (`r110`) and $0.8869$ (`r90`) AUC on the binary blanket label.
- In Round 3 (`R3-D3`, zero-overlap $12\times 10$, where one-pass `P0` has a large multi-step error), we directly tested whether $\beta_{\text{int}}(i \mid S)$, plain confidence $\ln 2 - H(q_\theta(X_i \mid x_S))$, or 1-step self-consistency $\beta_{\text{TD}}(i \mid S)$ predicts **low epistemic error** $-\mathrm{KL}(P^*(X_i \mid x_S) \,\|\, q_\theta(X_i \mid x_S))$. On Rule 110, all three tied within $0.003$ Spearman: $\beta_{\text{int}}$ **`0.700 [0.648, 0.752]`**, plain confidence **`0.697 [0.649, 0.746]`**, and 1-step self-consistency **`0.697 [0.650, 0.743]`** (and on Rule 90: `0.385 / 0.379 / 0.342`). Within matched predictive-entropy strata, correlations dropped to `0.172 / 0.201 / 0.132` on Rule 110 (`0.175 / 0.166 / -0.008` on Rule 90), with plain confidence slightly ahead on Rule 110. Because expected outcome log-loss decomposes as $\mathbb{E}_{P^*}[-\log q_\theta] = H(P^*) + \mathrm{KL}(P^* \| q_\theta)$ and is dominated by aleatoric entropy $H(P^*)$, a proper-loss-trained network's own entropy $H(q_\theta)$ already captures what Mechanism A learns.

Three findings stood out from Rounds 1 and 2 on $8\times 6$:
1. **Why Round-1's unanchored `lw` pool failed, and how `sw_mix` fixed it:** In Round 1, `lw` multiplied $(\mathrm{logit}(q_r) - \mathrm{logit}(p_0))$ by unnormalized $\beta_{\text{int}, r} \in [0, 98.6]$ with a single global scalar $u$ across all families. On moderate-confidence `F` queries ($\beta_{\text{int}} \approx 0.2$), $16 u \beta_{\text{int}} \approx 0.04 \ll 1$, shrinking `P0` at $M = 0$ toward $0.50$ and inflating `F` KL from `0.0245` to `0.2128`. In Round 2 (`R2b`), anchoring the normalized stiffness-weighted pool at $P_0(Y)$ (`sw_mix`) guarantees exact agreement at $M = 0$ and turns **14 of 20** selector $\times$ family cells at $M = 16$ into statistically significant improvements over `P0` (compared to only random-order bridges and `voiu` on `B1` under plain `mix`).
2. **Why random order beat stiffness-first on 4-layer $8\times 6$ CA bridges, and why variance-gated `VoI` helped at $T = 6$:** On a 4-layer network over $T = 6$ rows that already computes deterministic propagation (`row 0 -> row 1` and `row 5 -> row 4`) in one forward pass, cells with high $\beta_{\text{int}}(z \mid E)$ have $q_\theta(z \mid E) \approx 0.99$ (`Var(z|E) \approx 0`). Materializing them across $K = 16$ chains produces 16 near-identical trajectories that tell the network what it already knows. Switching `VoI`'s candidate gate from the 6 stiffest cells (`voi`) to the 6 highest-variance cells $q(1-q)$ (`voiu` in `R2b`) on $T = 6$ immediately cut `B1` KL at $M = 8$ by **$31.0\% \, [14.6\%, 47.4\%]$** relative to `voi` (`0.0985 -> 0.0676` under `mix`; `0.0665` under `mix` and `0.0647` under `sw` already at $M = 1$!). However, uniform random chain sampling (`P4-Rand`) still achieved the lowest Bridge KL (`0.0470` at $M = 8$ in R1, `0.0454` at $M = 16$ in R2b) at $1/13\text{th}$ the forward passes of `VoI` (`144` vs. `1,680` passes at $M = 8$).
3. **1-Layer Depth-Bottleneck Test (`R2a`):** Reducing the `E-CA` transformer to 1 layer opened a large long-range gap (`r110` test `P0` KL: `F` `0.3346`, `B1` `0.4464`, `B2` `0.2637`), and on `F` under `sw_mix`, `P2-Stiff` cut KL by `23.7% [8.7%, 38.7%]` (`0.3346 -> 0.2557`) and significantly beat `P4-Rand` (`0.3215`, difference `+0.0658 [0.0494, 0.0822]` nats). However, 1 layer was too shallow to learn the 1-step CA rule accurately (`S1` KL `0.0370` on `r110`, `0.0765` on `r90`), so errors compounded when chaining across rows on bridges (`R2-D2a` and `R2-D2b` failed).

### 2.4 Key-Value Insight Fingerprinting and Redundancy Deduplication (`D3a`, `D3b`, `R2-D3a`, and Round 3)

In Round 1 (`D3a`), when 5 conditioning sets $S_1, \dots, S_5$ share the target's 3 past parents plus 3 random far-past distractors (making them conditionally redundant given the parents), un-deduplicated scalar pooling (`lw`) shifts the pooled logit by `159.6` (`r110-T1`) and `63.6` (`r90-T1`) from $m=1$ to $m=5$. Gram crowding (`dd`, `P5`) assigns redundant views near-identical keys ($\cos = 0.9927$ on `r110-T1`, $0.9911$ on `r90-T1`), cutting the logit drift to `6.85` (ratio **`0.0433`**, bar $< 0.20$) on `r110-T1` and `1.10` (ratio **`0.0169`**) on `r90-T1`.

However, in Round 1 (`D3b`), the key head concatenated $\bar{e}(i \mid S)$ (`std` $\approx 0.007$) with LayerNormed $h_i$ (`std` $= 1.0$). Because $h_i$ dominated the norm by $20{,}000:1$ in variance, forward and backward chains predicting the same target cell $i$ also had high key cosine (`0.950` on `B1`, `0.946` on `B2` for `r110-T1`). Removing $h_i$ in Round 2 (`R2b`, `R2-D3a`) and Round 3 resolved this:
- At $W = 8, T = 6$ (Round 2 `R2-D3a`), forward-vs-backward key cosine on Rule-110 dropped from `0.950` to **`-0.0957 [-0.7060, 0.5146]`** on `B1` and **`+0.0658 [-0.5689, 0.7005]`** on `B2` (passing the $\le 0.35$ bar), redundant-view key cosine remained **`0.9077 [0.8021, 1.0134]`** (bar $\ge 0.85$) with $m = 5$ drift ratio **`0.0517 [0.0293, 0.0741]`** (bar $< 0.20$), and Gram-crowded pooling of 1 forward + 1 backward chain beat both single chains on `B1` (`0.0892` vs. `0.1179` fwd / `0.1267` bwd) and `B2` (`0.0897` vs. `0.1204` fwd / `0.1367` bwd).
- At $W = 12, T = 10$ (Round 3), the $h_i$-free key head achieved redundant-view cosine **`0.9856 [0.9638, 1.0074]`** and $m = 5$ drift ratio **`0.0274 [0.0143, 0.0404]`** on Rule 110 (`0.9615` and `0.0565` on Rule 90), with forward-vs-backward chain cosine **`0.2529`** (`B1`) and **`0.2070`** (`B2`) on Rule 110 and **`-0.3998`** (`B1`) and **`-0.2970`** (`B2`) on Rule 90. (In Round 3's 16-chain search, however, because `dd` still uses the unanchored prior-corrected log-odds form rather than `sw_mix`'s $P_0$ anchor, `dd` lagged behind `sw_mix` on bridges.)

### 2.5 Computation as Evidence: Sudoku Null-Search Across Rounds 1, 1b, 2, and 3 (`D4`, `R2-D1`, `R3-D1`)

In `E-SUD`, the model is never given an external Sudoku verifier; it judges completion validity using its own `Solvable` token.
- **Failure when the local verifier is unlearned (Round 1, 30k steps):** In Seeds 0 and 1 (30k steps), the model's `Solvable` check had a `39–83%` false-positive rate on invalid completions. Search actively manufactured false witnesses on unsolvable boards, degrading unsolvable-board log-loss from `0.391` (`P0`) to `2.604` (`witness-only`) and `2.495` (`null`).
- **Success once the local verifier is learned, and the Round-2 recall floor (150k steps, Standard Split):** At 150k steps, all 3 seeds achieved $0\%$ false-positive witness rate on unsolvable boards. In Round 1b (unfloored $(1 - C_M)^\theta$), `P1` and `P3` passed `D4` with `98.1–100.0%` log-loss cuts, but `P2` failed `D4(a)`-solvable because 1 of 857 solvable boards in Seed 1 missed a witness in 8 chains and was pushed by $\theta = 79.43$ to $p = 10^{-6}$ (`13.82` nats loss). ## 3. What the Results Say About Kelvin's Thesis

1. **Are stiff local arcs discoverable without topology supervision? Yes ($0.89\text{--}0.95$ Spearman with exact information gain, $0.82\text{--}0.87$ blanket AUC [$0.8165\text{--}0.8708$ across all rounds and rules])—and the network's own calibrated predictive entropy already contains the full signal.** Across both $8\times 6$ and zero-overlap $12\times 10$ CA grids, an any-order transformer trained only on permuted outcomes identifies which conditioning sets $S \to i$ pin down target $i$. However, because expected outcome log-loss is $\mathbb{E}_{P^*}[-\log q_\theta] = H(P^*) + \mathrm{KL}(P^* \| q_\theta)$, a proper-loss-trained network's predictive confidence $\ln 2 - H(q_\theta)$ ties both Mechanism A's surprise head $\beta_{\text{int}}$ and 1-step self-consistency at predicting both information gain ($0.93\text{--}0.95$) and epistemic error $-\mathrm{KL}(P^* \| q_\theta)$ (`0.700` vs. `0.697` vs. `0.697` in `R3-D3`).
2. **When does sequential search through materialized variables beat one-pass inference? In the window between local-law competence (`S1` KL $\le 0.01$) and single-pass saturation—and the right policy depends on the geometry of the remaining gap:**
   - **Below local-law competence (`E-SUD` at 30k steps; `E-CA` 1-layer net in `R2a`):** When 1-step conditionals are inaccurate (`S1` KL $> 0.03$), chaining them compounds errors and manufactures false witnesses (`39–83%` false-positive rate in 30k-step Sudoku).
   - **Above single-pass saturation (`E-CA` $8\times 6$ Rule 90 and Rule-110 Forward in Round 1):** When $2^8 = 256$ initial states allow the 4-layer network to memorize attractors (`82%` train/test overlap, `P0` KL `0.006–0.025`), materializing already-stiff variables is redundant.
   - **Multi-step deterministic propagation beyond the 1-pass horizon (`E-SUD` at 150k steps; `E-CA` Round-3 zero-overlap $12\times 10$ Forward `F`):**
     - When every variable is globally coupled to the query (as in $6\times 6$ Sudoku), **greedy stiffness-first (`P3` confidence-first) wins at every $M$** (`-46.3%` cell KL, beating both `VoI` and random order at 1 pass/step).
     - When the grid is spatially wide ($12\times 10$ CA, $120$ cells) and the rollout budget $M = 16$ is smaller than the number of stiff frontier cells ($>24$ cells in rows 1–2), undirected `stiff`/`conf` wastes steps outside the target's lightcone (`-13%` to `-15%` KL, still beating `rand` by `+15%` to `+17%`), whereas **stiff-gated `VoI` (`voi`) routes stiff materializations inside the target's lightcone and cuts Forward KL by `31.0%`** on Rule 110 (`-75.3%` on Rule 90).
   - **Multimodal bridge uncertainty (`E-CA` Rule-110 Bridges `B1, B2`):** Here **random-order chains (`P4-Rand`) consistently win** (`27–51%` on `T1 B1`, `25–43%` on `T1 B2`, `42–44%` on `T2 B1`, `28–32%` on `T2 B2` at $8\times 6$; `24%` on `B1` and `23%` on `B2` at $12\times 10$). Variance-gated `voiu` helps on small $8\times 6$ bridges (`-31%` on `B1` where all unrevealed cells are near the boundary), but fails on $12\times 10$ bridges because the highest-variance cells are ungrounded deep-interior cells ($q \approx 0.50$).
   - **Aggregation:** Unanchored log-linear pooling (`lw`) distorts calibration at $M=0$; **one-pass-anchored convex pooling (`sw_mix` / `cell_est`)** preserves $M=0$ identically and makes search reliably improve over `P0`.
3. **Do key-value fingerprints deduplicate redundant arcs? Yes (`94–98%` drift reduction across Rounds 1–3), provided keys are built from source-attention rather than target hidden states.** Removing LayerNormed $h_i$ drops forward-vs-backward cosine from `0.95` to `-0.10 / +0.07` ($8\times 6$) and `0.25 / 0.21` (`r110`) or `-0.40 / -0.30` (`r90`) ($12\times 10$) while keeping redundant-view cosine at `0.91–0.99`.
4. **Does "searched and found nothing" work as calibrated negative evidence? Yes—cutting `Solvable` log-loss by `99.4–99.6%` even on deep-contradiction (`Depth >= 3`) unsolvable boards that completely fool the 4-layer network in one pass (`1.84` nats $\to 0.006\text{--}0.012$ nats).** Three lessons emerged: (a) the internal verifier must have near-zero false positives (150k steps), (b) the likelihood requires a recall floor $1 - \eta_{\max} > 0$, and (c) untempered believed mass $C_M^{\text{bel}}$ strongly ranks unresolved boards (`AUC 0.87–0.93` on `P3` vs. `0.50` by construction for count $M$), but converting $C_M^{\text{bel}}$ into log-loss requires a log-probability / length-normalized parameterization rather than $(1 - \prod_t q_t)^\theta$.

## 5. Next Experiment

Round 3 isolated the exact bottleneck on both tracks:
1. **Target-Directed Frontier Routing + Anchored Gram Pooling in `E-CA`:** On zero-overlap $12\times 10$ CA, undirected `stiff`/`conf` (`-13%` to `-15%`) and `P6-Hybrid` (branch share `0.000`, 1 branch step on `r110` and a few dozen on `r90 F`) spent nearly all $M = 16$ steps on stiff cells outside the target's lightcone, whereas `voi` (`-31%` on `r110 F`, `-75%` on `r90 F`) routed stiff steps inside the target's lightcone at $13\times$ the pass cost. Replacing `VoI`'s 12-pass forward lookahead with **attention-routed stiffness**—selecting unrevealed cells maximizing $\alpha_{Y \to z} \cdot (\ln 2 - H(q_\theta(z \mid S_t)))$ (cells that are both stiff *and* attended to by the query $Y$, at **1 pass/step**)—and combining `sw_mix`'s one-pass anchor $P_0(Y)$ with $k_{\text{v2}}$ Gram crowding (`sw_dd`) directly tests whether 1-pass-per-step search can match `VoI`'s `-31%` to `-75%` forward cuts while keeping `rand`'s bridge gains.
2. **Length-Normalized Log-Mass Likelihood for Null Search in `E-SUD`:** Because $-C_M^{\text{bel}}$ already achieves `0.87–0.93` AUC on unresolved boards (`R3-D1c` Part 1) while $(1 - \prod_{t=1}^L q_t)^\theta$ suffers from 22-step product underflow (`R3-D1c` Part 2), replacing $(1 - C_M^{\text{bel}})^\theta$ with the length-normalized geometric-mean survival form $(1 - \sum_{r} (P_{\theta, \tau=1}(z^{(r)}))^{1/L})^\theta$ on the existing saved Round-3 chain arrays (`results/r3/chains/`, $\$0$ GPU cost) directly tests whether proper trajectory-length scaling converts $C_M^{\text{bel}}$'s `0.87–0.93` ranking AUC into a log-loss improvement over chain count.
