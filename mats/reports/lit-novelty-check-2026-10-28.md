Model: Sonnet 5.5 (claude-sonnet-5-5). Read-only; sources fetched via WebFetch/WebSearch on 2026-10-28 UTC. I did not execute any downloaded content.

Our three claims:
- C1: in-context dedup in a trained set-style transformer/PFN vs an exact Bayes target, with copy-vs-fresh controls.
- C2: policy-invariance test at matched sufficient statistics (varying an exogenous sampling policy).
- C3: Bayes-correctness (not accuracy) at N >> N_train for count-dependent targets.

## Conclusions

- Neither named threat covers our claims. 2610.06192 is the nearest on C1 (exact-Bayes copy/fresh controls) but evaluates prompted LLMs, so "trained set-style transformers or PFNs doing dedup against an exact Bayes target" is not done there. 2610.16801 is the nearest on mechanism (production PFNs, repetition, count channel) but never scores a model against exact Bayes and leaves the draw-once pretraining test as future work. 2610.28977 is the nearest on C2 but varies data arrangements at matched sufficient statistics, not an exogenous policy, and stays within the training horizon.
- C3 (Bayes-correctness at N >> N_train for count-dependent targets): closest evidence is Teh et al. (<=4x, regret vs oracle, 8x loses to NPMLE in a figure note), Cannella et al. (theory) and Zhu et al. (K beyond training degrades). Nobody tests this with copies, and nobody tests beyond roughly 8x.
- Yan et al. 2023 is irrelevant to the novelty question.
- Corrections to the sweep's wording: Gao is not a trained model study (the sweep's A.1/A.2 rows already say LLM aggregators; fine); "GullibleBench" naming unconfirmed; Liu's draw-once test is explicitly future work (matches the sweep).
