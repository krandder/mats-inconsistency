1. **Major: the exploration does not establish an information limit or an accuracy ceiling.** P3.1 says “no readout of it can individualise the tail beyond what three cumulants allow” and “gets close to what four exact moments allow.” K3f retains a radial fourth-order component; weak empirical correlation does not prove that no readout can exploit it. The 0.61 result uses one particular readout of **estimated** moments from 2^26 paths, not exact moments or an optimum over four-moment methods. The reproduced exploration supports the performance comparisons, not these universal conclusions (`claims3_checks/explore.txt`; ARC `kprop_harmonic.py:get_r_x`). **Fix:** replace the first inference with “K3f retains only a radial fourth-order component; on these development nets its excess-kurtosis estimates correlate weakly with Monte Carlo estimates.” Replace the ceiling sentence with “RAD4 scores 0.55 using K3fa moments and 0.61 using 2^26-path Monte Carlo moments on these development events; this does not establish the best accuracy achievable from four moments.” Describe the proposed mechanism as an interpretation, not an isolated causal finding.

2. **Minor: the summary mislabels the correlation being measured.** Lines 778–790 call 0.50 versus 0.21 correlations of the “fourth cumulant.” They are correlations of **excess kurtosis**, κ4/κ2², pooled over 512 outputs from nets 100–101. Raw κ4 correlations at depth 8 are **0.849 versus 0.391**. The stated standardized correlations reproduce as 0.498 versus 0.212; at depth 4, 0.633 versus 0.256 (`independent.txt`, `MOMENTS` rows). **Fix:** use “excess kurtosis (standardized fourth cumulant)” consistently and identify the two development nets; use the radial-component wording above instead of “nearly every output the same fourth cumulant.”

3. **Minor: make the uncertainty scope explicit in Part 3.** `p3_score.py` bootstraps nets while holding estimated truth probabilities and validation masks fixed. Its intervals do not propagate the eight-run truth uncertainty; secondary intervals are pointwise. Filtering provisional events does not make the remaining references exact. This is especially relevant at depth 8, 1e-12: **59/160 provisional**, median relative SE **0.215**. **Fix:** add “CIs resample nets conditional on the splitting references; they omit reference-estimation uncertainty. Secondary intervals are pointwise. ‘Validated’ means passing the stated diagnostics.” Print numeric median-error intervals alongside the better-than-zero flags. This caveat does **not** overturn the result: independent resampling of truth runs and nets gives a primary sensitivity interval **[0.238, 0.450]**, and validated-only scoring passes (`independent.txt`). This sensitivity check does not test shared splitting bias.

4. **Minor: the reported P3A cost omits the final readout.** `p3_score.py:costs` charges only `flops_shared + flops_moments`, whereas the prereg also charges approximately 1e5 FLOPs/event for RAD4. `deduce.py` models **101,752 FLOPs/event**, before small setup costs: approximately **4.07e6** for the 40 scored events per net. This is negligible here and changes none of the rounded cost comparisons, but “for all thresholds” is not literally constant cost. **Fix:** report “1.99e11 FLOPs for propagation and moments, plus approximately 1.02e5 per output-threshold event”; make the analogous depth-4 change and include this term in the scorer. The radial term is analytically estimated, not measured by ARC’s counter.

The prereg itself correctly discloses that sequence and says no method had been scored. **Fix:** say “No candidate was scored on nets 44–63 before preregistration; threshold and reference generation had already begun under the fixed CPU protocol.” Retain the disclosure that Part 2 evaluation results, including this variant’s post-hoc performance, were already known.
