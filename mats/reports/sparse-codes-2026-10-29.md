## Summary

**Scope.**
- Single random nets: weight seed 65, plus seed 66 as a replicate at width 64 only.
- Widths 64-256, fixed depth 32, noise sigma = 0.01 relative to each layer's RMS.
- These are measurements of the models we trained and tested, not impossibility results. Fixed depth does not test
  M1's regime, where depth grows with width.

**Unit.** A noise unit is the per-coordinate reconstruction error divided by the noise variance; below 1 is below the
noise. At width 256, one noise unit is 185 bits per layer of path KL.

1. **Sparse structure at the noise precision: we found none beyond the ReLU gate pattern.**
   - The exact sparse code of a ReLU layer is the neuron code: on average 124-133 of 256 units are active.
   - KT1 (pre-registered) asks for L0 <= n/4. No TopK dictionary up to 16x width reached the noise in any ARC or
     chaotic-tanh cell. The best errors, ARC He-ReLU, layers 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 24 and 31, in noise units:
     - 465, 356, 166, 56, 17, 3.7 and 4.2 at n = 256;
     - 484, 392, 144, 27, 3.3, 1.8 and 1.06 at n = 128 (F <= 8n, no training controls);
     - 521, 411, 124, 15, 2.2, 1.05 and 1.25 at n = 64, seed 65.
   - The deep cells at n = 64 and 128 are within 1.05-3.3 noise units of the threshold. At n = 64, doubling the
     training cut their error by up to 35-40%; n = 128 had no training controls. For these cells convergence is
     unresolved.
   - At sigma = 0.001 (n = 64) the same cells sit about 100x farther from the noise: 38-52,000 noise units.
   - Chaotic tanh: 1,200-1,900 noise units at every layer.
   - Ordered tanh: from layer 4 a code with L0 <= n/4 is below the noise, and from layer 8 the mean alone is.
   - At L0 = n/2, a zero-origin SAE initialised at the neuron basis recovers the neuron code: 0.8 noise units at
     layer 1, n = 256.
   - In the collapsed deep layers, a sparse code beats PCA at equal L0 by about 3x (n = 256, k = 64, layer 24: 4.5
     against 14.8).
   - A larger dictionary barely helps: at L0 = n/4, the error at 16n is 0.7-1.1 times the error at n.
2. **The dependence risk, as Kelvin posed it, materialises: weak, dense pairwise couplings that matter in sum.**
   Measured on n = 256 SAEs (mean origin, F >= 4n, k <= n/4) and on the decoded reconstruction D f.
   - Pairwise, the features are nearly independent:
     - median on/off |phi| 0.009-0.024, against 0.04-0.06 between the network's own ReLU gates;
     - RMS amplitude correlation 0.008-0.028.
   - In sum, the off-diagonal covariances hold 36-69% of the reconstruction's variance, because the atoms of an
     overcomplete dictionary overlap.
   - Treating the features as independent (exact marginals, no couplings) moves the next layer's per-neuron variances
     by a median 35-69%.
     - The coordinator's estimate for random loadings, sqrt(2) rho_rms, predicts only 1-4%.
     - The coordinator's conclusion stands: any feature family must carry the full covariance.
   - Beyond the covariance, new test E5 (n = 64 with two seeds, plus n = 128 and n = 256): we replaced the exact latent
     by a Gaussian copula over the features' empirical marginals, recoloured to the state's mean and covariance
     (held-out covariance mismatch 0.4-3.1%).
     - Pushed to depth 32, this fitted copula is 2.3-34x better than one Gaussian, median about 5x over 12 cells. The
       edge shrinks with width: 2.3-2.7x at n = 256.
     - The exact latent's error is near or below our sampling floor (|nMSE| <= 3.6e-6). The Gaussian's is 1.7e-4 to
       1.3e-3.
     - So, with this construction, most of what the sparse latent buys lies beyond its marginals and covariance.
     - KT6 does not fire: 0 of 9 cells fire, because every copula beat the Gaussian by more than 2x. At n = 256 the
       margin is small (2.3-2.7x).
3. **If its law is given, a sparse latent carries the higher-order structure the next layer needs (oracle).**
   - One step (E2): exact SAE latent, plus a Gaussian residual with full covariance. At n = 256, k = 16 = n/16,
     layers 8-31, the next layer's mean error is 0.45-2.4e-6. One Gaussian (one step of K=2) gives 2.3-5.1e-5.
   - At equal L0, the sparse latent beats the PCA latent in 19 of 24 cells and the Gaussian in 12 of 12. KT2 and KT3
     do not fire. The 5 losses are deep layers at n = 64 and 128, where both latents are already below 1e-6.
   - Much of the edge at equal L0 is support information: up to 90-240 bits per sample at n = 256, k <= n/4 (an upper
     bound). Bracketing by latent size in bits (descriptive):
     - at n = 64, PCA latents of about the same size are as good or 2-5x better;
     - at n = 256, the sparse latents beat the linear PCA latents of about the same size by 1.4-10x.
   - Pushed from layer 24 through the real layers, the exact k = 16 latent gives 3.2e-6 at depth 32 (n = 256). ARC's
     K3f, end to end on the same net, gives 3.2e-4. KT4 does not fire. But the hard part is that latent's law; E4 and
     E5 test it.
   - Given the latent, the residual is not Gaussian neuron by neuron: median excess kurtosis 0.3-5.
   - What the next layer sees of it is closer to Gaussian. Projected by W_{l+1} and conditioned, the median excess
     kurtosis is 0.07-0.27 at layers 1-8 and 0.12-0.49 at layers 16-31 (n = 256), and up to 1.2 at n = 64.
4. **Transcoders did not give a usable propagation rule (KT5 fires).**
   - No transcoder reached the noise. The best was F = 4n, L0 = n/2: median 1.5 noise units per layer at n = 64 and
     5.4 at n = 256. The path KL over layers 1-31 was 5.5e3 and 8.4e4 bits.
   - On the input law they were trained on, chains look good: their biases memorise that law's per-layer means.
   - On a shifted input law they never saw (it moves E[a_L] by nMSE 0.04-1.2), we ran a deductive feature-level
     closure carrying the full feature covariance. At depth 8 it is worse than neuron-level K=2:
     - 1.8e-3 against 1.1e-3 at n = 64;
     - 2.6e-3 against 1.5e-4 at n = 256, where ARC's K3f gives 4.0e-6.
   - Where the error comes from depends on the width:
     - at n = 256, mostly from the chain: the closure tracks its own chain to 5e-5..8e-5 at depth 8;
     - at n = 64, the sampled chain alone beats K=2 (7.7e-4) and the closure's own error (9.3e-4 against its chain)
       makes it lose.

**What this means for M1.**
- With TopK dictionaries we found no compression beyond the gate pattern at the noise precision.
- Sparse features couple weakly, but the couplings must be carried: second order exactly, and beyond it too. A fitted
  marginals-plus-covariance copula recovers only part of the oracle latent's advantage.
- Our transcoders were far from the noise, and closures through them lost to K=2 on unseen input laws.
- The sparse latent is a good representation when its law is given. We did not find a compact way to carry or
  propagate that law. This is a research judgement on the tested models: we see no route to M1 through this family on
  random nets, but the experiments do not exclude one.

## 1. What was run

**Nets.**
- ARC's He-ReLU family, exactly as in work/arc-noisy: no bias, no LayerNorm. Noise s_l = sigma x the layer's RMS,
  from ARC's seeded pilot.
- Phase R's tanh + LayerNorm nets with bias N(0, 0.25), as in the gate census: gain 0.25 ("ordered") and 3
  ("chaotic").
- Depth 32; widths 64, 128 and 256; sigma = 0.01. ARC also at sigma = 0.001 (n = 64; n = 256 was dropped for time,
  amendment 6).
- New weight seeds: 65 for everything, 66 as a replicate (ARC, n = 64), 64 for exploration only.

**Units.** a_l is the noiseless post-activation and s its noise sd.
- An error E||a - a_hat||^2 is reported in noise units, E||a - a_hat||^2 / (n s^2).
- In bits it is E||a - a_hat||^2 / (2 s^2 ln 2), the path KL of replacing a by a_hat before the noise. At width n,
  one noise unit is 0.72 n bits per layer.

**E1, dictionaries.** TopK SAEs on 2^18 states per layer, scored on 2^15 held-out states.
- F = n, 2n, 4n, 8n, 16n; k = n/16, n/8, n/4, n/2.
- A learned origin (b_dec initialised at the mean). For ReLU nets also a zero origin, with the first n atoms
  initialised at the neuron basis (F = n, 4n).
- Least-squares refits on the active support.
- Held-out learning curves; same-seed 2x-epoch and different-seed controls at layers 8 and 24.
- Baselines: PCA-k, the neuron basis (top-k neurons), and the mean alone.

**E2, one step.** Latent-conditional Gaussians, each carrying the state's mean and full covariance up to sampling.
- G: one Gaussian (one step of K=2).
- DENSE(k): top-k PCA coordinates exact, the rest independent Gaussian.
- DENSEQ(k): the same with a residual mean quadratic in the latent.
- SPARSE(F, k): SAE reconstruction exact, residual linear-Gaussian given it.
- The latent comes from true states. Each model is pushed one layer, and two things are scored:
  - E[act] of the next layer (nMSE in ARC's units, Rao-Blackwellised, debiased, block jackknife SE);
  - the share of the next pre-activations' per-neuron 3rd and 4th cumulants reproduced.
- Fits on a FIT set, selection on SEL, verdicts on CONF.

**E3, oracle representation at a cut.** The E2 models at layer m in {4, 8, 16, 24} are pushed through the real
remaining layers to depths 8, 16 and 32. 2^20 states, common random numbers. Compared with ARC's kprop K=2 and K3f
run end to end on the same nets.

**E4, transcoder chain.**
- One transcoder per layer, h_{l-1} -> a_l: TopK training, per-feature JumpReLU thresholds at inference.
  F = n and 4n; k = n/8, n/4, n/2.
- A deductive feature-level Gaussian closure through the chain, carrying the mean and the full feature covariance:
  exact per-feature moments, 60-term Hermite/Mehler off-diagonals.
- Scored under three input laws:
  - std, the law the transcoders were trained on;
  - shift, a mean shift N(0.5 g, I);
  - aniso, eigenvalues ~1/i.

**E5, couplings beyond the covariance.**
- At cuts 8, 16 and 24, the exact sparse latent is replaced by features drawn from a fitted family:
  - SPARSECOP: a Gaussian copula over the features' empirical zero-inflated marginals, with the normal-scores
    correlation;
  - SPARSEIND: independent features;
  - NEUCOP: a neuron copula.
  Each keeps the v2 residual and is recoloured to the state's estimated mean and covariance.
- The independent product without recolouring is the analytic case of the coordinator's first retarget.

**Couplings, direct.** On the saved SAEs, for the decoded reconstruction D f:
- pairwise amplitude correlation and on/off phi;
- the share of the reconstruction's variance in off-diagonal feature covariances;
- how far dropping them moves the next layer's per-neuron variances.
For comparison, phi between the network's own ReLU gates (`scratch/gatephi.py`).

## 7. Limits

- **Seeds and depth.** One weight seed per width. Seed 66 replicates n = 64 (E1-E3, E5) and agrees in every
  direction. Nothing is replicated at n = 128 or 256, and E4 has no replicate at all. All nets have depth 32, so
  depth growing with width (M1) is not tested.
- **Oracle diagnostics.** E2, E3 and E5 give models true latents and true or fitted moments. They show what a
  description would need, not that one can be computed from the weights at a cost that beats sampling.
- **Optimisation, not existence.**
  - KT1 is about TopK SAEs trained 30 epochs on 2^18 states. The deep n = 64 cells were still improving. n = 128 had
    no training controls at all, and layers 16 and 31 had none at any width.
  - OMP on a learned dictionary, JumpReLU SAEs and more data were not tried.
- **Latent size.** Support entropy is an upper bound from per-feature entropies, and amplitude bits are a
  Gaussian-channel proxy. The bracketing comparison is descriptive.
- **E5.** One fitted copula construction. Another family with the same marginals and covariance could do better.
- **Selection.** E2 separates fitting, selection and scoring. E3 and E5 take minima over models on the scored set,
  which favours families with many candidates, and floor-level ratios are unresolved. KT4 and KT6 use these minima, as
  registered.
- **sigma.** sigma = 0.001 was run at n = 64 only (E1, E2).
- **Incomplete nets.** These did not complete because of GPU memory on a shared VM (amendment 6): ordered tanh at
  n = 64 (layers 1-4 only) and at n = 256; chaotic tanh at n = 256 (layer 1 only). The ordered-tanh statements rest on
  n = 128, the chaotic-tanh ones on n = 64 and 128.

## 8. Changes after the reviews

**Design critique (GPT-6, before the main runs; amendment 2).**
- Fits are separated from scoring (FIT, SEL, CONF).
- Readouts are Rao-Blackwellised, with block standard errors.
- E3 uses common random numbers.
- Zero-origin SAEs and training-length controls were added.
- The equal-parameter pairing, which does not exist for F >= n, was removed; support entropy is reported instead.
- E1-E3 are framed as oracle screening.

**Our own fixes before claims.**
- E4's memorisation leak (amendment 1).
- E4's Hermite overflow (amendment 3).
- E3's dense-latent late-binding bug (amendment 5); the rerun reproduces TRUE and G exactly.

**Claims review (GPT-6, on a draft).**
- Conclusions are restricted to the tested models. "No sparse structure" became "we found none with TopK
  dictionaries", and the M1 paragraph now says the experiments do not exclude a route (findings 1 and 10).
- E5 is described as a fitted copula whose second order matches to 0.4-3.1%. Floor-level ratios against the exact
  latent were removed (findings 2, 5 and 6).
- The E4 attribution is now split by width: at n = 64 the closure's own error makes it lose (finding 3).
- KT6 was reported as incomplete until all 9 cells were in, and an unregistered floor in its rule was removed
  (finding 4).
