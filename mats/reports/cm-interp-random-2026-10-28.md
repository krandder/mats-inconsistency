## Verdict

On random noisy LN-MLPs the forward chain works as an estimator and fails as a certifier. For the three families measured
at every width (the Gaussian that ARC's K = 2 propagates, the product code and the know-nothing reference; three weight
seeds per width), the coverage cost per layer grows linearly with width wherever neurons are non-linear, because a
divergence between joint distributions adds up small per-neuron errors: 95% lower confidence bounds for the Gaussian
description reach 18 bits (KL) and 30-31 bits (D_inf) per layer at width 256 in the critical regime, 91-92 and 125-126 in the
chaotic regime and 88 and 121-126 for ReLU. The product code trades that per-neuron term for dependence; the copula code was
measured only up to width 32, so whether it escapes the linear growth is open. On the Gaussian chain the per-layer bounds
do not grow with depth (16 layers at width 32 and 8 at width 64, critical and chaotic tanh, three seeds), which says nothing
yet about the end-to-end divergence; and at half a bit per dimension the Gaussian is almost exact, so the costs sit in the
few-bits-per-dimension regime the program targets. No coverage cost could be certified tractably (the envelope costs about 5
bits per neuron per layer), the D_inf waste is infinite for any Gaussian-tailed family, and the defensive component floors
the output tail, so every certified bound is vacuous. With an uncertified coverage estimate measured on the same chain, the
bound loses about eps (15-36 bits at widths 8-32, critical tanh) and beats sampling only below 2^-29 to 2^-51; at width 256
the chain's eps over three layers is at least 95-122 bits (critical) with 95% confidence, so a certificate on that chain
can be non-vacuous only for events that the chain itself puts below about 2^-95. As an estimator, the same Gaussian chain
with only the last layer handled exactly (which still needs a rare-event computation under q_3) is within -0.2 to +2.6
bits of the reference values at the 42 corroborated levels from 2^-10 to 2^-60 on the 11 LN networks tested, within 4 bits
at the 17 provisional levels and within 0.2 bits at width 256, but off by 1.3-11 bits for ReLU at width 16. On these nets
K = 2's own output tails are up to 47 bits too high, because LN caps the output; that is ARC's K = 2 on LN nets, not ARC's
rare-event method (factorized K = 3 on nets without LN), which we did not run. The chain's backward functions make twisted
SMC 56-1,800x cheaper than splitting down to 2^-30 to 2^-40 for critical tanh; a black-box learned psi fails beyond 2^-15
and only splitting reaches 2^-60. On ARC's metric, E[output], K = 2 run on our nets (which differ from ARC's by LN, noise
and, for tanh, a bias) shows ARC's pattern for K = 2 rather than reproducing ARC's results: 12.6-283x better than sampling
at equal FLOPs at width 256 (up to 810x for the variant without the LN mean correction), less with depth, and not at every
depth at width 16. In the surrogate-chain framing, K = 2's implied linear surrogate is cheap only in the ordered regime
(68-547 bits per layer otherwise at width 256), so Donsker-Varadhan bounds built on it are 37-26,500x wider than sampling's
confidence interval; used as a control variate it gives an unbiased estimator that overtakes K = 2 after 10^3-10^5 samples
at width 256 and beats plain sampling by 2-5x at criticality and 70-1,100x in the ordered regime (where K = 2 alone is
already very accurate), with no reliable gain for ReLU or chaotic tanh.

## Setup

Network. Input x ~ N(0, I_d). Layer l: z_l = W_l h_{l-1} with W_l iid N(0, g^2/d) and no bias; s_l = LN(act(z_l)) with LN(a)
= sqrt(d)(a - mean a)/|a - mean a| (no affine), so s_l lies on a sphere of radius sqrt(d); h_l = s_l + sigma eps_l, noise
kept on. Outputs y = W_out h_L; rare events are y_1 = w.h_L > t with |w| = 1. Regimes: tanh with gain 0.25, 0.75, 3 (chaos
factor 0.92, 1.01, 1.99 at sigma = 0.3: ordered, critical, chaotic) and ReLU with He gain (ARC's default; under LN without
bias the gain drops out and the layer is mildly chaotic, factor 1.35). Noise sigma = 0.1, 0.3, 1.0, i.e. 3.3, 1.8 and 0.5
bits per dimension; sigma = 0.3 unless stated. Widths 8 to 256, depth 4 to 16; three weight seeds for the width and depth
lower bounds, one weight seed elsewhere unless stated.

Forward chain. q_0 = N(0, I) is the input law exactly. At each layer K q_{l-1} is sampled exactly (draw from q_{l-1}, apply
the noiseless layer, add noise; no samples of the real activations after layer 1) and q_l is fitted from 2^15 draws, with the
defensive component q_l = 0.99 q~ + 0.01 N(mu, 4 Sigma). Families q~: the moment-matched Gaussian (the object ARC's cumulant
propagation carries at K = 2; "Gaussian" below); the neuron code with singleton groups (a product of the exact per-neuron
marginals; "product code"); the neuron code with one Gaussian dependence group (a Gaussian copula over the same marginals;
"copula code"); and the know-nothing reference (uniform on the LN sphere convolved with the noise). Sparse codes were not
run: random layers have no sparse structure for them to use.

How each number is obtained.
- Absolute per-layer values (d <= 32): log K q(h) by tempered SMC over the previous layer's state, checked against
  2^21-2^23-node mixtures at d = 4-8 (mean error <= 0.1 nats at K q-typical points). KL(Kq‖q) is the coverage-direction KL
  and a lower bound on the coverage cost c_l. The coverage search c is the largest log Kq/q over 96 K q-typical points,
  re-evaluated with 4x particles, so it underestimates the supremum. KL(q‖Kq) is the waste; it is over-estimated because
  log Kq is biased low at points outside K q's bulk (converged within 0.7 bits for tanh, not for ReLU and know-nothing at
  d = 32, where 4x more particles lowered it by 3-7 bits).
- Lower confidence bounds at any width, relative to a fresh copula fit q*: KL(Kq‖q) >= E_Kq[log q*/q], and
  D_inf(Kq‖q) >= tau + log Kq(log q*/q >= tau) for every tau (Markov, since E_q[q*/q] = 1). The inequalities are exact;
  their sampled versions are reported as 95% lower confidence bounds ("LCB"). For KL: a one-sided bootstrap over the sampled
  paths (2,000 resamples; approximate). For D_inf: tau is chosen on one half of the samples and Kq(T >= tau) is bounded
  below on the other half by the exact Clopper-Pearson bound, so the statement holds with probability at least 95%. Since
  D_inf >= KL, the reported D_inf bound is the larger of the two, each at 97.5%; sums over layers split the 5% (Bonferroni).
  Before the review these were a sample mean and an in-sample maximum over tau, and were called rigorous; they were
  estimates of lower bounds.
- Decoupled prediction: the sum over neurons of the 1-D divergence between each neuron's exact marginal and q's marginal.
- Rigorous upper bound on c_l (the envelope): K q(h) <= sup over |s| = sqrt(d) of N(h; s, sigma^2 I), for any q_{l-1}.

Rare-event reference values: fixed-level generalized splitting over (x, eps_1..eps_L) with pCN moves. The levels for 2^-10
... 2^-60 come from one adaptive pilot run, and the pCN scale of every level and move is frozen from a second, independent
pilot, so the estimator is unbiased (fixed levels, fixed invariant kernels). Each repeat is one run to the deepest level,
which estimates every level at once; 32 repeats of 16,384 particles at d <= 32, 24 at d = 64, 16 of 8,192 at d = 256, 10
moves per level. Checks: the bulk-twisted-SMC estimate saved at every level, brute force at 2^-10. A level is provisional
if its relative SE exceeds 0.25, the two halves of its repeats differ by more than 1 bit, or the bulk check or the
published (pre-review) value differs by more than 1 bit; this rule was fixed before the reruns. The published values used
a pCN scale adapted on each run's own particles, which the unbiasedness argument does not cover, and 8 repeats. FLOPs are
counted analytically (one forward sample costs L(2d^2 + 12d) + 2d^2; K = 2 costs about 4 d^3 per layer) and include each
estimator's setup.

The Gaussian (K = 2) description pays about one bit per layer for the LN norm constraint and residual dependence, plus a
per-neuron term set by how far each neuron's noisy marginal is from a Gaussian. The slope of the KL bound between
successive widths, in bits per neuron (seed means), is 0.00 in the ordered regime, 0.047, 0.069, 0.071 and 0.074 for
critical tanh (8-16, 16-32, 32-64, 64-256), 0.29-0.37 chaotic and 0.32-0.35 ReLU. These are the rates of the 1-D calculation
behind the predictions (0.005, 0.09, 0.44, 0.40 bits per neuron) to within a factor 1.3, the three seeds agree within a few
percent from d = 32, and the bounds reach the same rates at d = 256, so for these three regimes the linear growth belongs
to the Gaussian family and not to our estimator. The product code pays the layer's total correlation, 0.31 bits per neuron
for critical tanh (0.13 chaotic, 0.11 ReLU), and know-nothing pays everything (0.37-0.54); both grow linearly. These width
statements cover only the three families scored at every width (Gaussian, product code, know-nothing), on three weight
seeds; they say nothing about other fixed-size families. The copula code cannot be scored this way (it is the reference q*
itself) and was measured only by SMC, to d = 32 and on one seed: it removes the per-neuron term from the KL (1-3 bits from
d = 8 to 32 for critical tanh and ReLU) except in the chaotic regime (2.1, 3.4, 5.1 bits: the joint law of saturated gates
is not Gaussian-copula-shaped). It lowers the coverage cost for ReLU (13 against 29 bits at d = 32) but raises it for tanh
(12 against 7.5 critical, 31 against 21 chaotic), where its copula tails are too thin. Whether it escapes linear growth at
larger width is open.

### Depth, regime and noise
On the Gaussian chain, the per-layer lower bounds do not grow with depth (three weight seeds each). Over 16 layers at d = 32
the KL bound stays at 0.8-1.7 bits per layer for critical tanh and 6.6-9.2 for chaotic tanh, and over 8 layers at d = 64 at
3.2-4.0 and 18.9-20.5; the D_inf bounds stay at 1.1-2.5 and 11-14 bits (d = 32) and 5.6-7.3 and 30-33 (d = 64). The scatter
is layer to layer (each layer has its own random W) and shrinks with width; layer 1 is not cheaper than later layers. This
is a statement about the per-layer costs of these chains in these two regimes: it does not show that the end-to-end coverage
or waste is flat in depth, and the other families and the ReLU and ordered regimes were not profiled beyond four layers. The
chaotic regime costs about 2x the critical regime per layer at d = 8 (SMC estimates) and 5x at d = 64-256 (lower bounds) and
grows faster with width; LN caps the state, so nothing blows up with depth. Noise level (SMC estimates, one seed; d = 16,
critical, layer 1): sigma = 1.0, 0.3, 0.1 (0.5, 1.8, 3.3 bits per dimension) give Gaussian KL(Kq‖q) = 0.07, 2.0, 5.5 bits,
waste 0.3, 8.2, 81 bits and coverage 1.4, 6.1, 13.6 bits. At half a bit per dimension the Gaussian description is almost
exact. The cost is concentrated in the few-bits-per-dimension regime that the program targets, and the waste grows roughly
as 1/sigma^2 because the Gaussian puts mass outside the noise-width shell around the LN sphere and beyond each neuron's
bounded range.

### Theory checks
- The D_inf waste is infinite for every family with Gaussian tails. For any q_{l-1}, K q(h) <= (2 pi sigma^2)^(-d/2)
  exp(-(|h| - sqrt d)^2 / 2 sigma^2), while coverage forces q's tails to be wider than sigma, so q/Kq grows without bound
  along every ray. Numerically (d = 8, layer 1, three random rays), log2 q/Kq is about -1 bit on the LN shell, +11 at 1.5
  times its radius, +52 at twice and +222 at three times the radius, and +15 at half the radius, inside the shell where K q
  has almost no mass. The program's bound D_inf(q_L‖p_L) <= sum of D_inf wastes is therefore vacuous, and the tightness
  of a bound has to be measured event by event (Task 2).
- End-to-end, against the real p_4 (log p_4 by SMC over the input direction and all earlier noises; d = 8, 4 layers): for
  the Gaussian chain KL(p_4‖q_4) = 2.6 bits against 6.6 summed over the four layers (critical) and 4.0 against 12.1
  (chaotic); the waste direction KL(q_4‖p_4) = 17 against 25 and 11 against 32. Both are well below the per-layer sums (the
  noisy layers forget), consistent with KL(p_L‖q_L) <= sum_l E_{p_l}[log Kq_{l-1}/q_l]. At d = 16 (critical) the same
  comparison gives 2.5 against 9.4 bits and 17 against 36. The largest log p_4/q_4 over sampled points (a lower estimate
  of the end-to-end coverage cost) is 7-10 bits against summed per-layer coverage searches of 20-31 bits. The copula code's
  end-to-end numbers are close to the Gaussian's (2.2-2.9 bits; waste 6-12 bits).
- Certifying c_l. The envelope is the only rigorous upper bound we could compute. It costs 5.2-6 bits per neuron per layer
  with the defensive component used here, and at least 2.5 bits per neuron per layer plus 6.6 bits with the best isotropic
  one (sigma = 0.3). Anything tighter needs the supremum over R^d of the ratio between an intractable continuous mixture
  and q.

### Compute
Fitting one layer from 2^15 draws costs 2^15 (2d^2 + 12d) FLOPs of pushes (7e6 at d = 8, 4.3e9 at d = 256) plus the fit
(2^15 d^2 for the Gaussian; the copula code takes about 10x the Gaussian's wall time). The deductive K = 2 version costs
about 4 d^3 per layer. Measuring the coverage cost is the expensive part: 10-170 CPU-seconds of SMC per layer and family at
d <= 32, against 0.02-0.3 s for the fit.

## Task 2: certified rare-event bounds

361-370 for ReLU. A certificate p(A) <= 2^eps (K q_3)(A) on such a chain loses at least eps_LCB + log2((K q_3)(A) / p(A))
bits. Where the chain's estimate is within a few bits of p(A), as for every event measured here, that is about eps_LCB, so
at d = 256 a certificate on these chains can only be non-vacuous for events that the chain itself puts below about 2^-95
(critical) to 2^-390 (chaotic). Before the review these sums were in-sample values from an independently fitted chain
(0, 5, 19 and 119 bits critical; 92 and 395 chaotic), and the report concluded that no valid certificate is non-vacuous for
any event more likely than 2^-119. A lower bound on eps cannot show that alone, because the loss also depends on the
chain's own probability of the event.

- Nothing is certified in a useful sense. With the envelope eps (141 bits over three layers at d = 8; about 4,000 at
  d = 256 for critical tanh and 4,300 for ReLU) every bound is 1.
- With the uncertified search eps, measured on the same chain, the [last] bound loses about eps_1..3 at every rarity (15,
  20-21 and 36 bits at d = 8, 16, 32 for critical tanh; 42 chaotic and 44 ReLU at d = 16), because (K q_3)(A) is itself
  within a few bits of p (Task 3). It beats the sampling certificate only for events rarer than about 2^-29 (d = 8), 2^-34
  (d = 16) and 2^-51 (d = 32), not at all down to 2^-60 for chaotic tanh and ReLU at d = 16, and only if the SMC that
  estimates eps (100-1000x the chain's own cost) is not counted. (Before the review the search eps was taken from another
  fit of the same network: 14, 21.5 and 27 bits; 2^-30, 2^-35 and 2^-40.)
- The [qL] statement adds the Gaussian's output-tail error, from +3 bits at 2^-10 to +47 at 2^-60 (d = 16), because the
  defensive component's wide tail and the Gaussian tail both ignore the LN cap on y. Tight output bounds need the last layer
  handled exactly; the defensive component that coverage requires is what floors q_4(A).
- The free LN-cap bound loses 26 bits at 2^-56 (d = 8) and 44 at 2^-60 (d = 16), against 16 and 22 for the uncertified
  chain bound, and costs nothing; from d = 32 most of our thresholds sit below the cap and it is vacuous.
- The copula code gives the same picture, with a worse bound for tanh. Before the review its sampler cut each neuron's
  quantile table off at 8 standard deviations, so sampling and density disagreed in the far tails, and the defensive part
  of (K q_3)(A) came from crude Monte Carlo. With exact tails, latent splitting for the defensive part and frozen tuning,
  its last-layer-exact estimate is within -0.3 to +2.7 bits of the reference at d = 8 to 32 (critical tanh; chaotic at d = 16)
  and +3.5 bits at d = 64; for ReLU at d = 16 it is within 4.6 bits where the Gaussian chain is off by 11 (the fix moved the
  ReLU values by up to 3.6 bits at 2^-40 to 2^-60). Its [last] bound, with the coverage search measured on the same chain
  for each of the three layers (22 bits summed at d = 8; 25, 65 and 45 at d = 16 for critical, chaotic and ReLU), loses
  23-25 bits at d = 8 and 26-28 at d = 16 (critical), 5-8 bits more than the Gaussian's; for chaotic tanh it is vacuous, and

## Addition: surrogate chains and the direct comparison with ARC (requested 22:50Z)

Setup. The explanation is a chain of surrogate per-layer maps g_l run on the same path with the same Gaussian noise; by the
chain rule KL(real path ‖ surrogate path) = sum_l E_real |f_l(h) - g_l(h)|^2 / (2 sigma^2) exactly, estimated by sampling
real paths. Networks as in the E[output] study (tanh 0.25 / 0.75 / 3 with bias N(0, 0.25), ReLU without bias), sigma = 0.3,
d = 16 / 64 / 256, L = 2 / 4 / 8, one seed. Surrogates: STAT, the statistical linearisation that K = 2 propagates
implicitly (K = 2's mean of s_l plus E[ds/dh](h - mu)); LR, its rank-d/8 truncation; QUAD, a per-neuron degree-2 Hermite
fit of the activation with a fixed LN scale; PL, per-neuron piecewise-linear activation (hardtanh, or ReLU itself) with a
fixed LN scale; MFLN, the exact activation with a fixed LN scale (it isolates the LN error). Predictions were added to
`PREREG-R.md` before these runs (amendment 1, 23:00Z). Phase B is running the same analysis on the trained tiny PFNs
(`work/cm-interp-B/sur_small.py`, `sur_pfn.py`).

## Where it breaks, what it achieves, and the comparison with ARC

What it achieves. On the LN networks tested, a layer-by-layer Gaussian description, the K = 2 object, predicts rare output
events to within a few bits once the last layer is handled exactly (and integrated by a rare-event method), where Gaussian
output tails are wrong by tens of bits; the same chain supplies backward functions that make guided sampling the cheapest
unbiased estimator to 2^-30 to 2^-40, far ahead of a black-box learned psi; used as a control variate, the same Gaussian
path gives an unbiased E[output] estimator that beats K = 2 and plain sampling at moderate budgets in the ordered and
critical regimes; and the Gaussian chain's per-layer cost does not grow with depth.

Where it breaks. (1) Width: for the families measured at every width (Gaussian, product code, know-nothing; three weight
seeds) the per-layer coverage cost grows linearly with width wherever neurons are non-linear, because joint divergences add
up per-neuron shape errors (Gaussian) or pairwise dependence (product code); the copula code, measured to d = 32, adds up
gate-pattern structure in the chaotic regime, and its behaviour at larger width is untested. Near-Gaussian pre-activations
and near-independent gates make each term small, not their sum; the program's expectation that waste is small on random
networks holds per neuron, not per layer. (2) Certification: the coverage cost cannot be certified tractably, the D_inf
waste is infinite for Gaussian-tailed families, and the defensive component floors the output tail; the lower confidence
bounds on eps (19 bits over three layers at d = 64 and 95-122 at d = 256 for critical tanh) mean that a certificate on these
chains is non-vacuous only for events that the chain itself puts below about 2^-eps. (3) Extreme tails for guided sampling:
linear-probit psi stops working beyond 2^-30 to 2^-40, earlier for ReLU and chaotic tanh; only splitting, which needs no
psi, reaches 2^-60. (4) Precision: the costs vanish at half a bit per dimension and explode at three (waste 81 bits per
layer at sigma = 0.1, d = 16).

Comparison with ARC. Our nets are not ARC's: they have LayerNorm, Gaussian noise in every layer and, for tanh, a bias,
where ARC's are bias-free MLPs without LN or noise (ReLU by default). What we ran is ARC's K = 2 on our nets, for L <= 8.
On ARC's metric it behaves as ARC report for K = 2: it beats sampling at equal FLOPs, increasingly with width and less with
depth (12.6-283x at width 256 for the default variant). That reproduces ARC's pattern, not ARC's best method (factorized
K = 3 and 4), which we did not run. ARC do not certify; our certification is what fails, and it fails more with width
while ARC's estimation error falls with width. For rare events ARC propagate cumulants through the final non-linearity with
factorized K = 3 on nets without LN (30% error 100x below 1/N at n = 256 and four layers). At that shape (width 256, four
layers, ReLU; here with LN and noise) K = 2's output tail is off by 0.7 bits at 2^-20 and 2.2 at 2^-30, and by 9.7 at
2^-61; at smaller widths it is off by tens of bits (up to 47 at width 16). The cause is that LN bounds the output, so the
47-bit figure is a statement about K = 2 on LN nets, not about ARC's K = 3 on ARC's nets. The fix that works on our nets is
to stop the Gaussian description one layer early and treat the last layer exactly (within 0.2 bits at every level at
width 256), which still needs a rare-event computation under q_3. Without LN the Gaussian tail fails in the other direction
(heavy tails: K = 2 too low by up to 20 bits at width 16, the fitted chain's defensive part too high by 30-34 bits), so
neither version of a Gaussian output tail is reliable on its own. In the surrogate-chain framing, the linear map that K = 2
implies is a poor path surrogate outside the ordered regime, which makes KL-based (Donsker-Varadhan) bounds useless against
sampling, but it is a usable control variate: K = 2's mean plus a sampled coupled correction is unbiased and beats both K = 2
and plain sampling at moderate to large budgets in the ordered and critical regimes. Its 1,000x is in the ordered regime,
where K = 2 alone already has normalised MSE 5.5e-9 at width 256 and 8 layers, and for ReLU, ARC's default, it brings no
reliable gain.

## Caveats
- Weight seeds. The width and depth lower confidence bounds use three weight seeds per configuration. The SMC absolute
  numbers, the surrogate chains, the end-to-end check and the rare-event studies use one seed, except second seeds at
  d = 16 and 64 (critical), which reproduce the first (K = 2 off by +1.0 to +32.2 bits, the chain by +0.3 to +2.8); E[output]
  uses two seeds for ReLU at sigma = 0.3. Width and depth conclusions are about the families, regimes and seeds measured.
- Absolute per-layer numbers rely on tempered-SMC estimates of log K q, validated against exact mixtures only at d <= 8;
  waste numbers are over-estimates. The width claims rest on the lower confidence bounds, which do not use that estimator.
- The lower confidence bounds are statistical: the D_inf (Markov) part is an exact binomial bound on held-out samples, the
  KL part a bootstrap and approximate. The held-out Markov bound is weak, or 0, when the chosen threshold has few
  exceedances in the held-out half (one layer of one d = 256 chain); the KL bound then carries the D_inf bound.
- The coverage search is a lower estimate of a supremum, so the "search eps" bounds are optimistic.
- Reference values: 24 of 77 levels are provisional, 22 of them because the bulk-twisted-SMC check, itself heavy-tailed
  below 2^-30, disagrees by more than a bit; chaotic tanh at d = 64 is provisional at 2^-50 and 2^-60 on its own repeats
  (relative SE 0.42 and 0.80, one repeat dominating). Splitting with frozen tuning is unbiased, but where it is heavy-tailed
  a typical run under-estimates, so more repeats could still move those levels up.
- Not run: sparse codes, random transformer blocks, d = 128, ARC's factorized K = 3 and 4, ARC's own nets (no LN, no
  noise) beyond the two no-LN rare-event controls.
