## TL;DR

1. E1's learned cooperation (Hutter 2020) reproduces, but it is half partner-only: in 19 of 20 LOLA pairs (η = 1), one of the two learners cooperates with DefectBot with probability ≥ 0.1, and against such a learner an opponent can earn up to 9.9 more than mutual cooperation (R = 30) [registered].
2. With the opponent's exact probability of cooperating with DefectBot supplied as an input, and a training population that rewards using it, no LOLA learner learned PrudentBot's probe (0 of 40 in each arm). All of them became εGroundedFairBot, which cooperates with CooperateBot, although the training objective prefers a PrudentBot-like point (28.55 against 27.49) [registered; the objective comparison is post hoc].
3. Post hoc, plain gradient ascent with Adam does learn to use the probe: all 40 learners exploit CooperateBot, cross-seed mutual cooperation is 0.99, and no opponent profits. So which rule is learned depends on the learning rule, not on the objective. With a sampled probe, self-cooperation falls to 0.20 [post hoc].
4. For the open question: in this simulation class, payoffs teach the use of a probe whose value the environment supplies, for some learning rules. Nothing here learns the probe query itself, the grounding (imposed, as in E1), or any depth structure.
5. Toy 3's design gave a blocker before any training. A learned network that reads only the opponent's level-by-level play, up to a fixed horizon T, and cooperates with FairBot is suckered by "FairBot with a clock", □p ∧ □^T⊥, even with a DefectBot probe [proven, §4]. So unexploitability against a deeper trickster cannot come from learning in that design; it needs the opponent's syntax (to compute where play stops changing) or a certificate.

## 1. What ran

Toy 1 asks whether E1's learned grounded simulators stay unexploitable off their training pair, and whether payoffs
teach a DefectBot probe when its value is supplied. It is the lob-neural report's Toy 1, with its exploitability bar
corrected (§2.4).

Setup, as in E1 (arXiv:2012.02671v2 §§3-4; I read the PDF today):
- Policy: Pr[S] (simulate the opponent), Pr[C|¬S], Pr[C|C] and Pr[C|D], each a sigmoid of a parameter, with Pr[S]
  clamped to [0, 1 − ε] and ε = 10⁻³. Play comes from E1's eq. (11); the two players' actions are independent.
- Payoffs (R, S, T, P) = (30, 0, 40, 10).
- Training: init N(0, 0.1²); 1000 simultaneous steps with δ = 1; the naive gradient (eq. 1) or exact LOLA (eq. 2,
  differentiated through the opponent's step) with η ∈ {1, 3}; 20 seeds per arm, two learners per seed.

Stages:
- Stage A: the E1 class, trained on the pair only.
- Stage B: the simulated-action response becomes σ(w_a0 + w_a1·d), where d is the opponent's exact probability of
  cooperating against DefectBot, computed in closed form like E1's transparency. The pair's objective puts weight ½ on
  the partner and ½ on a fixed population: mixers that cooperate with probability 0.9 and 0.1, εGFB(0.05) and
  εG-PB(0.05, θ = 0.2). Here εGFB is εGroundedFairBot and εG-PB is εGroundedFairBot that also requires d ≤ θ.
- Stage Bs, the control: the probe is one sampled action of the opponent against DefectBot.

Held-out opponents (none of them in any training objective):
- DB and CB;
- εGFB at ε = 10⁻⁴, 10⁻³, 10⁻² and 10⁻¹ (FairBot's analogue);
- εG-PB at three (ε, θ) pairs (PrudentBot's analogue) and the sampled-probe εG-PB;
- mixers q ∈ {0.05, 0.2, …, 0.8, 0.95};
- every learner of every other seed;
- the worst-case opponent, as the deeper trickster. In E1's model a simulated opponent plays like the real one, so
  every opponent reduces to (x_O, d_O): its probability of cooperating against the learner and against DB. All pairs
  are realisable, and the adversary maximises over a 401 × 401 grid.

Reading [inference]:
- The registered failure is not that the objective lacks a reason to use the probe. LOLA's opponent shaping pushes
  the probe weight positive, apparently because early partners have high d, and then the cooperative response
  saturates and its gradient vanishes.
- Plain gradient ascent with Adam finds a PrudentBot-like rule in the same class and against the same population.
- Without the probe, the same learner defects against everything. This is the rank-0 limit (Bárász et al. Thm 4.10)
  showing up in practice: it cannot cooperate with εGFB without also cooperating with the mixer that cooperates with
  probability 0.9.
- With a sampled probe, the learners that exploit CB lose most of their self-cooperation, as the in-house
  1/(2 − ε) result predicts.

These are single configurations with 20 seeds each, chosen after the fact.

## 4. Toy 3: designed, not registered, not run

Toy 3 was to train a fixed-size GRU (321 parameters) by evolution strategies on payoffs only. The GRU reads the
opponent's level-by-level play against it and, in the probe arm, against DefectBot. It plays the exact level game:
- each modal opponent's level-n value reads the learner's levels below n;
- the learner halts at its horizon;
- the opponent is read at its stable value.

The held-out set was DB, CB, FB, PB and the tricksters; everything behaviourally equal to them was excluded from
training. What exists:

1. **A validated environment** (`lib/modal.py`, `tests/test_modal.py`, `logs/test_modal.log`) [checked]:
   - 0 level-table mismatches with formal_logic.md's verbatim `solve` on 600 random pairs (1,200 rows);
   - the PrudentBot table, n0 column included;
   - DD under the pessimistic base;
   - the batched level game reproduces the modal tables exactly when FB or PB is run as a stream reader (69
     opponents, T = 12).

   Critch's Payor agent (C iff □(□x → x), x = "both cooperate") plays CC with itself, FB, PB and CB, and DD with DB.
   On these opponents it behaves like FairBot, including cooperating with CooperateBot [checked].

2. **Two limits on any stream reader**, learned or not [proven]. A stream reader's level-n action (n ≤ T) depends only
   on the opponent's level values below n, against it and against fixed reference agents. After T its action stays at
   a_T.
   - (a) If it plays CC with FairBot, then against Y_T = □p ∧ □^T⊥ it plays C while Y_T's stable action is D.
     Proof: □^T⊥ holds below level T, so Y_T's values below T, against the learner and against any reference, equal
     FairBot's whenever the learner's lower-level actions agree. By induction on n ≤ T, the learner's actions agree,
     so a_T(Y_T) = a_T(FB) = C. From level T on, Y_T plays D. ∎
     The DefectBot probe does not help: Y_T against DB equals FB against DB below T. The same argument with PB and
     PB ∧ □^T⊥ covers learners that cooperate with PB. So every fixed-horizon reader that cooperates with FairBot is
     exploitable, and the best a learned stream reader can be is PrudentBot read at a fixed level, which the lanes
     already showed exploitable (Prop 5(b)).
   - (b) Without a probe, CC with FairBot forces CC with CooperateBot. Proof: FB's stable C needs a_m = C at every
     m ≤ T, so FB's stream against the learner is all C up to T, identical to CB's, and the learner's actions against
     CB are the same. ∎
     This is Thm 4.10 for stream readers.

3. **The training distribution decides whether the probe pays** [checked, dev only]. At horizon 6, with the
   hand-written readers on L's random modal formulas (rnd_mod(3)):

   | bank | FB per game | PB per game |
   |---|---|---|
   | 3000 raw draws | 2.437 | 2.825 |
   | one formula per behaviour, unweighted, held-out equivalents excluded (265 behaviours) | 2.230 | 2.223 |
   | same, weighted by draw frequency (207 behaviours, 56.5% of 20,000 draws kept) | 2.638 | 2.815 |

   PB loses to "slow FairBots" such as □□p, which cooperate with DB at level 1 (a PA+2 effect). The weighted bank was
   going to be Toy 3's training set. A 21-generation dev run of the ES trainer ran without errors; nothing was
   evaluated on held-out opponents.

## 5. Bearing on the probabilistic direction (Payor, Critch, Demski)

- Demski's probabilistic Payor lemma (2023, read today in full) uses B(s) := p(s) > c and needs two rules:
  necessitation (⊢s ⇒ ⊢p(s) = 1) and weak distributivity (⊢a → b ⇒ ⊢p(a) ≤ p(b)). Its author says he has not
  confirmed that these are reasonable. Toy 1's working PrudentBot is a threshold of exactly this kind on an exact
  probability, d ≤ θ. Whether a learner acquires it depended on the learning rule. The same threshold on a sampled
  probe fails: self-cooperation 0.20 post hoc, 1/(2 − ε) in theory.
- The only cooperation the learners found is ε-grounded and imposed, as in E1, not a Löbian or Payor handshake. The
  Payor agent on the level chain cooperates with CooperateBot like FairBot (§4.1), so Payor's lemma alone gives no
  PrudentBot.
- Nothing here tests a learned credence operator over depth. That was Toy 2's role, and Toy 2 was not run.
