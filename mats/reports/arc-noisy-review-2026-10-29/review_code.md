1. **High: low-budget Kerdock results exceed their stated FLOP budgets.** `gpu_samplers.py:40,113,129`; `samplers.py:50-61`. The scorer allocates `floor(B/c)` points and discards the returned setup count. The `+1600` per-point allowance roughly amortizes the rotation over a *complete* 66,048-point block, but cannot pay for the initial QR/fold at small budgets. By the code's own conventions, one rotation costs `6*256^3 + 16*256^2 = 101,711,872` FLOPs. At B=1e9 the code allocates 982 points at L=8 or 232 at L=32; even assigning the entire 1600-per-point allowance to setup leaves approximately 100.1M or 101.3M uncharged. The per-point first-layer matvec charge already pays for the basis multiplication: do **not** blindly add the entire returned setup count and double-charge that multiplication. Charge rotation setup per started block and solve for affordable N, including radius generation. This favors MUB against MC/ARC at small budgets and propagates into H1/H2. Evidence: `check.out`.

2. **High: H1's “equal cost” comparator is modeled, not measured, and its claimed conservative direction is unsupported.** `hybrid.py:66-67` rescales the measured Kerdock error by `N*c/(N*c+pilot+closure)`. A randomized structured design need not have error proportional to 1/N, especially across partial bases and rotation blocks. The stored dev-net-100 data already show this: at L=8, nMSE*N changes from 0.348 at N=19,656 to 0.189 at 66,836; at L=32 it changes from 0.391 at 4,678 to 0.643 at 11,698. These finite-replicate values do not establish a scaling law in either direction. The hybrid estimate uses paired carrier samples correctly, but there is no paired **equal-budget** Kerdock measurement. Store the additional checkpoints and score both methods directly. H1 currently prints only medians, without the mean/CI win checks required by PREREG. Evidence: `check_extra.out`; `hybrid.py:80-87`.

3. **Medium: H1 omits the OLS fit from its charged pilot cost.** `gpu_samplers.py:106-110` solves a 4096-by-257 least-squares problem with 256 responses, then records only `4096*flops_path(L)`. Even one chosen control needs roughly 0.54G FLOPs for QR and another 0.54G for applying Q to the responses, plus the triangular solve; rank-revealing details can add work. The code computes several fits. A deployed, preselected control need only pay for its own fit, but it must pay for one. Offline application of A also has a small uncharged cost. This violates PREREG's explicit inclusion of fits and favors H1. The shared RMS pilot is different: excluding it is consistent with the preregistered object definition.

4. **Medium: the active-subspace result does not imply the stated 1.4x upper bound.** `scratch/agop.py:19-28`; `LEDGER.md:47-49`. The Hutchinson calculation correctly estimates derivative energy `E[J^T J]`. Its normalized eigenvalue shares are not shares of output variance, much less shares of Kerdock estimator variance. Gaussian Poincare supplies a bound on residual variance using discarded *absolute* eigenvalues; it does not justify `gain <= 1/(1-0.30)`. Concrete counterexample: for independent standard Gaussians, `f=x1+0.1*(x2^2-1)/sqrt(2)`, the top direction has derivative-energy share 1/1.02 but variance share 1/1.01. Removing its contribution gives gain 101, exceeding the putative derivative-share cap 51. The measured spectrum is useful; the cap and consequent rejection of all description-guided RQMC are unsupported.

5. **Medium: H2 leaves substantial budget unused while reporting the nominal budget.** `combine.py:75-89`. Variant selection predicts using nH, but evaluation rounds down to the previous *stored budget checkpoint*, rather than sampling nH points. For example, at B=5e9, a cheap closure can leave nearly 5e9 for sampling, yet the chosen checkpoint is the 2e9 one. This can discard more than half the available sampling budget and selects the variant against a different sample count than the one evaluated. It disadvantages H2, so a negative result does not reject the intended algorithm. Store exact residual-budget checkpoints, or report actual expenditure and select against that expenditure. The development/evaluation split itself is correct: b2 and V are fitted only on seeds 100-107, not 0-23.

6. **Medium, limited to noiseless checks: GPU sampler forwarding is wrong when sigma=0.** `gpu_samplers.py:51-56`. `h` is updated from `a` only inside `if s[l-1] > 0`; with zero noise, all later matrices act on the first activation. Reproduction: z1=ones, W2=2I, W3=3I gives output 3 instead of 6 (`check_extra.out`). Use `h=a` each iteration before optionally adding noise. Positive-sigma main runs are unaffected. Separately, `analyze.py:64-66` charges noise even when sigma=0, although the main SIGMAS list is positive.

7. **Medium: oracle error floors are not calibrated for fitted, shared truth.** `hybrid.py:52-63`; `truthlib.py:17-23`; `scratch/seeded_oracle.py:9-12`; `scratch/copula_seed.py:13-30,43`; `scratch/edgeworth_oracle.py:21-22,37-45`. The ordinary `1/N_truth` subtraction assumes an estimate independent of truth. H1's oracle uses the *same* truth trajectories at m and L: its truth-error term is proportional to `Var(a_L-A*a_m)/N_truth`, not `Var(a_L)/N_truth`. Subtracting the latter makes the oracle optimistic, potentially negative near the floor. Seeded Gaussian moments and target truth also share trajectories; copula fits add uncertainty from 2^18 training paths that its printed `1/NB` floor omits. Edgeworth reports one estimated-cumulant realization without replicate uncertainty, and does not debias at all. Use independent training/reference batches and repeated oracle fits, or account for the covariance explicitly. This matters for tiny residual errors and close comparisons; it does **not** plausibly explain away the Gaussian-seed result solely by its printed sampling floor: 2.09e-4 is about 227 times 9.2e-7. Existing `logs/seeded_m24.log` supports that numerical result, with estimated rather than literally exact moments.

8. **Low-to-medium: closure cost scaling is an approximation, not the ARC counter at the chosen depth.** `hybrid.py:28-33`. Quadratic growth is defensible asymptotically, but `(m/8)^2` drops linear and constant terms. Direct width-256 checks on net 100 give m=4: 3,072,826,668 versus 3,156,046,678 charged (+2.7%); m=12: 28,651,552,132 versus 28,404,420,102 charged (-0.86%). Stored m=32 is 205,928,459,872 versus 201,986,987,392 (-1.91%). Record actual prefix costs or fit the complete polynomial. Evidence: `cost_check.out`, `check.out`.

9. **Low-to-medium: oracle interpretations overreach the quantities tested.** `scratch/edgeworth_oracle.py:12,18,37`; `LEDGER.md:43-46,50-53`. Edgeworth measures marginal cumulants of **next-layer preactivations** `W*h`, which already depend on the joint distribution of h. Its success does not establish that marginals of the current h suffice for one-step readout, as the copula discussion implies. Also, the output labeled “k3 only” includes the `g3^2*H4/72` term: it is a skewness-based second-order expansion, not a lone third-cumulant correction. The survival code correctly couples paths, but tests one random perturbation direction per depth on one net; “no re-projection scheme can use it” is stronger than that experiment establishes. Retain the measured outcomes and narrow these interpretations.

10. **Low: weight identity and validation are overstated.** `anet.py:37-43`; `kprop_noisy.py:32-35`; `truth_gpu.py:40-43`; `truthlib.py:13`; `analyze.py:41-60`. The common numpy RandomState correctly reproduces the source weights. Actual GPU truth uses float32-rounded weights while kprop uses the original float64 values, so the executed weights are not bit-identical. On net 100 the largest discrepancy is 1.49e-8, likely negligible here, but no output-sensitivity bound was established. The saved checksum is of W64, not the executed W32; analysis never checks it despite the truth module's claim. The net-100 saved checksum matches within 1.14e-13 and r matches exactly in our check. Store a byte hash of the canonical executed weights and verify r/checksums on load, or explicitly describe the precision approximation.

11. **Low: the cost conventions differ beyond symmetric-tensor discounts.** `ext/mlp_cumulant_propagation/src/mlp_kprop/flop_utils.py:191-240`; `anet.py:17-28`; `samplers.py:69-72`. ARC's counter charges exp/log/pow as one operation, unlike the sampling convention's 16, and its torch matmul convention differs from `2K-1`. Sobol initialization/scrambling and ndtri are assigned the cost of a normal draw without a measured flopscope derivation. These are declared modeling choices in part, not evidence of incorrect estimates, but “one convention” and explanations mentioning only symmetric-tensor discounts are incomplete. Label the comparison as using ARC's counter versus analytic sampler costs and show sensitivity before interpreting narrow wins.
