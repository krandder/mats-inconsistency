Checked the report, `world.py`, cited abstracts, and selected full texts. No files edited.

1. **High — “dense linear-Gaussian dependence is cheap”**  
   Correct for Gaussian means, covariances, conditioning, and normalization: dense linear algebra remains polynomial at any treewidth. It does **not** establish tractability of arbitrary nonlinear expectations or tail events under Gaussian inputs. Specify the queries.

2. **High — “bounded treewidth (provably the only structural route; linear-Gaussian parts exempt)”**  
   Chandrasekaran et al. concern graph-only guarantees allowing arbitrary potentials, under complexity assumptions; their lower bound is in **treewidth**, not simply graph size. Width \(O(\log n)\) already permits polynomial inference for bounded discrete domains. Special potentials and query restrictions provide further routes. Keep **P**, explicitly conditional and scoped.

3. **Medium — “random graphs with over 1.073 edges per node have treewidth linear in size”**  
   Gao proves this **with high probability for Erdős–Rényi \(G(n,m)\)** when the fixed ratio \(m/n>1.073\). This is neither a universal random-graph statement nor a sharp threshold; average degree is \(2m/n\). It does not automatically apply to sparse neural-network graphs.

4. **Medium — “compositional sparsity is bounded fan-in, which every Boolean circuit has”**  
   Poggio’s 2025 definition includes **polynomial size**, not merely bounded fan-in. Boolean circuits over a fixed bounded-arity basis have bounded fan-in; other circuit conventions do not. The narrower conclusion stands: bounded fan-in alone guarantees neither low treewidth nor efficient expectation computation.

5. **High — “The exponential is in input dimension, not depth.”**  
   Hanin–Rolnick bound the **expected activation-pattern count at initialization**, under their assumptions, by a quantity polynomial in neuron count with exponent input dimension; training evidence is empirical. Worst-case depth-exponential expressivity remains valid. This is not an inference-complexity theorem.

6. **High — “dense nets that learn features find the tree’s exact inference from polynomially many samples”**  
   This combines separate empirical results into a stronger claim. Garnier-Brun et al. report evidence that transformers **approximate** exact inference; Allen-Zhu–Li report representations and attention resembling CFG dynamic programming. Cagnetta’s sample scaling concerns a particular synthetic hierarchy. Replace with model-specific empirical findings; label **M**, not a general guarantee.

7. **High — “message of dimension k(s), at least the task’s epsilon-rank across that cut”**  
   False for unrestricted nonlinear messages. Sending scalar \(x\) lets a receiver compute \(e^{xy}\), despite that function having infinite exact separation rank. Rank bounds message dimension for specified linear/multilinear representations, not arbitrary neural encoders. Define the function, norm, input domain, precision, and permitted decoder.

8. **High — “k proportional to s (a volume law) is fully tangled”**  
   This conflates coordinate count, information capacity, and tensor rank. Tensor-network volume-law entanglement concerns entropy, typically requiring **exponential bond dimension**. Furthermore, “bags may grow with s” imposes no useful restriction without a growth bound. Present this as a proposed definition, with explicit units and constraints.

9. **Medium — “sources are independent given the answer y*”**  
   Correct for **residual-based source summaries**, but not literally for raw source tokens: these share labelled answers and question embeddings. State independence conditional on all shared variables, or explicitly use \(o_{s,*}\) and residuals \(r_{s,q}=o_{s,q}-y_q\).

10. **High — “each enters Bayes through its query reading and its labelled errors’ sum of squares, and Bayes multiplies per-source likelihoods of y*”**  
    This is correct in `world.py`, with the prior and normalization included:
    \[
    p(y\mid D)\propto \phi_1(y)\prod_s\sum_{v\in VLEV}\tfrac13\,p(r_s\mid v)\phi_v(o_{s,*}-y).
    \]
    However, posterior variance assignments are **coupled after integrating out \(y\)**, exactly as `references()` documents. Do not confuse this factorization with independent posterior source reliabilities.

11. **High — “On the PFN prior exact Bayes is a tree” / “Bayes-sized messages”**  
    A tree factorization does not imply fixed-dimensional, closed messages. Each leaf has two sufficient statistics, but combining source likelihoods produces a growing function of \(y\); the reference implementation enumerates \(3^N\) variance assignments. Distinguish small leaf statistics from the representation and integration cost of accumulated messages.

12. **High — “epsilon-rank of the summed per-source log-likelihoods … flat in N at ρ = 0”**  
    The ranked object and partition are unspecified. At fixed \(y\), a source-partitioned additive log-likelihood has separation rank at most two; that says little about the **normalized posterior**. If ranking functions jointly over data and \(y\), fixed approximation dimension needs proof, including how errors accumulate with \(N\).

13. **High — “given y* and the variance levels, messages are independent and the root’s law is a convolution”**  
    Sound at \(\rho=0\) for messages depending only on source-local residuals and query readings. The **sum before readout** has a convolution law; the final output requires pushing that distribution through the readout. At \(\rho>0\), conditioning on \(y^*\) and variances does not remove shared errors; condition additionally on their generating latents.

14. **High — “Compute E[KL(Bayes‖net)] and P(|mean − Bayes| > t)”**  
    The network root’s marginal law alone is insufficient. The tail requires the **joint law of network prediction and Bayes mean**. Expected KL can instead use conditional network NLL minus a separately computed Bayes conditional entropy. Specify how these extra quantities enter propagation.

15. **High — “grid propagation with interval bounds”**  
    This is a proposed numerical method, not an established certificate. Certification needs bounds for Gaussian-domain truncation, discretization, likelihood normalization, readout evaluation, and conditioning-variable integration. Grid cost can grow exponentially with message dimension. Label feasibility **C** until demonstrated.

16. **High — “certified tail bounds below sampling’s 1/compute floor”**  
    There is no universal sampling floor of \(1/\text{compute}\). For ordinary independent Bernoulli sampling, zero events in \(M\) trials gives a one-sided upper confidence bound approximately \(-\log\alpha/M\). Specify confidence, sample cost, and whether importance sampling or other variance reduction is included.

17. **High — “at ρ > 0 the tree needs k proportional to N where the dense net does not”**  
    This cannot isolate a structural advantage as written. Fixed transformer width still leaves \(O(Nd)\) token state. Shared correlation may also admit a small latent representation. Compare total information crossing the same partition, and specify the coupling topology and whether group membership is observed.

18. **Medium — “KL to Bayes within the dense seeds’ spread” / “if distilling the dense net then works, tangling helps learning”**  
    Seed spread is not an equivalence margin; a variable or inaccurate dense baseline makes this criterion easy to satisfy. Predefine an absolute Bayes-gap tolerance and uncertainty calculation. Distillation success demonstrates benefit from a changed training signal, not uniquely from tangled representations; include Bayes-teacher distillation.

19. **Medium — “N = 3, 6, 12, 24 … σ = 0.05”**  
    `world.py` fixes three sources, four labelled questions, and variances `[0.1, 0.5, 2.0]`; it contains no shared-error groups or stated \(\sigma=0.05\) parameter. Identify these as proposed extensions and define \(\sigma\). Recompute the Bayes reference for every extension.

20. **High — “All cost capability … only mixture-of-experts reaches billions of parameters.”**  
    False. The cited [SoLU study](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/solu/index.html) tests models up to approximately **50B parameters** and reports approximately matched loss and NLP evaluations. The report’s own bilinear comparison also improves over ReLU. Describe architecture-specific measured tradeoffs.

21. **High — “Only tractable probabilistic models … [give] exact or certified expectations and tail probabilities” / “yes, linear in size”**  
    Tractability is **query-specific**. Smooth, decomposable probabilistic circuits support efficient standard marginals; arbitrary predicates, losses, and tail events need not be tractable. Even independent bits can encode a hard counting query. MERA’s exact local expectations likewise do not imply arbitrary-tail tractability.

22. **Medium — “ReLU verification is NP-complete; counting unsafe inputs #P-complete”**  
    Specify **existence of a violating input** for NP-completeness; universal safety is the complementary decision problem. Marzari’s counting formulation uses a discrete input space, not cardinality of arbitrary continuous unsafe regions. These are **P** results with those qualifications.

23. **Medium — “Backdoors undetectable given weights and data”**  
    Goldwasser et al. establish computational indistinguishability against **efficient** distinguishers, under cryptographic assumptions and for specified constructions. They do not establish information-theoretic undetectability or a universal limitation for every trained model. Label **P**, conditional.

24. **High — “L/n sets non-Gaussianity; 1/n^k terms grow like L^k”**  
    Qualify the architecture, initialization, activation assumptions, and observables; Hanin says such terms **often** grow this way. Crucially, randomness over weights at fixed inputs differs from randomness over inputs for one fixed network. These results alone do not prove the report’s compression or estimation-hardness conclusion.

25. **Medium — “Cumulant propagation beats sampling … error at most c_K (L/n)^K”**  
    ARC’s conjectured quantity is **mean squared error**, not an unspecified error bound. Its wide-network advantage has both theoretical and empirical support, so use **P, M; C; C**, with the theorem’s assumptions. The general mechanistic-matching conjecture is correctly labelled **C**.

26. **Medium — “Wide random nets with zero-mean activations have nearly independent outputs … | P”**  
    Dunbar–Aaronson additionally require nonlinear activations, suitable hyperparameters, and depth/width scaling; their analysis explicitly uses a first-order perturbative approximation. The proposed no-coincidence application is **C**, not a proved hardness result. Split the claims and state the approximation regime.

27. **Medium — “m features computed with about sqrt(m) neurons, also necessary up to logs”**  
    Adler–Shavit support this scaling for specified computational tasks and assumptions, not arbitrary feature computation. Hänni et al. give different constructions and exponents. Attribute the bounds separately and distinguish representation from computation.

28. **Medium — “mild over-parameterization removes [spurious minima]”**  
    Safran–Shamir’s empirical wording is that it **appears to drastically reduce** them. Their computer-assisted existence theorem concerns a particular teacher–student ReLU regression problem at 6–20 units. Keep **P** for that theorem and **M** for optimization observations.

29. **Medium — “good sparse subnetworks … are found through dense training” / “paths … pass through dense space”**  
    These are not necessities. Malach proves existence of subnetworks requiring no further weight training; RigL trains with a sparse parameter budget. Evci finds dense connecting paths and fails to find certain sparse ones, which does not prove sparse paths absent. State the algorithm-specific empirical results.

30. **Medium — “16M-latent SAE in GPT-4: loss of 10% of its compute”**  
    [Gao §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04093) means reconstruction substitution yields language-model loss comparable to a model trained with **10% of GPT-4’s pretraining compute**. It does not mean a 10% compute loss. Rewrite that sentence explicitly; **M** is appropriate.

31. **Medium — “each layer’s state is k of C codes”**  
    Codebook constraints apply to specified components, heads, or groups; this does not establish that the entire residual-stream state has only \(\binom Ck\) possibilities. The reported **2.74 versus 2.41** numbers are correct for attention codes with \(k=8\); Table 2 also reports **2.55 at \(k=64\)**. Identify the configuration.

32. **Low — “χ-nets 1.5–2 points below ReLU (SVHN)”**  
    Table 2 gives deficits of **1.9, 2.2, 1.9, and 1.5 percentage points**. Correct the range to **1.5–2.2**. Shazeer’s quoted **1.960/1.997/1.944** numbers are correct, but identify them as the **65,536-step** comparison.

33. **Medium — “bounded-state sequence models lose mainly on recall, provably on copying” / “1-bit fusion trees often lose nothing”**  
    Copying bounds require bounded **information capacity**, not merely finitely many unlimited-precision real coordinates. Zoology’s **82%** is specific to its evaluated models and corpus. Fusion-tree results concern asymptotic Neyman–Pearson error exponents under conditional-i.i.d. and topology assumptions, not general lossless inference.

34. **Medium — “Depth gives exponentially higher separation rank across cuts” / “tree tensor networks train in linear time”**  
    The rank results concern specified architectures and **particular partitions**, not every cut or generic transformer. Stoudenmire’s linear scaling concerns a particular training procedure with other dimensions controlled; bond-dimension costs remain. Preserve those restrictions.

35. **Medium — “near VAEs and flows only with transformer-distilled latents”**  
    LVD demonstrates a successful remedy for the plateau observed with its tested optimizers. It does not prove distillation necessary for all probabilistic circuits. GeLaTo instead uses distilled HMMs to guide constrained generation. Keep these as separate **M** findings.

36. **Medium — “no compression at 256 x 32”; “6–16%”; “85–92%”; “E20 showed … removes the big-miss bias”**  
    **Cannot verify** these internal experimental claims from the report or `world.py`. Supply the corresponding artifacts, metric definitions, and uncertainty. Likewise, the reported World FA status and AlgZoo result are not established by the supplied code.
