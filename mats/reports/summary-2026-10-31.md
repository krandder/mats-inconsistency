## Theory on ARC's agenda (asymptotic, as Kelvin accepted)
- **The exp(c k*) premise is refuted.** Proven instead, for one-way linear summaries: an exponential in precision,
  (1/eps)^Theta(n). It holds for merged fronts, and for the actual ensemble with tiny constants. The mechanism is the
  gate kinks.
- **The extension shows information bounds cannot cover ARC's real method.**
  - The exponential survives only for bounded-degree readouts and for bit counts.
  - Lipschitz readouts and real-valued back-and-forth are exact with about n^2 numbers.
  - ARC's closure is biased at every fixed order K, so K must grow like log/loglog.
- **Unrestricted estimators: deduction never beats RQMC.**
  - Branch-and-bound cost grows about 10x per unit of width.
  - Determinism is free on random weights.
  - Restricted classes (designs, low-degree polynomials, stable estimators) provably fail.
  - A multilevel shared-trajectory sampler beats MC by Omega(n/log^2 n) at depth proportional to width; it breaks
    even at 256x32.
  - Revised conjecture: nothing beats the best multilevel sampler polynomially.
- **Analogies (threshold-circuit derandomisation, convex-body volume, spin glasses):** none transfers hardness to the
  mean of random nets.

## The stationary state and input bits (M1)
- **The stationary state.**
  - It exists and is unique (proven), and sigma is a pure scale (proven).
  - The radius follows an exact chain with a closed-form finite-n law.
  - The state is a thin, near-Gaussian cloud whose size is set by the ReLU kernel's finite-angle contraction; that
    law passed a fresh test.
  - Bits are many: about 0.5-0.6 sqrt(n) gate flips per layer. Each flip causes about 0.2n more and lasts about 1.5n
    layers.
- **Interactions.**
  - They grow with width: mean order 1.4 -> 3.9 at n = 8-256, and the two lanes agree after reconciliation.
  - Units within about 4 noise SDs of their gate boundary carry them.
  - The low-order shares do not change with width.
  - RQMC's falling gain is a dimension effect: removing pairs needs N >> d.
- **Estimators.**
  - Every unbiased method's MSE falls like 1/compute, so only the constants differ (antithetic 2.4x; the RQMC gain
    shrinks with width).
  - Multilevel gains grow with width at L ~ n (1.3 -> 1.9; dyadic 1.6 -> 2.5).
- **ARC's 256x32 is far from stationary.**
  - The input carries about 99.9% of the variance: amplitude (input-dependent gain) 0.49, output direction 0.47,
    |x| 0.034.
  - 38 input gate bits remain at layer 31; gate differences decay like 3n/l.
  - The mean ANOVA dimension is 9-12, and about 30% of the variance is additive.
- **Width 8, depth 256:** no reliable deductive win over RQMC, and no cut is simple on both sides.

## Phase B (M2)
- **Head count, not width, sets the A/B type (E71).** With 2 heads most nets are B; with 3 heads 9/12 are A; with 4
  heads they are A. KL to Bayes about halves as the count rises. This is supported, not proven, as the cause.
- **Code-content coupling in LayerNorm (E64b).** Normalising codes and content separately reaches the
  membership-given KL. It is a bundled intervention.
- **The big-miss preference is made in the source's own first-layer summary (E72).** In training, A nets improve the
  summary, while B nets read around one that never improves (E73).
- **In uniform-pool nets, the cap scale predicts the departure** through constrained Bayes (E58), across sizes (E61).
