# Tiny PFNs on a three-source reliability world (2026-10-27)

**TL;DR.** One config gets within 0.02 nats of exact Bayes: width 32, 2 heads, 2 layers, MLP, 18,402 parameters. Its test NLL gap is 0.0160 ± 0.0010 (5 seeds). The exact expected gap, KL(Bayes‖model), is 0.0195 ± 0.0013, which is borderline: 2 of the 5 seeds are above 0.02. About half of that gap (0.0101) is a floor that no Gaussian output head can remove. The rest sits in episodes where the exact Bayes answer is bimodal, i.e. where the three query readings disagree. Every 1-layer model, including all micro models (158–738 parameters), stays near "query-only Bayes" and barely uses the labelled questions.

## What was run

- **World.** Coordinator changes from Kelvin were applied before any training: no duplicates, no shared-error groups, keys arm dropped. Each episode has three independent sources with o = y + e, e ~ N(0, v_s). Each source's v_s is drawn uniformly from {0.1, 0.5, 2.0} and fixed for the episode. There are 4 labelled questions and 1 query question, which gives 15 context tokens plus 1 query token, in the parent's token format.
- **Exact Bayes.** A mixture over the 27 variance combinations. The weights use the labelled residuals and also the marginal of the query readings; the query readings couple the sources.
- **References.**
  - (a) equal weights at the prior-mean variance.
  - (b) oracle precision weighting with the true variances.
  - Two diagnostics: "lab-only Bayes" (weights from the labelled questions only) and "query-only Bayes" (weights from the query readings only, ignoring the labelled questions).
- **Tests.** All 9 unit tests pass: Bayes vs an independent full-joint enumeration, vs Monte Carlo (errors about 1e-3), calibration on 40k independently simulated episodes, the reductions, and the token layout.
- **Grid.** 75 CPU runs, 8 in parallel, 1 thread each, run as pre-registered:
  - Main grid: width {16, 32} × heads {1, 2} × depth {1, 2}, MLP at 2× width, plus a width-32 2-layer attention-only model, with identity vectors in R^16.
  - Kelvin's micro arms: width {4, 8}, 1 head, 1 layer, with and without MLP, identity vectors in R^4.
  - A control repeating w16_h2_L2 and w32_h2_L2 with R^4 identity vectors.
  - 5 seeds for everything.
  - Training: lr 1e-3 constant, the parent's convergence rule (with one extension to 80k steps), then a 10k-step cooldown.
- **Test set.** n = 16,000, seed 20000.

**Deviation from the parent setup:** I added the lr cooldown, decided before training. It is what gets the best config under 0.02: the gap is 0.027 before the cooldown and 0.016 after.

**Unconverged runs, not hidden:** 26 of the 75 runs failed the convergence rule at 80k steps. They are listed in `runs/summary.md`, and the gaps are still falling slowly.

## Where the best config's deviation lives

Pooled over 5 seeds, the mean KL is 0.0195. It splits into:
- the Gaussian-head floor: 0.0101
- mean mismatch: 0.0061
- scale mismatch: 0.0034

The learnable excess (KL minus the floor) tracks how bimodal the exact posterior is. As a function of the Bayes sufficient statistics:
- **Spread of the three query readings:** Spearman ρ = 0.46. The top quintile holds 33% of the total KL.
- **Between-component variance of the Bayes mixture:** ρ = 0.47. The top quintile holds 42%.
- **Query-reading coupling,** KL(w_Bayes‖w_lab), i.e. how much the query readings move the reliability posterior: ρ = 0.19. Its top quintile alone holds 37%.
- **Ambiguity of each source's reliability from the labelled residuals:** ρ ≈ 0. Both per-source posterior entropy and total 27-way entropy are essentially flat.

By true variances, the worst episodes have one reliable and two noisy sources (0.1, 2, 2): KL 0.031, of which 0.020 is the floor. When all three sources are noisy, the mean-mismatch part is largest (0.011).

The residual mean error has almost no component along the lab-only, query-only or equal-weight directions (regression slopes ≤ 0.06), so it is not a simple shrink toward a cruder estimator. The model's predictive sd is about 1% too wide on average. The top 10% of episodes carry 39% of the KL. Per-episode excess correlates only 0.38 between seeds, so part of the deviation is systematic and part depends on the seed.

## Where w32_h2_L2 + mixture still deviates (4 non-stuck seeds, mean KL 0.0092)

- **Same place as before:** episodes where exact Bayes is multimodal, i.e. where the query readings disagree and also move the reliability posterior.
  - Top quintile of between-component variance: KL 0.0195 vs 0.0044 in the bottom quintile; 42% of the total.
  - Top quintile of query-reading spread: 34% of the total.
  - Top quintile of query-reading coupling, KL(w_Bayes‖w_lab): 40% of the total (KL 0.018 vs 0.006–0.008 in the other quintiles).
  - How ambiguous a source's reliability is from its labelled residuals still does not matter (flat across quintiles).
  - The top 10% of episodes carry 45% of the KL.
- **The model under-expresses that multimodality.** It uses about 2.9 effective components. Its between-component variance fraction tracks Bayes's (r = 0.89) but is smaller on average (0.109 vs 0.132).
- **By true variances:** the worst case is three noisy sources (2, 2, 2): KL 0.014, mostly mean mismatch. The best is three reliable sources: KL 0.004.
- **No systematic pull toward a cruder estimator:** slopes of the mean error on the lab-only, query-only and equal-weight directions are all ≤ 0.05.
- **Seed agreement:** per-episode KL correlates 0.53 between seeds.

The 4,892-parameter model has the same profile at about twice the level: 35–39% of its KL is in the top quintiles of coupling and between-component variance.

# Warm-started mixture head and plateau diagnostic (2026-10-27, 07:50Z)

Pre-registered as PREREG.md Amendment 3 at 06:55Z, before any warm-start result. 10 CPU runs, 6 in parallel.

**TL;DR.** The warm start fixes the stuck seeds. Both warm-started configs pass the pre-registered 0.02 rule with all seeds counted:
- **w32_h2_L2 + warm-started mixture head (18,732 params):** gap 0.0089 ± 0.0010, KL 0.0101 ± 0.0013. All 5 seeds are under 0.02 (gaps 0.0056–0.0110).
- **w16_h2_L2 at R^4 + warm-started mixture head (4,892 params):** gap 0.0168 ± 0.0034, KL 0.0178 ± 0.0031. Only 3 of 5 seeds are under 0.02 (seeds 1 and 3: 0.022 and 0.027).

**Recommended for interpretability step 1: w32_h2_L2 + warm-started mixture head.** It is the smallest config with every seed under 0.02. Its seed 4 is the best single checkpoint (gap 0.0056, KL 0.0067). A model about 4× smaller is available if you are willing to work from individual seeds: w16_h2_L2 at R^4, seeds 2 or 4 (KL 0.0107 and 0.0131). That config does not reach 5/5 seeds.

## Warm start

**Setup.**
- Each seed starts from that seed's 160k-step Gaussian checkpoint.
- Body weights are copied. The mixture components start around the Gaussian output, with bias offsets of ±0.05 and ±0.15 on the means.
- Training then uses the standard schedule.
- All 10 runs passed the convergence rule at 40k.

**Results.** Each warm-started model improves on its own Gaussian source. Gaps by seed:

| Config | Gaussian source | Warm-started mixture |
|---|---|---|
| w32 | 0.012–0.017 | 0.006–0.011 |
| w16 at R^4 | 0.016–0.035 | 0.009–0.027 |

The two w16 seeds that miss 0.02 come from the two weakest Gaussian sources (0.031 and 0.035), so the warm start inherits the body's quality.

**Where the w32 model's remaining KL (0.0101) lives.** This is unchanged from the cold-start picture:
- It is concentrated where exact Bayes is multimodal. The top quintiles of between-component variance, query-reading coupling and query-reading spread hold 41%, 39% and 33% of the total.
- The top 10% of episodes carry 43%.
- Per-source reliability ambiguity is flat.
- By true variances, the worst case is three noisy sources (KL 0.0135) and the best is three reliable ones (0.0047).
- The model uses about 3.3 effective components. Its between-component variance fraction tracks Bayes's (r = 0.80), but it still under-expresses the bimodality (0.122 vs 0.132).
- There is no pull toward any cruder estimator (slopes ≤ 0.05).
- Per-episode KL correlates 0.41 across seeds.

The 4,892-param model has the same profile at about 1.8× the level.

## Plateau diagnostic (all 135 runs, no new training)

"On the plateau" means a validation gap between 0.14 and 0.18 for at least 10k consecutive steps; full tables are in `runs/plateau.md`.

- **The plateau is a trap, not a waypoint.** None of the depth-2 runs that ended near Bayes ever sat on it; they descended through the band in under 10k steps. 21 of the 95 depth-2 runs did sit on it, entering at 3k–79k steps. Of those 21, exactly one later left it for real: w16_h2_L2 seed 2 left the band at 41k and reached a gap under 0.05 at 87k, during its cooldown.
- **The pre-registered "escape" count is misleading.** That definition counts 5 escapes (median 26k, range 16k–41k). But 4 of the 5 are the stuck mixture seeds, which only slid from about 0.15 to 0.131–0.133. A post-hoc check (first gap under 0.05 after entering the plateau) leaves only the one genuine escape.
- **Which runs got stuck:**
  - 16 depth-2 runs never left the plateau: every width-8 micro run, 4 of the 10 width-4 micro runs, and the R^16 w16_h2_L2 seeds 3 and 4.
  - All 4 stuck cold-start mixture seeds.
  - Among 1-layer models, every 2-head run at width 16 or 32 ends there (final gap 0.157–0.164), which is their capacity limit.
- **So the plateau-escape step distribution has almost no mass.** Within 50–90k steps a run either never lands on the query-only solution, or it lands there and stays. The warm start avoids the plateau because it begins below it (initial gap 0.015–0.035).
