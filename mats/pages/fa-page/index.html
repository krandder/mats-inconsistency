Forecast aggregation world

Kelvin Azevedo Santos, MATS autumn 2026 (mentor: Deger Turan). 28 October 2026. Notes on World FA as built: what the networks are asked to learn, what the first pilot learned, and what is running. Status of the running arms read from their training logs at 20:15Z. File paths at the end.

In short

In the main family of World FA, four traders report a value plus an error. Every error has variance 1, and the errors of one pair of traders, unknown which, have correlation −0.8. Those two errors nearly cancel, so the best forecast gives the two reports of the pair weight 5 each and the other two reports weight 1. Past rounds, in which the value was revealed, show which pair it is. The query round on its own hardly does.

To use the past rounds, a network has to collect each trader's past errors, compare traders round by round, turn the comparison into weights, and put each weight on the right trader's report in the query round. The networks trained so far learn the rest of the task but not this. Their log loss is within 0.016 nats of the exact Bayes forecast that ignores the history; they recover none of the 0.21–0.25 nats the history is worth; and a trader's query-report token attends to its own trader's past reports no more than to other traders' (own/other ratio 1.00). Seven runs with a deeper network and aids for binding started at 19:02Z. The one that has finished (60,000 steps) recovers none of the history value either, and the other six had not moved off the history-blind level at 20:15Z.

In this family no single step lowers the achievable loss on its own; only all four together do. We think this is why training stalls, but we have not tested that directly.

1. The world, in one episode

An episode has \(N\) traders, \(T\) past rounds and one query round. In round \(t\) the value is \(v_t\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and trader \(i\) reports \(r_{t,i}=v_t+e_{t,i}\). The error vector \(e_t\sim\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\) is drawn afresh each round, but \(\Sigma\), the information structure, is drawn once per episode and never shown. Past rounds show every report and the realised \(v_t\); the query round shows only the \(N\) reports. The target is the query round's value \(v\), as a predictive distribution.

The runs so far use the main family of the pre-registration (the pilot also had null arms, section 4): \(N=4\), every error has variance 1, and exactly one pair of traders has error correlation −0.8, all other pairs 0. The six possible pairs are equally likely. Their covariance matrices have the same diagonal and the same eigenvalues (1.8, 1, 1, 0.2), so the pair shows only in how particular traders' errors move together.

Below is one episode drawn with the actual generator (fa_core.gen, torch seed 18) at \(N=4\), \(T=8\). How it was chosen is at the end of this section.

Hidden structure

Traders 1 and 3 carry the same hidden source with opposite signs, so \(e_1=\sqrt{0.8}\,\eta+z_1\) and \(e_3=-\sqrt{0.8}\,\eta+z_3\) with \(\eta\sim\mathcal N(0,1)\) and own noise of variance 0.2. The generator draws \(e_t\) from \(\mathcal N(0,\Sigma)\) directly; the picture describes the same \(\Sigma\).

$$\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&-0.8&0\\0&1&0&0\\-0.8&0&1&0\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$$

Why the forecasts differ

If the pair were known to be \((a,b)\), the Bayes forecast would be Gaussian with variance 1/13 (sd 0.277) and mean

$$m_{ab}=\frac{5r_a+5r_b+r_c+r_d}{13}.$$

The weights are \(\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1\): the inverse of the pair's 2×2 block has row sums \(1/(1+\rho)=5\) at \(\rho=-0.8\), the other traders get 1, and 13 is the prior precision 1 plus \(5+5+1+1\). Exact Bayes is the mixture of the six candidates \(\mathcal N(m_{ab},1/13)\), each weighted by the posterior probability of its pair:

From the query reports alone, each pair gets a probability between 0.13 and 0.20, so the history-blind forecast blends six candidates that range from 0.126 to 0.578: mean 0.347, sd 0.315, wider than any single candidate. The history removes this uncertainty. Up to a constant that is the same for all pairs, its log likelihood under pair \((a,b)\) is

$$\mathrm{score}(a,b)=-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(e_{t,a}+e_{t,b}\big)^2+\frac19\sum_{t=1}^{T}\big(e_{t,a}-e_{t,b}\big)^2,$$

because under that pair the sum of the two errors has variance 0.4 and their difference 3.6, against 2 and 2 for an uncorrelated pair. Pair 1–3 scores +3.6 and the next best −13.4, so with the history its probability is above 0.9999999 and exact Bayes is the candidate 0.493, sd 0.277.

That candidate is closer to \(v\) because the pair's errors also cancel in the query round: trader 1 is off by +0.50 and trader 3 by −0.37, so their average, 0.610, is off by 0.06. The equal-weight forecasts are pulled down mainly by trader 4, which is off by −0.69. The trained network says 0.348 with sd 0.312, almost exactly the history-blind forecast: its KL divergence from history-blind Bayes is 0.0002 nats, and from exact Bayes 0.12. The other three main-family pilot networks are within 0.0012 nats (KL) of history-blind Bayes on this episode too.

How typical the episode is. Over 4,000 episodes at \(N=4\), \(T=8\), the expected gain of exact Bayes over history-blind Bayes (their KL divergence, given the data) averages 0.20 nats with median 0.11, and the posterior puts more than 0.9 on the true pair in 84% of episodes. This episode's expected gain is 0.12 nats, the 54th percentile. It is the first seed, counting from 0, whose expected gain lies between the median and the 75th percentile and whose \(v\) falls in the middle half of the Bayes forecast. Seed 0 met the first condition, but its \(v\) lay 2.1 sd from the Bayes mean, which made its single-episode log losses misleading.

There are no positions; the tokens form a set. The pilot also tried a “column” layout, proposed in the Gemini critique, with \(2N+1\) tokens: one history token per trader holding \(h_i=T^{-1/2}\sum_t e_{t,i}u_t\), where \(u_t\) is \(\sqrt{32}\) times round \(t\)'s key, so that \(\langle h_i,h_j\rangle/32\approx S_{ij}/T\) with \(S_{ij}=\sum_t e_{t,i}e_{t,j}\); then the \(N\) query reports and the query token. This layout does the round-by-round matching in the input. A trader's history token and its query report are still linked only by the trader key.

Output. The final state of the query token is mapped to a mixture of 4 Gaussians (4 weights, 4 means, 4 log standard deviations). The loss is the negative log density of the true \(v\) under that mixture. Every batch is a fresh draw of episodes from the prior, so the output that minimises the expected loss is the exact Bayes predictive.

Network. A transformer without positional encoding (harness.SetPFN): a linear map from the 72 numbers to width 64, then pre-norm blocks (layer norm, 4-head self-attention, layer norm, MLP 64→256→64 with GELU, each with a residual connection), then a layer norm and a linear readout of the query token. Keys and values come from the report tokens only: every token, including the query token, attends to all report tokens, and no token attends to the query token.

3. What it has to compute

Exact Bayes in this family can be computed in four steps. A network may organise the computation differently, but to recover the history value it needs the same links between tokens, and in the report layout each step uses a different one:

Each cell is one token of the example episode: past rounds show the error \(e\) (the token also holds \(r\) and \(v\)), the query round shows \(r\) only. (a) The tokens of one trader share its key. (b) The tokens of one round share its key. (d) The query report of trader \(i\) shares trader \(i\)'s key with its past reports; nothing else links them. Shaded: the pair, traders 1 and 3.

  1. Gather each trader's past errors \(e_{1,i},\dots,e_{T,i}\): the tokens with trader \(i\)'s key.
  2. Compare traders round by round: for each pair, combine the two errors of the same round (tokens with the same round key) and add over rounds to get the score above, which uses the sums \(S_{aa}\), \(S_{bb}\), \(S_{ab}\). The six scores, plus a small term from the query reports, give the posterior over pairs.
  3. Turn the posterior into weights: \(\beta_i=\mathbb E\big[(\Sigma^{-1}\mathbf 1)_i\mid\text{data}\big]=1+4\,P(i\text{ is in the pair}\mid\text{data})\). In the example \(\beta=(5,1,5,1)\).
  4. Put each weight on the same trader's query report, again through the trader key. The mean is \(\sum_i\beta_ir_i/13\), in the example \((5\cdot1.046+0.452+5\cdot0.175-0.148)/13=0.493\), and the forecast is the mixture of the \(\mathcal N(m_{ab},1/13)\).

The four steps on the example episode. Each box passes its result to the next; the history value needs all four.

Why the loss gives almost no signal

First, the history-blind forecast already gets most of the achievable loss. Average log loss at \(T=32\) on 2,000 fresh episodes (pilot arm with the report layout, seed 0):

forecastlog loss (nats)
Prior \(\mathcal N(0,1)\)1.427
Correlation-blind Bayes0.445
History-blind Bayes0.348
Trained network0.354
Exact Bayes0.130

Of the 1.30 nats between the prior and exact Bayes, history-blind Bayes gets 1.08 (83%), and the trained network comes within 0.007 of it. The remaining 0.22 nats need the history.

Second, no single step earns any of those 0.22 nats on its own. This follows from the symmetries of the family:

  • One trader's own errors carry nothing: under all six pairs they are independent draws from \(\mathcal N(0,1)\). Step (a) alone gains nothing.
  • Comparisons that lose track of which trader is which carry nothing. The six pairs are relabellings of one another, so any summary of the history that stays the same when the traders' columns are permuted, for example the six pair correlations as an unlabelled list, has the same likelihood under every pair, and Bayes given such a summary is history-blind Bayes. (On the example, the log likelihood of the unlabelled set of columns is −42.756 under each of the six pairs.) Step (b) without the trader labels gains nothing.
  • Weights help only on the reports of the traders they belong to; on the wrong traders they hurt. Exact Bayes applied to histories whose columns were permuted among traders scores 1.23 nats at \(T=32\), against 0.348 for history-blind Bayes. So steps (a)–(c) gain nothing without (d), and (d) has nothing to carry without (a)–(c).

The gain therefore needs all four steps at once, and improving one step while the others are missing leaves the best achievable loss where it was. Our reading is that, once the history-blind solution is learned, gradient descent gets almost no signal toward any of the steps. We have not measured this directly, but the measurements so far fit it. In the report-layout pilot networks (512 episodes at \(T=32\), computed for this page with astra's diagnostic), attention from a trader's query-report token to its own trader's past reports, per token, equals attention to other traders' past reports: ratio 0.999–1.000 in both layers and 0.995–1.001 across heads, although these tokens put 43–66% of their attention on past reports. Permuting the history columns among traders changes the main-family networks' log loss by at most 1.1×10−4 nats at \(T=8\), 32 and 128, whereas it ruins exact Bayes (bullet above).

Compare the tiny three-source world in astra's binding paper (28 October). There, a reference that forgot which source each group of readings came from still kept 0.059 of the 0.185 nats the context was worth; stalled models used some of it (0.02–0.04 nats better than query-only Bayes), and 39 of 50 width-16 seeds met the acquisition criterion within 80k updates; first-layer own/other attention ratios were 14.6–233 in acquired models and 1.0–1.24 in stalled ones. In the FA main family the part of the history value that needs no binding is exactly zero.

How one pair's correlation sets the weights

Four traders with error variance 1; traders 1 and 3 have error correlation \(\rho\) and the rest are independent. With \(\Sigma\) known, Bayes gives each of the pair weight \(1/(1+\rho)\) and the others weight 1. The last line is the expected log-loss cost of ignoring the correlation (all weights 1), averaged over episodes, \(\mathbb E\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\mathcal N(m,V)\,\|\,\mathcal N(m',V')\big)\) in closed form.

The shares add up to less than 1; the rest is shrinkage toward the prior mean 0. Check against the generator (40,000 episodes): at \(\rho=-0.8\) the cost is 0.320 ± 0.003 nats (formula 0.318), at \(\rho=+0.8\) 0.061 ± 0.002 (formula 0.062). These costs assume \(\Sigma\) known. The history value is smaller because the query reports reveal part of the structure: about 0.22 nats at \(\rho=-0.8\) and 0.02 at \(\rho=+0.8\), which is why the pre-registered pilot used the negative pair as its main family and the positive pair as a null.
