# Ngo's scale-free agency and our open-latent definition (29 October 2026)

For Kelvin (theory lane, brief from main). Tags: [exact], [cited], [measured], [interpretation], [conjecture]. Sources and checks at the end.

## Summary
- Ngo (March 2025) wants one theory of agency that holds at every scale. Agents are coalitions of cooperating and competing subagents, recursively. Beliefs are aggregated by prediction markets, actions by auctions and values by voting, each chosen for incentive compatibility. He calls it "vague and tentative"; I found no proof of his central hypothesis since.
- Our definition formalises only the belief part, with honest components. There the link is exact: in a log market scoring rule (LMSR) market over the open latents, a Bayesian trader's trade is its log-likelihood and the market state is the sum of trades. The open latents at a cut are the securities a bounded consensus must carry.
- Main mismatches: his subagents are strategic and have goals. His formal follow-up pools beliefs geometrically with weights summing to one, which suits shared information; we pool likelihoods multiplicatively, the only Bayesian rule for private information.
- Ours gives him a communication reason for posted-consensus mechanisms, a candidate precise version of his "well-foundedness", and a cost for demarcating subagents. His gives us a caveat: our two-round result assumes an honest first round, which market theory guarantees only when the target is the only open latent.
- No decision needed. Three experiments in section 4.

## 1. What Ngo claims
"Towards a scale-free theory of intelligent agency" (LessWrong, 21 March 2025): a theory of agency should stay true when you "zoom in" or "zoom out". Expected-utility maximisation separates goals from beliefs, and averaging subagents' utilities is inflexible and not incentive compatible. Active inference makes goals rigid beliefs, but its agents are "cooperative within themselves", which privileges one level. His alternative, coalitional agency: subagents (a "roughly-internally-consistent cluster of beliefs and goals", a matter of degree) bargain over incentive-compatible decision procedures, not outcomes. Inside an active-inference agent, precision-weighted averaging (not incentive compatible) gives way to a prediction market for observations (modelled by Garrabrant induction), auctions for actions (Demski's BRIA) and random-ballot voting for values. Hypothesis: the two routes meet, with beliefs as market prices and goals as vote outputs.

Comments: answering cousin_it, he restricts to "merges that are constructible via some set of calls to the existing agents"; answering Nesov, "clustering is efficient". RogerDearnaley doubts strict scale-freedom; Pallavi Sudhir argues the revelation principle makes "incentive-compatible procedure" vacuous.

Since then (posts): well-foundedness (April 2025); goal-models, preferring "scale-free approaches under which inconsistencies mostly get resolved locally" (February 2026); Garrabrant-induction wealth as degree of truth, with "wealth is rivalrous, whereas degree of truth isn't" (June 2026); belief webs, which are locally but not globally consistent, with actions as self-fulfilling beliefs, goals as drives against empirical anchors, and an agent as "a densely-connected region" that "trusts updates from within" (June 2026); Knightian uncertainty (September); social theory (October). The formal output is Lee, Kondor and Ngo (arXiv:2509.06701, ICML 2026). Agents are outcome distributions with log-score welfare and a coalition is their log pool with weights summing to one. Strict unanimous gain is impossible under linear pooling or with two outcomes, and possible with three or more.

## 2. Correspondences and mismatches
| Ours | Ngo | Status |
|---|---|---|
| subtree with few open latents | subagent; "densely-connected region" | interpretation; ours adds a size: latent states separating inside from outside (nonnegative rank across the cut) |
| message: likelihood of the open latents | a trade. In LMSR over outcomes k of the open latents (liquidity b), a risk-neutral trader who takes the price p as prior, with evidence x conditionally independent of earlier traders' given k, buys b log L(x \| k) + c and is paid b log[L(x \| k*) / sum_k p_k L(x \| k)] | exact (Hanson 2007 for LMSR). LMSR is one way to run his market; Garrabrant induction is a different construction |
| merge: add log-likelihoods, sum out closing latents | the market maker adds shares; a junction-tree market maker marginalises (Laskey et al. 2018; your bayes-markets code); markets can run message passing (Storkey 2011) | cited |
| second-round message, Gaussian sources: score and curvature at the broadcast | predictive coding's precision-weighted prediction error tau(x - mu) and precision | exact |
| routing by labels | "each subagent gets one type of decision"; delegation | interpretation |
| rounds | trading against a posted price; predictive-coding iterations | interpretation |

Your 22 October design lines fit the same table [interpretation]. The bounded consensus is a market maker over a bounded set of securities (few open latents; exponential-family statistics if the size must be fixed for all N, T3). "Query before compression" is the posted price. "Forecast of what the consensus deems important, plus own contribution" is a trade on existing securities plus a proposed new one: a latent open at the agent's cut that the consensus does not yet carry. Ngo's framework has no mechanism for the second part.

Mismatches:
1. **Incentives.** Our components are honest or jointly trained; his markets exist for incentive compatibility. Our trained combining layers do the precision-weighted average he rejects: layer-2 weights track each source's log precision, median r = 0.87 [measured]. That is safe here because precision is inferred from each source's settled past errors, not reported by the source.
2. **Pooling.** Lee et al. pool beliefs geometrically with weights summing to one ("externally Bayesian", suited to shared information). We pool likelihoods multiplicatively with the prior counted once, the only "individualwise Bayesian" rule, suited to private information (Dietrich and List 2016, Thm 3). Our prior-as-sink entering once at the root is their calibration factor. With a common prior and conditionally independent evidence, no weights summing to one make the log pool of N >= 2 posteriors equal Bayes for all evidence: every weight would have to be 1 [exact].
3. **Mixture and product.** Markets of Kelly bettors price the wealth-weighted average belief, with wealth updated by Bayes' rule (Beygelzimer, Langford, Pennock 2012): a mixture. Random ballot is a mixture too. Our cross-source merge is a product. In the tiny-PFN world Bayes needs both: each source's likelihood mixes its reliability classes with weights from its settled track record (the Kelly-wealth update), and sources multiply [exact]. His "wealth is rivalrous, truth isn't" is this sum/product split [interpretation].
4. **Scope.** We have no actions, goals, self-reference or Knightian uncertainty, and use exact Bayes in a known model as the reference. His current programme is explicitly non-Bayesian.
5. **What is scale-free.** His: the whole theory, incentives included, at every scale. Ours: the merge rule. Message dimension may grow, and with bounded noisy messages must grow like log N (T11d, proven for a Gaussian model). That answers RogerDearnaley's doubt in a narrow sense.

## 3. What each side says about the other
Ours to Ngo:
- **Posted consensus.** A reason for markets and predictive coding that is independent of incentives. Without a broadcast, each report must describe its likelihood wherever the posterior might land: 48-64 numbers per source for KL <= 1e-3, and an empirical linear rank above 1,600 with three open latents. After a broadcast, 8 numbers reach KL of about 1e-7 at N = 1024 [measured; fixed codes, window-conditional KL]. Trained two-pass trees match a dense set transformer up to N = 64; one-pass trees do not [measured, draft today].
- **Which securities.** Garrabrant induction prices every sentence, so the choice never arises; a coalition with a bounded state must choose. The minimal exact choice at a cut is the open latents (T2, T2m). A market in the target alone fails on parity: Ostrovsky's (2012) motivating non-separable example is the XNOR of two traders' signals, where each trader's expectation stays fixed and truthful announcements never reveal the value, while a 1-bit message across the cut suffices. This is your learned-securities question.
- **Well-foundedness** ("conflicts between its subagents don't propagate down"; he does not yet know how to define it). His Fractistan (factions intermingled, "fractally dividing the country in two at every level") against Holistan (factions by territory) is our straddling-groups count. With intermingled shared-error groups, every node carries the latent of every group it splits; with a group-aligned hierarchy, cuts between groups carry only y* [exact, from the model]. Candidate definition, belief side: the number of latents open at each node [interpretation].
- **Demarcation**, which he sets aside ("how to demarcate regions in the first place"). For inference it is the routing cost: log N label bits when membership is visible (T5), about min(T, 1/rho^2) bits per member when it must be inferred from correlated behaviour (T7). Trained dense nets stall on this step: KL 0.194 without the routing mask against 0.011 with it at N = 12, no escape within 10M episodes at N = 24 [measured, draft].
- **Local against global consistency.** On a junction tree, locally consistent beliefs are globally consistent; with cycles they need not be (Wainwright and Jordan 2008, sec. 4.1). Conjecture: his locally-but-not-globally consistent belief webs are what bounded agents get on tangled tasks, where no hierarchy has few open latents.

Ngo to ours:
- **Honest first round.** With market losses each source trades twice. In Chen et al.'s (2010) finite-stage LMSR model, truthful betting is the unique weak perfect Bayesian equilibrium when signals are conditionally independent given the state, and is not one when signals are unconditionally independent. Conditional independence given y* is "only the target is open" (W0). With shared errors (W1, W2), first-round reticence is possible.
- **Herding** (his October open problem). Cascades need coarse public actions; with continuous unbounded actions they do not arise (Lee 1993, as summarised by Bikhchandani, Hirshleifer and Welch). Our second-round messages are private likelihoods at the consensus, not posteriors, so each source still counts once.

## 4. Experiments on our small worlds
**D1. Local market losses against end-to-end.** W0 and W1, N = 8 and 64. Agents are per-source networks over their own history; they read a posted market state and trade a few numbers in each of two passes. Train (a) end-to-end on the root's log loss, (b) each agent on its own LMSR payoff. Prediction: (b) matches (a) in W0. In W1, (b) is worse and first-pass trades carry less of each agent's evidence, more so with more straddling groups; securities on the groups' errors close the gap. Evidence: KL to exact Bayes per arm; probe R^2 of each agent's own sufficient statistic from its first-pass trade. Refuted if (b) matches (a) in W1 or fails in W0. Chen et al. cover complete markets, so whether their condition survives few-number trades is part of the test. Moves your design point 4 (structure from local market signals).

**D2. Learned securities are the open latents.** W1 with region hierarchies aligned with the groups (Holistan) or splitting them (Fractistan), and W2 (hidden pairs). Each region writes k learned slots after reading the root's broadcast. Prediction: the smallest k with KL <= 0.01 is about 1 when aligned and about 1 plus the straddling groups when split, flat in N. Slots linearly decode y* and the straddling groups' errors (R^2 >= 0.5). In W2, k stays large until a pilot re-routes the pairs. Refuted if k grows with N, or if KL <= 0.01 is reached without the slots decoding the open latents (then compare with the single-target minimum, T2m). Moves M2 and the bounded-consensus requirement.

**D3. Predictive coding inside trained transformers.** Dense set nets on W0 at N = 64 and 256, where rounds pay. Prediction: layer 1 forms a pilot, and layer-2 per-source features are each source's score and curvature at it. Evidence: probes (R^2 >= 0.5), and patching the pilot from another episode should move those features as the score predicts. Refuted if per-source features ignore the pilot while KL matches the two-pass tree. Moves M2.
