## 3. Deductive estimation where the whole state is small

**Setup.**
- Estimand: E[output], a vector. Score: nMSE = |estimate - truth|^2 / tr Var(output), as ARC uses. Truth: plain MC
  with 2^21-2^22 paths, whose own nMSE is 2-5e-7.
- Cost: FLOPs under the flopscope conventions of `work/arc-noisy` (matmul M N (2K - 1), normal draw 16, cdf 96,
  ...), quoted in units of one full MC path. "35 paths" means the FLOPs of 35 sampled paths. With N paths, plain
  MC's expected nMSE is exactly 1/N.
- Simple samplers:
  - MC;
  - antithetic pairs (ANT);
  - scrambled-Sobol RQMC on the input;
  - the regression control variate on the input x (E x = 0 is known), applied to MC and to RQMC (MC-reg,
    RQMC-reg).
  - The "best simple sampler" is the lowest of these at each budget. Each figure uses 16 replicates.
- Deductive, sample-free estimators:
  - K2: ARC's Gaussian closure. Mean and covariance pass through each ReLU in closed form, using exact bivariate
    ReLU moments (copied from `work/arc-noisy`).
  - K2-Stein: the same with the covariance linearised (Stein's lemma; means and variances stay exact). It costs n^3
    rather than n^2 x 2,500 FLOPs per layer.
- Hybrid HYB(l0): MC for the first l0 layers, then a deductive deep part. The sampled states are split into 8
  log-radius bins, and each bin is pushed through the remaining layers either by K2-Stein ("st") or by the noiseless
  map applied to the bin mean ("lin").

- **At its own budget, K2-Stein beats every simple sampler by 16-250x (median 43x) in MSE, at every depth (MEASURED).**
  - Its budget is 35 MC paths at n = 16 and 18-19 at n = 8.
  - RQMC is the strongest sampler. Its error falls like N^-1.5 to N^-2, and it reaches K2's accuracy at about
    100-1,000 paths.
  - Put as cost: K2-Stein reaches in 18-35 paths' FLOPs an accuracy that RQMC needs 5-30x more FLOPs for, and plain
    MC needs 2,500-43,000 paths for.
- **It has a floor.** K2's error is a bias, nMSE 2e-5 to 4e-4. Above about 1,000 paths RQMC wins, reaching 1e-7 to
  1e-6 at 16,384 paths (`runs/p3/*_samplers.json`).
  - The exact-moment K2 costs 15-23x more than K2-Stein for the same accuracy, so it only ties RQMC at n = 16 and
    loses 2-4x at n = 8.
- **Depth does not hurt it (MEASURED; prediction P10).** [RD-DEPTH]
  - Its cost grows linearly in L, as a sample's does, so the advantage at equal FLOPs does not shrink with depth.
  - Why (HEURISTIC): each layer adds V_l b_l / sqrt(L), and V_l is independent of everything before it. A closure
    error made in layer l therefore enters along a fresh random direction. The output error is then a random walk of
    L steps of size 1/sqrt(L), the size of a typical one-layer error whatever L is.
- **The problem is not trivial.**
  - The zero estimate has nMSE 0.17-0.32, and so does pushing the mean through the net.
  - A closure that keeps only variances, dropping all correlations, gets 2.2-2.8e-3, 20-100x worse than full K2
    (`logs/k2variants.log`).
- **What makes this regime easy (MEASURED).** The input is not forgotten:
  - the input direction explains 90-97% of the output variance, and its norm 0.4-4%;
  - every one of 2^20 inputs has its own activation pattern by layer 8 (n = 16).
- The map, however, is close to quadratic:
  - a linear function of x explains 82-87% of the output variance and a quadratic one 96-97%, at every depth. The
    plain nets score 8-60% and 10-90%.
  - The pre-activations stay close to Gaussian (`runs/gauss_*.json`; L = 256, 2 nets per width). Mean |skewness|:
    - n = 16: 0.02-0.03 at layer 8, 0.04-0.05 at 32, 0.08-0.13 at 128;
    - n = 8: 0.03-0.05, 0.07-0.13 and 0.10-0.20 at the same layers;
    - it grows to 0.13-0.46 at the output layer;
    - plain nets: 1.0-1.7 from layer 8 on.
  - The mean, which the linear part cannot produce, comes from the ReLU means. A Gaussian closure gets those right
    when the projections of the state are close to Gaussian.
- **Hybrids do not help here.** Without synchronisation, the radius-binned deep part is biased: 4e-5 to 1e-4 for
    HYB-st and 0.05-0.4 for HYB-lin. HYB loses to RQMC by 10-100x at every budget.

### 3.2 Plain nets: the deep part is free, the shallow part is the blocker

**K2 from the input fails (MEASURED).** It is off by nMSE 0.016-1.0 on the four n = 16, L = 256 nets and by 2-27 on
the n = 8 nets, and on a net whose output is mostly noise it reaches 1.2e7. The collapsed state is a ray whose
radius has a log-normal spread, and a Gaussian with the same mean and covariance puts mass at negative radius.
Dropping correlations makes it worse (16-218).

**The deep part, given the exact law at layer l0 (an oracle for the shallow part).** The radius-binned K2 runs from
layer l0 to L = 256. nMSE, truth resolution about 5e-7. The first column is the share of the output variance that the
input explains:

| net (input share) | l0 = 4 | 8 | 16 | 32 | 64 | 128 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| n = 16 seed 0 (0.994) | 0.18 | 0.054 | 6.7e-7 | 3.3e-5 | 1.8e-7 | 2.0e-7 |
| n = 16 seed 1 (0.998) | 5e-4 | 4e-4 | 3.2e-4 | 3.6e-4 | 3.7e-7 | 3.7e-7 |
| n = 16 seed 3 (0.988) | 1.0e-3 | 1.7e-3 | 3.1e-3 | 2.8e-3 | 1.9e-5 | 2.0e-5 |
| n = 8 seed 7 (0.994) | 0.053 | 1.7e-3 | 1.4e-5 | 1.7e-5 | 1.7e-5 | 2.1e-5 |
| n = 8 seed 2 (0.964): binned K2 / mean push | 3.9 / 7.9e-3 | 0.076 / 5.8e-5 | 0.068 / 7.1e-5 | 0.065 / 2.7e-5 | 0.031 / 8.5e-6 | 6.8e-4 / 1.4e-5 |

- On the five nets whose input explains >= 96% of the output variance, the deep part beyond l0 = 16-64 costs one
  weight-derived trajectory per radius bin. Its error is then 1e-7 to 2e-5 (MEASURED).
  - On n = 8 seed 2 that holds for the noiseless push of the mean, not for the binned K2.
  - The l0 needed to get below 1e-4 is 8-16 at n = 8 and 32-64 at n = 16, consistent with a synchronisation length
    of about n.
- On the three near-dead nets, where noise carries 23-89% of the output variance, neither deep part works.

**Hybrid at equal FLOPs (column (b) of ARC's bar).** n = 16, L = 256, seed 0, nMSE:

| budget (MC paths) | MC | best simple sampler | best hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256 | 3.6e-3 | 1.3e-3 (RQMC-reg) | 3.1e-4 (HYB16-lin) |
| 1,024 | 6.2e-4 | 2.2e-4 (MC-reg) | 1.1e-4 (HYB32-st) |
| 4,096 | 3.5e-4 | 9.3e-5 (RQMC) | 5.8e-5 (HYB32-st) |

The hybrid gains 2-4x over the best sampler at 256-1,024 paths, 1.6x at 4,096 and nothing at 16k paths, where its
deep-part bias of about 1e-5 shows (MEASURED on one net; the planned second seed was dropped for time).

**Why no sample-free method gets through the shallow part (MEASURED).**
- The shallow part is a narrow net of depth l0 ~ 16-64 on Gaussian input.
- An exact cell representation needs the joint activation patterns of layers 1..l. Among 2^20 inputs:
  - n = 8 (sigma = 0): 3,600-5,300 distinct patterns at layer 2, 77,000-82,000 at layer 8, and 90,000-116,000 from
    layer 16 on. Synchronisation stops further splitting. About half of these patterns are seen once, so the true
    count is higher.
  - n = 16: about 1,000,000 distinct patterns by layer 8, i.e. nearly every input has its own cell.
- Each cell would also need a Gaussian integral over a polyhedral cone in 8-16 dimensions.
- A grid at noise resolution needs (1/sigma)^(n-1) cells.
- So in the plain family the M1 difficulty does not disappear in narrow-deep nets. It moves into the first
  ~n ln(1/tolerance) layers: a net whose depth is proportional to its width, the regime M1 is about.

### 3.3 Tails: P(|output| > t) at the 1e-3 and 1e-4 quantiles

**The Gaussian closure is wrong in the tails (MEASURED).**
- We computed the exact tail of K2's output Gaussian with Imhof's formula, checked against sampling that Gaussian.
- At n = 16 it is 3-77x too small, at n = 8 400-60,000x. The quadratic part of the map makes the true tails heavier.

**Positive homogeneity gives a much better sampler (MEASURED).**
- A bias-free ReLU net satisfies out(r u) = r out(u) without noise. So the radius can be integrated exactly for
  each sampled direction ("RB"): P = E_u Q_chi_n(t / |out(u)|).
- At equal FLOPs RB lowers the relative MSE 2-90x against MC. Examples:
  - residual n = 16 at q = 1e-3 and 4,096 paths: 0.011 and 0.005 vs MC 0.18 and 0.29;
  - plain n = 8 at q = 1e-4 and 16,384 paths: 0.008 vs 0.45.
- With sigma = 1e-3 the identity is approximate (the noise is rescaled to a typical radius). A direct check against
  2^21 MC paths agrees to within 2% at both quantiles, with and without noise (`logs/check_rb.log`).

**The plain hybrid in the tails varies by net.**
- With the radius integrated, the attractor-linear deep map (HYB32-RB) is 9-200x better than MC on three nets: for
  example 0.0046 vs 0.94 at q = 1e-4, n = 16 seed 1.
- On the other two its error stays at 35-140% relative.

**Not M3.** None of these is a certified bound.
