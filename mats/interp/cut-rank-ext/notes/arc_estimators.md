### E1. Christiano, Neyman, Xu (2022), "Formalizing the presumption of independence"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06738 (PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.06738). Submitted 12 Nov 2022 (v1), 61 pp. Authors: Paul Christiano, Eric Neyman, Mark Xu.
TAG: [FT-part]. Read: Sec. 3, 6.1-6.3, App. B.4-B.5, App. D.1-D.7.3 (pp.43-56), App. F. Not read: Sec. 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, App. A, C, E.

E1.0 Heuristic estimator (formalism, not an algorithm)
- SOURCE: Sec. 3 (p.5-6). "a heuristic estimator ~P: an efficient program which takes as input a statement phi and a set of heuristic arguments pi_1,...,pi_n, then outputs a best guess ~P(phi, pi_1,...,pi_n) in [0,1]" (p.5); generalized to ~E(X, pi_1..pi_n) for a real quantity X (p.6).
- STATE/NONLINEARITY/COST: none; no general algorithm given. App. D (p.43-44): "We believe that there likely exists an estimation algorithm that corrects these deficiencies. Finding such an algorithm is our current research priority for formalizing the presumption of independence."
- Deduction vs induction (App. B.4, p.31-32): Monte Carlo is called "inductive"; ARC is "particularly interested in deductive heuristic estimators, which deduce the relevant structural facts about C directly from the definition". B.4 also separates "Forwards" reasoning and "Backwards" reasoning (Bayes), and says a heuristic estimator is "more like calculating the prior distribution over x2 by calculating p(x1)p(x2|x1)".

E1.1 Independent-gates estimator for boolean circuits
- SOURCE: Sec. 6.2 (p.11), Fig. 2 (p.12). ESTIMATES: P(C)=Pr[C(z)=1], z uniform. STATE: one number E~(x_k) per wire. NONLINEARITY: gate formulas under independence (AND: product; OR: 1-(1-a)(1-b)). READOUT: value at the output wire. STRUCTURE: gate-by-gate, forward only, no samples, no adaptivity. COST: linear in circuit size (derived). ERROR: none stated; example E~(C)=30/64 vs true 2/8. STATUS: worked example only.

E1.2 Mean propagation (arithmetic circuits, Gaussian inputs)
- SOURCE: App. D.2 (p.45). ESTIMATES: E_{N(0,I)}[C] for an arithmetic circuit (sum, product, constant gates). STATE: mean mu_k per wire. RULES: sum: mu_a+mu_b; product: mu_a*mu_b; input wire 0. READOUT: mu at output wire. No ReLU or other activation is defined in this paper's circuits. STRUCTURE: forward over wires; deterministic. ERROR: none ("easy to construct circuits where it gives a worse estimate than nothing", p.46). STATUS: described, no experiments. (Wu et al. 2026 App. B fn.12 notes their "mean propagation" differs: it also tracks the covariance trace.)

E1.3 Covariance propagation, and sparse covariance propagation
- SOURCE: App. D.3 (p.46), D.4 (p.47). STATE: mu_k per wire and sigma_jk for all pairs j<=k (all pairs: O(|C|^2) numbers). NONLINEARITY: for a product gate x_k=x_i x_j uses "assume that xi, xj, and xl are jointly Gaussian": mu_k=mu_i mu_j+sigma_ij; sigma_kl=sigma_il mu_j+mu_i sigma_jl; sigma_kk=sigma_ij^2+2 sigma_ij mu_i mu_j+sigma_ii sigma_jj+sigma_ii mu_j^2+sigma_jj mu_i^2 (eq., from extracted text). Justification: "maximum entropy distribution given a particular covariance matrix".
- STRUCTURE: forward over wires, deterministic. Sparse version: argument pi = set of pairs (i,j) to track; untracked covariances replaced by 0; time O(|C|+|union pi_i|) (D.4). COST (full): "a n^2 time algorithm for estimating the output of a circuit with n gates" (D.4 p.47). ERROR: none. STATUS: "Empirically we've found this estimate is often much more reasonable than simply propagating means; for example we've evaluated it for small random circuits or shallow neural networks" (p.46); no numbers given.

E1.4 Cumulant propagation (origin of ARC's later cumulant propagation)
- SOURCE: App. D.5-D.6 (p.47-50), Algorithm 1 (p.50). ESTIMATES: E[C], arithmetic circuit, Gaussian inputs. STATE: joint cumulants kappa(x_i1,...,x_ir) for tuples in a set pi of tuples (e.g. all tuples of order <= n); "Whenever a cumulant we are not tracking appears in an equation, we assume that it is zero". RULES: derived from the cumulant/moment partition identity (Eq. 4); sum gates linear; product gates via partition sum. READOUT: cumulant of the single output wire (= mean). STRUCTURE: forward over wires; deterministic; adaptivity only through the choice of the tuple set pi ("advice"). COST: as written exponential in partitions; "can easily be sped up to O~(|pi|^2)" (p.49). ERROR: none; "cumulant propagation often produces negative estimates E~(X^2) < 0" (D.7, p.49). STATUS: "In practice, we find that tracking these higher cumulants continues to improve our estimates at the expense of additional compute (we don't report experiments here)" (p.49).

E1.5 Subset-of-terms estimators
- D.7.2 (p.53-54): sparse covariance propagation for linear circuits y=Az, x=By, estimate Var(x_i)=sum_{j,k,l} B_ij B_ik A_jl A_kl by keeping O(m) of the Cov(y_j,y_k) terms; time O(m^2) vs O(m^omega); shown to give a negative variance (m - 1.8(m-1) < 0) for alternating-sign rows; maximum-entropy fix described only for the tridiagonal special case.
- D.7.3 (p.55-56): E~(perm(A),pi)=sum over a chosen set pi of permutations of prod_i A_{i,sigma(i)}; "usually a poor estimate unless the set pi is exponentially large"; can be negative for PSD A; better estimator only for commuting permutations.
- Both are proposed/analysed examples; no experiments read.

### E2. Wu, Lecomte, Winer, Robinson, Hilton, Christiano (2026), "Estimating the expected output of wide random MLPs more efficiently than sampling"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05179 ; PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.05179 (v2, 14 May 2026; v1 6 May 2026; 68 pp.). Companion post: https://www.alignment.org/blog/mechanistic-estimation-for-wide-random-mlps/ (Jacob Hilton, May 7th, 2026) [FT]. Code: https://github.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation (package src/mlp_kprop; 9 commits, MIT; README and TODO.md read 2026-10-31 ~10:56 UTC).
TAG: [FT-part]. Read: Sec. 1-9, App. A-J (all), App. S.1, S.3.3-S.3.5 (part), S.4 (all), S.5 intro and S.5.2 start, S.6 excerpt, S.7 start, S.8.5 (part). Not read in detail: proofs in S.2.3-S.2.6, S.5.3, S.7, S.8.

Common setting (Sec. 2.1-2.2, p.3). M_theta(x)=W^(L+1) phi_L(W^(L) ... phi_1(W^(1) x)), all dimensions n (input = hidden = output), no biases (biases in App. F), theta entries i.i.d. N(0,2/n) (He), X ~ N(0,I_n). Estimate the vector E_X[M_theta(X)] from the weights; MSE O(eps^2) averaged over theta per output neuron ("quenched" average over X for fixed weights, Sec. 1 p.2). MC baseline: N=ceil(1/eps^2) samples, time Theta(n^2/eps^2) (Sec. 2.3, p.4); "variance-normalized MSE" of MC with N samples is exactly 1/N (Sec. 6.1, p.7). Depth L treated as a constant in the theorems.

E2a. Basic K-th order cumulant propagation ("basic"), general K; K=1 and K=2 are the special cases "mean propagation" and "covariance propagation"
- WHAT IT ESTIMATES: E_X[M_theta(X)] (mean of last layer); also, with a threshold final activation, a low probability (E2f).
- STATE (S.4.1, p.46-48): after each layer, tensors eta_r[X^(l)] in S^{r-2 s_K(r)}(R^n) for r<=R, where s_K(r)=0 for r<=K, r/2 for r=K+1 even, infinity otherwise; R=K (K even) or K+1 (K odd). In words: the full symmetric cumulant tensors of orders 1..K of the activation vector X^(l) in R^n, plus, for odd K only, ONE extra scalar (the full trace of the (K+1)th-order cumulant, "tracked via the scalar harmonic component of kappa_{K+1}", S.1.1 p.29). Dimension (derived): sum_{r=1..K} C(n+r-1,r) (+1 if K odd), leading term n^K/K!. K=1: mean vector mu in R^n and a scalar sigma^2_mean (the average variance); K=2: mu in R^n and Sigma in R^{n x n} (Alg. 1, 2, App. B p.16). The algorithm also forms the pre-activation cumulants Z^(l+1) by the linear step (see next line) but the object handed to the next layer is the activation tensors.
- LINEAR STEP: cumulants are multilinear, kappa_r[Z]_{i1..ir} = sum_{j1..jr} kappa_r[X]_{j1..jr} prod_s W_{is,js} ("symmetric contraction", S.1.2 p.30), time O(n^{r+1}) per order, so O(n^{K+1}) per layer. Pre-activation mean and variance vectors are read off (S.4.1 step 2b).
- NONLINEARITY (activation step): "truncation of the diagram summation formula for cumulants" (Sec. 4.1 p.5): the Hermite-based diagram summation formula (Thm A.2, App. A p.14; proved for polynomial phi): kappa[phi(Z_j1),...,phi(Z_jr)] = sum_{k in Z^r_{>=0}} prod_v (1/k_v!) bhat phi^{(E[Z_jv],Var[Z_jv])}_{k_v} * sum over connected [<=2]-mixed diagrams pi of prod_{S in pi} kappa[Z_jv : (v,w) in S] (eq., from extracted text). "we do not use a single Hermite expansion of phi, but rather take separate Hermite expansions of phi with respect to Gaussian distributions with means and variances matching each of Z_1..Z_n" (p.15). Two adjustments (Sec. 4.1): (1) "power cumulants": apply the expansion to powers of phi to get cumulants with distinct indices, then convert to cumulants by the partition formula (Prop. S.3.7); (2) the extra trace for odd K. Then project onto the (<= r-2 s_K)-harmonic components (S.4.1 step 2d). Truncation rule: sum over k with |k|<=2K-1 and vector partitions with k(nu)<=K (eq. (13)-(14), p.47). For POLYNOMIAL phi the Hermite series is finite and Thm A.2 is an exact identity ("for polynomials phi, all Hermite coefficients of degree larger than deg(phi) are zero, so this summation is finite", S.3.2 p.44); "When phi is not a polynomial, the sum in (10) is infinite and possibly diverges", and "a careful truncation of the series via a condition (14) on vector partitions results in an approximation of the cumulant that is sufficient to obtain the desired MSE bound; see Section S.6" (p.44).
- CLOSURE: everything not in the list above is set to zero (cumulants of order > K, except the one extra scalar). K=2 is the Gaussian closure: "we approximate the mean and covariance matrix of phi(Z) by the mean and covariance matrix of phi(Y), where Y is a multivariate Gaussian with the same mean and covariance matrix as Z" (App. B.2 p.17). Higher K = Gaussian plus K-th-order deviations; S.6.2 (p.57) says the formula can be "motivate[d] ... through the theory of Edgeworth expansions". Sec. 8.1: precursor Fischer et al. 2022 use "Hermite expansions (or equivalently Gram-Charlier expansions)". The repo calls Hermite coefficients "Wick (i.e. Hermite) coefficients" (src/mlp_kprop/wick.py).
- RELU HANDLING: closed form, not sampled. "When the activation functions phi_1..phi_L are piecewise polynomials, such as when they are all ReLU, the Gaussian expectations in these algorithms can be computed straightforwardly ... expressed in terms of the standard Gaussian density function and cumulative distribution function evaluated at the interval endpoints" (App. B p.17). Hermite coefficients of ReLU and of its powers: Prop. S.3.8 (p.45), three cases k=p, k>p, k<p, in terms of the standard Gaussian pdf and cdf (the paper writes them phi and Phi, the same letter as the activation) and the Hermite polynomials He_i, with a singularity at sigma^2=0. S.4.1 (p.48): "each Hermite coefficient is computed using Proposition S.3.8 if phi_l = ReLU and Gauss-Hermite otherwise". GELU and tanh: Gauss-Hermite quadrature with m=100 nodes (App. F.3 p.21), with the footnote (p.21) "This method is arguably not 'sample-free' and hence not a 'mechanistic' way to estimate this one-dimensional integral". K=2 pseudocode (Alg. 2, p.16): a_i=E[phi(Y)], b_i=E[phi(Y)^2], c_i=(1/sqrt(Sigma_ii)) E[phi(Y)(Y-mu_i)/sqrt(Sigma_ii)] with Y~N(mu_i,Sigma_ii); then mu_i<-a_i, Sigma_ii<-b_i-a_i^2, Sigma_ij<-Sigma_ij c_i c_j (i != j): the off-diagonal covariance uses only the leading Hermite term (Cor. A.3 p.15; App. B.2).
- READOUT: "Once we reach the output of the network, we return the final estimated first-order cumulant vector" (Sec. 4.1 p.5); S.4.1 step 3 (p.48): "Return W^(L+1) eta_1[X^(L)]". So the last step is a fixed linear map (the last weight matrix) of the estimated mean of the last activation layer. The map from a layer-l summary to that mean is NOT linear: it passes through Hermite coefficients that are nonlinear functions (built from the Gaussian pdf and cdf, for ReLU) of the per-neuron estimated mean and variance, and is polynomial in the higher-order cumulant entries (derived from Thm A.2 / S.2.1).
- STRUCTURE: layer-by-layer, forward only. "We start with the input distribution and propagate this forward through the network layer by layer until we reach the output." (Sec. 1 p.2). No backward pass, no samples inside the estimator (samples are used only for ground truth, baselines, and the Gauss-Hermite nodes are deterministic). Not adaptive: K is fixed from eps ("we take K to be the smallest integer such that 1/n^K <= eps^2", S.1.1 p.29; "roughly speaking, if eps(n)^2 ~ 1/n^K, then we track cumulants of order at most K", Sec. 1 p.2). Flexibility: augmented variant (E2b); the paper says it "serves as an example of how our algorithm can be flexibly adapted based on the computational budget" (Sec. 4.2).
- COST: basic time O(n^{K+1}) per layer, total <= c'_K L n^{K+1} (conjectured form, Sec. 5 p.6); Thm 5.1: time O(n^2/eps^2); FLOP counts (Table 1, App. J p.27, viewed as image): K=1: 4n^2 L+66nL; K=2: 7n^3 L+26n^2 L; K=3 basic: (7/3)n^4 L+252 n^3 L; K=4 basic: (5/4)n^5 L+224 n^4 L (these count symmetric-tensor savings by a mock kernel, App. J p.26). Wall-clock: "In most cases, our algorithms underperform sampling" (App. I p.25; PyTorch, B200 GPUs).
- ERROR (stated): Thm 5.1 (p.6, polynomial activations only): for fixed L, noticeable eps(n) (eps(n)>=1/n^k for some k, fn.8), E_theta[(G(theta,eps)_i - E_X M_theta(X)_i)^2]=O(eps(n)^2) as n->infinity, time O(n^2/eps^2). Thm 5.2: if also eps(n)^2=O(1/n^2), time O(n/eps^2) (factorized version). Conjecture (Sec. 5 p.6): "we conjecture that our algorithms have mean squared error at most c_K (L/n)^K, although the dependence on L is difficult to analyze precisely"; runtime "at most c'_K L n^{K+1}" (basic) and "at most c''_K L^2 n^K whenever K >= 3" (factorized). Sec. 5 (p.6): "up to constant factors, the basic version of our algorithm matches the performance of Monte Carlo sampling in terms of mean squared error for a given computational budget, while the factorized version outperforms Monte Carlo sampling by a factor equal to the network width n, providing eps is not too large"; Sec. 1 (p.2): "for any given depth, our algorithms are guaranteed to outperform Monte Carlo sampling at sufficiently large width, although the dependence of our algorithms on depth is worse." Footnote 9: "A mean squared error of eps(n)^2=O(1/n^2) is achieved with K>=2, which costs as much as sampling with Omega(n) samples." S.4 (p.46): "Our basic cumulant propagation estimator takes O(n^{K+1}) time and incurs O(n^{-K}) MSE for all integral K>=0, thus matching sampling for non-integral K as well (by taking the floor)." Blog fn.2: the O(n/eps^2) result "works providing that eps^2 grows like 1/n^k for some k>=2".
- STATUS: proofs only for polynomial activations: "We believe that with additional effort, both results can be extended from polynomials to general activation functions" (Sec. 5 p.6, fn.10); S.6 (p.56-57): tameness not proven for ReLU ("the Hermite coefficients of ReLU have a singularity at sigma^2=0, so much more care is required"); "we do not expect this infinite summation formula to converge at any fixed width n, but instead that a fixed truncation should be an asymptotically good approximation as n -> infinity". Implemented and tested on ReLU MLPs: widths 4..256, hidden layers 2..12, K=1..4, 5 seeds, ground truth from 2^34 samples (Sec. 6.1 p.7); Fig. 1 (L=4, n=256): beats MC over 7 orders of magnitude of FLOPs; Sec. 6.2: factorized and factorized-augmented beat MC by about 1,000x (L=2) and 10-100x (L=4) at n=256, underperform MC at L=8 once K=4; empirical MSE ~ 1/n^K (Fig. 3a) and ~ L^K (Fig. 8, App. D). GELU and tanh (App. F, qualitatively similar to ReLU).
- DEPTH, NARROW NETS, eps->0: App. D (p.19): "This depth scaling is worse than Monte Carlo sampling, whose error does not increase with depth. We believe that a sample-free algorithm can be developed whose error also does not increase with depth either, but we leave this problem to future work." App. C (p.18): "Our algorithms perform poorly at low width, especially when the number of hidden layers L is large, and can even get worse with the maximum cumulant order K in this setting. However, for any fixed L and K, our algorithms match or exceed the performance of sampling as the width grows." Sec. 7.3 (p.10): "Even for random MLPs, we do not know whether it is possible to match the performance of random sampling at large depth (i.e., asymptotically as the number of hidden layers L tends to infinity), or for narrow networks (i.e., asymptotically as the error tolerance eps -> 0 while the width n is held fixed)." Blog fn.7: "It remains an open problem to match the performance of random sampling for random MLPs in other regimes, for example, with the depth growing linearly in the width."
- CODE vs PAPER (present-state claim, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alignment-research-center/mlp_cumulant_propagation/main/TODO.md, read 2026-10-31 ~10:56 UTC): TODO.md says the nonlinear-step truncation "was tightened to match equations (13)-(14) of the paper; the implementation previously summed strictly more terms of the Edgeworth expansion than the paper describes", that "All cached kprop outputs, data files, and FLOP counts (and any downstream figures) for k_max >= 2 are stale and need to be refreshed", and that factorized-augmented "computes a different estimate than (unfactorized) augmented" and "The paper needs updating".

E2b. Augmented ("augmented", "factorized augmented")
- Sec. 4.2 (p.5), S.4.2 (p.48). Tracks additionally "the entire tensor except for its traceless component" of the (K+1)th cumulant (K odd), i.e. replaces s_K by s_K^aug(r)=0 (r<=K), 1 (r=K+1), r/2 (r=K+2 even), infinity otherwise. Effect: better MSE constant, same leading-order time when unfactorized and K>=3 (fn.20: for K<3 augmenting raises leading FLOPs). Table 1: K=2 augmented 11n^3 L+150n^2 L; K=3 (7/3)n^4 L+260n^3 L; K=4 (5/4)n^5 L+3083 n^4 L. All other fields as E2a.

E2c. Factorized ("factorized", "factorized augmented")
- Sec. 4.3 (p.6), S.4.3 (p.48-50), S.8.5 (p.67, factorization for arbitrary K>=3: top tensor T=(1/K!) sum_sigma sum_{s=2..floor(K/2)} sum_{t=1..T} A^(s)_{i_sigma(1..s),t} B^(s)_{i_sigma(s+1..K),t} with T=Theta(n) factors, "Lower-degree cumulants are left unfactored"). "For K >= 3, the tensor of Kth-order cumulants admits a factorized representation of size n^{K-1} that can be preserved through each step of the algorithm. By representing this tensor in factorized form, we can improve the runtime of our algorithm by a factor of n, up to constant factors, without changing its final output." STATE: top-order tensor kept as J=O(n) factors (K=3: T = (1/6) sum_sigma sum_j A_{i_sigma(1),j} B_{i_sigma(2),j} C_{i_sigma(3),j}, A,B,C in R^{n x J}); lower-order cumulants unfactored; "a running list of factors is propagated through the network, adding a constant number of new factors at each layer" (S.4.3), hence time grows like L^2. COST: <= c''_K L^2 n^K for K>=3 (Sec. 5); Table 1: K=3 30n^3 L^2+39n^3 L; K=4 24n^4 L^2+(79/3)n^4 L; factorized-augmented K=3 72n^3 L^2+264n^3 L, K=4 60n^4 L^2+1069 n^4 L. Same estimate as E2a (paper's claim; but see CODE vs PAPER note above for the augmented case). At low depth factorized uses fewer FLOPs, at high enough depth (fixed width) more (App. C p.18).

E2d. Ablated cumulant propagation (diagnostic, not proposed)
- Sec. 6.4, App. E (p.7-8, 20): no power cumulants and no extra trace. MSE is O(1) in n instead of O(1/n^K) (Fig. 3b), "no longer competitive with Monte Carlo sampling".

E2e. Polynomial algorithm (proof device)
- S.1.4 and S.2 (p.29-42 area): uses the polynomial diagram summation formula (Thm S.2.1) with no Hermite coefficients, cannot be truncated; "sufficient to prove Theorem 5.1 ... but it is of limited practical interest" (S.1 p.29). Not run.

E2f. Low-probability variant and its baselines
- Sec. 6.6 (p.8), App. G (p.23). Final ReLU replaced by the threshold 1_{z>3}; factorized K=3. Width 256, 4 hidden layers (3 ReLU + threshold), 205 seeds over 7 probability buckets. "using a similar number of FLOPs to Monte Carlo sampling with N = 5,695 samples, our algorithm achieves a relative error of under 30% for probabilities 100 times lower than 1/N, and continues to outperform all baselines until the probabilities are nearly 10,000 lower." Baselines (ARC-defined hybrids): MC with Gaussian extrapolation (sample mean and variance of the inputs to the threshold, Gaussian tail; "similar to the Gaussian Logit Difference method"); MC with cumulant extrapolation (sample mean, variance and third cumulant of the threshold inputs, then the usual Hermite expansion through the threshold); Zero; plain MC. Result: "once the probability drops below 1/(number of samples), cumulant propagation performs best, followed by Monte Carlo sampling with cumulant extrapolation ...". At sufficiently small probabilities "none of the methods significantly outperform zero".

E2g. Mechanistic distillation (training, not estimation of a fixed net)
- Sec. 6.7, App. H (p.8-9, 24). Basic K=2 on a block-diagonal stacked (teacher, student) network with duplicated-input covariance [[I,I],[I,I]]; loss E[(A-B)^2]=(E A-E B)^2+Var A+Var B-2 Cov(A,B). Teacher width 16, student 8, 4 hidden layers, ReLU, 256 steps. "slightly less efficient than distillation using stochastic gradient descent" (batch 36); estimate "has bias but no variance".

E2h. Statements about extending beyond random nets (not estimators)
- Sec. 7.2 (p.10): treat trained weights as "a structured component ... together with a random component" (structure vs randomness); Blog (wide random MLPs, "Extending to trained networks"): "We suspect that this can be made to work with some sort of auxiliary 'advice' given to the estimation algorithm, which somehow points out which of these higher-order deviations to track, and which gets updated incrementally with each gradient step." Fn.9 of the blog: "We don't think this approach works quite as we have stated it. For example, if the model starts by computing a one-way function of the weights, we may want an efficient compression of the output of this one-way function, rather than of the weights themselves." Sec. 7.1 (p.9): conjecture that "the performance of any estimation procedure can be matched (or exceeded) by a mechanistic one, providing we are given the training trajectory or selection process of any parameters involved."

### E3. Hilton, "Deductive estimation for random halfspace intersections" (ARC note) : deduction-projection with harmonic projection
URL: https://www.alignment.org/content/files/2026/05/deductive_estimation_for_random_halfspace_intersections.pdf (10 pp., dated "May 2, 2025" in the PDF; hosted May 2026; linked from the random-products post). Author: Jacob Hilton. TAG: [FT] (all 10 pages).
- ESTIMATES: Pr_{x~N(0,I_n)}[x.v^(i) >= 0 for all i=1..k] (orthant probability of N(0,Sigma), Sigma_ij=v^(i).v^(j)); equivalently the probability a 1-layer bias-free ReLU net has all k outputs on. Instances: v^(i) with i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries; MSE over random instances (Sec. 1).
- GENERAL SCHEME (Sec. 2, p.2): "deduction-projection estimator [Wu, 2025]": propagate "one layer at a time" (here k sub-layers, one halfspace each); after each deduction apply a "projection" to avoid exponential blowup. "Importantly, however, the object we propagate through the network need not represent an actual distribution ... We call an object that can be queried for expectations an expectation machine. More precisely, an expectation machine for a distribution D over R^n is an algorithm that accepts the representation of a function f: R^n -> R and produces an estimate for E_{x~D}[f(x)]." Here the machine is g~ with estimate <f|g~> := E_{x~N(0,I_n)}[f(x) g~(x)] (an unnormalised signed "density" g~ times the Gaussian).
- STATE: g~^(i): a polynomial of degree <= D on the sphere S^{n-1} (Sec. 4-5). Dimension: m=C(n+D,n)-C(n+D-2,n) harmonic polynomials of degree <= D (Sec. 6, Dai-Xu Cor. 1.1.5); implementation carries monomial coefficients, m'=C(n+D,n) ~ m for D=o(n).
- STEP: deduction g^(i)=g~^(i-1) * 1_{x.v^(i)>=0}; projection g~^(i)=sum_{d<=D} proj_d g^(i), proj_d g(x)=(1/|S^{n-1}|) int g(y) ((d+alpha)/alpha) C_d^(alpha)(x.y) dsigma(y), alpha=(n-2)/2 (Funk-Hecke; Sec. 4). Combined formula Eq. (1) p.5. Output <1|g~^(k)> = (1/|S^{n-1}|) int g~^(k) dsigma (Eq. (2)).
- WHAT IS PROJECTED ONTO WHAT: the projection subspace is the span of the top-m eigenfunctions of E_f[|f><f|] where f^(i)=prod of the remaining k-i halfspace indicators ("instead of tailoring our approximation to the specific set of remaining halfspaces, we merely tailor our approximation to the fact that some random set of halfspaces remain", Sec. 3). Kernel K(x,y)=E[f(x)f(y)] = ((1/2)(1-theta(x,y)/pi))^{k-i}; eigenfunctions are harmonic polynomials of x/|x|; eigenvalue depends only on degree (Sec. 4). The subspace is precomputed before seeing the instance ("Before seeing the problem instance, precompute the eigenfunctions ... with the largest m=O(1/eps^2) eigenvalues", Sec. 3).
- SKETCHING THEOREM (Sec. 3, p.3), verbatim: "Theorem 3.1 (Sketching theorem, informal). Let f be a random function R^n -> R and eps>0. Then there is a 'sketching function' ~(.) on functions R^n -> R such that, for any g: R^n -> R: ~g has low mean squared error on inner products with f: E_f[(<f|~g> - <f|g>)^2] < eps^2 E_f[<f|f>] <g|g>; ~g can be specified using O(1/eps^2) bits." Proof sketch: orthogonal projection of g onto the top m=floor(1/eps^2) eigenvectors of E_f[f f^T]; error <= (1/(m+1)) tr(E_f[f f^T]) g^T g. "A more formal statement can be made using the framework of communication complexity: the 'sketch' must communicate enough information about g to be able to accurately estimate the required inner products." "The required number of bits has been chosen to roughly match what we can achieve with sampling" (draw O(1/eps^2) samples from g^2 N(0,I)/<g|g>).
- READOUT (derived from Eq. (1)-(2)): at every step the map g~^(i-1) -> g~^(i) is linear (multiplication by an indicator, then an orthogonal projection onto a fixed subspace), and the output is a linear functional of g~^(k). Hence, for fixed remaining halfspaces, the final estimate is a LINEAR functional of the carried coefficient vector g~^(i). The operators depend on the instance through v^(i) only.
- STRUCTURE: forward over halfspaces (sub-layers); the "back" (remaining halfspaces) enters only through its distribution (the eigenproblem), not through a pass on the instance; no samples; not adaptive (fixed D, fixed subspace).
- COST (Sec. 6, p.6): "O(k m D^2) = O(k m (log m)^2) = O(k (log 1/eps)^2 / eps^2)" vs MC "O(nk/eps^2)", "a factor of only (log 1/eps)^2/n"; uses n-1 Givens rotations to axis-align v^(i) and a Hermite-polynomial route for the O(m D^2) step.
- ERROR (Sec. 7, 9): MSE "O(eps^2/2^k), and possibly even better"; the crude telescoping bound is k^2 eps^2/2^k and the sharper bound is argued in a Google Drive write-up that I did not open. Spectrum (Fig. 1): m-th largest eigenvalue "around Theta(1/m^2) when k-i is small compared to n", "we suspect that the mth largest eigenvalue gets closer to Theta(1/m) as k-i becomes large".
- STATUS: implemented (footnote links https://github.com/alignment-research-center/deduct-est ... harmonic_prop.py, a commit URL; the repo page returned HTTP 404 on 2026-10-31 ~10:55 UTC); tested on n in {2,4,8,16} x k in {2,4,8,16}, 256 instances (importance-sampled by intersection size), ground truth from SciPy's GHK/Genz code (Sec. 8). "Our deductive algorithm significantly outperforms the naive Monte Carlo sampling algorithm if we trade off one polynomial term for one sample. That being said, a single sample is much faster to deal with than a single term in practice." (Sec. 8 p.9).
- Same algorithm described in "Competing with sampling" (E12) as "compute the best low-degree polynomial approximation H~_{<=t} to H~_{<=t-1} h_t ... degree d is the largest integer such that n^d <= 1/eps^2 ... working in the Hermite basis".

### E4. W. Wu, "Products of symmetric random functions" (ARC note): deduction-projection for Gelfand pairs
URL: https://www.alignment.org/content/files/2026/05/rand_sym_dedproj.pdf (12 pp., no date in the PDF; cited as "[Wu25] ... 2025" in the permanent note). TAG: [FT] (all 12 pages).
- ESTIMATES: E_x[prod_{i<=k} f_i(x)] for f_i i.i.d. ~ nu, a G-invariant law on L^2(X), X=G/H with (G,H) a compact Gelfand pair; MSE over the f's (Sec. 2.1).
- STATE: f~_{<=i} in W=direct sum of the first D+1 irreps of L^2(X), dim m=sum_{d<=D} dim V_d (Sec. 2.2). Step: f~_{<=i}=proj(f~_{<=i-1} f_i), projection onto the same subspace at every step, chosen so that all kernels K^{(i)}(x,y)=E_f[f(x)f(y)]^i share it (Sec. 2.2-2.4). Readout: int f~_{<=k} dmu.
- READOUT (derived): each step is linear in the carried f~ (multiply by f_i, orthogonal projection); final readout linear in f~.
- STRUCTURE: forward over factors; subspace chosen from the symmetry/spectrum of the tail distribution, before seeing instances; deterministic; not adaptive.
- ERROR: Thm 20 (p.10): projecting onto the first D+1 irreps each of k iterations gives total MSE <= c_D (tr K)^k, c_D := max_{d>D, j>=0} lambda_d^(j)/(tr K)^j; sampling with m samples has MSE about (tr K)^k/m, "to match sampling, we need c_D in O(m^{-1})", true under Examples 18-19 (simultaneously non-increasing spectrum, or exponentially increasing irrep dimension). The errors eps_i of the steps are uncorrelated (Sec. 2.3, uses G-linearity). Examples: sphere (SO(n),SO(n-1)) -> harmonic polynomials (Ex. 4); Boolean cube (Ex. 5-6).
- COST: k times one deduction-projection step; for uniform random 3-CNF on {+-1}^n (group (Z/2)wr S_n): O(km) time, O(m) space, MSE O((7/8)^k m^{-1}), matching sampling (Sec. 2.5, p.11). Each step multiplies by a clause (O(1) nonzero coefficients) and "Drop all but the m highest/lowest-degree coefficients".
- STATUS: theory; no experiments in the note.

### E6. M. Winer, "Worst-case-up-to-signs mechanistic estimations for products of functions" (ARC note): random-walk Fourier sketching
URL: https://www.alignment.org/content/files/2026/05/products_fourier_simple_v5.pdf (7 pp.; author per the blog post, not on the PDF). TAG: [FT].
- ESTIMATES: E_x[prod_i F(c_i,x)], x uniform on {-1,1}^d, F with few Fourier coefficients and random signs (linear factors <c_i,x>; q-local factors, incl. random #3-SAT).
- STATE: g^(i) carried in the Fourier basis, restricted to a candidate set C_i of characters, |C_i|<=B=max|C_i|. Deduction: coefficient-wise convolution g^(i+1)(T)=sum_S Fhat(c_{i+1},S) ghat^(i)(T xor S) (Eq. 1). Projection: keep only characters visited by a backward "subset-toggle" random walk (Sec. 5-7): eigenvalue lambda_{i,T}=Pr[S_i=T] of the covariance kernel of the tail; "Keep the Fourier characters most frequently visited by the subset-toggle walk on beta_i." (Sec. 6).
- STEPS (Sec. 7): (1) backward walk simulation, m walks, record visited sets S_i^(r) -> candidate sets C_i; (2) forward propagation truncated to C_i (terms with T xor S not in C_i are "the projected-away mass"); (3) output ghat^(k)(emptyset).
- READOUT: given the candidate sets, linear in the carried coefficients (derived); the candidate sets are random and depend on the instance's magnitudes beta, so the whole map is not a fixed linear functional.
- STRUCTURE: uses BOTH a backward pass (random walks from the tail end) and a forward pass; uses samples (O(m) random walks); adaptive (support chosen per instance). Quote (Sec. 10): "Unlike most of the other mechanistic estimators developed by ARC, this one is not deterministic: it involves sampling O(m) random walks. How, then, is it mechanistic? In short, because we are randomly looking at mechanisms to analyze but not assuming the mechanisms we fail to analyze look like the ones we do analyze."
- COST: O(k B |supp F|) plus O(k m) for the walk simulator; per propagated character per level O(d) (linear) or O(2^q) (q-local).
- ERROR: unrecovered MSE bounded by the largest unused eigenvalue times E_x[g^2] (Sec. 3, the L^2 sketching bound); for the walk-based version only a heuristic argument ("A character T with lambda_{i,T} >> 1/m is hit with high probability and is included; one with lambda_{i,T} << 1/m ... dropping it is safe in mean square", Sec. 7), no theorem in the note.
- STATUS: tested on one instance of each case: linear factors d=12, k=6 (c_i uniform on the unit sphere); q-local d=10, k=8, q=3; MSE vs budget m over 200 runs; "In the linear case the MSE of the mechanistic estimator decays faster than the 1/m rate of sampling"; q-local: the walk enumerates the full Fourier support at m about 2000 and the estimator becomes exact (Sec. 9, Fig. 1-2).

### E3-E6 common framing: Hilton, Robinson, Neyman, Christiano, Winer, Lecomte, W. Wu, G. Wu (May 15th, 2026), "Mechanistic estimation for expectations of random products" (ARC post)
URL: https://www.alignment.org/blog/mechanistic-estimation-for-expectations-of-random-products/ . TAG: [FT] (whole post; the four PDFs are E3-E6).
- Problem class: "M(c,x)=F(c_1,x)...F(c_k,x)" with no parameters theta (so no explanation pi): random halfspaces (c_i,x ~ N(0,I_n), F=1_{c_i.x>=0}), random #3-SAT (c_i a random 3-CNF clause, x in {-1,1}^n), random {0,1}-permanents (c_i in {0,1}^n, x in S_n, F(c_i,x)=(c_i)_{x(i)}, k=n). The post says {-1,1}-permanents are trivial to match "since all of the terms are pairwise uncorrelated".
- Scheme: head g^(i)=F(c_1,x)...F(c_i,x), tail f^(i)=F(c_{i+1},x)...F(c_k,x); deduction multiplies in the next factor, projection simplifies the head. "Not all information about this function is equally useful: the most useful information is the information that most improves our estimate of E_x[f^(i)(x) g^(i)(x)]". "mechanistic L^2 sketching": E_f[(E_x[f g~]-E_x[f g])^2] small relative to E_f E_x[f^2] and E_x[g^2]; "take g~ to be the orthogonal projection of g onto the space spanned by the eigenfunctions of this kernel [E_f f f^T] with the largest eigenvalues. The maximum mean squared error is then determined by the largest unused eigenvalue". "Generally speaking, we have been able to do this in cases where the function F and the distributions from which c_i and x are drawn have sufficient symmetry".
- Cumulant propagation is described as the same pattern: "in the 'deduction' step, we use the activation cumulants at one layer to deduce the activation cumulants at the next layer exactly, and in the 'projection' step, we drop terms that are insignificant relative to their computational expense" (fn.2: "In practice, the deduction and projection steps are not so cleanly separated, and instead we perform an approximate deduction step that combines both").
- Summary claim: "In each case, we roughly match or outperform random sampling up to logarithmic factors, except for the third case, where we are further behind." "We hope to describe more of the details of this approach in a future paper, but we may not get around to this for some time." Random-instance framing: "they are all choices in which M has no learned or worst-case parameters theta ... similar to randomly-initialized networks rather than trained networks".

### E7. G. Wu (2025), "Deduction-Projection Estimators for Understanding Neural Networks" (Harvard senior thesis)
URL: https://gabrieldwu.com/assets/thesis.pdf (identical bytes at https://gabrieldwu.github.io/assets/thesis.pdf), 90 pp., March 2025; work done as a visiting researcher at ARC. Page numbers below are the thesis's printed numbers (PDF page = printed + 5). TAG: [FT-part]. Read: abstract, ToC, Sec. 2 (DPE definition, 2.1), Sec. 5 intro and Remark 5.3, Sec. 6.1-6.2, 6.4-6.7. Not read: Sec. 1, 3, 4, 5.3-5.7, 6.3, appendices.
E7a. Deduction-Projection Estimator (definition) and covariance propagation for an MLP
- Def. 2.1 (informal, p.11): "A Deduction-Projection Estimate (DPE) of a mathematical quantity X is a calculation that consists of two types of operations: (Deduction) A provably exact operation to compute a state. (Projection) Replacing a complex intermediate state with a deductive approximation of that state lying in a simpler 'subspace.'" For f=f_l o ... o f_1: choose a tractable family D of distributions, a closeness notion, a projection; iterate D_i = Proj(f_i(D_{i-1})); output E[D_l].
- Covariance propagation (Sec. 2.1, p.12-13): D = all Gaussians, closeness = KL, projection = moment matching; MLP f(x)=A_l ReLU(...ReLU(A_1 x+b_1)...)+b_l under N(0,I). STATE: (mu^(i), Sigma^(i)) per layer. NONLINEARITY: E[ReLU(x)]_j = mu_j Phi(alpha)+sqrt(Sigma_jj) phi(alpha), alpha=mu_j/sqrt(Sigma_jj); the second moment E[ReLU(x_j)ReLU(x_k)] "requires using the bivariate normal CDF", which "has no closed form"; alternative: ReLU one coordinate at a time with a Gaussian projection after each ("an 'algebraic circuit re-write'"), "less accurate" but "possible to compute deductively". Forward only; deterministic (or numerical quadrature/MC for the 2-D CDF).
- Remark 5.3 (p.36): activation extrapolation (E8c-d) "can be viewed as a hybrid between a method that uses end-to-end sampling and a Deduction-Projection Estimator"; "During the exploratory phase of this work, we tried more mechanistic estimators based on covariance propagation (Section 2.1). Unfortunately, these did not perform well in practice because the ground-truth distributions of activations are highly non-Gaussian in our setting, especially on input distributions supported over a small number of points."
E7b. Gaussian Mixture Half-space Pruning (GMHP), Sec. 6.4 (p.50-53)
- ESTIMATES: accuracy Acc(theta)=Pr_{x~N(0,I_n)}[argmax_i l_i(x)=SAM(x)] of a small ReLU RNN (theta=(w_hi,W_hh,W_oh), hidden size d, no biases) trained for the second-argmax task SAM of n numbers; used as a differentiable training objective.
- STATE: after each single-neuron ReLU, a list of <= C Gaussian components (weight p, mean mu in R^n, covariance Sigma in R^{n x n}, matrix A in R^{d x n}, index i2), representing the joint law of (x,h^(t),y); each ReLU splits every component into a "positive" and a "negative" child (conditioning on a half-space by assumed-density-filtering moment matching, O(n^2) per half-space, Sec. 6.4.1), then all but the C highest-weight components are pruned and weights renormalised. Final: for each component, probability that the y-th logit is largest via n-1 more half-space constraints; weighted sum.
- READOUT: nonlinear (Gaussian half-space probabilities of moment-matched components). STRUCTURE: forward, breadth-first over the "tree" of sign patterns (Fig. 13), adaptive (pruning by weight), deterministic. COST: O(C n^3 d) (Sec. 6.4). ERROR: no guarantee; "GMHP itself is not a particularly good absolute estimator for accuracy -- it consistently underestimates the accuracy by many percentage points" (Sec. 6.6.2); output sensitive to constraint order (6.4.1).
- STATUS: tested (as a training objective) on 16 RNNs, (n,d) in {4,5,6,8} x {3,4,5,6}, C=10^4, fewer than 100 parameters (Sec. 6.5-6.7); thesis limitation (Sec. 6.7.1): the algorithms "are somewhat bespoke to the second-argmax task we chose to analyze".
E7c. Exact "Girard accuracy" for n=3 (Sec. 6.3): exact region enumeration with Girard's formula; exponential in n*d. [FT-part, only referenced; algorithm text not read.]

### E8. G. Wu and J. Hilton (2025), "Estimating the Probabilities of Rare Outputs in Language Models" (ICLR 2025)
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13211 (v1 17 Oct 2024; v2 6 Feb 2025; PDF read is the ICLR 2025 version). Blog: https://www.alignment.org/blog/low-probability-estimation-in-language-models/ (Gabriel Wu, October 18th, 2024) [FT-part]. Code: https://github.com/alignment-research-center/low-probability-estimation (page fetched, HTTP 200; not inspected). TAG (paper): [FT-part]. Read: Sec. 2-6.4, App. B.i-ii, abstract page. - E8a. ITGIS (Independent Token Gradient Importance Sampling), Sec. 3.1.1, App. B.i: proposal q(x)=prod_i q_i(x_i), q_i(x_i) proportional to p_i(x_i) exp(s_i(x_i)/T), s_i(x_i)=E_{x'~q}[grad_{x'} M_t(x')]_{i,x_i} (token treated as one-hot; average linear contribution to the target logit). Score and q defined in terms of each other, so built by iteration s^(0), s^(1), ... with exponentially weighted averages (alpha=0.9); final output = average over batches of the importance-sampling estimate p(x)/q(x) * 1[M(x)=t]. STATE: score tables s_i in R^{|V|} per position. Uses samples, gradients, adaptive proposals; unbiased per batch.
- E8b. MHIS (Metropolis-Hastings Importance Sampling), Sec. 3.1.2, App. B.ii: q(x) proportional to p(x) exp(M_t(x)/T); sampled by an MH random walk with a Greedy-Coordinate-Gradient-style proposal (random position, gradient-Boltzmann replacement token); output = average importance-sampling estimate after burn-in. Non-independent tokens.
- E8c. QLD (Quadratic Logit Decomposition), Sec. 3.2.1: take n samples of the pre-unembed activation v(x) in R^d; choose a direction d (the shortest vector, in whitened space, that makes the model output t; computed by the algorithm in App. D); split v=a+b with a in span(d), b in the whitened-orthogonal complement, "uncorrelated across the n samples"; "by treating the random vectors a and b as independent, we can use our n samples of each to obtain n^2 'synthetic' samples"; output (1/n^2)|{(i,j): a^(i)+b^(j) in S}| with S the acceptance region; computed in O~(n) by sorting. A presumption-of-independence method. Uses samples only at the last layer (no layer-by-layer propagation); "the theoretical quadratic gains from QLD" at budget 2^16 (2^32 > 1e9, Sec. 4).
- E8d. GLD (Gaussian Logit Difference), Sec. 3.2.2: fit a Gaussian to the logit difference Delta_t=M_t(x)-max_i M_i(x) from samples (mean mu, std sigma) and output exp(-((a mu/sigma + eps)^2)+b)+c with (a,b,c,eps) fit per input distribution (Sec. 4.2).
- RESULTS (Sec. 5): "both importance sampling methods outperform Quadratic Logit Decomposition, which in turn outperforms Gaussian Logit Difference. GLD is barely better than outputting an optimal constant"; ITGIS better on smaller models, MHIS on larger. Discussion 6.3: "In place of importance sampling, we may be able to use deductive estimates based on a presumption of independence ... Our Quadratic Logit Decomposition method is an early proof of concept of this, even though it is outperformed by importance sampling in our setting." Limitation 6.4: independent-token input distributions only; single-token behaviours.
- No error guarantee; READOUT: ITGIS/MHIS are sample averages (unbiased IS estimators); QLD/GLD nonlinear in sampled summaries.

### E10. Christiano, Hilton, Lincoln, Neyman, Xu (2024), "Towards a Law of Iterated Expectations for Heuristic Estimators"
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01290 (v1, 2 Oct 2024; 47 pp.); blog: https://www.alignment.org/blog/research-update-towards-a-law-of-iterated-expectations-for-heuristic-estimators/ (Eric Neyman, October 7th, 2024). TAG: [FT-part]. Read: abstract, Sec. 4-4.0 definitions, 5 intro and 5.1 start, 7.2-7.3; blog partial (grep and closing sections). Not read: Sec. 1-3, 4.1-4.2 details, 5.2, 6, appendices.
E10.0 Formalism: heuristic estimator G(Y | pi) takes "a mathematical expression Y and a formal 'heuristic argument' pi" (abstract). Properties discussed: linearity, respect for proofs, iterated estimation, error orthogonality, accuracy, multiaccuracy. No NN estimator; the estimators below are for expected products and permanents.
E10.1 Expected product of jointly normal variables E[Z_1...Z_n]=haf(Sigma) (Isserlis, Thm 4.2), Sigma with N(0,1) off-diagonals (Sec. 4, p.18-20). Estimators X_S=sum_{p in S} prod_{(i,j) in p} Sigma_ij for a set S of pairings of {1..n}; Claim 4.5: X_S is 1-accurate and self-accurate; Claim 4.6: E[X_{S1} X_{S2}]=|S1 intersect S2|. Pairing structures T (Def. 4.8: base, union, product) represent exponentially large S with X_T computable "in linear time in rsize(T)" (advice = the structure). Merging two such estimators by regression: Sec. 4.1: "finding a multiaccurate linear merge ... is #P-hard" for m=2 arguments; Sec. 4.2: a randomized approximately accurate merge, polynomial time only under non-overlap assumptions. Proposed/analysed; no experiments seen.
E10.2 Permanent of a Gaussian matrix (Sec. 5): row-sum estimate E_row(A)=(n!/n^n) prod_i sum_j A_ij (presume sigma(1..n) independent), column-sum and matrix-sum estimates; their linear-regression merge is accurate but "gives negative estimates for the permanent of some matrices with only positive entries".
E10.3 "Activation modeling as a potential path forward" (Sec. 7.3, p.35-36; blog end): "one could successively fit Gaussian models to each layer of activations, and then use the model to answer questions about the neural network's behavior. ... a much more sophisticated model class would be necessary." Loss would have "a term for the consistency between consecutive-layer activation models, and another term for explanation quality". Blog: "we have mostly focused on a different approach. Our new approach reframes the heuristic estimation problem as an activation modeling problem". Proposed only.

### E11. G. Wu (Jan 2024), "max of k Heuristic Estimator" (ARC draft)
URL: https://www.alignment.org/content/files/2024/06/max_of_k_writeup.pdf (7 pp.; linked from Hilton's post https://www.alignment.org/blog/formal-verification-heuristic-explanations-and-surprise-accounting/ , June 25th, 2024). TAG: [FT] (7 pages read; accuracy-estimate details of Sec. 5-6 read).
- ESTIMATES: the soft accuracy (expected probability on the correct answer, over distinct-integer inputs) of a TRAINED one-layer attention-only transformer (k=10 inputs in [n], n=64, hidden d=32) trained to output the max.
- STATE: bin propagation (Sec. 3.2): each real-valued random variable stored as b equiprobable bins, by the mean of each (b=10 normally; "mean propagation is equivalent to bin propagation when b=1"). Functions of one variable: apply f to bin means and sort. Bivariate: assume independence, take the b^2 evaluations, regroup into b bins. Mixtures and i.i.d. sums handled by square-and-multiply convolution, O(b^2 log b log n). The estimator conditions on x_m=v (position of the max) and on x_k, computes the attention pre-softmax and probability distributions "exactly modulo binning error" except where independence is presumed ("This is the first time we use the presumption of independence", Sec. 3.3), then the logits (four terms treated as independent) and the output probability; final estimate sum_v A_v Pr[x_m=v].
- STRUCTURE: operation-by-operation over the computation, forward, deterministic, uses case splitting on (x_m, x_k) and exact enumeration of the discrete inputs. COST: O(n^3 + n^2 log k b^2 log b) (Sec. 4); about 3 s per estimate.
- ERROR/STATUS: on 100 samples from "an overtrained model plus Gaussian noise sigma=0.1 on every parameter", estimated vs empirical soft accuracy: "on average, the estimate is about 2.5 percentage points higher the ground truth"; on the overtrained model (soft accuracy 0.99990) outputs 0.99992; more bins lowers the estimate (Sec. 5). Open questions (Sec. 6): surprise accounting not done; "Analog of Wick propagation" for applying the estimator to a specific input.

### E12. Neyman, Lecomte, Wu, Winer, Hilton, Robinson (Nov 18th, 2025), "Competing with sampling" (ARC post)
URL: https://www.alignment.org/blog/competing-with-sampling/ . TAG: [FT] (whole post).
E12a. Matching sampling principle (MSP) and the advice ("explanation") formulation
- Mainline MSP (section "Our actual MSP statement"): "For all architectures M (with parameters theta) mapping pairs (c, x) to R, there exists an estimator G_M mapping tuples (theta, pi, c, eps) to R, such that: For all parameters theta, there exists a short explanation pi (|pi| <= O(|theta|)), such that: For all tolerance parameters eps>0, G_M(theta,pi,c,eps) satisfies: It runs in time O((1/eps^2) Time(M_theta)). Its error is competitive with sampling, on average over random c: E_c[(G_M - E_x[M_theta(c,x)])^2] <= eps^2 E_c[Var_x[M_theta(c,x)]]. It is mechanistic."
- "Train and explain" variant: "For all 'training' algorithms T mapping random seeds s to parameters theta, there exists an 'explaining' algorithm E mapping random seeds s to explanations pi, with Time(E) <= O(Time(T))" ... error averaged over (c, s). "Note that our mainline MSP statement is the special case of the 'train and explain' formulation where T and E are both computationally unbounded". Footnote 7 (why |pi| short): "we will be interested in learning pi in parallel with learning the parameters theta, and so we will want to be able to do a backward pass through pi as quickly as doing a backward pass through theta."
- "Mechanistic" is informal: estimates "deductively, based on the structure of M_theta"; "A simple, though not entirely correct, heuristic for whether an estimation algorithm is deductive, is whether it avoids any random or pseudorandom sampling." Mean/covariance/cumulant propagation and "deduction-projection estimators -- estimators that successively model each layer of M_theta by finding the best-fit model from some parameterized class" are called mechanistic. Example behaviour: with only a mean-propagation explanation, if G_M checks 100 inputs and sees M=1 on all, "G_M should return 100/2^n"; a sampler "would return 1".
- Why only matching: "we expect that there will often be a range of eps-values for which the best mechanistic estimate is only slightly better than sampling-based estimation."
- Footnote 9: covariance propagation "does not require an explanation pi. However, some modifications of covariance propagation could require advice. For example, if eps is too large to allow for G_M to compute all of the covariances, then pi could advise G_M to only keep track of some particular covariances. Or, pi could tell G_M about some important third-order correlations to keep track of."
E12b. Estimators listed as progress (section "Our progress so far")
- (i) Random half-spaces: "build up a polynomial approximation of M(c,x) by considering one vector v_t at a time": H~_{<=0}=1, H~_{<=t}= best low-degree polynomial approximation to H~_{<=t-1} h_t; degree d is "the largest integer such that n^d <= 1/eps^2"; exact expectation of H~_{<=k} output; runtime O(k (log 1/eps)^2/eps^2) (fn.17), "working in the Hermite basis ... instead of the standard monomial basis". Generalised to "the expected product of symmetric random functions": k-CNF satisfaction probability, {-1,1} permanents. (See E3-E5.)
- (ii) Random MLPs, "cumulant propagation": start with cumulants of the Gaussian input; guess layer-l cumulants from a partial list of layer l-1 cumulants "going up to d-th order cumulants, where d is the largest integer such that n^d < 1/eps^2"; output the guessed mean of the output. Footnote 21: "Our strategy is to find a polynomial approximation to the ReLU function ... we can take the polynomial that minimizes mean squared error if X is assumed to be normally distributed with mean equal to our estimate of kappa(X) and covariance equal to our estimate of kappa(X, X). This is equivalent to taking the first several terms of the Hermite expansion of ReLU (appropriately centered and scaled)." Footnote 22: "it is more important to keep track of cumulants in which the same activation appears multiple times, so we need to keep track of some cumulants of order higher than d that involve repeated indices." Status then: "an empirical demonstration of competitiveness with sampling, and a proof sketch that we are working on expanding into a full proof" (the 2026 paper is E2). Related questions listed: random RNNs ("much more difficult ... because of the weight sharing"), narrow MLPs "(as opposed to the infinite-width limit)", CNNs, transformers.
- (iii) Two-layer MLP with a trained second layer (proposed, not complete): "Unlike in the case of random MLPs, we do not expect cumulant propagation to work. That's because, for worst-case v, the largest cumulants will not necessarily be the low-order ones." Approach: the final pre-activation f(x) is "well-approximated by a high-degree, n_0-variable polynomial in the inputs"; cumulants of a polynomial are infinite sums over tensor networks of the Hermite coefficient tensors (the "shin" tensors, d-dimensional n_0 x ... x n_0 for each degree d); "Our solution to the first problem [computing the shin-tensors] is to receive the shin-tensors as advice ... so long as 1/eps^2 < n_1 ... we have enough room in pi", and for train-and-explain "we believe that we can learn the shin-tensors in parallel with SGD". Second problem (summing the tensor networks): brute force if the hidden layer is huge; otherwise "finding efficient ways to contract arbitrary tensor networks and being able to notice when a tensor network can only contribute negligibly to the sum" (fn.24: drop a network if some tensor has small operator norm). Status: "substantial partial progress"; no code or paper seen.
E12c. "Compression as a possible MSP approach" (appendix): estimator G_M^inc for non-compressible theta with no explanation; G_M(theta,pi,c,eps) checks that pi is an efficient compression of theta and returns G^inc(pi,c,eps). Proposed only.
Also: Returning to ARC (Paul Christiano, AF, 4th Aug 2026, https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/vLFh8HP3hyNy9MCwe) [FT-part, grep only]: "ARC believes the bold conjecture that for all problems the approximation quality for mechanistic estimates improves faster than the best empirical method".

### E13. Tiny trained models (AlgZoo): region enumeration and symmetry-reduced estimates
Source: Hilton, "AlgZoo: uninterpreted models with fewer than 1,500 parameters", https://www.alignment.org/blog/algzoo-uninterpreted-models-with-fewer-than-1-500-parameters/ (January 26th, 2026) [FT-part]; Hilton, "Heuristic explanations for 2nd argmax models" (Dec 21, 2024, 6 pp.) https://drive.google.com/file/d/1t2rXhDQqWrOXRwjqstbrBPWiCmYlMts_/view [FT]; Robinson, "Symmetric RNNs" (Dec 2024, 7 pp.) https://drive.google.com/file/d/1qH61_WshTrgYh0YLj5vkONzk8x00zImN/view [FT-part, first page only].
- ESTIMATES: accuracy of RNNs trained for 2nd argmax. M_{2,2} (10 parameters, accuracy ~100%): Delta(x0,x1)=logit_1-logit_0 is piecewise linear with rays through the origin; after "standardizing", the pieces are checked region by region: "Using only a handful of computational operations, we were able to mechanistically estimate the model's accuracy to within under 1 part in 13,000, which would have taken tens of thousands of samples." The post notes this brute-force region check would not scale ("the number of regions would grow exponentially with depth ... would also prevent us from matching the efficiency of sampling").
- M_{4,3} (32 parameters, accuracy 98.5%), two estimators (Hilton note, Dec 2024): "original version": first derive h_2 exactly as a piecewise-linear function of (x0,x1) on 4 regions (using approximate equalities among the weights, e.g. a_3 ~ -a_0), then the 6 orderings of x0,x1,x2 give 14 likely + 4 unlikely subregions (18 subregions, each an intersection of 4 half-spaces; 56 + 28 further sub-subregions); in each likely subregion the ReLU-on indicators of z_3 are treated as random variables independent of z_3 but coupled to each other through a latent Bernoulli(q) variable (q=0.2, "a hyperparameter"), matched to the subregion probability p and sigma=sqrt(p(1-p)); each unlikely subregion gets accuracy 1/3; volumes by the polygonal generalization of Girard's theorem (about 71,000 operations by the note's crude count); and "George's approach": approximate symmetry (W_hi ~ U^T W'_hi, etc.), covariance propagation of the noise terms eps with presumptions of independence, 24 subregions in 6 dimensions "each of which is the intersection of 4 half-spaces. We avoid the problem of figuring out how to do this exactly for now and just use sampling" (about 7,680 operations). Tested on 256 copies of M_{4,3} with N(0,0.05^2) weight noise: RMS error about 3% (original) and about 5% (George's, lower bias); sampling with the same compute would reach about 0.8% and 2.3%, "So we are underperforming sampling by around a factor of 2 in terms of error (i.e, a factor of 4 in terms of compute). But the half-space algorithm could almost certainly be done more efficiently."
- M_{16,10} (432 parameters): "a brute force approach is no longer viable"; feature analysis only; no estimator; the post poses it as an open challenge: "Design a method for mechanistically estimating the accuracy of the 432-parameter model M_{16,10} that matches the performance of random sampling in terms of mean squared error versus compute."
- Structure: forward, case-splitting (region enumeration) plus Gaussian/independence closures; one variant uses sampling inside.

### E14. ARC statements on sampling hybrids, adaptivity and real data (Winer, "A Mike's-Eye View of ARC's Research", June 9th, 2026) [FT]
URL: https://www.alignment.org/blog/a-mikes-eye-view-of-arcs-research/
- "Using randomness to determine what aspects of a system to analyze more carefully doesn't violate mechanisticness. The only violation would be to assume the things we haven't carefully examined yet look like the things we have." Example (Liouville function): evaluate lambda on a chosen subset of m inputs and return (1/2^30) sum over the subset (a "valid mechanistic estimator", differing from sampling "only by a factor out front").
- Real data: "use the samples to infer the mean, and then mechanistically compute the expectation of f over the inferred Gaussian" (known variance, unknown mean, 1-D Gaussian prior); better constant in the 1/T MSE than averaging f over the T samples. "ARC has spent very little time thinking concretely about these problems."
- Footnote 2: "The general strategy is something we call cumulant propagation ... For most values of theta, including more and more cumulants will give a more and more accurate estimate in a way that beats Monte Carlo estimation."
- Definition of mechanistic: "never assume the input-output behavior you haven't seen looks like the behavior you have seen, for any object." Includes: never assume that "if you construct a ridiculously complicated object mechanistically from the model, and give that object 100 samples, the sample average equals that ridiculously complicated object's average behavior."

## 4. (iii) Ambiguities and open questions in the sources
1. Wu et al. say factorized "without changing its final output" (Sec. 4.3), but the repo's TODO.md (read ~10:56 UTC) says factorized-augmented is a different estimate from unfactorized augmented, and that cached outputs for k_max>=2 are stale after the truncation rule was changed to match the paper's Eq. (13)-(14). Which code version produced the paper's v2 figures is not stated.
2. State size of the factorized variant: the paper says "size n^{K-1}", with J=O(n) factors, but also that the factor list grows "by a constant number of new factors at each layer" (S.4.3); whether this is a fixed-size state per layer is not stated (cost goes like L^2 n^K).
3. The error conjecture MSE <= c_K (L/n)^K is stated for the MLP at width n with L hidden layers; the units are "mean squared error" (variance-normalised in the experiments, where outputs have variance Theta(1)). The paper does not state whether the conjecture is meant to hold uniformly for ReLU (it is proved only for polynomials; S.6 says tameness is not established).
4. The sharper halfspace error bound (O(eps^2/2^k) rather than k^2 eps^2/2^k) is asserted in the note and proved only in an unopened document.
5. "Third case, where we are further behind" in the random-products post: I read this as the permanent note (third in its list); the post does not name it.
6. Winer's note: the candidate-set walk uses magnitudes beta_{j,S}=E[alpha(c_j,S)^2]; in the "worst-case-up-to-signs" setting the note does not say whether the estimator reads the instance's actual magnitudes (an adaptive, instance-dependent choice) or a distributional average. I read it as instance-dependent (the sketch must beat symmetric low-degree truncation for adversarial magnitudes).
7. "Mechanistic" is informal in ARC's own text ("We do not have a formal definition"); the posts disagree slightly on whether randomness is allowed: Competing with sampling gives "whether it avoids any random or pseudorandom sampling" as a heuristic "not entirely correct", while Winer's Mike's-Eye View and the Fourier note say randomly choosing what to analyse is allowed.
8. The brief mentions "mixtures over activation patterns" and "case splitting" as possible ARC proposals. In the sources I opened, these occur only in G. Wu's GMHP (tiny RNNs, objective for training), in Hilton's Dec 2024 M_{4,3} estimates, and in the AlgZoo region check; Wu et al. 2026 does none of this. I found no ARC estimator that runs an explicit backward pass on the instance (backward information enters via the distribution of the tail in E3-E5, or via sampled walks in E6).
9. Whether the "explanation" pi for a trained net has been implemented anywhere: only the shin-tensor advice (E12b-iii) is described, as unfinished; the advice for trained nets in the wide-MLP post is a suspicion ("We suspect that this can be made to work"), and its own footnote 9 says the compression idea "doesn't work quite as we have stated it".
10. Thesis/paper naming: ARC's blog uses "deduction-projection estimator" for cumulant propagation too ("in the 'deduction' step, we use the activation cumulants at one layer to deduce the activation cumulants at the next layer exactly, and in the 'projection' step, we drop terms that are insignificant relative to their computational expense", random-products post, fn.2: "In practice, the deduction and projection steps are not so cleanly separated, and instead we perform an approximate deduction step that combines both").
