1. **[critical] E1–E3 cannot establish M1.** E1 tests achievable reconstruction by particular trained dictionaries; E2 tests oracle one-step distribution replacement; E3 tests oracle replacement at a cut followed by exact sampled propagation. None supplies a deductively propagated latent law or an error-versus-total-compute comparison with sampling. These are useful screening experiments, provided their conclusions stay within those limits.

2. **[critical] KT4 is not a necessary condition.** The downstream error of one fitted replacement distribution is not the minimum error achievable by the family. Reconstruction-optimal parameters need not minimize final-mean error; another fit or propagation approximation can compensate for its bias. Even exact propagation of the fitted replacement need not outperform approximate propagation on this particular estimand. Replace “no propagation rule can…” with “these fitted representations failed this screening threshold.” Passing KT4 also does not establish deductive feasibility.

3. **[major] KT1 cannot rule out sparse structure generally.** Failure establishes only that this training procedure found no qualifying dictionary within its search budget. Moreover, \(k\le n/4\) asks for compression beyond the existing ReLU neuron code, whose support averages roughly \(n/2\). That is a defensible engineering target, but different from “do random layers have sparse structure?” Report both the exact neuron-code support distribution and the stronger compression question.

4. **[major] Learned decoder bias is expressively fair but needs a zero-origin optimization control.** A learned `b_dec` can become zero, so the model class contains zero-origin codes. Initializing at the mean nevertheless makes discovery of the ReLU identity code an optimization question; centered ReLU states are generally dense. Train zero-fixed, zero-initialized and mean-initialized variants, including an identity initialization. Fixed TopK at \(n/2\) cannot reproduce paths having more than \(n/2\) active neurons; use variable support or \(k=n\) for the exact identity control.

5. **[major] Thirty epochs do not justify a negative conclusion at noise precision.** The default schedule is only 3,840 updates. There is no demonstrated convergence to relative MSE near \(10^{-4}\), or the tighter secondary-noise target. The supplied exploration logs contain PCA/neuron results, not evidence of SAE convergence. The doubled-training control changes the initialization seed, confounding duration with initialization, and covers only \(F=4n\). Save held-out learning curves, continue identical checkpoints, and use several restarts for cells near KT1. Failures far above threshold remain evidence about achieved performance, not nonexistence.

6. **[major] The covariance construction is substantially correct, but the conditional-family claim is broader than the implementation.** DENSE preserves covariance through PCA orthogonality. DENSEQ and SPARSE preserve empirical covariance through regression orthogonality and the residual covariance, up to pseudoinverse truncation, PSD clipping and numerical error. For SPARSE the generated state is
   \[
   \mu+(I+B)(\hat a-E\hat a)+\epsilon.
   \]
   However, its residual covariance is constant across latents. This tests an affine-mean, homoscedastic conditional Gaussian, not general \(N(m(\xi),\Sigma(\xi))\). Gaussian residuals can also move generated states outside their encoder’s original support region; this is a valid generative mixture, but not an exact encoder-consistent conditional distribution. Check held-out moment matching and report clipping/truncation errors.

7. **[critical] “Matched size” is not implemented or defined fairly.** A sparse latent carries support identity as well as \(k\) amplitudes, with up to \(\log_2 {F\choose k}\) support bits. Equal \(k\) therefore does not equal information or propagation cost. The proposed parameter-matched pairing requires \(F\le n/2\), while every tested dictionary has \(F\ge n\): there are no such pairs. `params_map=n*F` also omits the encoder, regression matrix \(B\), and representation of the latent distribution. Report support entropy, total parameters, and costs of representing and propagating the latent law; remove the nonexistent matched-parameter result.

8. **[major] Nonlinear encoding versus PCA does not isolate sparsity.** Both encoders see the full state; that alone is fair. The asymmetry is adaptive nonlinear support selection versus a fixed linear projection. DENSEQ changes the conditional decoder, not its PCA encoder. A sparse advantage could reflect nonlinear coordinates, extra support information, or more fitting capacity. Add a nonlinear dense bottleneck with comparable resources, or describe KT2 narrowly as “TopK versus PCA-based oracle models.”

9. **[major] E2 fits and scores on the same states.** `A2` supplies PCA, \(B\), residual covariance, oracle latents and the reference cumulants/means. Consequently, fitted models inherit fluctuations in their evaluation target; this is particularly favorable to flexible regressions. The conditional-variance half split does not repair this because the residual mean was fitted on all `A2`. Use separate fitting and scoring states, with selection followed by fresh confirmation. E1’s PCA baseline is likewise an in-sample eigentail, whereas SAEs are scored held out.

10. **[major] cap3/cap4 measure selected projected cumulants, not all higher-order structure.** They test the \(n\) contractions selected by the actual next-layer rows. That is relevant, but leaves other joint structure unmeasured. Small true cumulant norms make the ratios unstable, and empirical third/fourth cumulants have substantial sampling variance. Under Gaussianity their standardized standard errors are approximately \(\sqrt{6/N}=0.0048\) and \(\sqrt{24/N}=0.0096\) at \(N=2^{18}\), before non-Gaussian tail effects. Report uncertainty, denominator signal strength and absolute errors; treat near-zero denominators as unresolved.

11. **[major] E2’s sampling floor is comparator-specific.** `TRUE_indep` does not estimate the floor for models sharing the target’s latent and activation noise. G/EW use analytic readouts of the reference sample’s moments, whereas latent models add residual sampling noise. These differences can change KT2/KT3 rankings. For ReLU, integrate the conditional Gaussian next-layer activation analytically and average only over held-out latents. Estimate paired uncertainty over whole paths, preserving neuron correlations. Edgeworth readouts are useful diagnostics, but are neither K3f propagation nor bounds on achievable error; their assigned cap values are bookkeeping, not measured capture.

12. **[major] The residual diagnostics cannot establish conditional Gaussianity.** Marginal kurtosis after fitted variance normalization can hide conditional skewness, non-Gaussian dependence and latent-dependent covariance. Conversely, imperfect conditional-mean/variance regression can make a genuinely conditional Gaussian fail. Signed medians and p90s can miss negative kurtosis and important near-boundary neurons. Test held-out conditional skewness and absolute excess kurtosis, residual–latent dependence, and relevant projections. The sparse 512-feature regression and dense quadratic regression also have different capacities and regularization; DENSEQ’s own residual diagnostics are missing.

13. **[major] E3 grants substantial oracle information.** Fresh true states provide the exact joint support-and-amplitude law of the sparse latent, together with fitted true-state moments. A deductive method must construct and propagate that law from weights. Dense models receive analogous oracle coordinates, but equal \(k\) does not equal the amount of supplied information. Label E3 as an oracle replacement diagnostic and separately measure the cost/error of a finite latent-law representation.

14. **[major] E3 has two concrete statistical implementation defects.** In `run_main.py:488–508`, models consume successive random draws from one generator; downstream noises are not common across models as preregistered. Shared cut states provide only partial coupling. At line 514, subtracting \(1/N_3\) assumes model output variance equals truth variance. The appropriate model contribution is
   \[
   \frac{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}_{q}(a_L)}
        {N_3\,\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}_{p}(a_L)}.
   \]
   Accumulate model second moments, reuse explicit downstream noise arrays, and report uncertainty. The subtraction also leaves finite-fit error from the E2 parameters; it does not isolate population representation bias.

15. **[major] The kill rules need explicit statistical and logical definitions.** KT2 selects the best sparse setting and compares noisy point estimates without a margin or confirmation set. KT3 references KT2’s cells, which include \(k\), while optimizing over \(k\), leaving the denominator ambiguous. KT4 needs a same-network, same-noise K3f result, not the contextual median bar. KT5 must specify whether failure is required at both widths or either width. Define ties, missing/failed runs, uncertainty, model selection and exact cell counts. A single evaluation weight seed cannot support a general conclusion about random networks; replication should be mandatory for family-level claims.

16. **[major] KT1’s refit and “bits” labels need qualification.** In `sae.evaluate`, least squares uses the first \(k\) indices even when their TopK activations are zero, and permits negative coefficients. It therefore evaluates a larger signed-code model than the trained nonnegative SAE; reported `L0` describes the raw code, not the refit. Report separate support counts and semantics. The bits formula is a conditional Gaussian-channel KL under a specified coupling, not code length or support entropy. Noise units below one still permits roughly \(0.721n\) bits per layer. For E4, the path-KL identity requires the proposed replacement to define each transition conditional on the same preceding state.

17. **[major] E2’s tanh readout omits LayerNorm.** `elem_act` uses `tanh(z)`, while `SNet.act` uses `LN(tanh(z))`. This matches the preregistered pre-LN diagnostic, but does not evaluate the network’s actual next-state mean or closure. LayerNorm depends on the joint vector distribution, so per-neuron cumulants cannot resolve that difference. Add actual post-LN metrics. Static inspection found no corresponding obvious ARC weight, bias or noise-indexing error in `nets.py`.

18. **[minor] Several execution details weaken reproducibility.** `--skip-e1-train` is parsed but unused; E3 cuts absent from `--layers` cause a cache failure; long-training controls default to off; saved SAEs are converted to float16 despite noise-precision claims. AuxK operates on already-ReLUed activations, so persistently negative dead features receive no revival gradient. Validate arguments, save full-precision checkpoints and training histories, and verify reconstruction after reload. E4/KT5 are not implemented in the supplied driver, so their execution cannot be validated from these files.
