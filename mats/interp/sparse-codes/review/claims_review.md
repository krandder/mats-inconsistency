Checked 2026-10-29, read-only. `scratch/analyze2.py` reproduces `results/tables.md` exactly; inspected source hashes match their latest entries in `prereg_hashes.txt`. Most headline numbers reproduce, but several central interpretations do not follow.

1. **[critical] The experiments cannot establish the family-wide negative conclusion.** E1 measures achieved reconstruction under one training procedure; E2 measures oracle one-step replacement; E3/E5 insert fitted distributions at cuts and sample the real downstream network. E4 tests particular learned transcoders under two shifted laws. None establishes impossibility of compact sparse descriptions or sample-free propagation at depths growing with width. Replace “no compression beyond the gate pattern” and “that law is a dense object” with conclusions about the tested models. “We found no route” is defensible as a research assessment, not an experimental exclusion.

2. **[major] E5 does not hold feature marginals and feature covariance exactly fixed.** `run_e5.py::Copula` uses empirical quantiles and correlations of censored normal scores; it does not fit the original feature Pearson covariance. Recolouring then matches the **decoded state’s** estimated moments, not the original feature covariance while preserving the decoder. Marginals are estimated, rare features are discarded, and recolouring uses finite samples. Recorded held-out SPARSECOP covariance discrepancies are approximately **0.4–3.1%**. Thus “they differ only beyond second order” and “fixes … exactly” are false as stated. The result supports a deficit of this fitted copula construction, not a clean isolation of higher-order dependence.

3. **[major] “The error comes from the chain, not the closure” is contradicted by E4.** At \(n=64,F=256,k=32\), shift law, depth 8: sampled-chain error is **7.7e−4**, better than neuron K=2’s **1.1e−3**; closure error is **1.8e−3**, worse. Closure-versus-chain error is **9.3e−4**. The closure changes the comparison’s outcome. Moreover, the quoted **5e−5–3e−3** range omits settings with enormous closure errors; it needs an explicit setting restriction.

4. **[major] KT6 is incomplete, not globally settled.** Amendment 4 requires failure in **at least 5 of 9** seed-65 cells. Present data contain **0 qualifying failures among 3 observed cells**; six unobserved cells can still trigger KT6. The summary’s “does not fire on 3 of 9 cells” should say exactly that. `analyze2.py` also introduces an unregistered `max(exact_error, 1e-9)` floor; it does not change these observed verdicts but is rule drift.

5. **[major] Sampling-floor point estimates cannot support “at least 240–860×” improvements.** E5 exact-latent estimates include **−4.65e−7**, **−4.61e−7**, **1.56e−6**, and **1.44e−6**. Substituting \(10^{-6}\) for unresolved errors is not a confidence bound. E3/E5 provide no uncertainty intervals for these selected minima. The **3–8× copula/Gaussian** comparison is numerically supported; the asserted lower bounds against the exact latent are not.

6. **[major] Test-set selection does not “flatter every family equally.”** E3/E5 select minima on their scored data, as preregistered. Sparse families have many candidates, G has one, and candidate errors have different variances and correlations. Debiasing individual squared errors does not debias their minimum. This particularly compromises floor-level ratios and “most of what the sparse latent buys.” E2’s FIT/SEL/CONF separation avoids this particular problem.

7. **[major] The “matched latent size in bits” result is not an equal-size comparison.** `scratch/infomatch.py` reports PCA candidates **below and above** each sparse bit estimate; it neither evaluates equal budgets nor specifies interpolation. It also takes the better DENSE/DENSEQ error on CONF, so the comparator is not consistently “linear PCA.” The reported **1.4–10× at matched bits** is therefore insufficiently defined. Support numbers are sums of marginal binary entropies, an **upper bound**, not measured joint support information; amplitude numbers are a Gaussian-channel proxy, not a demonstrated coding rate.

8. **[major] The direct dependence statistics concern the reconstruction, not the full state model.** `featcorr.py` computes covariance contributions for \(Df\) and variances of \(W'Df\), omitting the conditional residual and added noise. The **36–69%** and **35–69%** ranges reproduce, but should be labelled decoded-reconstruction statistics. Also, neuron “on/off” phi is computed after centring activations: its indicator is \(a_j>E[a_j]\), not the ReLU gate \(a_j>0\). The neuron/feature comparison needs that qualification. The **0.05–0.08 near-boundary-gate** reference is not measured by the supplied tables/JSON; cite its external source.

9. **[major] E4 repeats an incorrect Monte Carlo correction.** `run_e4.py` subtracts `1/NC` from sampled-chain and closure-versus-chain errors. Correct normalization requires
   \[
   \operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}_{chain}(a_L)/
   \bigl(N_C\,\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}_{truth}(a_L)\bigr).
   \]
   Chain output variances are not accumulated. E3 implements this correction properly. This does not change KT5, which uses deductive closure errors, but weakens the chain/closure attribution.

10. **[major] KT1’s numerical verdicts pass the audit; its convergence qualification remains unresolved.** No tested eligible dictionary crosses one noise unit. But amendment 2 says **1–3-unit cells are settled by controls**, while controls cover layers 8/24 only; near-threshold layers 16/31 lack direct controls. The report itself shows continued improvement at layer 24. State “threshold failed in tested runs; convergence unresolved,” rather than treating these cells as established absence of sparse structure.

11. **[minor] KT2–KT5 otherwise follow the amended rules.** KT2 has **14 wins**, exceeding the required 12 even counting missing cells as losses; KT3 has **8**, exceeding 6. KT4 does not fire: the registered minimum is **−2.3e−7**, while **3.2e−6** is a valid, more conservative \(k=16\) example. KT5 fires because no setting beats the specified neuron baseline under **both** unseen laws at both widths. These verdicts should remain distinct from broader claims about capability.

12. **[minor] Several wording and numerical qualifications remain.** The reconstruction sequences, 185-bit conversion, correlation ranges, E2 errors, E3 \(k=16\) comparison, and E4 headline values are substantially supported. However, the \(n=256\), layer-8 KT1 minimum is **56.452**, reported as **57**; the \(n=64\), layer-16 minimum is **2.247**, reported as **2.3**. “Exact Gaussian closure” is also too strong for a **60-term truncated** Mehler series. Finally, replication is only at width 64; E4 has no weight-seed replicate, and fixed depth 32 does not test the M1 depth-with-width regime.
