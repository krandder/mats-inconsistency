1. **high — §4:** “the profile is a property of the law, not of a chosen parametrisation.” Counting latent *variables* is not invariant: any latent vector can be renamed one variable. Nonnegative rank counts latent **states**, and does not equal task-specific Nerode complexity. **Correction:** define SFU relative to the task and law, with explicit state cardinality, approximation norm, precision, and contraction-cost budgets. Otherwise arbitrary computation fits inside “one latent” and “sum out.”

2. **high — T2:** “two contents with non-proportional messages are Nerode-inequivalent.” This holds for recovering the full boundary posterior under suitable separating observations; it need not hold for a particular target posterior. Distinguishable nuisance-latent messages can leave the target unchanged. **Correction:** specify target-sensitive richness, and retain the target until the root rather than summing out every latent that closes.

3. **medium — T2b:** “the exact message is the whole history E_S: T |S| numbers”; “for the predictive of the query, generically.” The cross-block argument establishes injectivity for recovering the **full covariance posterior**, but does not establish predictive minimality or a real-coordinate lower bound without encoding regularity. **Correction:** retain the full-posterior result; prove predictive distinguishability separately and state continuity/precision assumptions for the dimension claim.

4. **medium — D3:** “an exponential-family log-likelihood has rank d + 1 in log space and infinite rank as a likelihood.” Neither is universally true. Carrier terms can add another rank-one component; finite-support exponential families have finite likelihood rank. Moreover, parameter-versus-observation rank differs from the item-cut rank defining D3. **Correction:** specify the matricisation, remove parameter-independent terms explicitly, and restrict infinite rank to appropriate families.

5. **medium — D4:** “D1 is the knowledge-compilation notion of structured decomposability.” Structured d-DNNFs and deterministic Nerode messages are different resources. A width-\(w\) structured circuit can require a vector of \(w\) truth values, allowing \(2^w\) residual classes. **Correction:** state the quantitative simulation rather than identifying the notions. Amarilli et al.’s compilation theorem does not itself establish their equivalence.

6. **medium — §3:** “items are pairwise dependent (a clique, treewidth N - 1).” Marginal pairwise dependence does not imply a clique in a conditional-independence graph; a Markov chain is a counterexample. **Correction:** establish conditional dependence given all other observations for the particular nondegenerate shared-latent model.

7. **medium — T3 / Liu–Ihler:** “one-shot combination of local estimates loses information exactly to the extent the family deviates from a full exponential family.” Liu–Ihler establish an **asymptotic, second-order result for combining local MLEs**, under regularity, not this unrestricted exact statement. **Correction:** name those restrictions. Also spell out KPD’s statistic/model regularity; parameter-independent support alone does not exclude pathological scalar encodings.

8. **medium — T3a:** “PROVEN UNDER analyticity … uniformly in x.” Uniform exponential approximation needs a common complex neighbourhood **and a uniform analytic bound**, possibly after removing irrelevant constants. The experiments’ unbounded Gaussian observations/prior do not automatically satisfy a compact, uniformly bounded theorem. **Correction:** state those bounds and handle prior tails. The \(2N\epsilon\) KL bound itself is valid when the global sup-error premise holds.

9. **high — T4:** “log-posterior error about N (log N / N)^{(p+1)/2} … PROVEN UNDER local asymptotic normality.” Taylor remainder gives this rate **inside the window**. LAN and a consistent pilot do not alone control posterior normalisation or KL outside it. **Correction:** add posterior-concentration, prior, and tail-extension assumptions; distinguish local likelihood approximation from a full-posterior theorem.

10. **high — T4 citation:** “root-N pilot … true for … Gaussian location mixtures; Wu and Yang.” Wu–Yang do not give unrestricted root-\(N\) estimation of mixture parameters: their general mixing-distribution rates include \(N^{-1/(4k-2)}\). **Correction:** restrict to the identifiable common-location models actually simulated and prove their moment pilot directly. Huang–Huo and Jordan–Lee–Yang support related distributed methods, not the entire stated theorem.

11. **high — N1c/N2:** “KL(Bayes || approx).” Both scripts normalise predictions only on an **oracle-selected grid around the exact posterior**. Approximation mass elsewhere is ignored. Their windowed approximations also assign zero density outside the window, making full forward KL infinite whenever Bayes has positive tails. **Correction:** label these conditional-grid diagnostics, or use a positive global tail extension and globally normalised KL. N1c additionally records 2.5% window failures at \(N=64\).

12. **medium — N1d:** “the same budget spent over two rounds”; “two rounds, 2 + 3 numbers”; “three rounds, 7 numbers.” The comparison uses different budgets. The implemented pilot sends two sums, followed by two derivative sums per additional round: **4 and 6 numbers**, not 5 and 7. **Correction:** fix the counts and compare equal-budget encoders before attributing the advantage solely to rounds.

13. **medium — N2:** “optimal one-round linear code”; “the two-round size grows polynomially.” The SVD is optimal only for the sampled matrix and empirical RMS objective. The implemented tensor basis has \((p+1)^D\) coefficients, which is exponential in \(D\); the polynomial total-degree construction was not run. **Correction:** report empirical linear ranks and tested upper bounds, not general message-size lower bounds or measured polynomial scaling.

14. **high — T5:** “The Bayes version … is the same”; “So World FA’s failure was … a learning failure, not a representational one.” The disjointness reduction establishes the keyed Boolean task. It does not establish the asserted Bayesian lower bound or expressivity of the complete trained architecture. **Correction:** supply a separate Bayesian indistinguishability argument and full architectural construction; downgrade the causal diagnosis.

15. **high — T6a:** “Sharp routing is exactly the hard regime”; “the logit gap must be at least about log(N / delta).” The nearest-competitor gap need not grow as \(\log N\); the necessary condition concerns log-sum-exp, or the total logit range. The \(QK^\top/d\) calculation and range bound \(2B^2\) are correct. **Correction:** state the necessary range bound and Alman–Song’s **worst-case** theorem with \(d=O(\log n)\), inverse-polynomial error, and its constant quantifiers. It does not characterise all sharp routing as hard.

16. **medium — T7:** “n = 4/rho^2 … repeated T rho^2 / 4 times.” This construction requires integral lengths/repetitions and covers only the stated \(\rho\ge2/\sqrt T\) regime. **Correction:** handle rounding and prove the smaller-\(\rho\) regime using the length-\(T\) gap-Hamming instance. Define correlation as the normalised inner product of sign strings and specify public randomness for coordinate sampling; this is not automatically a Pearson-correlation theorem.

17. **high — T8:** “multi-hop lookups are volume-law for any one-pass hierarchy.” A tree pass is not generally a communication round in the cited two-party model. 21. **medium — T11c:** “same f” with “generic real a”; “P(f > t) … #P-complete.” Complexity classes require finite encodings, and a probability is not directly a #P counting function. **Correction:** use binary-encoded integer/rational weights and threshold; state that computing \(2^nP(f>t)\) is a #P-complete counting problem in the worst case.
